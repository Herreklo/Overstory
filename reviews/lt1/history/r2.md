# Step 3, R2: the date, the history include and the page quad

22 October, on the M4 (Metal), Unity 6000.6.6f1. Built in `db34f2a` (the include, the arms, the quad) and the commit that adds this file (the runner's keywords made in `Start`).

What was built:
- **The date.** `_OS_Date` is set by `Overstory.World.FabricDate`. Its x is the year with the month as a fraction, y the day of the year, z the hour, w reserved. `LT1Stone.Stage` sets it to 1989.
- **`Shaders/History.hlsl`:**
  - `OS_History(address, layer)` reads the layer's page from the global array `_OS_PagesRGBA` (P0, P1 and H1, a slice each), through the piece table. The table is a global `StructuredBuffer` read in the vertex stage by UV3's piece index and passed flat.
  - The law is a named stub, `OS_HistoryLaw`, which returns the stored input. It already receives the piece's resets and age.
  - Under `OS_HISTORY_OFF` it reads nothing and returns the material's quiet means.
- **The two arms:**
  - `OS_HISTORY_OFF` (`history-off`): in today's `Stone.shader`, the vertex history interpolator is gone. The six quantities R1 will move to pages (polish, lichen years, moss, water held, the water layer, years since bare) are held at the material's quiet means. The seed, face kind and sky/ground bake are kept.
  - `OS_HAZE_OFF` (`haze-off`): `OS_Atmosphere` returns the lit colour unchanged, in Stone, Street, Tree and AirMotes, its only callers.
- **The quad:** `LT1History`, with `Experiments/LT1/History/HistoryQuad.shader`.

## R0's two Metal questions

- **Does `EditorUtility.CompressTexture` keep our mips?** Yes.
  - Two 256×128 textures share mip 0. One has our own mip 1, the box filter inverted, which no filter of mip 0 makes; the other has the box. After BC7 at Best, both keep 3 mips.
  - Their compressed mip 1 bytes differ.
  - Ours decodes to ours within BC7's error (mean 1.6 to 1.8, max 13 steps), and is 84 to 89 steps (mean) from the box.
  - Three 1024×512 slices with 3 mips compress in 0.19 s.
- **Does Metal copy a `Texture2D` into an array slice?** Yes, for RGBA8 and BC7, every mip.
  - `Graphics.CopyTexture(tex, 0, mip, array, slice, mip)` was used into a non-readable array (`Apply(false, true)`), so nothing CPU-side was drawn.
  - The quad drawn from it is bit-identical to the quad drawn from a CPU-filled array, at 2560, 640 and 160 wide (magnified, about 1:1, and minified to mip 2).
  - `SystemInfo.copyTextureSupport`: Basic, Copy3D, DifferentTypes, TextureToRT, RTToTexture.

All three answers are in `r2-pages.json`, with the page and frame hashes.

## Against R0 and the schemas

- **BC7 error is about four times R0's bound on these pages.**
  - Mip 0: mean 1.8 to 1.9, 99th 6 to 7, max 11 to 15 steps, against R0's 0.46/2/5.
  - Mip 2: 3.7 to 4.9 / 17 to 18 / 27 to 34.
  - Our synthetic pages carry ±5-step grain, uncorrelated in all four channels. That may be harsher than R0's pages; R1's real pages will tell.
  - By R0's own rule (over 2/255, H1 becomes two BC5 pages), these pages would go to BC5.
- **The 4-texel grid keeps BC blocks inside a chart only at mip 0.** A block at mip *m* covers 4·2^*m* texels of mip 0, so at mip 2 it spans two of the quad's 8-texel-high charts. That is the jump in error at mip 2. Keeping three mips block-pure needs a 16-texel grid, or accepting the error at mip 2.
- **The GPU piece entry is 16 bytes, not `chunk-format.md`'s 24.** It holds the first slice, two pairs of u16 resets, and the age (low u16) with flags (high u16). Ids stay on disk.
- **History off holds six quantities, not the whole vertex record.** The seed, face kind and bake stay on the vertices.
- **The quiet means are vertex means** over the gully's FBX meshes (`LT1History.QuietMeans`), set in `LT1Stone`:
  - slate: polish 0.099, lichen years/200 0.237, moss 0.171, held 0.010, years since bare/200 0.569, water 0.375;
  - rubble: 0.023, 0.385, 0.108, 0, 0.555, 0.269.
  - The gully has no granite, so granite takes the slate's until a granite stair is measured.

## R2b: per sample, minified

22 October, the verdict's decision 7: one run of two rounds, the quad alone, with R2's arms beside the new ones. The summary is in `reviews/perf/2026-10-22/history-r2b/run-1/summary.json`.

The arms are options after a quad scene's format:
- `half`: that scene's offscreen frames at 1280×720.
- `two`: P0 and H1 only (the quad's local `OS_QUAD_TWO_PAGES`).
- `tile<k>`: each cell's UVs span k times its chart (`LT1History.QuadMesh(k)`). The verdict's ×4 is barely minified (LOD 0.26), so ×8 (LOD 1.26, mips 1 and 2) was added. The street stop's grazing treads read at mip 2 or past it (estimated).

At ×1 the UVs are R2's to the bit. The golden views draw neither the quad nor the harness, so they were not re-rendered.

What it shows:
- **The cost is per pixel.** BC7 at a quarter of the pixels costs a quarter (0.16 against 0.66), with the vertices and piece reads unchanged.
- **RGBA8 falls only to 0.4 to 0.7.** Its full-size arms are noisy here (R2 had +0.85), and it seems to pay for the doubled texels a pixel.
- **A magnified page costs 0.11 ms.** Dropping P1 saves that in BC7, so about 0.33 ms of on minus off is not per page.
- **Minified, a sample costs about three times as much** (BC7: 2.8 at ×4, 3.5 at ×8). RGBA8 and BC7 agree within 0.1 ms, so the cost is not bandwidth. It jumps already at LOD 0.26, which points at the blend of two mips.
- **The presented frames' GPU time agrees:** +0.9 to +1.1 ms magnified, +2.4 at ×4, +3.4 at ×8.
- **Without those 0.33 ms, a minified page is still 0.48 to 0.67 ms.**

**Against the budget: fail.**
- Magnified, a BC7 page's 0.11 ms passes 0.17 (0.22 by R2's division).
- Minified, a sample is 0.59 to 0.78 ms: 3.5 to 4.6 times 0.17, and 2.4 to 3.1 times the 0.25 line.
- The stone's P0 and H1 minified would cost about 1.2 to 1.6 ms a screen, against 0.35 and S2's 0.5.
- By decision 7, texels coarsen (S3). One arm is worth a run first: a sampler that takes the nearest mip bilinearly instead of blending two.
- The grid's triangle edges add perhaps a tenth to a fifth more lanes than one surface would. That changes no verdict.

## R2c: why minified costs

22 October, decision 7 again: one run of two rounds (`run-2`), the quad alone, each arm at ×1 and ×8 beside a fresh control. Each arm is a quad-local keyword (`OS_QUAD_<ARM>`, set by `PerfRunner` from the scene name) that defines `History.hlsl`'s new measuring hook, `OS_PAGE_SAMPLE`. Undefined, the include preprocesses to the same text as before, so the golden views were not re-rendered; the quad's fragment variants go from 4 to 40. Every sampling path cost the same, so two arms were added: `quiet` keeps all three samples (the Metal source in the shader cache still has three `.sample` calls) and writes a flat colour; `noisy` samples nothing and writes a hash at texel rate, as busy as a minified page.

**Finding.** Sampling is not the cost. Every path and format costs the same within 0.1 ms (SampleGrad 0.4 ms more). The quiet arm keeps every sample and removes the whole excess (×8: +2.39 to +0.05 ms; GPU time +3.2 to −0.3); the noisy arm reads nothing and reproduces 93% of it. The cost follows how busy the output colour is, paid in the passes after the draw (most likely their framebuffer compression; which pass is unmeasured). The include's default path stays: no arm beats it.

**Against the budget: pass.** A minified sample costs about 0.02 ms (at most 0.04 within the spread), against 0.17. The two-page quiet arm, the stone's P0 and H1 read minified, costs +0.04 ms a screen against S2's 0.5. Texels need not coarsen for cost; S3 does not trigger. R2's and R2b's quad figures measured output busyness, not sampling, and `measuring.md`'s pass budget, taken on quiet frames, understates a busy screen by about 2.2 ms. History's real cost is its change to the stone's already-textured output: R7 measures it in the gully.
