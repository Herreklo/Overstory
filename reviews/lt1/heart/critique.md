# A master's critique of the heart's light (20 October)

Øystein asked for the work to be judged as a master would: critically, at the highest bar, with a line between what must be approved now and what can be decided later. Two reviews went into this: a fresh reviewer on Fable 5.1, who saw only the frames and the bar (none of the reasoning behind them), and Claude's own as the technical artist who built it. They agree on almost everything; where they differ, it is said.

Frames: `night/` (blue hour in sea fog, the moods), `light/` (the four hours, the value sheet), `style/` (the three styles). "Neutral 3" means `night/sheet-neutral.png`, camera 3.

## The verdict

Not yet stunning. One frame is already a picture: Gully Steps in sea fog (neutral 3), a chain of lamps climbing the steps, each softer than the last, the near lamp washing the wall, the ground darkest, the lamps lightest. That is Grimshaw's structure and it is right. The town at night from the far stop (neutral 5) passes the project's own test: the plan reads as strands of light. The 16:00 roofs (light sheet 1) have the right warm and cool split and air laid in bands. Everything else is a capable system showing what it can do, not yet a picture anyone designed.

And the process has a gap: no target frame has been painted yet, so the bar is being set by the person who builds the work. That is the first thing to fix.

## What costs the beauty most, in order

1. **Value design.** No reserved dark, no single white. The morning and the drizzle sit in one 40 to 60 percent grey in every camera (`light/sheet-values.png`); at night sky, walls and water share a band. Silhouettes dissolve (neutral 1: near roofs, water and sky one value). Focal fights: on Harbour Row (neutral 9) the shopfronts and their white fascia beat the lamp at the vanishing point. The nearest unlit wall in neutral 3 should be the darkest mass and is the tone of the far houses. *A master's fix:* a value key for each hour and camera before any colour (where the darkest dark lives, what the one white is, how much sits in the middle), drawn as a five-value notan and matched in greyscale.
2. **The sky.** Tiled noise blobs: the same smudges repeat between frames; at 16:00 soft puffs with no underside and no lit top, the same size at the zenith as at the horizon, so the sky has no perspective; at blue hour comet streaks that read as motion blur; the night sky too light and even. *Fix:* a designed sky: a gradient that darkens to the zenith and glows low at the sun's bearing, two or three bands of cloud flattened towards the horizon, their undersides caught by the last light or by the town's glow.
3. **The fog.** One density and one colour everywhere: it lifts the quay at three metres as much as the far shore. Real sea fog is a bank, thick over the cold water and thinner over the land, with roofs coming through it and the glow of lit streets in it. From the Roads (neutral 6) the frame is empty. *Fix:* fog as a bank with structure, and the town's glow; the 16:00 haze already has the banded structure the fog should inherit.
4. **The lights' craft.** Lamps are flat glowing boxes with a perfect disc of halo; halos do not grow with distance; far lamps go to hard dots where fog would make them larger, softer and dimmer. Windows are identical flat cream rectangles at full strength, with no spill onto the reveal, the wall or the pavement (Hopper's trapezoid of window light on the street is the cure for "consistent but empty"). The lamps' eras do not read apart: all one amber. *Fix:* windows that light the world round them, each varied a little in warmth and brightness (seeded texture, claiming nothing); lamps and windows softened by the fog alike; sodium visibly orange, incandescent warm white, mercury blue-green.
5. **Colour.** At 16:00 the sun's colour tints the fog, the sky and every surface until the frame is one warm monochrome, a sepia filter. The night's blue is unchosen (a dull slate), and a lilac band creeps into one sky. Chroma is even everywhere instead of placed at the focus and drained elsewhere.
6. **Shade.** Right in direction, wrong in body: one flat blue-grey with no darkening where wall meets ground and no warm bounce from the lit ground; at night the steps' risers and treads do not separate.
7. **Cameras.** Composed to check the layout, not to make pictures: camera 1 with its horizon dead centre and a jumble of cropped boxes in front; camera 2 the strongest street stop but its lane dies round the bend (it should end on a light); camera 3 near-symmetrical, which suits a stair, but it would climb better from 2.5 m; camera 5 the best night frame with a vast empty field of water; camera 6 with no foreground; camera 7's park a dead plane; camera 8 with the tower dead centre.
8. **The moods.** Distinct, but presets, not one light seen six ways. Hopeless lifts the blacks until it reads as thicker fog, which is a claim about the weather; hopeful paints shade pink; grand goes violet; success only oversaturates. A cinematographer never changes the hue of the source; he moves exposure, contrast and how much colour the shade keeps.
9. **The styles.** Nocturne is the best of the three in fog (warm grey, restrained) but at 16:00 it reads as an overcast afternoon. Luminous gives the most striking single image (the steps at 16:00) but its cobalt shade is uniform full chroma, so the eye goes to the shade, and it will fight real materials (Stavanger's white, ochre and red houses; cobalt over red goes to mud). Painted has the best colour over the roofs at 16:00 but is barely distinct from the unstylised look. *The strongest direction:* painted's restraint in chroma as the system, luminous's warm and cool split as the rule at the focus, nocturne's handling of the fog. That is Stålenhag and Hopper: strong value, moderate chroma, one warm accent.

Also: the clay's marbled brush pattern gets in the way of judging light; light tests should render on plain clay.

## The latest pass

- *Reflections now work* (after the reviewer's frames were made): the puddles on Harbour Row's quay mirror the lit shopfronts and the lamp, and the Basin carries the Holm's lamps as soft streaks. The puddles' edges are hard cut-outs; the water's streaks are too soft and short.
- *Sun shafts and motes do not read yet.* The haze at 16:00 is too thin to give a beam a body, none of the cameras looks into the low sun, and the motes are too faint. Cinematographers haze the set: the beams need their own measure of what the air scatters, and a camera into the light.

## Where Claude adds to or differs from the reviewer

- *Frame rate is not yet measured.* The passes that make the stills (every lamp in the fog per pixel, the shafts' forty steps, the sky map's sixty-four samples) are built for stills. The look must hold at city scale and speed (exit criterion 5), so the cost is measured before the style is locked on effects.
- *Camera 6 stays*, but gets a foreground and is the frame for clear nights; in sea fog it is an honest empty frame, and a bank of fog with the town's glow above it would give it its picture.
- *Agree that fog should be clearer over land:* sea fog forms over cold water and thins over warmer ground, so this is true as well as better.

## To approve now (later work depends on it)

1. **The style's direction:** painted's restraint, luminous's warm and cool split at the focus, nocturne's fog. *Why now:* every part's colours and every material's albedo are chosen inside it; houses built first would be built to the wrong light.
2. **The value rule:** every frame designed as a notan of four or five values, one darkest dark, one white (a light that means people), the middle at most about sixty percent of the frame, checked in greyscale at one-eighth before colour. *Why now:* materials, trees and lamps are authored to fit it; a white house built before the rule blows out.
3. **The mood rule, tightened:** a mood moves exposure, contrast and how much colour the shade keeps, and the lens (grain, vignette); never the hue of a light, and nothing that reads as weather (fog, lifted blacks). *Why now:* it belongs in the engine contract, so a mood can never change a fact.
4. **The sky's approach:** designed from the hour and the weather (gradient, horizon glow at the sun's bearing, banded cloud with lit undersides), not noise. *Why now:* every material and tree is judged against the sky.
5. **Light from windows and lamps:** spill onto reveals and pavement, varied per window, softened by the fog. *Why now:* the building parts need reveals, curtains and frames that take the spill.
6. **The street stop:** 2.5 to 3 m, lens 28 to 35 mm. *Why now:* parts are made at the scale that reads from there.
7. **The first target frame:** camera 3 (Gully Steps), blue hour in sea fog, painted over the render; and who paints it (Øystein, a concept artist, or a rough paintover by Claude, which would set a lower bar). *Why now:* without it "stunning" has no picture to be judged against, and its grade becomes the game's lookup table.

## Later (once materials, trees and people exist)

The fog's exact density and colour; cloud forms beyond the sky's system; the moods' final grades and the blind test; each street's lamp type in detail; cameras 6 and 7; the puddles' detail; falling rain and snow; the seasons; the town's paint colours (with the parts); the performance budget in detail (but measured soon, see above).

## The next five steps, in order

1. **Values first.** A notan for 16:00, blue hour and sea fog for cameras 1, 3 and 9; render to match in greyscale, on plain clay.
2. **The sky.** The designed dome, zenith darkening, horizon glow, banded cloud with lit undersides; the noise goes.
3. **Windows and lamps that light the world.** Spill, variation, fog softening, halos that grow with distance, the eras distinct.
4. **The fog as a bank.** Dense on the water, thinner over land, the town's glow in it; haze that gives the sun's beams a body, and a camera into the low sun.
5. **Lock the style and shrink the moods** to the allowed axes; paint the first target frame and judge the next render beside it.
