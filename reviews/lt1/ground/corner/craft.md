# The knuckle: what it is and how it is built

**The rock.** Stavanger stands on phyllite (fyllitt), a soft slaty rock of thin plates (0.05 to 0.3 m) bundled into packages that break together (NGU rapport 2024.034). Its foliation strikes 150 to 180 and dips 45 to 90 to the WSW, mostly 60 to 70; steep cross joints run near 045, 135 and 075 to 090 (NGU 2024.034; SVV rapport 315, the Eiganes tunnel's mapping). Glaciated bedrock in Norway also carries sheeting, unloading joints near the ground's own slope, thin near the top and thicker with depth; the reports do not map it, so it is a fixture value. The ice rounds an outcrop's top and plucks its lee face into steps; in cuts, phyllite breaks along its foliation and sheds plates (Norconsult 2025, Stord). Here the foliation dips toward the street, so the street face is a foliation plane leaning back: the batter is the rock's own, not a mason's.

**What the eye sees on such a face** (the 1951 Erfjordgata photograph, `docs/references/` 41; the Aksla rock, 16): a few big planes, each a joint; steps where a block or a plate has gone; a handful of open cracks, most joints tight and unseen; the darkest value at the foot and in the cracks; lichen on the open tops.

**How it is built** (`blender/generators/bedrock.py`), as block theory treats jointed rock: each joint family is a set of parallel planes at seeded spacings, and together they cut space into convex cells that meet face to face. The fin's street face is one foliation plane from its foot to the ice's cap, the rock behind it whole to the 1874 cuts; only some big blocks stand at its foot. A seeded share of each family's planes is open; a whole block (cells across tight joints) sheds the same plate, so steps and cracks fall only at open joints. Nothing is bent: cells are kept, dropped, or cut along a plane parallel to their own face. Nothing floats: every block is carried to the ground. Edges meet point to point (T-junctions put in), so there are no cracks in the mesh but the rock's own.

**The surface** (`tools/corner_ground.py`). Per vertex: the sky it sees (64 rays against the rock, the land, the houses and the cheek walls), its height over the ground and its distance to an open joint; growth's `on_rock` turns those and the 115 years since 1874 into lichen and moss. The contact shade (the dark foot, a crack's narrow dark margin, kept narrow by a first ring of vertices 0.1 m inside each facet's edge) is baked into the sky term, so the painted surface and Stone.shader both carry it. Levels by script: each at most 60 % of the one before.
