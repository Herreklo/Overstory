# Exit interview

## 1. Overall pull
6 out of 10. I'd keep going from the rug, but only if the shop decision were mine to make.

## 2. Compared with the real game
The mechanics matched. I audited every number you gave me: pile counts through two reshuffles, Vulnerable ticks, the 13.5 that rounds to 13, Lagavulin losing Metallicize and being stunned when he's woken by damage, the Siphon Soul values, combat-only Anger copies, a flat Fire Potion. I found no errors. As a rules engine it's trustworthy, and that matters most to me: an optimiser can only optimise against rules that hold.

The feel was different, though. In the real game the decisions live inside the fight, card by card, with the draw in front of you. On paper I wrote a policy and you executed it. Two elite fights went by and I made almost no decisions in them. For an optimiser, writing the bot is a real pleasure, but by the second fight I was a spectator checking your maths. The real game is also roughly ten times faster: three rounds of text covered two fights and a shop.

Nothing broke, and I found no exploit that wasn't already in the real game. Potions not counting as Skills against Enrage, and steering the Pen Nib counter with Anger, are real-game tricks. The format did add two leaks of its own:
- **Hidden information.** You told me Shrug It Off would draw Anger off a freshly shuffled pile. In the real game I can't know that.
- **You did my lookahead.** "Three Strikes make 27… falls 2 short. With the Fire Potion, he dies." That sum is the player's job. If the game master runs the search, conditional rules plus your narration mean you play perfectly on my behalf. That's the dominant strategy of a paper game: write loose rules and let the game master optimise.

## 3. Best and worst
**Best:**
- The Nob kill, decided by 2 HP and only with the potion. It was a tight, fair sum.
- You named rule conflicts out loud and said how you'd break the tie ("Bash+ first, the rest to Block") instead of picking silently.
- The shop is the best screen of the session. Orichalcum costs 155 against my 157, which is a real temptation. Shockwave plus a removal comes to exactly 150. With the Membership Card just out of reach, every line costs something.

**Worst:**
- The session ended at the one decision I'd asked to make myself. I'd fought two rounds for that shop.
- Fights became spectator rounds once my policy was written.
- The opening never told a new player that Nob punishes Skills. I won on memory of the real game. A cold player plays Defends on turn 2 and finds out the hard way.

For the record, my shop line was to remove a Strike and buy Shockwave, 150 gold with 7 left over. Shockwave's Weak 3 and Vulnerable 3 against Hexaghost's Divider and Inferno is worth more than Orichalcum. A thinner deck draws Uppercut, Bash+ and Inflame more often, and that feeds the Pen Nib steering. Orichalcum would have come second, since it fits my "take hits, don't block" policy. I was saving the Blood Potion for after a treasure relic.

## 4. Marks for round 3
- Turn 3.1: engaged. Pen Nib came with a visible counter, as I'd asked, and the steering exploit was set up. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.2: engaged. This was a real decision at last, priced to bite: Orichalcum against Shockwave plus a removal, with 7 gold to spare either way. Surprise: delight. The 155-against-157 pricing felt like a deliberate trap.
- Turn 3.3: bored. Confirming a standing rule isn't a turn. Then the session ended before the shop resolved, which was frustrating more than boring. I wouldn't quit, but I'd want the next session to open at the rug, not after it.
