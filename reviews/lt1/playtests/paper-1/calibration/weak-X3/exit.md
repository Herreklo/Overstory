# Exit interview (X3, cozy player, Saltlight, Chapter One)

## 1. Overall pull
5 out of 10. It was 7 at the start and slid down. The lantern walk pulled me back up for a moment, but most of what happened before it was the same two or three things.

## 2. Best and worst
**Best**
- Holding Wilf's rag while the plank turned honey-coloured. A small shared act, and "I talked to every one" made him a person.
- Ivy's sailor in Montevideo who married a girl in Valparaíso, and the same laugh from both sisters. It's the only story that was told to me, not described.
- The red concrete tiles stopping exactly at the party wall, and the paint going white over green over ochre. I could read the street's history off the buildings without anyone telling me.
- The lantern walk: June pressing a lit jar into my hands, Dot and Ivy waving at their own empty window, the band with no tuba, a row of flames and a row of reflections.

**Worst**
- **My words were replaced.** I typed my own lines three times (the cardigan, a jam jar and May; then the Pennocks) and got menu lines instead: "What a lovely place", "Anything for me?", "Anything in the paper?". The game promised I could put it in my own words.
- **Canned and looping.** June said "Tea's on the house: you live here now" twice, word for word. Four people gave the same answer about the cardigan ("a visitor's"). The sisters answered "what happened to the Pennocks?" with "Pretty, isn't it, all those colours coming through?"
- **"You can't take that with you."** Twice, for the only two things I tried to pick up: the cardigan, which was the one kind act I'd set myself, and a jam jar, with a whole row of them waiting. No reason given. Then June handed me a lit jar anyway.
- **Belonging is a clock, not a response.** +16, +18, +19, +16, whatever I did. A geranium, a cat and a pressed fern appeared in a room I never entered. When progress ignores my choices, I stop caring about the number.
- **Empty counters.** Sketchbook 1 of 48 and keepsakes 0 of 20 after a whole chapter. I never found one keepsake.
- **I didn't choose the day.** Each turn swallowed three or four hours, and the evening walk simply played.

## 3. Would I keep playing, and would I buy it?
Chapter Two, maybe, on the strength of the first sentence of its blurb and the walk. I would give it one more session and watch whether my own words ever land. Buy at $14.99? Not on this evidence. I'd wishlist it. I paid, in effect, to be told it was lovely, and I had no say in anything.

## 4. What I was trying to do, and what I risked
I was trying to be useful to somebody: take the cardigan home, hold the rag, bring a jar, work out who people were. I risked nothing. The page says I can't fail or miss anything, and that turned out to be true in a way that made my kindness cost nothing. Compare Stardew: there I spent my energy, my stone and my money on a coop and a birthday leek. Here, every act was free and every result was the same, so none of it felt like mine.

## 5. Marks for round 3
- Turn 3.1 (Dot and Ivy): curious, then confused — the sailor story was a delight, but my Pennock question got a non sequitur and my words were swapped for a menu line. Surprise: delight (the sailor) and cheat (my words replaced).
- Turn 3.2 (the red roof): engaged — tiles that stop at the party wall as if drawn with a line is the one thing here I worked out myself. Surprise: delight.
- Turn 3.3 (the chapel jars): confused — DAMSON '93 on a label is lovely; "You can't take that with you" is a wall. Surprise: cheat.
- Turn 3.4 (the wall and the lantern walk): engaged — warm and quiet, though it played without me. Surprise: delight — June and the lit jar.
