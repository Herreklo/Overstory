# Round 1

## Pull
7 — The prose is lovely and I want the toast, the jam jar and the cardigan. But the store page promises no timers, no fail states and nothing to miss, and I can't yet see what I'd be doing day to day.

## Thinking aloud
I like this a lot on the surface. A steep harbour town, wooden houses stacked up a hill, a radio reading the shipping forecast, someone making toast. That's the kind of place I'd want to live in for an autumn. The details are the good bit: the mooring rings polished on top where ropes have worked them, and the corner of the chandler's window rubbed clear at about a child's nose height. Somebody small keeps looking into a shop that's closed. I want to know who, and what's inside.

There are small, kind jobs lying around already: the grey cardigan on the board (ask at the Kettle), the jam jar I need for tonight's lantern walk, a boat being varnished one plank at a time, a brass band that wants a tuba. I'd like to start with Wilf, since he spoke to me. Offering a hand is what I'd do.

What I can't see yet is the loop. In Stardew I have a farm that grows and a list of chores. Here the room above the café is the thing that grows, with "belonging" as a percentage, and the rest is looking, talking and sitting. It could be wonderful, or it could be a very pretty list. One line already bothers me: the lantern walk is "tonight", but the page says I can't miss anything. If nothing can be missed, nothing is urgent, and I worry I'll drift. I'll go slow and see if the people carry it.

## Prediction
Wilf will turn down my offer of a hand, warmly, saying the varnish has to go on slowly and alone. Then he'll tell me Marigold was his father's boat, because he can't help it, and he'll ask me whether I'm staying for the lantern walk.

## Needs
- What do I have in my bag? Money? Anything to give people?
- Does the clock run? The ferry has times and the walk is tonight, so is any of that missable, or really not?
- What does Belonging need from me: seeing places, meeting people, or doing something for them?
- What does my room look like now?
- What is a keepsake, and how do I get one?

## Turns
1. Answer Wilf with option 2: "Do you need a hand with that?" — Expect: he says no with a smile, then starts talking about Marigold.
2. Go into the Kettle and talk to June: say hello, ask about my room, the grey cardigan and a jam jar for the lantern walk. — Expect: warm and a bit brisk, she gives me a key and a jar, and the cardigan is on a hook behind the counter.
3. Come back out and inspect the rubbed-clear corner of Tolley & Son's whitewashed window and look through it. — Expect: a dark shop with a few dusty things, and something that tells me a child's been here often.
4. Sit on the bench with the brass plaque and watch the quay for a while. — Expect: I read a name on the plaque and notice what the town does by itself: Sal goes up the steps, the ferry fills, Teddy catches (or doesn't catch) a crab.
