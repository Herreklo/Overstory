# Round 3

**Turn 3.1.** The window at step 40 is still open a crack. Dot's needles; Ivy turning a page.

You say: "Anything in the paper?"

Ivy shakes the Gazette straight and reads: "'The council has repainted the railings on the Strand.'" Dot: "We see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down." Ivy: "And we wave at all of them." Then Ivy, folding the paper on her knee: "I was engaged to a sailor once. He wrote every week from Montevideo." Dot: "And then he married a girl in Valparaíso." They both laugh, the same laugh. Ivy: "Best thing that ever happened to her."

You ask about the Pennocks, down at step 20. Dot: "Mrs Pennock went to her son's in the spring." Ivy: "Pretty, isn't it, all those colours coming through?" And the red roof? Ivy: "The Carrs did theirs in the summer." Dot: "Smart, isn't it?"

Next time, Dot and Ivy:
1. "Good morning, ladies."
2. "What are you knitting?"
3. "Anything in the paper?"

**Turn 3.2.** Twenty steps up, the house at step 60 has a new roof of bright red concrete tiles, sharp-edged and clean, the ridge capped in the same red. Next door the old clay tiles are soft orange under moss and lichen, a few slipped, a gull on the chimney. The new tiles stop exactly at the party wall, as if someone drew a line. The windows are shut and the step is swept.

**Turn 3.3.** Chapel Lane at the top of the steps, the Seamen's Chapel white and small with its bell. Along the low wall in front of it the jam jars stand in a row, two dozen or more, each with a wire twisted round its neck for a handle and a stub of candle inside, some with old labels still half on: BRAMBLE, LEMON CURD, a hand-written DAMSON '93.

You reach for one. *You can't take that with you.*

**Turn 3.4.** You sit on the chapel wall beside the jars. From here the whole harbour lies below: the roofs going down to the water like steps, the Puffin's wake on her last crossing, the island dark against the light. The yellow band in the west turns orange, then rose, then goes. Lights come on in the windows one by one down the hill.

## The lantern walk

They come up the lane as the dusk thickens: June with her apron off, Wilf with varnish on his knuckles, Teddy with his bucket empty (Admiral let go at teatime), Sal out of uniform in a red anorak, Dot and Ivy arm in arm and a step at a time, Rob, Cyril off the last Puffin with his binoculars round his neck, Skipper on a lead at the back. The brass band tunes up by the chapel door: a cornet, a trombone, a euphonium and a drum. No tuba.

June presses a spare jar into your hands as she passes, already lit.

The candles go round from match to match, and then you all go down Wren Steps together, slowly, the lights bobbing on the walls of the houses, the band playing something slow and a bit flat, Dot and Ivy waving from the middle of the crowd at their own empty window as you pass it. At the bottom, on the Strand, everyone sets their jar down along the edge of the quay, a long row of small flames, and the water under them holds a second row, wobbling. Nobody says much. The band stops. A gull complains somewhere in the dark. You set yours down at the end of the row, beside Teddy's.

The lights burn down, and people drift home in twos and threes up the steps and along the Strand, until it is just the jars, and the water, and you.
