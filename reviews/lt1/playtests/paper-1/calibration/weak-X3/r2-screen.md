# Round 2

The rain comes in about one o'clock, soft and fine, more a wetness in the air than drops. Wilf rigs a tarpaulin over the trestles and goes on varnishing underneath it. Teddy is in a yellow oilskin two sizes too big, the crab line still in the water.

**Turn 2.1.** You stand at the chandler's window and draw it: the faded gold letters, the whitewash, the one clear corner low down, and through it a shelf and the calendar's square. The rain spots the page and you tuck it under your coat to finish.

*Sketch: Tolley & Son's window. A clear corner in the whitewash, and March 1988 on the wall inside.*

**Turn 2.2.** The bell on its spring. June is at the window table with her cloth.

You say: "What a lovely place."

"Isn't it? Mum chose these curtains in 1962." She looks at them, yellow checks gone soft with washing. "Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house: you live here now." The cloth goes round. "You'll come to the lantern walk tonight? Everyone goes. You'll love it."

You reach for the cardigan on its hook. *You can't take that with you.*

You ask who May was. "May Ord. She sat there every afternoon. Lovely woman."

Next time, June:
1. "Is my room ready?"
2. "What's good here?"
3. "What a lovely place."

**Turn 2.3.** Sal has moved in under the Kettle's awning out of the rain, her bicycle against the window and the bundle of letters on her knee.

You say: "Anything for me?"

"Give me a minute. I'm in the right street, at least." She flicks through. "Up and up, and then you're there. That's Wrenmouth for you." She looks up the steps, at the rain on them. "Eighty-seven steps, twice a day, eleven years. Calves like a footballer."

You ask about the grey cardigan. "That? Somebody's visitor, I expect. It's been on June's hook since August."

Next time, Sal:
1. "Anything for me?"
2. "Busy morning?"
3. "Which way's the chapel?"

**Turn 2.4.** Up Wren Steps in the wet. The stone is dark and slick, and the iron handrail cold under your hand, polished bright in the middle and rough at both ends. At step 20 a house with its paint coming off in layers, white over green over a first ochre; one window boarded, a FOR SALE card in another. At step 40 the geranium window, and two white heads behind it. The window opens a crack as you come level.

You say: "Good morning, ladies."

"Good morning yourself!" says Dot, needles going. Ivy lowers the Gazette. Dot: "We see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down." Ivy: "And we wave at all of them." And they do, at you, though you're right there. Dot: "We were born in this house. Both of us, in the back bedroom."

You ask about the grey cardigan. Ivy: "Oh, that'll be a visitor's, love." Dot: "June's got it on her hook."

Next time, Dot and Ivy:
1. "Good morning, ladies."
2. "What are you knitting?"
3. "Anything in the paper?"

**About 5 pm. Wren Steps, step 40.** The rain has stopped. The sky is opening in the west, a long band of pale yellow over the headland, and every wet roof below you has caught it.

**In view**
- **Dot and Ivy Hale**, at their window, open a crack. Dot is knitting a long blue scarf; Ivy has the Gazette.
- **The handrail**, wet and bright.
- Below, at step 20: **the Pennock house**, the flaking paint and the FOR SALE card.
- Above, at step 60: **a new red roof** beside a mossy old one.
- At the foot of the steps: **the Kettle**, and **Sal Brennan** under its awning.
- Far below: **the Strand**, **the Marigold** under her tarpaulin, the harbour gone gold.
- At the top of the steps: **the Seamen's Chapel**, white boards and a small bell. Along its wall, a row of **jam jars**, waiting, catching the light.
