# Round 3

## Marks
- Turn 2.1: curious — My prediction failed: the key is plain, old, two notches, nothing to chase, and the lock is clean, so no one picked it. What I got instead is the soft bread and the checked galley cloth, which point at the galley and not at the bread room. The worn ankle (raw, healed, raw again) is far older than nine days, so the nine scraped marks cannot be "days chained". They look like days since someone last came. Surprise: delight — a wrong guess that sent me somewhere better.
- Turn 2.2: engaged — Neat tarred twine and three-cornered holes against cotton twine, torn holes and a cook's trussing needle left lying on the canvas: the hands are different, and I worked it out by looking. The empty peg with the darkened wood and the needle case with its cap off are loose ends I like. Surprise: delight — the trussing needle, left in plain view, is fair.
- Turn 2.3: engaged — The Welsh voice and the Yorkshire-coast voice name Morgan and Dunning from the crew list. "Tom" holding the lantern is Thomas Pell. Sewn through the nose: grim, and it tells me a sailmaker did this one. I have nobody to blame for this death, which I didn't expect. Brothers are side by side in the sketch, so why is George not in the hold? Surprise: delight.

## Pull
8 — A lot of threads (chained brother, galley bread, empty log shelf, missing jolly boat), but I am three rounds in and can't confidently write a fate beyond George. I want something to close soon.

## Thinking aloud
Settled by voice and look: Sketch 4 is Isaac Dunning, crushed in the pressure on 3 December, by the ship and not by a person. Sketch 5 is Rhys Morgan, Welsh, whistle. Sketch 6 is Antti Rautio, fur hat, palm pad. Sketch 13 is Ned. Sketches 3, 10, 11 and 12 are four men for Ekström, Reid, Holm and Quill, and I can't split them without hearing them talk.

The sail locker gives the best chain so far. The sailmaker sewed Dunning neat. By 9 February the shrouds are sewn with the cook's cotton and trussing needle. So the sailmaker stopped between those dates, and the second neat shroud, if it is dated, brackets exactly when. Either Rautio fell ill, died or left. Since Moss now sews, Rautio's own body may be the second clumsy shroud. The thing that hung for years on the empty peg may be his palm. The cap is off the needle case, so he left mid-job. I need to open both remaining shrouds. The stout shape of Carr and the lean shape of Rautio should show from outside.

Thomas: the cloth and bread come from the galley, so Moss or Ned fed him once. The dry cask and the untouched bread mean he was past eating. The key hangs just out of reach, in plain view. That reads as either cruelty or a leaver's bad conscience.

## Prediction
The second clumsy shroud holds Carr, killed within weeks of 9 February by Thomas Pell, hence the irons. If it holds Rautio instead, my theory is wrong and the irons have a different reason.

## Needs
- Who ironed Thomas, when, and why; nothing yet separates 9 February from 23 April.
- When the sailmaker stopped sewing, and where he is.
- Who cut the bright bread-room key, and when (a file, filings, a carpenter's chest?).
- Where the cat went; it is there in February and not in December.
- Who was Moss's lamp-and-keys for: did he catch George or let him in?
- The log: who has the brown card book (Lind?) and the shelf's contents.
- Voices for sketches 3, 10, 11 and 12.

## Turns
1. Look at the two uncut shrouds from outside (size, bulk, anything sewn in), then cut open a clumsy one and hold the watch. — Expect: a stout body with mutton-chops and a blue coat, Carr, killed by Thomas Pell.
2. If turn 1 is Carr, cut the remaining neat shroud and hold the watch. If not, open the other clumsy one. — Expect: a death between December and February, the last one the sailmaker sewed.
3. Go aft to the captain's cabin and search: the stove ash, the pencil line on the chart and whose hand and dates, the shelf where the log sat (dust outline, loose pages), the bunk and chest. — Expect: burnt log pages in the stove and a dated route across the ice to the island, in a pencil like Lind's.
