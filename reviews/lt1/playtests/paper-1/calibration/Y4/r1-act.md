# Round 1

## Pull
8 — Good opening: a murder in a dark store, a cast I can already half-sort, and a man in irons I want to explain. I'd keep going.

## Thinking aloud
The first scene hands me more than it admits. The slow Tyneside voice is called "Mr Carr" by the second Hull voice, and Carr is the only Newcastle man on the list, so I take the stout man in the blue coat with the belaying pin (sketch 2) to be Edmund Carr, First Mate. The close, breathless Hull voice is called "George" by the voice from above. That makes the thief George Pell, and George is the one face down in the biscuit with the bread bag in his fist. The man on the ladder is the second Hull voice, so he is Thomas Pell. Only sketch 9 has the split lip, and the man on the ladder has it, so 9 is Thomas and the dead man is 8, George. The shroud I opened (young man, crushed temple, clumsy stitching) fits a blow from a pin. So I have Pell by Carr as a working guess, but I don't trust it until I see faces. The red cap fallen by the head could also be a plant, because caps move.

Other pegs. The man with the ladle and apron is 7, the cook Abel Moss. The man in spectacles and nightshirt with the frock coat is 14, and the sketch is signed "O.L.", so he is probably Oskar Lind. The boy with the cat is 13, Ned Fairley. Sketch 6, with the palm pad and fur hat, is the sailmaker, and he is also the local man, Antti Rautio. I don't like that Moss holds keys and a lamp. Why is the cook up at night with the keys to the bread room? Who else had a key?

I also want to know why the man in the cable tier is in leg irons and has no shroud. Someone ironed him and left him. If Carr killed a hungry man over biscuit, the captain may have ironed Carr. That is a guess.

## Prediction
The man in the leg irons is Carr. Whitlock put him in irons for the killing, and he was left there. I expect he died of cold or hunger rather than by a blow. If it turns out to be someone else, then whoever it is saw something worse than this.

## Needs
- A clearer view of the two red-cap faces side by side, so I can tell which brother is which without relying only on the lip.
- The age of the dead man. Is he "young" because he is a Pell, or is there another young man aboard?
- Who sewed the shrouds. Even small stitches suggest the sailmaker. Clumsy stitches might mean the crew did it in a hurry, or after the sailmaker died.
- The date of death for each body, so I can order the deaths.

## Turns
1. Walk the frozen scene and look hard at the two red-cap men: the face-down man's face and hands, and the man on the ladder (split lip), side by side. Check the pin for blood or hair, and what the dead man's bread bag holds. — Expect: they are brothers, the lip separates them, and the pin is clean or fresh with blood. That tells me whether this was the first blow.
2. Look closely at the stout man with the pin: face, coat, buttons, belt (keys?), and whether he looks furious or shocked. Then check Moss's keys and lamp, and what Lind has in his hands. — Expect: Carr looks furious, not horrified, which makes this a killing in anger. Moss's keys say he opened the room, so he came to catch the thief.
3. Go forward to the cable tier and look at the man in leg irons. Check his clothes, build and hands against the sketch, and see how long he has been there. Then hold the watch to him. — Expect: he is Carr, put in irons after this killing, and the watch will show his last hours in the dark.
