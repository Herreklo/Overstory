# Round 3

## Turn 3.1: the clumsy shroud

**From outside.** Of the two still sewn, the clumsy one is short and wide, bulging at the middle so the canvas strains at the big stitches and gapes between two of them. The neat one is longer and narrow, the canvas drawn smooth. Nothing is sewn onto either, and nothing shows through.

You cut open the clumsy one. A stout man, short in the leg, his mutton-chop whiskers flattened against his cheeks by the canvas. He is sewn in his plain blue coat with its dark horn buttons, fastened to the throat. On the left side, below the ribs, the coat is slit an inch and a half across, and the cloth round the slit is stiff and brown. The canvas was pulled across his face without care; there is no stitch through the nose.

You hold the watch to him.

**The Watch, 1 March 1812, night.** *Wind. A lantern creaking on its hook. Footsteps on a frozen deck, slow, then stopping.*
The slow voice, Tyneside: "Pell? You're not on this watch. Get below."
A Hull voice, quiet, very close: "No. This is for George."
A grunt.
A body falls against the rail.

**The frozen moment.** The quarterdeck under stars. Ice to the horizon on every side, grey-white and ridged, the masts bare above. The stout man with mutton-chop whiskers sags against the binnacle, one hand flat on its brass hood, a knife in his left side to the handle. Holding the knife, still close against him: the man in the red knitted cap with the split lower lip. At the rail, turned half round and frozen in shock: a pockmarked man in a neckerchief, his hands still on the rail behind him. Climbing out of the forecastle hatch, one leg over the coaming, mouth wide in a shout: a man with a ginger beard and a bonnet.

## Turn 3.2: the last neat shroud

You cut open the neat one. A long-haired man, his hair pale and loose to his shoulders, a small gold ring in his left ear. He is sewn in a dry linen shirt and drawers, not a seaman's working clothes. There is no wound on him anywhere. The skin of his hands and feet is waxy white, and the ends of his fingers and toes have gone dark. At the head of the shroud, the last stitch goes through the end of his nose.

You hold the watch to him.

**The Stove, 15 January 1812, night.** *The stove ticking as it cools and heats.*
A Swedish voice, urgent, half in Swedish: "Jens. Jens! Stay awake, you hear me? Håll dig vaken."
A Finnish voice, few words, flat: "The water had him to the chest. Too long."
An older voice, Hull, tired: "More blankets. Mr Ekström, you'll try again at first light, you and Rautio both."
Breathing that stops.

**The frozen moment.** The captain's cabin, the stove's door open and red inside. Laid on blankets on the deck before it: the long-haired man with the earring, soaked through and blue at the lips, ice still in his hair. Kneeling at his head, one hand under it: a young man with a fair beard in a short blue jacket, a pipe in his jacket pocket. By the door, pulling a fur hat down over his ears: the lean man with the leather pad on his palm. Holding out a folded blanket: an older man in a coat with brass buttons. At the stove with a steaming pot: the heavy man in the apron. Beside the chart table, a man in spectacles holds up a lamp in one hand and writes in a small book with the other.

## Turn 3.3: the captain's cabin

The cabin as it is now: cold, a grey light through the stern windows, frost ferns in the corners of the glass.

**The stove.** Cold. Grey wood ash and a few charred ends of split wood. No paper ash.

**The chart.** Pinned flat on the chart table: the head of the Gulf of Bothnia, printed, its soundings in small figures. Off the coast near the top, a small cross in pencil. From the cross a straight line, ruled firm in pencil, runs across the printed sea to the south shore of an island lettered CARLÖ. There are no words or dates along the line.

**The log shelf.** Over the chart table, a shelf with a brass rail. On it, in the dust, a clean oblong the size of a large ledger where a book stood. Nothing else: no loose pages, no papers behind the rail.

**The bunk.** Stripped: no blankets, no pillow, the straw mattress bare. Pinned to the panelling beside it with a tack: a drawing on a scrap of paper, in pencil, of a cat sitting with its tail round its feet, signed "Ned" in large letters.

**The captain's chest.** Open, at the bunk's foot. Inside, two folded linen shirts and nothing else.
