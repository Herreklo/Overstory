# Round 2

## Turn 2.1: the cable tier, close

**The key.** The nail is driven into the upright beside the top of the ladder, just under the hatch coaming, where someone coming down would hang a key without thinking. From the end of the chain it is the ladder's height away. The key is small, iron, black with age, a plain ring bow and a short bit with two notches. Nothing about it is new.

**The band.** Shut and locked. The keyhole is clean; there are no marks of a tool round it. The band has worn the ankle under it: the skin there is a thick ridge of old scab over new, rubbed raw and healed and rubbed raw again.

**The crust and its cloth.** A heel of soft bread, not ship's biscuit, gone hard. The cloth is a square of blue-and-white checked cotton, hemmed, the kind that hangs in a galley. No mark on it. It is folded over the bread twice, neatly, and has not been unfolded.

**The cask.** A small water breaker, a gallon or so. The bung lies beside it on the deck. Inside, dry, with a pale ring of stain a finger's width from the bottom.

**The nine marks.** Shallow and ragged, scraped rather than cut, each about an inch long, in a straight row on the beam above his head. No knife within his reach. In a fold of the blanket by his right hand, a bent iron nail, its point rubbed bright.

**His jacket.** No name in the collar. The pockets are empty but for a little grit and a few grains of tobacco.

## Turn 2.2: the sail locker

A long, low room amidships smelling of canvas and tallow, light coming grey through a scuttle. Bolts of sailcloth on racks. On the deck, the shrouds laid in a row: the clumsy one you cut open, then one clumsy and two neat still sewn.

**George's opened shroud.** The young man lies in his canvas jacket and trousers, barefoot, the red knitted cap pushed onto his head crookedly by whoever sewed him in. Hands as in the bread room: knuckles raw, nails bitten. In his pockets, crumbs of biscuit, nothing else. No key, no file, nothing of brass or iron on him.

**The four shrouds side by side.**
- *The two neat ones:* a flat seam down the middle, the stitches small, even and tight, each the same length as the last, sewn with tarred sail twine. The holes are clean, three-cornered, the mark a sail needle leaves. The canvas is drawn smooth over the body with no slack. The head end is closed with the same care as the rest.
- *The two clumsy ones:* big, uneven stitches, some an inch long and some three, the thread a plain cotton twine, loose in places so the canvas gapes. The holes are round and torn wide, as if a thick needle had been forced through. Lying on the canvas between them, still threaded with the same cotton twine: a cook's trussing needle, long and thick, with a broad eye.

**The sailmaker's bench.** Under the scuttle, a low bench worn hollow where a man sat. On it, laid in order: a cow-horn case of sail needles, three sizes, its cap off; a lump of beeswax grooved by twine; a ball of tarred twine; a wooden seam rubber. Above the bench, a row of pegs. One peg is empty, and the wood round it is darker and smoother, the shape of something that hung there for years.

## Turn 2.3: the neat shroud nearest the door

The first neat shroud lies nearest the door. You cut it open.

A thin, balding man, sewn in his shirt and breeches and a leather apron, its ties still knotted behind him. His chest is crushed in on the left side, the ribs flattened, the apron dented with them. His hands are hard and scarred across the palms; there is old tar in the cracks. At the head of the shroud, the last stitch of the seam goes through the canvas, through the end of his nose, and out again.

You hold the watch to him.

**The Pressure, 3 December 1811, afternoon.** *Timber groaning, long and slow, a creak like a door in wind.*
A Welsh voice, sing-song even when shouting: "Out of there, Isaac, she's going!"
A Yorkshire-coast voice, tight with effort: "One more shore. Hold that lamp steady, Tom!"
A crack like a musket.
A scream, cut off.

**The frozen moment.** The hold, deep in the ship. The hull planking on the starboard side bowed inward in a long curve, split along a seam, grey ice showing through the split. A thin, balding man in a leather apron lies pinned under a frame timber that has sprung from its place; a maul is still in his hand. Shores of raw timber stand wedged against the bulge, one of them snapped. Holding a lantern up towards the split: a man in a red knitted cap with a split lower lip. Halfway down the ladder from above: a broad man, a whistle on a cord swinging out from his neck, his mouth open mid-shout. Passing a length of timber forward out of the shadows: a lean man in a fur hat, a leather pad strapped across his palm. At the foot of the ladder: the boy, holding a wooden bucket of tools against his chest. There is no cat.
