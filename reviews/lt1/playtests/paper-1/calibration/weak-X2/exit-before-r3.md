# Exit interview: Saltlight

The last screen I was given (`r2-screen.md`) had no exit questions on it, and my round 3 turns were never resolved. So I've answered the questions from my previous game's exit screen, with marks for the last round that was resolved.

## 1. Marks, last resolved round (round 2)
- Turn 2.1 (Sal): bored. My one live hook, the letter she kept moving to the back, was "a catalogue". Surprise: none.
- Turn 2.2 (up Wren Steps): curious. The worn steps and the Pennock house's paint layers were the only things that showed time. Surprise: none.
- Turn 2.3 (Dot and Ivy): would quit. Every question I'd asked was answered with "nothing to see". Surprise: cheat. The hooks were set up and then defused one by one.
- Turn 2.4 (sitting in the rain): bored. The writing was beautiful and the town was a diorama. Surprise: none.

## 2. Overall pull
2. It was 4 on the store page and 3 after round 1. I'd have quit at round 2, and I told the game master so before the round was resolved.

## 3. How it compares with the real game
I don't know a real Saltlight, so I'm comparing it with games like it and with the paper BG3 I played before. The prose is as good as BG3's, maybe better sentence by sentence. The worn mooring rings, the paint white over green over ochre, and the light breaking at five are all lovely. But in BG3 every person wanted something and every failure left something behind. In Saltlight nobody wants anything, nothing can fail, and each question closes a door instead of opening one. Talking to people turns into a vending machine: a charming line, then the same three menu options again. I asked things in my own words, as the store page invited me to, and the game swapped in a menu line first ("Anything for me?", "Anything in the paper?") and then gave my question a dead-end answer. Belonging went up whatever I did, and my room filled with a geranium and a cat for turns I'd call wasted. A reward that comes regardless isn't a reward.

## 4. Best and worst
**Best:**
- Places that tell their history without anyone speaking. The Pennock house with its FOR SALE card, the steps worn into dishes, the calendar stopped at March 1988 under the dust. I believed those more than anything a person said.
- The Canada letter. It's the only secret anyone mentioned.
- The quality of the writing, line by line.

**Worst:**
- Every hook was defused: "just rope", "a visitor's", "a lovely man who retired", "a catalogue". The game seemed to set up mysteries in order to say there aren't any.
- Nobody wants anything, from me or from each other. There's no stake, no choice, no consequence.
- My own words were overridden by menu lines.
- The rewards come no matter what. Belonging rewarded me for nothing, and that made me feel patronised, not welcomed.
- Who am I, and why am I here? The game never cared.
- "You can't miss anything" turned out to mean there's nothing to miss.

To make me stay, the game would need just one of these: a person who needs something, a secret someone is keeping, or a choice that leaves the town different from how I found it. Its places already tell stories. Its people need to have some too.
