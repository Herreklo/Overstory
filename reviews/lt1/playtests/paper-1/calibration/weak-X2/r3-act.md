# Round 3

## Marks
- Turn 2.1: bored. The one person with a hint of a problem turned out to have "a catalogue". The Canada letter is a crumb, but she gives it away and moves on. Once again the menu line went into my mouth before my own question. Surprise: none.
- Turn 2.2: curious. The steps worn into dishes and the Pennock house, white over green over ochre with a FOR SALE card, are the first things in this game that show time passing. That house reads as someone's story, and I could read it. Surprise: none.
- Turn 2.3: would quit. Every question I've asked in this game has been answered with "nothing to see": a visitor's cardigan, a lovely man who retired, just rope, a catalogue. The sisters echo each other like a music box. Surprise: cheat. The opening laid out hooks (a whitewashed shop, a peephole at a child's height, a cardigan dropped on a watched stair), and then the game defused each one as I touched it.
- Turn 2.4: bored. The light breaking at five is beautiful writing. But "nobody goes up, nobody comes down" sums the game up: the town is a diorama that waits to be looked at. The cat in my room for sitting in the rain is a reward for nothing. Surprise: none.

## Pull
1. I said last round that if the Hales gave me pleasantries I'd stop, and they did. On Steam I'd have closed it and asked for a refund inside the two hours.

## Thinking aloud
I can see this is made with care, sentence by sentence. But nobody here wants anything, nobody hides anything, nothing is at stake, and nothing I do changes anyone. "You can't miss anything" turns out to mean there's nothing to miss. Belonging is going up for turns I'd call wasted, and the room filling with things is the game telling me I'm doing well when I'm not doing anything. Two things still pull faintly. The Pennock house is a story told by its paint. The Canada letters are the only secret anyone has mentioned. The lantern walk is the one thing in the game that happens at a set time. If I'm asked to play on, that's where I go. As a player, though, I'm done.

## Prediction
The lantern walk will be lovely and everybody will be there: Wilf, Teddy, Sal, June and the Hales carried down the steps. Nothing will happen except that my Belonging goes up and something else appears on my windowsill.

## Needs
- One person who wants something from me, or from someone else.
- Any sign that what I do changes anything.
- A reason to be me, here.

## Turns (only if made to play on)
1. **Talk to Dot and Ivy**, in my own words: "Sal says a letter comes from Canada on the first of every month. Who writes to you?" Expect: they look at each other, and either it's the first real answer in the game or it's "our cousin, lovely woman".
2. **Inspect the Pennock house** at step 20 on the way. Who lived here, and how long has it stood empty? Expect: the paint tells more than any person has.
3. **Walk up to the Seamen's Chapel** for the lantern walk and take a jam jar. Expect: candles, the town, the view.
4. **At the walk, find whoever is alone** and talk to them. Expect: if there's a story in Wrenmouth, it's with whoever isn't on the store page.
