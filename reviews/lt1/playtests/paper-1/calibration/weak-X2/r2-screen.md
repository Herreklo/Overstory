# Round 2

**Saturday 8 October 1994, the afternoon.** Soft rain from about one, the fine kind that hangs in the air more than it falls. By five it is thinning.

## Turn 2.1: Sal Brennan

You say: "Anything for me?"

"Give me a minute. I'm in the right street, at least." "Up and up, and then you're there. That's Wrenmouth for you." She snaps a rubber band round a handful of letters. "Dot and Ivy get a letter from Canada on the first of every month. Same handwriting since before my time."

You nod at the bundle: the letter she keeps moving to the back. "That? A catalogue." She rolls her eyes. "Saturdays are all catalogues."

The first drops land on her letters. She tucks the bundle inside her jacket and wheels the red bicycle under the Kettle's awning, and sits there on the step, sorting.

**Sal, next time**
1. "Anything for me?"
2. "Busy morning?"
3. "Which way's the chapel?"

## Turn 2.2: Up Wren Steps

The steps are worn into shallow dishes in the middle, where feet have gone for a long time, and the rain gathers in them. The iron handrail is cold and polished bright under your hand. At step 20 you pass a house whose paint is flaking in layers, white over green over a first coat of ochre, one window boarded, a FOR SALE card propped in another. The rain starts properly.

At step 40, the window crowded with red geraniums. Behind the glass, two white heads turn to watch you come up the last few steps.

## Turn 2.3: Dot and Ivy Hale

The window swings open, and a smell of warm wool and toast comes out with it.

You say: "Anything in the paper?"

Ivy shakes out the Wrenmouth Gazette and reads, with feeling: "The council has repainted the railings on the Strand."

Dot, needles going: "We see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down." Ivy: "And we wave at all of them." They both wave at you, though you're right there.

"We were born in this house," Dot says. "Both of us, in the back bedroom."

You ask about the grey cardigan found on the steps. Dot: "That's on June's hook. Since August, isn't it? A visitor's." Ivy: "A visitor's."

You ask about Tolley's, and March 1988. Ivy: "Arthur Tolley? Retired to his daughter's, inland, in '88. Lovely man." Dot: "Lovely man."

**Dot and Ivy, next time**
1. "Good morning, ladies."
2. "What are you knitting?"
3. "Anything in the paper?"

## Turn 2.4: Sit on the steps

You sit a step above the Hales' window, under the drip of the eaves. The rain comes over the harbour in slow grey veils.

Below, the town goes on doing what it does. Wilf has rigged a tarpaulin over the trestles and is under it, brushing the third plank. Teddy has a yellow oilskin on, the hood up, the line still in the water. Sal is under the Kettle's awning with her bundle on her knee. The Puffin knocks against the quay steps with Rob in the stern. Gulls sit hunched on the chimneys with their backs to the wind. Ten steps above you, a marmalade cat has folded itself into a doorway out of the wet, and watches the rain with you.

Nobody goes up. Nobody comes down. Behind you, Dot's needles tick.

Towards five the rain thins and stops. A long crack opens in the west and a low, clean light comes through it, gilding the wet roofs all the way down to the water. Up at the top of the steps, along the chapel wall, the jam jars stand in a row, waiting.
