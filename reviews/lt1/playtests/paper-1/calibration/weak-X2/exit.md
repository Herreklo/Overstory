# Exit interview: Saltlight

## 1. Marks, round 3
- Turn 3.1 (Dot and Ivy): bored. Another menu line went into my mouth ("Good morning, ladies", at sunset). Ivy's sailor who married a girl in Valparaíso is the first personal story anyone has told me, but it came as a punchline and closed straight away. Canada is "Cousin Edie, eleven grandchildren", defused like everything else. Dot not looking up from her needles might have been a tell, but there's nothing I can do with it. Surprise: none. I expected the defusal.
- Turn 3.2 (the Pennock house): bored. I asked who lived there and how long it's been empty, and got the same paint and FOR SALE card I'd already seen, plus "you can sketch this now". Inspecting added nothing. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.3 (up to the chapel): curious. The new tiles stopping dead at the party wall are a good bit of history to read. June pressing a jar into my empty hands, with "You can't walk without a light", is the first time anyone in Wrenmouth did something for me without being asked. Surprise: delight, a small one. The fern on my journal did nothing for me.
- Turn 3.4 (Rob Garrow): would quit. I found the one person standing alone, and he said "Island?" and nothing more. It fits Wilf saying Rob doesn't talk, and it's still a dead end. Surprise: none.
- The lantern walk: curious. The two rows of lights, one on the stone and one shaking on the water, got me for a moment. But it was a cutscene. I did nothing in it and nothing changed because of it. It came out almost exactly as I predicted last round: lovely, everyone there, nothing happens, Belonging goes up, something new in my room. A game I can predict that precisely has nothing hidden in it. Surprise: none.

## 2. Overall pull
2.

## 3. Best and worst
**Best:**
- The places tell history and I believed them: the steps worn into dishes, the paint white over green over ochre, the calendar stopped at March 1988, the new tiles against the mossy ones. They were more honest than any person in the game.
- June's jar.
- The two rows of lights on the quay.
- The prose, line by line.

**Worst:**
- Every hook was defused: "just rope", "a visitor's", "a lovely man who retired", "a catalogue", "Cousin Edie". The opening set up mysteries and then told me, one by one, that there weren't any.
- Nobody wants anything, from me or from each other.
- My own words were replaced by menu lines, and the menus loop.
- Inspecting something a second time gave me nothing new.
- Rewards come whatever I do. I got 55% Belonging, a geranium, a cat and a fern, with no sketches, no keepsakes and no decisions. Being praised for nothing felt patronising.
- I never learned who I am or why I came.

## 4. Chapter Two? $14.99?
No to both. "New places, new sketches and keepsakes" for Chapter Two is a promise of more of the same. I'd have refunded inside two hours. This isn't me failing to relax. I'd play a gentle game if anyone in it needed something or hid something.

## 5. What was I trying to achieve, and what did I risk?
I was trying to find one real story: why the chandler's shut, whose cardigan it was, what Sal was holding back, who writes from Canada, why Rob stands alone. In other words, I wanted to find one person with a problem I could do something about. I achieved none of it, because the game had nothing behind those doors.

I risked nothing. There were no fail states, no costs and no choices that closed off another. The only thing I spent was my time. That's the heart of it: with nothing at stake, nothing I found could feel earned, and nothing I did could matter. Compared with the paper BG3 I played before, a failed roll there gave me more story than every successful question here put together.
