# Round 3

**Saturday 8 October 1994, the evening.** The sky has cleared from the west. The wet roofs steam faintly in the last of the sun.

## Turn 3.1: Dot and Ivy Hale

You say: "Good morning, ladies."

"Good morning yourself!" they say together, though the light behind you is going gold. Dot: "We see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down." Ivy: "And we wave at all of them."

Ivy folds the Gazette on her knee. "I was engaged to a sailor once. He wrote every week from Montevideo." Dot: "And then he married a girl in Valparaíso." They both laugh, the same laugh. Ivy: "Best thing that ever happened to her."

You ask who writes to them from Canada on the first of every month. Dot doesn't look up from her needles. "Cousin Edie, in Halifax. She writes about her grandchildren." Ivy: "Eleven, now."

**Dot and Ivy, next time**

## Turn 3.2: The Pennock house

Step 20. The paint is coming away in layers, curled like old bark: white on top, green under the white, and under the green, in the deepest cracks, the first ochre. One window is boarded with a sheet of ply. In the other, a FOR SALE card is propped against the glass, its corners beginning to curl.

*You can sketch this now.*

## Turn 3.3: Up to the Seamen's Chapel

All eighty-seven steps. Past the Hales' window; past the marmalade cat, back on its warm stone at step 50; past the house at step 60, where the bright new tiles stop dead at the party wall against the mossy old ones. At the top, Chapel Lane levels out, and the whole harbour opens below you: the roofs going down to the water like steps, the island dark against a pale sea, the Puffin coming back across with her wake behind her.

The Seamen's Chapel is white boards and a small bell. People are gathering at its wall, where the jam jars wait in a row. June Askew comes up the lane with her apron off and her cheeks pink from the climb, sees your empty hands and presses a jar into them, a candle stub inside and a wire handle. "Here, love. You can't walk without a light."

*Something new in your room:* a pressed fern on the cover of your journal.

## Turn 3.4: Whoever is alone

Rob Garrow stands apart by the chapel gate, his cap on, his jar unlit at his feet. You go over and talk to him.

He nods. "Island?"

Nothing more.

## The lantern walk

At dusk the town comes up to the chapel. June. Wilf, with varnish on his knuckles. Teddy, who let Admiral go at teatime. Sal, out of uniform, in a red jumper. Dot and Ivy, arm in arm, a step at a time. Rob. Cyril Bowe, off the last Puffin, binoculars round his neck and a thermos under his arm. Skipper, on a lead. And the brass band, four of them, no tuba. Someone lights the first candle from a taper and the flame goes from jar to jar along the wall.

The band strikes up, and the walk goes down Wren Steps, the lights bobbing between the houses: past step 60, past the cat, past the geraniums, past the Pennock house with its curling card. On the Strand the jars are set down one by one along the edge of the quay, and the harbour holds two rows of lights, one on the stone and one on the black water under it, shaking a little when the swell comes in.

You set yours down at the end of the row. For a while nobody says anything. Then June says it's getting cold, and people begin to drift home in twos and threes, and the candles burn down on the quay, and the gulls settle on the chimneys for the night.
