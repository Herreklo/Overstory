Admiral the crab is sweet, and that's all there was. Surprise: cheat. The store page says I can put things in my own words, and my words were swapped for a menu line.

## Pull
3. I'm close to quitting. Every thread I pull comes loose in my hand, and every "next time" menu offers me the same line again.

## Thinking aloud
The prose is doing all the work. Each person gets one charming detail and then a wall: the crab, the footballer's calves, the varnish. Nobody wants anything from me, so nothing I say lands. The one thing that caught my eye is Sal. She frowned at a letter, moved it to the back, and after all that sorting her bundle is no smaller. A postwoman not delivering something is the first hint of a person with a problem. The chandler's stopped in March 1988, and the Hales have watched these steps all their lives. If anyone in this town knows a real story, it's them. I'll give the game one honest round to show me anything happening that wasn't put there to be looked at.

## Prediction
The letter Sal keeps moving to the back is addressed to Tolley & Son. She has nowhere to deliver it and can't bring herself to send it back.

## Needs
- Do my own words count, or only the numbered lines?
- Does anything in this town change unless I look at it?
- What is Belonging for? A geranium isn't a goal.

## Turns

4. If the Hales give me pleasantries too, I **sit on the steps** and watch for a while, to see whether the town does anything on its own. Expect: it doesn't, and I'd stop playing here.
