# Exit interview: X4, the Hesper

## 1. Overall pull
8. I'd have kept going without a pause. The session stopped just as the hard part began: who left the ship alive, and how.

## 2. Compared with the real game
The core of it survives paper. Faces, clothes, accents and the place of death are matched against a sketch and a crew list. The accents did real work here: Tyneside, Welsh, Scarborough, Swedish, Finnish, the Leith bonnet. Two things are better than Obra Dinn's own. The ship as it is now held evidence I had to read (the stitches, the chests, the torn hasp). And I chose the order of the memories. The stitches gave me the order of the deaths before any date did, and that is exactly the pleasure I play these games for.

Three things are weaker:
- **Names are spoken.** "George", "Mr Carr", "Isaac", "Tom", "Jens", "Mr Ekström". Most of my identifications were me hearing a name and finding its face. In the real game the satisfying ones come by elimination, across scenes, with no one saying the name. I made only one such link here (12 is Quill because 10 is Reid and 11 is Holm), and it was the one I enjoyed.
- **The frozen moment lists everyone and points at what matters.** In the real game you walk the scene and might miss the man in the corner, and finding him is half the joy. On paper the game master has already found him for me. There is no noise to sift, no red herring, and nothing I could have overlooked.
- **The three-at-a-time rule never bit.** I wrote only fates I was sure of, so confirmation was relief, not triumph. The rule needs a pile of half-sure guesses to hurt, and the session ended before I had one.

## 3. Best and worst
**Best:**
- The stitches. The neat ones stop because the sailmaker, an Uleåborg man, walked home, not because he died. I think. I want to be told I'm right.
- J.H.'s full chest, clothes folded square, of a man who never came back for them.
- The hasp torn out with the padlock still shut.
- Being wrong about the man in irons, and having the split lip show me why.
- Ned's shaded cat pinned by the captain's bunk. It made me care where the boy went, and I can't stop thinking about it.

**Worst:**
- The spoken names.
- The Carr–Pell revenge line was the most obvious story the bread room could lead to, and it played out exactly so.
- The cable tier memory was beautiful and nearly empty: one voice and one word, with nothing to deduce except the date.
- The session ended before any fate was hard. All three I wrote were near-given.

Where I stood at the end, for what it's worth:
- 1 Whitlock, 3 Ekström, 6 Rautio, 10 Reid, 12 Quill and 14 Lind left alive across the ice to Hailuoto, taking the coats, blankets and log. The speaking trumpet stayed, so maybe not the captain.
- 11 Holm froze after going through the ice on the first attempt (or drowned; I'd have argued that one).
- 9 Thomas froze.
- 5 Morgan, 7 Moss and 13 Ned are open. I suspect Ned went last, in the jolly boat when the ice broke up. The nine scratches would then be the days after he left.

## 4. Marks for round 3
- Turn 3.1: engaged — the ink settling darker is a good beat, but I was certain of all three, so it confirmed rather than rewarded. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.2: engaged — I predicted Holm and early, and it was right. The scene is the richest yet: six people matched in one room, the captain alive in January, and a crossing already tried and failed. "Håll dig vaken" landed. Surprise: delight — the walk over the ice began in January, not at the end.
- Turn 3.3: curious — the trumpet left green in its bracket, the hooks bare, the pencil line to Hailuoto with no initials, and Ned's drawing. Every object raised a question I wanted to chase. Surprise: delight — the cat drawing by the captain's bunk.
