# Round 2

## Marks
- Turn 1.1: engaged — the hasp is torn out with the padlock still shut, so the cook kept the key and George broke in. That's a clue I worked out, not one I was handed. The pin's dark end, the cook in his stockings with his lamp lit (a night alarm) and the clerk's notebook all count. Surprise: none.
- Turn 1.2: curious — I was wrong. A split lip and no whiskers make it Thomas, not Carr. I should have seen that the irons were for the brother. Surprise: delight — the world overturned my story with something I could check.
- Turn 1.3: curious — "Ned?" is haunting, but the memory is nearly empty. No one is in it, so it tells me when he died and nothing about who chained him. If many memories are this thin, I'll get restless. Surprise: none.

## Pull
8. I was wrong about the man in irons, and now I need to know who chained a grieving brother and walked away.

## Thinking aloud
Thomas Pell (9), chained 23 April, with a key hung just out of reach. Somebody did that on purpose, or as a cruel rule. The bread in its cloth is still knotted, someone brought it, and he never untied it: his hands are inside the blanket with frost on his lips. I read that as **froze**, not starved. The nine scratches could be nine days alone, or nine days since Ned stopped coming. He was waiting for the boy, so the boy was his last link and didn't come.

My working story: Thomas went for Carr over George. The remaining clumsy shroud is Carr. The crew chained Thomas, then most of them left over the ice for the island on the chart, taking the log and the boat. Who went and who stayed is what I most want.

Fates pencilled, not spent: **Sketch 8 is George Pell: clubbed, by Edmund Carr.** **Sketch 9 is Thomas Pell: froze.** I'll write them when I have a third solid one.

I also want the clerk's notebook. He drew the sketch, he was awake and he writes things down. If the log is gone, his notebook may be the ship's real memory.

## Prediction
The second clumsy shroud is Edmund Carr, stabbed or clubbed by Thomas Pell soon after 9 February. Thomas is chained for it.

## Needs
- Does the key on the nail fit Thomas's shackle?
- Who chained him? Nothing so far shows me.
- How does the watch let me tell froze from starved? I'm guessing from the frost and the untouched crust.

## Turns
1. In the sail locker, cut open the other clumsy shroud and hold the watch to it. Expect: Carr, killed by Thomas, in February or March, with several crew present whom I can match to the sketch.
2. Cut open one of the neat shrouds and hold the watch to it. Expect: an early death, perhaps in December when the ship first froze in. The sailmaker is still alive and sews it afterwards. I'll see who held command before Carr did (is the captain alive?).
3. Search the forecastle as it is now: count the sea chests, boots, oilskins, hammocks and bedding that are gone, and look for any names cut in bunks or chests. Expect: about half the crew's cold-weather gear is gone, and that shows who walked to the island.
