# Condensed: Y5, Papers, Please (paper)

Sceptic (sonnet), first game. Three rounds, nine entrants.

**Arc.** The player did the day's sums in round 1 and played a clean shift: every stamp correct, one detention (Brasko's forged passport), two questions, no citations. Danik's offer to frame the journalist arrived in round 2; with the detention bonus the sums no longer bit, so the player refused it at no cost and said so. All bills paid, the family all well, 9 credits left; the next morning's paper carried Moravec's column, naming no one.

**Turns by verb.** Approve 3 (one after a question), deny 5 (one after a question, one with a bribe pushed back), detain 1.

**Pull by round.** r1 5, r2 5, r3 6; exit 5.

**Marks.** Engaged: Halbert (then sour: suspected he let a forger go), Brasko (the shut door), Liskov (Danik's offer, "the first time the world asked me for something"), Moravec (she turned his question round). Curious: Ostrova's silent exit, Soler taking the coins back. Bored: Korsak, Dubova ("free pathos"), Volkov. No would-quit mark, but the exit says he would quit on day 8 unless Danik or the paper reaches him at the window.

**Predictions.** Three: wrong, partly, right (the part due). Misreadings were his own leaps.

**Needs that recurred.** What a wrongful detention costs (asked three times, never answered); what happens if a bill goes unpaid; a way to answer people, not only stamp.

**Stakes.** The player named the risk he ran (warnings, the margin, the son's medicine) and found it never came due: "my refusal of Danik cost me nothing and proved nothing about me."

**GM-simulated.** Which guard took Brasko (roll); Moravec's answer to an uncarded question; recovery of the uncle and mother-in-law when food and heat were paid (the real game's rule).
