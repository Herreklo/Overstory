# Round 1

## Pull
5 — I know this game. The sums already bite (see below), which is the only reason I'm still here. Show me the pressure is real and not a spreadsheet.

## Thinking aloud
Twelve hours at 75 minutes each is about nine and a half entrants. Every correct call pays 5, so a perfect shift with no questions earns about 48. I need 55 for all the bills and have 14 saved, so 41 more: nine clean entrants, which is nearly every slot. Rent (20) is non-negotiable. After that it's food, heat or medicine, and the son is sick, so medicine goes first and someone goes cold or hungry. That is the real game: every question I ask (15 minutes) eats a fifth of an entrant. Two free warnings are the one cushion, and I'd rather spend them on a bold call than a timid one.

Reading the three. Korsak: Arstotzkan, passport city is ours, not expired, name and birthday agree on the ID. Clean. Ostrova: Kolechian, Vedor is a real Kolechian city, permit matches her passport number and hasn't expired. But the bulletin says WORK needs a work pass from Labour, and she has none. The iron works "expects" her, which is exactly what the bulletin was written for. Denial is in the rules, no question needed. Halbert: passport ends B31XM, permit says B31XW. One letter off. Could be a clerk's slip or an altered permit. The guards pay 5 to detain forgery, so I'm tempted, and I notice I'm tempted. I'm treating that as a warning about myself. A single wrong letter is thin evidence, so I'll make him explain it before I decide, and the warning absorbs the risk if I'm wrong.

## Prediction
Ostrova will say the work pass is "coming by post" or "my employer has it", then plead about the iron works, and may try to slip something across the counter. Cold, tired, in a hurry, and she will still be refused.

## Needs

- How the queue ends: do I see every entrant who is left, or does the booth just close?
- What happens at home if a bill goes unpaid, in plain terms.
- Whether I can measure the person against the ID (height, weight) or only believe the paper.
