# Round 2

The rain comes in at about one, soft and fine, more a wetness in the air than a fall. Nobody hurries. Wilf rigs a tarpaulin over the trestles and goes on varnishing underneath it. Teddy puts on a yellow oilskin that comes down to his knees and doesn't move from the edge. Sal carries her bundle across to the Kettle and sorts it under the awning.

**Turn 2.1. Rob Garrow.**
You go down the quay steps to the Puffin and say it: "Your father says he talked to every passenger for forty-one years. You don't talk to any."
Rob looks up from the rope. Rain is beading on the peak of his cap. He nods towards the island. "Island?"
He waits. When you don't step aboard, he goes back to the rope.

**Turn 2.2. Sal Brennan.**
Under the Kettle's awning, you ask whether she still delivers anything to Tolley & Son.
"Arthur Tolley? Retired to his daughter's, inland, in '88. Lovely man." She squares the bundle on her knee and doesn't look for anything in it. "Up and up, and then you're there. That's Wrenmouth for you." She nods at the steps, shining in the wet. "Eighty-seven steps, twice a day, eleven years. Calves like a footballer."

**Turn 2.3. Sitting on May's bench.**
You sit, and the rain sits with you. The Puffin goes out, Rob at the tiller, the wake folding back towards the steps. Under the tarpaulin Wilf's brush goes along the plank and back. Two gulls argue over a crust on the cobbles until one of them wins. The light moves on the water, from pewter to something paler, as a thinner patch of cloud goes over. Nobody goes to the chandler's door. High on the steps, in the geranium window, Dot's needles go and Ivy turns a page.

---

**About five. The Strand.** The rain has stopped. In the west the sky is opening over the headland in a long yellow seam, and every wet roof on the hill has caught it. The cobbles steam a little. At the very top of Wren Steps, along the low wall of the Seamen's Chapel, a row of jam jars is catching the light.

This evening: the lantern walk, from the Seamen's Chapel at dusk.
