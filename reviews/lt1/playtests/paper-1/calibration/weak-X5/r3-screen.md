# Round 3

**Turn 3.1. June Askew.**
The Kettle's bell goes as you open the door. Warm air, the radio low, cakes under their glass domes. June straightens up from the table by the window and smiles as if she's been expecting you all day. "There you are. You'll want this." She puts a key on a wooden fob in your hand: your room. You ask her about the grey cardigan. She nods at the hook by the door, where it hangs, darned at both elbows in a slightly different grey. "Been there since August. Must be a visitor's." She pulls out a chair at the window table. "Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house: you live here now." The cloth goes round the table once more. "It was Mum's café, then mine. I've wiped this table every morning for thirty-one years."

**Turn 3.2. Up Wren Steps.**
You climb as the light goes. The handrail is polished bright in the middle and rough at both ends. At step 20, a house with its paint flaking in layers and a FOR SALE card in the window; at step 50, a marmalade cat on the warm stone. At step 40 the geranium window opens before you reach it.
You say: "Good morning, ladies."
"Good morning yourself!" says Dot, though the lamps are on. Then Dot: "We see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down." Ivy: "And we wave at all of them." Ivy puts the paper down on her knee. "We were born in this house. Both of us, in the back bedroom." They wave you on up.
At the top, the Seamen's Chapel, white boards and a small bell, and along its wall the row of jam jars, each with a wire handle and a candle stub.

---

**The lantern walk.**

They come up the steps and along the lane in ones and twos as the dark comes down. June, with her apron off. Wilf, with varnish on his knuckles. Teddy, without the bucket: Admiral went back in at teatime. Sal, out of uniform, in a green jumper. Dot and Ivy, arm in arm and a step at a time, waved up by everyone they pass. Rob, who nods to you. A man with binoculars round his neck, off the last Puffin. Skipper, on a lead, at the back. Four players from the brass band, a cornet, two horns and a drum, with a gap in the row where the tuba should be.

The candles are lit one from another. June presses a spare jar into your hands, already burning. The band starts something slow, and the walk goes down Wren Steps, the lights bobbing between the houses, past the cat, past the geranium window, past the house with the flaking paint, down to the Strand. One by one the jars are set along the quay's edge, and the harbour holds two rows of lights, one on the granite and one in the water.

You set yours down at the end of the row. For a while nobody says anything much. The band stops. The tide makes small sounds against the steps. Then people start to drift home, and the jars are left to burn down on their own.
