**Arc.** Nine turns over Saturday in Wrenmouth. Round 1 chased what looked like clues (the chandler's peephole, the plaque); round 2 pressed for friction (Rob, Sal, watching the shop door); round 3, under protest, the cardigan and the lantern walk. Belonging 0, 16, 29, 51%; sketches 0 of 48, keepsakes 0 of 20.

**Turns by verb.** Talk 6 (4 in own words), inspect 2, sit 1, walk 1.

**Pull by round.** r1 3, r2 2, r3 1; exit 2 (anchor 6).

**Marks.** Engaged 2 (Wilf's rag; the lantern walk, "as a reader, not a player"), curious 1, bored 6, would quit 1 (the bench sit). Delight 2 (the rag, the tuba gap), cheat 1 (Belonging rose for a needling line that bounced off). After round 2 it said it would quit at home and played round 3 only because made to.

**Predictions.** 3 right, 1 partly, 3 wrong. Wrong ones were its leaps that threads led somewhere (the Son on the plaque, letters for the shut shop, the cardigan as a Hale's).

**Needs that recurred.** A goal, a cost or a deadline; any way to be wrong; something held back to earn by working it out.

**Hollows named.** All three of the plan's: nothing at stake (a meter that fills whatever you do, rewards unearned, the sketch verb never used); choices that change nothing (Rob); people and history as closed décor ("one repeated gesture plus a number of years"). Its sharpest line: the game trains you to stop looking, so its own best details (the sisters as witnesses, the house for sale) went unread.

**GM notes.** Nothing simulated beyond the plan. "Good morning, ladies" at dusk came from the nearest-line rule when the player named no line; it noticed.
