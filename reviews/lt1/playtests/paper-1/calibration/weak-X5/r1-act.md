# Round 1

## Pull
3 — the writing is sharp. Mooring rings polished on top where the ropes have worked them is better observation than this genre usually bothers with. But the store page tells me twice that I can't fail and can't miss anything, and the one number on screen is a percentage for friendship. I'm here for one round because the chandler's window is a real question.

## Thinking aloud
Ask the usual two things. What am I trying to win? 48 sketches, 20 keepsakes and a Belonging bar, which is a checklist with a nice view. What do I risk? Nothing, and the game says so as a selling point. My last game was Papers, Please, and by mid-afternoon nothing in it bit. This one is built so that nothing ever can. So what's left is whether the town holds a question I want answered. Two things in view might. Tolley & Son is whitewashed from inside, with one corner rubbed clear at a child's nose height. Somebody small looks in there often, and the boy with the crab line is standing right there. Then there's the grey cardigan found on the steps, right under the window where the two sisters "have watched the same stair all their lives". Those are clues, not postcards. If inspecting gives me a lovely paragraph and +1 sketch and nothing I can reason from, I'm gone. I'll pick the line to Wilf that offers to do something, because doing beats admiring.

## Prediction
Teddy is the one who rubbed the window clear. The shop has been shut since the "Son" died or left, and the bench plaque will name him. Wilf will either turn down my help kindly or hand me the tin to hold, and Belonging will tick up a few percent whatever I say.

## Needs
- What does Belonging do, beyond decorating my room? Does it open doors, people or places?
- Is there a goal for Chapter One, or any reason the autumn ends?
- Does what I say ever change anything, if there are "no wrong answers"?
- Does a turn use the clock? There's a 10:30 ferry and a walk at dusk. Can I miss them, and does missing them matter?

## Turns
1. Talk to Wilf: "Do you need a hand with that?" (2). — Expect: a kind no, or a job holding the tin, and the boat turns out to be his father's. Belonging goes up.
2. Inspect the rubbed-clear corner of Tolley & Son's window and look through it. — Expect: a shop shut for years with the stock still on the shelves, maybe a model boat or a half-built one, the thing a boy would come back to look at.
3. Talk to Teddy, in my own words: "Is that your peephole in the chandler's window? What's in there?" — Expect: he goes red, then tells me the big plan, which has something to do with that shop.
4. Inspect the brass plaque on the bench. — Expect: a memorial, probably to the "Son" of Tolley & Son, which ties the shut shop to the town's grief.
