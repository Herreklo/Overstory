# Round 1

**Turn 1.1: water.** The can goes into the pond with a glug and comes out heavy. You start with the cauliflowers: twenty-four big pale-green plants, the heads closed tight inside their leaves like fists, one more night to go. Then the bean trellises, the vines curling round the poles and a few white flowers out; then the kale, ruffled and blue-green. At the fortieth tile the can coughs and runs dry; back to the pond, and twelve more. Every watered tile turns dark. **7:20 am.** Energy 180/270. The can has 28 of 40 left.

**Turn 1.2: harvest, replant, ship.** The parsnips come up with a soft pop, fifteen of them, cream-coloured with dirt in the creases; then the potatoes, twenty, knobbly and brown. All normal quality. You keep one parsnip and one potato aside for the Community Center, and drop the rest in the shipping bin with that satisfying thunk: 14 parsnips and 19 potatoes, **2,010g**, paid overnight.

The harvested tiles are still tilled, so you go straight down the rows with seeds: the 12 parsnip seeds, then the 5 strawberry seeds from the Egg Festival in a little row of their own by the fence. Planting costs you nothing but time. **8:10 am.** The new seed tiles are pale and dry beside the dark, watered rows. Eighteen tilled tiles stand empty.

**Turn 1.3: Pam's birthday.** South along the road to town and over the bridge, 25 minutes. The salmonberry bushes along the way are heavy with fruit, dark pink, the last of them; you walk past. **8:35 am.** Pam's trailer sits by the river with its door propped open, a radio going inside. Pam is at the little table in her dressing gown, a mug in front of her.

"Well, look who's up with the birds."

You hold out the leek. She turns it over, sniffs it.

"Huh. A leek. For my birthday?" A pause. "Well. That's real nice of you, kid. Thanks. It'll go in a soup."

A heart floats up over her head. **Pam: 3 hearts.** **8:40 am.**

**Your prediction** (Pam home, pleased, about 3 hearts): she was home and pleased, and she's at 3 hearts.
