# Exit interview (X3, cozy player, paper Stardew Valley)

## 1. Overall pull
8 out of 10. I woke to rain on the roof, 24 cauliflowers beaded with it, a letter from Jodi asking for one and 4,055g in the bank. That is a morning I want to play. It is not a 9 or 10 because nothing is at risk (see the worst list).

## 2. How does it compare with the real game?
What carried over: the tone. Pierre counting packets two at a time, Pam's gruff "kid", Lewis humming at his tulips, Robin with a pencil behind her ear. Those lines sounded like the villagers. The routine also felt right: water, harvest, ship, replant, then wander to people. The shipping tally the next morning is the best part of the loop and it worked on paper.

What is missing:
- **My hands.** In the real game I choose every tile, take the odd detour, and meet someone by accident. Here I only meet people I walk to, and "water 52 tiles" is one sentence. Mistakes can't happen, so nothing is learnt.
- **Hidden information.** The paper screen hands me the whole calendar, every friendship number and every price up front. In the real game I'd dig for those, and finding a thing out is half the fun.
- **Surprise.** No random forageable, no weather swing, no stranger on the road. Everything I asked for happened as asked.
- **Time pressure I feel.** The clock was a number. I never felt a day slip away.

## 3. Best and worst
**Best**
- The robin that watched me pick salmonberries, and my pink fingers.
- Pam's birthday leek: small, kind, and it paid off exactly as I predicted.
- The scurrying green thing in the Community Center, and the shut door with music at Robin's. Both are things I want to follow.
- The rain morning: dark tiles, cauliflowers open, a letter from Jodi. It turned a crop into a favour for someone.
- The overnight shipping tally.

**Worst**
- **The afternoon was played for me.** After my three turns the game master wrote "the rest of the day, as you planned it", and I ended it with energy 10/270 and the stone still at 85. I said "afternoon free for chopping wood" and "berries for tomorrow". I did not say chop ten trees until I drop. With twelve salmonberries (25 energy each) in my pocket, I would have eaten some and mined or bought the missing 15 stone. That is a real cheat, and it left the coop blocked by a few stones.
- **Nothing can go wrong.** Deadlines (daffodil, Jodi) are soft and my own predictions keep coming true. For a cozy player this is nice for a while, then it makes me careless.
- **The Flower Dance letter.** It made the dance sound like a wall ("no one will say yes below 4 hearts") and Lewis only explained watching from the edge when I asked. I'd have liked that line on the first screen.
- **Gifts are opaque.** I never learnt what anyone but Pam likes, so Shane's birthday on the 20th is a blank.

## 4. Marks for round 3
- Turn 3.1 (Lewis): engaged — warm, and "watch from the edge with the rest of us" took the pressure off the dance. Surprise: delight — relief. (No heart bump showed; I wanted one.)
- Turn 3.2 (Demetrius and Robin): engaged — the walk past the lake, 90g, Robin selling wood at 10g and stone at 20g. Surprise: delight — the music behind the shut door.
- Turn 3.3 (planting): bored — pure routine, resolved in two lines, and fine as that. Surprise: none.
- The afternoon (not mine to mark, but it happened): would quit, for a moment — I lost the choice and my energy. Surprise: cheat — see above.
- Morning of Spring 19: curious, delight — the rain, the open cauliflowers, Jodi's request.

## What I'd do next
Rain day, full energy. Pick the best cauliflower for Jodi (350g), put another in the Community Center bundle, ship the rest, eat nothing, then buy the 15 stone from Robin and order the coop. Then think about Shane's gift, if someone tells me what he likes.
