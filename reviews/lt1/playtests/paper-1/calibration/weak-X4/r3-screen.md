# Round 3

The evening comes on clear and still. The water in the harbour goes from gold to the blue of a mussel shell, and the first lights come on in the houses up the hill, one and then another.

**Turn 3.1. Talk: Rob Garrow.**
The Puffin ties up at the quay steps, her engine ticking as it cools. Rob makes the stern rope fast to a ring without looking at it.
You say: "You'd have carried the visitors over in August. Do you remember anyone in a grey cardigan, darned at the elbows?"
Rob looks at you for a moment, then nods towards the island. "Island?"
He waits. When you don't step aboard, he goes back to coiling the rope.
Next time, Rob:
1. Board the Puffin.

**Turn 3.2. Talk: Sal Brennan.**
Sal is still under the café's awning, the bundle of letters on her knee, the red bicycle beside her.
You say: "The house at step 20, the boarded one. Who lives there? Do you still take post up?"
"Mrs Pennock? She went to her son's in the spring." Sal glances up the steps. "Pretty, isn't it, all those colours coming through?" She squares the bundle against her knee. "Up and up, and then you're there. That's Wrenmouth for you."
She flexes one foot in its black shoe. "Eighty-seven steps, twice a day, eleven years. Calves like a footballer."
Next time, Sal:
1. "Anything for me?"
2. "Busy morning?"
3. "Which way's the chapel?"

**Turn 3.3. Walk: Wren Steps, to step 20. Inspect: the Pennock house.**
A narrow house of weatherboard, gable to the harbour. Its paint is flaking in layers, so the front is a map of the colours it has been: white on top, green showing through where the white has gone, and under the green, in the oldest places by the sill, the first ochre. One window is boarded with a sheet of ply. In another, a FOR SALE card is propped against the glass. The door is shut, white over green, with no name beside it. The letterbox is closed.
*The Pennock house can now be sketched.*

**Turn 3.4. Walk: Chapel Lane, at dusk.**
You go up to the top of the steps as the light goes. The Seamen's Chapel stands white against the last of the sky, its door open, candlelight inside. The jam jars wait along the chapel wall. You watch who comes.

## The lantern walk

They come up the lane in ones and twos. June with her apron off. Wilf with varnish on his knuckles. Teddy, his bucket left at home: he let Admiral go at teatime. Sal out of uniform, in a duffle coat. Dot and Ivy, arm in arm, a step at a time. Rob. Cyril, off the last Puffin, binoculars still round his neck. Skipper, on a lead. And the Wrenmouth Brass Band, all four of them, no tuba.

Each takes a jar from the wall and lights the candle inside. June sees your empty hands and presses a spare into them, its wire handle cold. There are as many jars as people, and every jar is lit.

The band starts a slow tune, the bass line missing where the tuba should be, and the walk goes down Wren Steps, the lights bobbing between the houses. Dot and Ivy's window is dark as they pass it. At the Strand the jars are set one by one along the quay's edge, and the harbour holds two rows of lights, one on the stone and one in the water. No one says anything for a while. Then June says, "Well," and people start to go home.

You stand at the end of the row. The candles burn down. Someone has left the Kettle's light on for you.
