# Round 1

## Pull
5. The town is written with care, but the store page told me there is nothing to get wrong. That's the sound of a game that won't let me deduce.

## Thinking aloud
The prose is good. The mooring rings are polished on top where the ropes work them, and you can smell toast. But "no fail states, no wrong answers, you can't miss anything", a journal that writes itself and a Belonging percentage? That's a checklist with a fiddle score. Everything I love in a mystery is the chance of being wrong, and this game has promised me I can't be. I'm wary.

Still, the screen has more grit than the store page. A ship chandler's whitewashed from inside, with one corner rubbed clear at a child's height: someone small keeps looking into a shop that's shut. It's "Tolley & Son", so where's the son? There's a brass plaque on a bench, which is usually a death. Wilf is varnishing **one** plank, which means a new plank in an old boat, a repair, so something happened to the *Marigold*. Wilf and Rob Garrow share a name. The grey cardigan found on the steps, and two sisters who have watched that stair all their lives: if anyone saw who dropped it, they did.

I'll give it an hour to show me it hides something. If every story is told to me in full on the first talk, I'm off.

## Prediction
The chandler's closed when the "Son" died or left, and the bench plaque is his. Teddy is the one with his nose to the clear corner, because something inside the shop matters to him: a boat, a model, a chart.

## Needs
- Is there anything to *work out* in this game, or only to collect? "A story to uncover" and "no wrong answers" pull against each other.
- What does Belonging do besides fill up? "Watch your room" is vague.

## Turns
1. Talk to Wilf, in my own words: "Morning. I am. Why only the one plank?" Expect: the *Marigold* was damaged and that plank is new. He tells me his father built her and dodges the how.
2. Inspect the bench's brass plaque. Expect: a name and dates, maybe a Tolley, maybe "lost at sea", with a short line of verse.
3. Inspect the rubbed-clear corner of Tolley & Son's window and look through it. Expect: a shop shut for years with its stock still on the shelves, and one thing in sightline from that corner that a boy would stare at.
4. Talk to Teddy at the quay's edge: "Have you ever been inside Tolley's?" Expect: he's cagey, or he tells me what he looks at through the window. Either way, it's the boy's "very big plans".
