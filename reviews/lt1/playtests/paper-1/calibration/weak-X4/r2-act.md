## Pull
4. The town is pretty, but every time I find an edge, the game sands it smooth.

## Thinking aloud
The journal is the problem. It writes what I think ("maybe here there isn't") and it answers my questions before I can ask them. I got +16% Belonging and a geranium for small talk. That's a reward for being nice, not for noticing anything.

Threads left: Tolley's closed in March 1988 (why?), Rob who won't talk, May's bench, and the grey cardigan found on the steps. The Hale sisters have watched that stair all their lives, which makes them the only witnesses this game has offered. If they can't give me a disagreement, a date or a lie, then this is a postcard rack and I'll say so.

## Prediction
The cardigan is a fetch quest. The sisters know whose it is, I carry it back, and I get a keepsake and some Belonging. No one is hiding anything.

## Needs
- Does "your own words" actually work? It didn't this round.
- Can I switch the journal's voice off, or at least stop it drawing my conclusions for me?

## Turns
1. Walk into the Kettle and talk to June, in my own words: "The grey cardigan on the board: who found it, and where on the steps?" Expect: it's behind the counter. She tells me who found it and maybe guesses whose it is.
2. If she shows it to me, inspect the cardigan: its labels, the pockets, the wear and any mending. Expect: a name tape or something in a pocket, and a darn I can match to someone.
3. Walk up Wren Steps to the Hales' window and talk to Dot and Ivy: "You see everyone on these steps. Who dropped the cardigan? And when did Tolley's shut?" Expect: the two of them remember it differently, and one of them knows why March 1988.
4. If none of that gives me a real question, sit on May's bench and watch the Strand until dusk: who comes to the bench, whether Rob speaks to anyone, and who walks past Tolley's. Expect: Wilf sits on it, or no one does.
