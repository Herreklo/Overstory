# Round 2

A little after one the rain comes in off the water, soft and fine, more a wet air than a falling. The gulls go up to the chimneys and sit there hunched.

**Turn 2.1. Talk: June Askew.**
The Kettle's door has a bell on a spring. Inside it is warm and close: six tables, cakes under glass domes, the radio low, the windows running. June straightens from the table by the window with a cloth in her hand. "You'll be wanting this, love." She gives you a key on a loop of red wool. *You have the key to your room.*
You say: "The grey cardigan on the board: who found it, and where on the steps?"
June looks over at the hook by the door, where it hangs. "Been there since August. Must be a visitor's." She goes back to the table. "Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house: you live here now."
She folds the cloth and starts again at the corner. "It was Mum's café, then mine. I've wiped this table every morning for thirty-one years."
Next time, June:
1. "Is my room ready?"
2. "What's good here?"
3. "What a lovely place."

**Turn 2.2. Inspect: the grey cardigan.**
Grey wool, hand-knitted in a plain rib, gone soft and a little felted with wear. Darned at both elbows, neatly, in a grey just slightly different from the rest, so the darns show as two pale islands. No label. The pockets are empty. It smells of nothing but the café: coffee and damp coats.

**Turn 2.3. Walk: Wren Steps, to the Hales' window. Talk: Dot and Ivy Hale.**
The steps are wet and shining, the handrail cold and slick. At step 20 a house with its paint flaking in layers, one window boarded. At step 40 the window full of geraniums swings open before you reach it, and two white heads lean out under the drip from the sill.
You say: "You see everyone on these steps. Who dropped the cardigan? And when did Tolley's shut?"
Dot, knitting without looking at her needles: "The cardigan? June's had it on her hook since August. A visitor's, we expect." Ivy: "And Arthur Tolley retired to his daughter's, inland, in '88. Lovely man."
Dot: "We see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down." Ivy: "And we wave at all of them."
Dot holds the long blue scarf up to the light, considers it, and lets it fall back into her lap. "We were born in this house. Both of us, in the back bedroom."
Next time, Dot and Ivy:
1. "Good morning, ladies."
2. "What are you knitting?"
3. "Anything in the paper?"

**Turn 2.4. Sit: May's bench.**
You go back down and sit on the bench in the rain, which is easing. Rob takes the Puffin out with nobody aboard, standing at the tiller, and does not look back. Wilf works on under a tarpaulin rigged over the trestles, the brush going along the same plank. Teddy, in a yellow oilskin two sizes too big, has not moved from the quay's edge. Sal has taken her letters under the café's awning. Two gulls argue over a crust on the cobbles until one of them wins. The light moves on the water, pewter, then silver, then pewter again. No one comes to the bench. No one walks past Tolley's.

---

**About five.** The rain has gone over. In the west the cloud lifts off the sea like a lid, and a long bar of yellow light comes in under it and lays itself across the harbour. Every wet roof on the hill shines at once. High up on Chapel Lane, along the chapel wall, a row of jam jars catches the light.

*This evening: the lantern walk, from the Seamen's Chapel at dusk.*

**Where you are: May's bench, on the Strand.** In view:
