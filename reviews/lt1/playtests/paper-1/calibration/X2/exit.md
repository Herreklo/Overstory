# Exit interview: Hob's Ford (paper Baldur's Gate 3)

## 1. Marks, round 3
- Turn 3.1: curious. Vask alone with a cup he isn't drinking and a locked iron door behind him. It's a good picture, and it gave Astarion's lockpicking something to want. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.2: engaged. The mill's own bar with the wheel stamp, found in the reeds, was the first real clue I dug up myself, and spending Inspiration on the reroll felt good. Then the Perception check failed and gave nothing again. Surprise: delight at the bar.
- Turn 3.3: curious. Twenty gold bought me "stay with Crane", which is close to nothing. Astarion's disapproval and his haircut line saved it, and the hundred-gold price to make the Kettles walk is a real lever I'd have used. Surprise: none.
- Night camp: engaged. Karlach tying a boy on a post to her own sale to Zariel is exactly why I play BG3: a companion's past reacting to this particular choice. Surprise: delight.
- First light: engaged, with a complaint. The dilemma is finally fully loaded. The truth clears Tobin of the assault, but it hangs both boys under Crane's law and takes Pell's only roof. Then the session ends. Worse, the most important scene of the case, the witness waking, happened offscreen while I slept and reached me as Shadowheart's report. Surprise: cheat. Not unfair, but the game took the climax out of my hands.

## 2. Overall pull
7 for the session. 9 to play the next round, because now every choice costs someone something.

## 3. Compared with the real game
The voices held up. Karlach, Astarion and Shadowheart sounded like themselves, and the approval lines landed. The NPCs weren't cardboard either: Maud's "I'll not lie to him" is BG3-grade writing. The dice drama works on paper, and the best moment in the session was a failed roll that turned into "Tobin, run".

What was missing:
- **Failures that leave something behind.** Three of my failures (Insight twice, Perception) gave me nothing at all. BG3 isn't perfect at this either, but it usually opens another path when you fail.
- **The reroll at the moment of failure.** I only found out halfway through that Inspiration could cover a companion's roll.
- **Free movement.** Walking costs a turn and I get three turns for four people, so the party played like one person with three extra faces. My bard, the talker, spent a whole round in a sickroom with nothing to say.
- **Fighting as a real option.** It was listed but never felt like one. Fifteen farmers plus the miller's men isn't a fight, it's a massacre.
- **A thread of my own.** Tav had no stake in any of it. In BG3 even a blank Tav has the tadpole and the dream visitor.
- **Pace.** Three rounds here would be about fifteen minutes of the real game. I enjoyed it, but slowly.

I also couldn't test the thing I care about most: whether choices come back acts later. Three rounds can show you a choice has teeth, but not that it bites.

## 4. Best and worst
**Best:**
- "Tobin, *run*." A failed Medicine check produced evidence that cuts both ways, held by a witness who won't lie about it.
- The natural 19 that didn't solve anything. It moved the problem onto one sleeping boy, and my own persuasion came back to bite me.
- Karlach at the fire, with her past answering the present.
- "Nobody hit him." It overturned my prediction and turned a whodunit into a problem with the system itself: the weir, the moot, and a law that hangs whoever touches the sluice. That's more interesting than a culprit.

**Worst:**
- The climax happened offscreen. I wanted to be at the cot when Pell spoke, with my Deception +7, deciding whether to coach him.
- Dead failures that left nothing behind.
- The clock went soft after my persuasion. For a round I could poke around with no pressure, because only my own choice to wake Pell could set anything off.
- The opening gave away too much. A higher weir and dry barley told me the motive in the first paragraph. The mystery was only ever who touched the boy, and the answer was nobody.
- The turn budget made the party feel small, and walking cost as much as a decision.

Would I play on? Yes, for this next choice: tell Crane the truth and hang them both under his law, lie for them, take it to the moot, or cut Tobin loose and run. If the next round handed me a solution instead of that choice, I'd quit.
