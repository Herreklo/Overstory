# Round 2

## Marks
- Turn 1.1: curious. The roll failed, but the thumb on the keys and the hayfork in my line of sight were still something to go on, and the failure cost me nothing. Surprise: none.
- Turn 1.2: engaged. A natural 19, two approvals, Karlach's line, and the miller walking off fast after a word to his man. The win didn't solve anything: it moved the whole problem onto one sleeping witness, which is how a good win should work. Surprise: delight. I bought time and in the same move made Pell the most dangerous person in the Ford.
- Turn 1.3: engaged. A lot came back and all of it was useful. My prediction was wrong: nobody hit Pell, he hit stone. The clean knuckles, the soaked smock, and "thick as thieves" all point the same way. Surprise: delight. Investigating paid off and the case changed shape.

## Pull
8. I now think the real question is what I'm willing to do to a witness, and I want to find out.

## Thinking aloud
Tobin has confessed the gate to Astarion, and under Crane's law the gate is a hanging matter on its own. My persuasion tied the rope to "who hit him", and if Pell wakes and says nobody did, Crane may simply hang Tobin for the sluice. So clearing him of the assault doesn't save him. The weir is the real lever: it was raised two foot without the moot. Pell is wet to the knee, torn at the hem, and Tobin's best friend. Either he was up there with Tobin, or Vask sent him out in the dark to shut the gate and he slipped. In both cases, what Pell says first decides everything, and I want to be the first person he says it to. One honest grumble: the clock has gone soft. Dusk only comes if I leave or rest, so right now I could poke around forever. That takes the edge off.

## Prediction
Pell will say he went to the sluice in the night, slipped on the mossy step and went in, and that nobody struck him. Crane will then fall back on the gate itself and Tobin's confession, and the fight will move to the weir and who allowed it. Vask has gone up to the mill to make sure the gate looks as if it was never his fault.
