# Round 3

**Saturday evening.**

## Turn 3.1

You sit on the low wall across from the Hales' window. The stone is still damp through your coat.

The gold light slides up the roofs and off the top of the hill, and the harbour darkens from its edges in. Down the slope, windows come on one at a time, yellow squares stacked above each other. Behind the geraniums Dot's needles go on; Ivy turns a page of the Gazette. On step 50 the marmalade cat gets up, turns round once, and lies down again in the same place.

Nobody comes up the steps, and nobody goes down.

## Turn 3.2

You keep sitting. Dusk.

At the top of the steps, small lights appear along the chapel wall, one, then three, then a whole row, as the jars are lit. Voices drift down, and a trombone tries one low note and stops. The Hales' window closes, and the lamp behind it goes out.

Nobody comes down to you.

## Turn 3.3

You keep sitting. The lantern walk comes down Wren Steps.

The band comes first, four of them, a cornet, two trombones and a drum, playing something slow, a step at a time. Behind them the lights come bobbing, held out on their wire handles. Dot and Ivy step out of their front door arm in arm and fall in, Dot's free hand on the rail. Wilf goes past with varnish still on his knuckles and nods to you. Teddy, his bucket left at home, carries his jar in both hands. Sal, out of uniform, in a red jumper. Rob, saying nothing. A man with binoculars round his neck, **Cyril Bowe**, off the last Puffin. An old black dog on a lead: **Skipper**.

June, with her apron off, stops at your wall and holds out a jam jar.

**You say:** "Not tonight, thanks."

June puts it into your hands anyway, wire handle and all, with a candle stub inside, and tips her own flame to light it. "For tonight." Then she goes on down with the others.

## Turn 3.4

You keep sitting until it's over.

From step 40 you watch the lights wind down the stair between the houses, out onto the Strand and along the quay. One by one the jars are set down along the quay's edge, until there is a row of small flames on the granite and another row under it in the black water: the harbour holds two rows of lights. The band plays something you don't know. People stand a while in twos and threes. Then the lights start to go, one at a time, as people pick up their jars and go home.

The candle in your jar burns down to a blue bead and goes out.

## The evening

Later, in your room above the Kettle, the window is black and the harbour is a few lights on the water. The geranium is on the sill, and the cat beside it, asleep, and the jam jar beside them, its glass still faintly warm. Downstairs the radio says the shipping forecast to an empty café. Somewhere below, a rope creaks against a ring. You lie on the narrow bed under the patchwork quilt and listen to the gulls a while, and then you don't hear them.
