# Exit interview: Saltlight

## 1. Overall pull
2 out of 10.

## 2. Best and worst
**Best:** the traces. The handrail polished bright in the middle and rough at both ends. The Pennock house showing white over green over ochre, with a FOR SALE card in the window. A new red roof against mossy neighbours. The darns in a slightly different grey. The harbour holding two rows of lights. These are things a reader can draw a history from, and for a moment they made me want to act. Cyril Bowe, a stranger with binoculars off the last Puffin, is the one hook left standing. The cardigan is "a visitor's", and here at last is a visitor. I noticed. I don't trust the game to pay it off.

**Worst:** nothing I did mattered, and I proved it.
- **Belonging is a clock.** It gave +16 for a round of trying, +13 for a round where every attempt failed, and +24, the biggest gain of the game, for four turns of sitting on a wall and refusing the one event. Doing nothing is the dominant strategy, and the chapter completed itself around me.
- **My refusal was overruled.** I said "Not tonight, thanks" and June put the jar in my hands anyway. That jar was my only keepsake. I was rewarded for declining.
- **Deduction paid nothing.** I took the clue (darned, so local) to the witnesses who "see everyone", and three people gave me the same canned line. Thinking earned exactly what small talk earns.
- **The score was opaque.** Sketchbook 0 of 48 after twelve turns, with nothing ever marked as sketchable and a whole turn lost to "nothing here". I asked three times for Belonging per turn and got it per round.
- **"You can't take that with you"**, on the only obvious action, with no reason given.
- **The game's two promises cancel out.** "You can't miss anything" turned out to be true only because the game pushes content onto you whether you take part or not.

## 3. Chapter Two? $14.99?
No, and no. Chapter Two promises "new sketches and keepsakes to find", which means more counters, and these counters fill themselves. I'd only open it to check whether Cyril and the cardigan connect, and I'd quit within a round if the answer was another "a visitor's, we expect". At $14.99 I'd rather pay for a game where a choice can be wrong. If I want this mood, a video of a harbour costs nothing and asks just as much of me.

## 4. What I was trying to achieve, and what I risked
I was trying to find the economy: what raises Belonging, what earns a sketch, which loop to grind. I was also trying to solve the one thread that looked solvable, the cardigan, and to test the game's two claims ("no wrong answers", "can't miss anything") by breaking them. When the deduction stonewalled, I switched to the degenerate test, idling through the set piece. It confirmed the exploit.

What I risked: nothing, and that's the finding. I deliberately tried to lose something by missing the lantern walk, and the game wouldn't even let me lose that. With no stakes, there is no decision. With no decision, an optimiser has nothing to optimise, and a reader has nothing to read for.

## 5. Marks for round 3
- Turn 3.1: bored. A pretty dusk, and nobody came up or down. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.2: bored. Jars lit at the top of the stair while I sat out of it. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.3: would quit. I said no, and June gave me the jar and lit it anyway. The game overrode the one real choice I made. Cyril Bowe made me briefly curious. Surprise: cheat. The keepsake came as a reward for refusing.
- Turn 3.4: would quit. Sitting out four turns earned +24 Belonging, a keepsake and "Chapter One complete". Surprise: cheat. The exploit works: AFK is the best strategy, and the game praises it.

## Compared with the first game
Slay the Spire's paper port held up under audit: every number mattered, and I could lose. Saltlight has no rules to audit. The game I was able to play here was the test of whether it was a game.
