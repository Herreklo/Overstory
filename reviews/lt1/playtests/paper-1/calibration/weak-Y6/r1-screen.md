# Round 1

**Saturday morning.**

## Turn 1.1

**You say:** "Do you need a hand with that?"

Wilf sits back on his heels and fishes a rag out of his coat pocket. "Catch that drip. There. Good." You hold the rag under the plank's edge while his brush goes along it in one long stroke. The varnish smells of pine and something sharper. The third plank up from the keel shines like toffee; the others are dull and waiting.

"She'll see me out, this one."

He dips the brush and wipes it on the tin's lip. "Forty-one years I took the Puffin over to the island. Rob has her now. He doesn't talk to the passengers. I talked to every one."

Down at the steps, Rob goes on coiling his rope.

**Wilf, next time:**
1. "How's she coming along?"
2. "Can I help today?"
3. "Tell me about the Puffin."

## Turn 1.2

**The bench.** Grey-painted slats, salt-bleached at the ends, facing straight out over the water. The brass plaque on the backrest has gone soft brown except where the letters stand up:

FOR MAY, WHO LIKED IT HERE.
1921–1990

A gull lands on the far end of the bench, looks at you, and leaves.

## Turn 1.3

There's nothing here for your sketchbook.

## Turn 1.4

The Kettle's door has a bell on a spring. Inside it is warm and close: six tables with oilcloth covers, cakes under glass domes on the counter, the radio murmuring about Dogger and German Bight. June is wiping the table by the window in slow circles.

**You say:** "About the cardigan on the noticeboard..."

June looks over at the hook by the door, where a grey cardigan hangs, hand-knitted, darned at both elbows in a slightly different grey. "Been there since August. Must be a visitor's."

She dries her hands on her apron and takes a key on a wooden fob from her pocket. "Here, love, that's yours. Up the side door, first on the left." She presses it into your hand. "Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house: you live here now."

She goes back to the table, and the cloth goes round. "It was Mum's café, then mine. I've wiped this table every morning for thirty-one years."

By the till, a photograph in a plain frame: a woman squinting into the sun outside the café, beside this same window table, 1962 in biro in the corner.

**June, next time:**
1. "Is my room ready?"
2. "What's good here?"
3. "What a lovely place."

## Where you are

**The Kettle**, about noon. Through the steamed window, the cloud over the harbour has thickened to the colour of slate, and the water has gone flat and pewter.

**In view**
- **June Askew**, wiping the table by the window.
- **The window table**, its oilcloth still damp from the cloth.
- **The cakes**, under glass domes: lemon, a Victoria sponge, flapjack.
- **The radio**, on the shelf behind the counter.
- **The photograph**, by the till.
- **The grey cardigan**, on its hook by the door.
- **The side door**, and the stairs up to your room.
- **The door to the Strand.**
