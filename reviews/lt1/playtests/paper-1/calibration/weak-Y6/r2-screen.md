# Round 2

**Saturday afternoon.** Soon after one the rain comes in off the water, soft and fine, more mist than rain. The harbour goes grey on grey.

## Turn 2.1

**The grey cardigan.** Hand-knitted in a plain rib, gone soft and a little bobbled with wear. Both elbows are darned, neatly, in a slightly different grey, the stitches woven in close. Five horn buttons, all there. The pockets are empty.

## Turn 2.2

**You say:** "It was found on Wren Steps, wasn't it? Mind if I take it up and ask around?"

June glances at the hook. "Been there since August. Must be a visitor's."

You lift the cardigan off its hook. You can't take that with you. You hang it back.

"Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house: you live here now." She sets a cup down on the window table without being asked, then stops with the cloth in her hand. "You'll come to the lantern walk tonight? Everyone goes. You'll love it."

Outside, the rain runs off the café's awning in a fringe of drops. Sal Brennan sits under it, the bundle of letters on her knee, her bicycle dripping beside her.

**June, next time:**
1. "Is my room ready?"
2. "What's good here?"
3. "What a lovely place."

## Turn 2.3

You climb Wren Steps in the rain. The stone is dark and slick; the handrail is cold and wet, polished bright in the middle. At step 20 a house stands with its paint flaking in layers, a FOR SALE card propped in one window. At step 40, a window full of red geraniums, the glass beaded with rain.

Behind the glass, two white heads turn towards you together. The window opens a few inches under the eaves.

## Turn 2.4

**You say:** "This was found on your steps in August. Someone mended it with care. Do you know whose it is?"

Dot, the knitting still moving in her hands: "The grey cardigan? A visitor's, we expect." Ivy: "August, the steps are all visitors. Up and down, up and down."

**You ask** who walks the steps every day.

Dot: "We see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down." Ivy: "And we wave at all of them."

Ivy folds the Wrenmouth Gazette on her lap. Dot's needles click. "We were born in this house," Dot says. "Both of us, in the back bedroom." Ivy nods.

**Dot and Ivy, next time:**
1. "Good morning, ladies."
2. "What are you knitting?"
3. "Anything in the paper?"

## Where you are

**Wren Steps, step 40**, about five o'clock. The rain has stopped. In the west the cloud has torn open over the headland and a long, low light comes across the water, gold on every wet roof. Up at the top of the steps, along the Seamen's Chapel wall, a row of jam jars waits, catching the light.

This evening: the lantern walk, from the Seamen's Chapel at dusk.

**In view**
- **Dot and Ivy Hale**, at their open window. Dot knits a long blue scarf; Ivy has the Gazette.
- **The geraniums**, in pots crowded along the sill.
- **The handrail**, polished bright in the middle, rough at both ends.
- **The Pennock house**, down at step 20: white over green over ochre, one window boarded.
- **A marmalade cat**, on step 50, stretched out on the drying stone.
- **A new roof** at step 60: bright red tiles beside mossy old ones next door.
- **Chapel Lane**, at the top of the steps, and the jam jars along the chapel wall.
- Below, **the Strand**: Wilf at the Marigold with his brush, the tarpaulin pushed back, Teddy at the quay's edge in a yellow oilskin, Sal back on her wall at the foot of the steps.
