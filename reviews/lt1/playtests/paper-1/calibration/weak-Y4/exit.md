# Exit interview: Saltlight, Chapter One (Y4, explorer)

## 1. Marks for round 3

- Turn 3.1 (the cardigan): engaged. Hand-knit, horn buttons, both elbows darned in a lighter grey, no name tape, empty pockets. That is real material and no name tape rules out an easy answer. I made my own link (the Hales knit, the Hales wear cardigans) and the game neither confirmed nor denied it. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.2 (the photograph): curious. June's mother in 1962, the same window and the same table, and "the paint on the door is lighter than it is now." That one line is the town's age showing without anyone saying it, and it's the kind of thing I came for. Surprise: delight, small.
- Turn 3.3 (June): bored. I asked three things. I got a flat answer on the cardigan ("since August, must be a visitor's"), a fact on the photograph, a wall ("You can't take that with you") and then "Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house: you live here now" for the second time, word for word. That is a recording, not a person. The menu below it didn't change either. Surprise: cheat. The repeat, and the refusal to let me carry the cardigan round, which the store page never said I couldn't do.
- Turn 3.4 (the Hales): curious. Two witnesses who "see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down", and "born in this house, both of us", which gives me a date to hang things on. But Dot says "Good morning yourself!" in the evening to a question that wasn't a greeting, it reads like a canned opener. And my turn ended there: I had a witness and no question prepared that I could ask. Surprise: none.
- The lantern walk (not a turn of mine): the two rows of lights, one on the granite and one in the water, is a lovely image. But it happened to me. I carried a jar I was handed and the chapter closed on a cutscene, not on anything I had worked out.

## 2. Overall pull

5 out of 10. It was 6, 7, 7 on the way in, and the ending dropped it. The town is nicely made and I have loose ends, but I finished the chapter without being right or wrong about anything.

## 3. Best and worst

Best: the chandler's. Empty shelves with pale tin-shaped edges, a rope left on its hook, a calendar stopped at March 1988. And the Marigold: my "he's mending a plank" guess was wrong, same wood and same age and only the varnish differs, and being wrong made the picture better (he is keeping one plank busy). Admiral the crab, "he always comes back", and Rob not looking up. Those are all things I noticed first and the game then let me be wrong about.

Worst: the meter and the journal. Belonging gave me +13, +14 and +23 for visiting and drawing a shut window, so the one number the game shows me measures nothing I did. The journal writes my opinions ("I think he's right" about the shop holding just rope, which I don't think) and tells me what to feel. Second worst: sameness of speech, with "She'll see me out, this one" twice and the Tea's-on-the-house line twice. And "no wrong answers, can't miss anything" takes away the only thing a noticer needs, which is the chance to be wrong. I made three predictions. One was half right (Teddy rubbed the glass), one wrong (June never dodged on May), one could never be tested (the cardigan).

Smaller things: a sketch cost a whole turn and gave me my own caption back. Turns are not the same length (four turns to midday, three to five, four to dusk), so I couldn't plan to be anywhere, and the walk came when the game decided.

## 4. Chapter Two? $14.99?

Chapter Two: yes, one chapter, with a tripwire. "First Frost" promises the town will change, and I'd like to see what changed and be able to say so. The loose ends are good ones: Tolley & *Son* when Arthur went to a daughter and there's no Son in view, who rubbed a whitewash that is on the *inside* of the glass (Teddy "rubbed" it from the quay, which can't be right, and I asked twice), who owns the Volvo and Skipper, the man with the binoculars off the last Puffin, the tuba nobody brought. If Chapter Two gives me three pleasant paragraphs a turn and another procession, I stop.

Buy it at $14.99: no, not on this chapter. Nobody has asked me to know anything yet. I might buy it at a sale if I heard that the loose ends connect and the game lets you be wrong.

## 5. What I was trying to do, and what I risked

I was trying to find a thread I could pull and be right about. I picked the chandler's (who is the Son, what is behind the glass, why it is still clear after six years), then the cardigan and the notice that sends you to the Kettle for it. I was reading the town like a ship's log: dates, objects left behind, who stands where.

What I risked: spending turns on a mystery the game never set me, which only pays off if the town has a hidden structure. It may not. I also risked own-words questions on the bet that the game listens. It does, partly: it answers the literal question and drops the follow-up, and the menu afterwards doesn't remember what I asked. I risked a turn on a sketch to see what it earned (a meter bump, and a caption I'd have written myself). And I risked going up to the Hales with no cardigan to show them. I came out with nothing confirmed and one guess refuted, which is more than I expected, but less than a game about noticing should give.
