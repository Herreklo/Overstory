# Round 3

## Turn 3.1: the grey cardigan

It hangs by its collar from a brass hook by the door, beside June's coat. Grey wool, hand-knitted in a plain stitch, the buttons horn. Both elbows have been darned, neatly, in a grey a shade lighter than the rest, so the patches show when the light comes through the door. No name tape in the collar. The pockets are empty. It smells of wool, and of the café.

## Turn 3.2: the photograph

A small black frame on the wall by the till. A woman in a flowered apron stands outside the Kettle with her hands folded in front of her, squinting into the sun, beside the window and the table behind its glass: the same window, the same table. The paint on the door is lighter than it is now. Along the white border, in faded blue ink: *1962*.

## Turn 3.3: June

You say: "Whose is the cardigan, and how long has it been there? And who's that in the photograph? Could I take the cardigan round and ask?"

June looks at the hook. "Been there since August. Must be a visitor's." She follows your eyes to the frame. "That's Mum, the summer she opened."

When you reach for the hook: *You can't take that with you.*

"Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house: you live here now." She folds her cloth over the back of a chair. "You'll come to the lantern walk tonight? Everyone goes. You'll love it."

**June's menu**

2. "What's good here?"
3. "What a lovely place."

## Turn 3.4: the Hales' window

Up Wren Steps in the last of the light, the handrail cold and wet under your hand, worn bright in the middle. At step 40 the window full of geraniums swings open before you can knock on the glass, and there they are, two white heads and two cardigans, one blue scarf in progress across both their laps.

You say: "What do you see from up here?"

"Good morning yourself!" says Dot, though it's evening, and Ivy laughs. Dot: "We see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down." Ivy: "And we wave at all of them."

Ivy folds the Gazette on her knee. "We were born in this house. Both of us, in the back bedroom."

**Dot and Ivy's menu, next time**
1. "Good morning, ladies."
2. "What are you knitting?"
3. "Anything in the paper?"

## The lantern walk

The bell goes at the top of the town, and they come up the steps past you in ones and twos. June with her apron off. Wilf, with varnish on his knuckles. Teddy, without his bucket: Admiral went back at teatime. Sal in a green jumper instead of her uniform. Dot and Ivy, arm in arm, a step at a time, and everyone slows behind them without seeming to. Rob, saying nothing. A man in a waxed jacket with binoculars round his neck, off the last Puffin. Skipper on a lead at the back, in no hurry. The brass band, four of them, cornet, two horns and a euphonium, and no tuba.

At the chapel wall the jars are handed out and the candles lit from one taper. You have no jar, so June presses a spare into your hands, warm from hers: a jam jar with a wire handle and a candle stub inside.

Then down they go, all of you, the band playing something slow, the lights swinging on their wires, down the eighty-seven steps between the dark houses, past the Hales' window with its geraniums black against the lamp, to the Strand. The jars are set one by one along the quay's edge. When the last is down, the harbour holds two rows of lights, one on the granite and one in the water under it, and for a while nobody says anything. Then June says it's cold, and people start to go home, and the lights are left to burn down on the quay.
