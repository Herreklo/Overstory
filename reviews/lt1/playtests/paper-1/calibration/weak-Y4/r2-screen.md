# Round 2

Just after one the rain comes in off the water, soft and fine, more a mist that has decided to fall. The island goes grey and then goes altogether. Nobody on the Strand moves for it.

## Turn 2.1: Teddy Marsh

Teddy is at the quay's edge in a yellow oilskin two sizes too big, the hood up, the crab line still round his finger. Rain beads on the rim of his red bucket.

You say: "Is it you who looks through the corner of Tolley's window? What do you see in there?"

"I rubbed it." He says it without looking round, watching the line where it goes into the water. "There's nothing in there. Just rope."

The line twitches. He lifts it slowly, hand over hand, and a crab comes up dangling from the bacon rind, lets go just above the bucket and drops in with a clack. "Just Admiral. He always comes back."

He lowers the line again. "I catch him every morning and let him go at teatime. He doesn't mind."

## Turn 2.2: the Marigold

Wilf has rigged a tarpaulin over the trestles on two oars and a length of line, and kneels under it out of the wet, still at the same plank. You crouch at the edge of the tarpaulin and look her over.

She's clinker-built, each plank lapping the one below, larch gone the colour of honey with age and oil; the copper rivets that hold the laps have gone green. Her stern is a small wineglass shape, the name painted across it in white capitals, MARIGOLD, with a thin line of gold leaf under it. The third plank up from the keel is glossy and wet-looking under Wilf's brush, and every plank above and below it is dull and dry and waiting. Look along the laps and it's the same wood all the way, the same grain, the same age: only the varnish is different.

Wilf doesn't look up. "She'll see me out, this one."

## Turn 2.3: June Askew, the Kettle

The bell on the door rings as you come in out of the rain. Warm air, steamed glass, lemon and soup. Six tables with oilcloth on them; cakes under glass domes on the counter; the radio on a shelf, talking quietly about rain. June is wiping the table by the window.

"There you are, love. You'll be wanting this." She takes a key on a wooden fob from her apron pocket and puts it in your hand: ROOM 1. "Side door, up the stairs. It's all aired."

You say: "When did Tolley & Son shut? And who was May, on the bench?"

"Arthur Tolley? Retired to his daughter's, inland, in '88. Lovely man." She turns the cloth over and carries on wiping. "And that's May Ord. She sat there every afternoon. Lovely woman." She nods at the chair across from her. "Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house: you live here now."

She straightens and looks round the room as if counting it. "It was Mum's café, then mine. I've wiped this table every morning for thirty-one years."

## Five o'clock

By five the rain has thinned to nothing. In the west the cloud lifts off the sea like a lid, and a long bar of yellow light comes in under it, across the harbour and up the wet roofs, so that every window on the hill catches fire at once. The cobbles steam a little. Up at the very top of the town, along the low wall of the Seamen's Chapel, a row of jam jars stands in the light, glinting.

This evening: the lantern walk, from the Seamen's Chapel at dusk.
