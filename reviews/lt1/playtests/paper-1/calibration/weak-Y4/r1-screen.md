# Round 1

## Turn 1.1: Wilf Garrow

You say: "I am. That's a lovely boat."

"She is that." Wilf sits back on his heels and looks along her keel the way you'd look at a dog that's done well. "She'll see me out, this one."

He dips the brush, draws off the drip on the rim of the tin, and goes back to the plank. "Forty-one years I took the Puffin over to the island. Rob has her now. He doesn't talk to the passengers. I talked to every one."

Down at the steps, Rob coils the same rope, and doesn't look up.

*Talking is one line from the menu at a time. Wilf's menu for next time is below.*

## Turn 1.2: Tolley & Son, the rubbed corner

You crouch until your eye is level with the clear patch, low in the corner of the whitewash. The glass is cold against your forehead. Inside, in the grey light the whitewash lets through: empty shelves running back into the dark, their edges pale where tins once stood; a coil of rope still on its hook behind the counter; on the wall by the door, a calendar from a feed merchant, open on March 1988. The door beside you is locked; the brass of its handle has gone brown.

## Turn 1.3: the bench's plaque

A small brass plate screwed to the backrest, polished by sleeves:

FOR MAY, WHO LIKED IT HERE.
1921–1990

The bench faces straight out across the harbour to the island, and the slats are worn pale where people sit.

## Turn 1.4: a sketch

Nothing on the Marigold has had your close attention yet, so you sit on the cold granite at the foot of the chandler's wall and draw its window: the gold letters, the flat white of the glass, the one small clear corner, low down. The pencil takes the whitewash's brush marks well.

*Sketchbook: Tolley & Son's window. A shop with its eyes shut, except for one corner.*

## Midday

The morning goes. The cloud that was grey at eight has thickened and come down onto the island's lighthouse; the water has gone the colour of pewter. A gull walks the quay's edge as if it owns it. The tide has turned and is coming in over the weed on the steps. From the Kettle, the radio and the smell of soup.
