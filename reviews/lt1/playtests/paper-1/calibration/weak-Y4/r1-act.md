# Round 1

## Pull
6 — The place is concrete and I want to poke at it, but "no wrong answers, can't miss anything" gives me no question to chase yet. Nobody has asked me for anything.

## Thinking aloud
The opening is good on things and thin on a hook. What catches me is small and physical: the whitewashed chandler's window with one corner rubbed clear at a child's nose height, the mooring rings polished on top where ropes have worked them, a man varnishing exactly one plank on a boat called MARIGOLD, and two old women watching the same stair. Those look like clues, or at least the kind of thing a town leaves behind. Wilf and Rob are both Garrow, so that's some family, and I can't tell how yet. The store page promised a retired ferryman and his father's boat, and Wilf is the only one at a boat, so I'd guess he's the one. The noticeboard makes me want to be back at dusk for the lantern walk, so there is at least a clock in the day even if nothing fails.

I'm cautious about one thing. If every inspect just hands me a pleasant paragraph, and nothing I notice ever connects to anything else, I'll be bored by the third place. I came out of a game where noticing was the whole job. Here I want the town to let me notice something and then be right about it. I'd rather find the cardigan notice and the sisters' window and link them myself than be told.

Wilf spoke to me first, so I'll answer him rather than walk past. Option 1 is the only one that asks him about the boat.

## Prediction
Teddy is the one who rubbed the chandler's window clear. Tolley & Son looks shut or half-shut, and he is peering at something inside (a model boat, a lamp, a face). The window will turn out to show something that connects to the boat or the ferry. I expect it to be visible only from nose height, which is why it matters that I stoop to look.

## Needs
- Whether I can peek through the rubbed patch myself, or whether the game says what is inside.
- Who Rob is to Wilf, and who is "retired", without being told outright.
- What the sketchbook counts as sketchable. I don't know what earns a sketch.
- How long a turn is. Is "sit" a whole morning or ten minutes?
- Whether the tide matters. It's half out, the ferry knocks on the steps, and the board gives times.

## Turns
1. Talk to Wilf: choose line 1 ("I am. That's a lovely boat."), then ask in my own words about the plank he's varnishing and why that one. — Expect: he tells me something about Marigold and his father. The one plank is probably a repair, or the one he did last.
2. Inspect the rubbed-clear corner of the Tolley & Son window, crouching to a child's height. — Expect: I see something inside that explains why someone keeps looking, and it hints the shop is shut or empty.
3. Inspect the plaque on the bench. — Expect: a name and dates. If it's a memorial it tells me who sat here, and it might connect to someone in town.
4. If something I've inspected can be sketched, sketch the best of them (Marigold's stern if I've seen it up close, otherwise the chandler's window). If nothing can be, talk to Teddy instead and ask in my own words what he's trying to catch. — Expect: a first sketch in the book, or Teddy telling me something he shouldn't.
