# Exit interview: Saltlight

## 1. Overall pull
2. The pull rose to 4 once, when the darned cardigan looked like evidence, and it has been sinking ever since.

## 2. Best and worst
**Best:** the things in the world that carried history without anyone telling it to me. The paint at step 20 coming off "white over green over the first ochre", with a FOR SALE card in the window. The calendar stuck on March 1988. A peephole rubbed clear at a child's height. Mooring rings polished on top by ropes. Elbows darned in a different grey. I could read a past off every one of them, and I believed them. They were the only things in Wrenmouth that didn't repeat themselves. The lantern walk is well written. The reflected row of lights is a good image.

**Worst:**
- **The people are on loops.** June gave me the same two lines twice, word for word. Dot and Ivy gave me the same four lines twice, even after I pressed them directly in my own words. Their "next time" options never change. Once I saw that, nothing anyone said could count as evidence, and that killed the only game I'd found.
- **The game puts up hooks it won't let you pull.** The noticeboard advertised the cardigan as a lead, and then the game said "You can't take that with you."
- **Belonging is a progress bar, not a system.** It rose by 17, 13 and 22 whatever I did, including a round where I got nowhere. It furnishes my room with a cat and a fern. Nothing I did could make it go up faster or slower.
- **Nothing does anything.** Sketches are a tick list. The clock jumps to whatever the next scene needs, so the ferry times and the dusk deadline weren't constraints at all.
- **"You can't miss anything" also means nothing matters.** No choice I made changed the lantern walk. I predicted that last round, and it played out exactly.

## 3. Chapter Two? $14.99?
No and no. "New sketches and keepsakes to find" is the one promise I care least about. I'd refund inside two hours. I might gift it to someone who likes tea and rain, and it would be honest with them, because the store page says exactly what it is. The problem is that what it is, is something to look at, with nothing to play.

## 4. What I was trying to achieve, and what I risked
I was trying to find the system under the scenery: what moves Belonging, what sketches do, and whether time was a budget. Then I tried to solve the cardigan by reading evidence and pressing on a contradiction. I risked nothing, because there was nothing to risk. No turn was scarce, no answer could be wrong, and no relationship could go badly. That's the core failure for a player like me. A deduction with no possible wrong answer isn't a deduction. Compared with the Football Manager session: there, every read I made was confirmed, which was too obliging. Here, no read was ever confirmed or denied. The world just absorbed them. Both fail in opposite directions. What I want is evidence that can be right or wrong, and a game that tells me which, at a cost.

## 5. Marks for round 3
- Turn 3.1: would quit — my direct, specific press got the same four lines back verbatim, plus an unrelated (charming) sailor story. That's the moment the town stopped being something I could investigate. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.2: curious — the paint layers and the FOR SALE card are a history I could read at a glance and believe. But I couldn't do anything with it: no owner, no door to knock on. Surprise: delight — the best single image in the game.
- Turn 3.3: bored — a lovely set-piece in which I carried a jar. The silent man with binoculars off the last Puffin was the only new hook, and it went nowhere. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.4: bored — Rob said "Island?" and then the band drowned him out. It was the only reply with any edge, and the scene cut away from it. Surprise: none.
