# Round 2

## Chapter One: Arrivals. The afternoon

A soft rain comes in off the water a little after one and settles over the town, fine enough to hang in the air. The gulls go quiet on the chimneys.

**Turn 2.1: inspect the grey cardigan.**
You lift the cardigan's sleeve off the hook and turn it over. It's hand-knitted in a plain stitch, the wool gone soft and a little furred with wear. There's no label. Both pockets are empty. At each elbow there's a darn, a close square of woven thread in a slightly different grey from the rest. It smells of wool and, faintly, of the café.

**Turn 2.2: inspect the photograph by the till.**
A small black-and-white print in a thin wooden frame. A woman in an apron stands outside the Kettle with her hands folded in front of her, squinting into the sun, and behind her, through the glass, you can just make out the table by the window. In pencil on the mount: *Mum, 1962.*

**Turn 2.3: June, and the cardigan.**
> You say: "Could I take the cardigan up the steps, and see if anyone knows whose it is?"

June glances at the hook. "That? Been there since August. Must be a visitor's."

*You can't take that with you.*

"Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house: you live here now." She pauses with the cloth. "You'll come to the lantern walk tonight? Everyone goes. You'll love it."

You let yourself out and climb Wren Steps in the rain. The stone is dark and slick in the middle of each step and pale at the edges; the handrail is cold and wet under your hand. Behind you, Sal has moved in under the Kettle's awning with her bundle of letters.

**Turn 2.4: Dot and Ivy Hale.**
At step 40, the window full of geraniums swings open before you reach it. Dot has her needles going; Ivy has the Gazette folded on her knee.

> You say: "There's a grey cardigan down at the Kettle. Somebody found it on the steps. Did you see who dropped it?"

Dot doesn't stop knitting. "A grey cardigan? Oh, that'll be a visitor's, love. They come up for the view and get warm halfway."
Ivy nods. "They always do."

Dot: "We see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down."
Ivy: "And we wave at all of them."

Dot looks past you, down the stair. "We were born in this house. Both of us, in the back bedroom."

*Next time, Dot and Ivy:* 1. "Good morning, ladies." · 2. "What are you knitting?" · 3. "Anything in the paper?"
*Next time, June:* 1. "Is my room ready?" · 2. "What's good here?" · 3. "What a lovely place."

## Where you are

**Wren Steps, step 40, about five o'clock.** The rain thinned out an hour ago. Now the sky is opening in the west, a long bar of pale yellow under the cloud, and every wet roof below you has gone bright. At the top of the steps, along the Seamen's Chapel wall, a row of empty jam jars catches the light.

**In view**
- **Dot and Ivy Hale**, at their open window: Dot knitting a long blue scarf, Ivy with the Gazette.
- **Red geraniums**, crowded in the window box.
- **The handrail**, running up the middle of the stair.
- **Up the steps:** a marmalade cat stretched on a warm stone near step 50; above it, a house with bright new red roof tiles beside mossy old ones; at the top, **Chapel Lane** and the chapel wall with its jam jars.
- **Down the steps:** at step 20, a house with flaking paint and one boarded window; the Kettle's roof; **Sal Brennan** under the awning; the Strand and the harbour, the Puffin at the steps.
