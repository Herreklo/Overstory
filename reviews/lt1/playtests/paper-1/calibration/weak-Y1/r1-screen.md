# Round 1

## Chapter One: Arrivals. The morning

**Turn 1.1: Wilf Garrow.**
> You say: "Do you need a hand with that?"

Wilf looks at you for a moment, then pulls a rag from his jacket pocket and holds it out. "Catch that drip. There. Good." A bead of varnish was sliding down the lower edge of the plank; it soaks into the rag instead of the cobbles. He goes back to his brush, working it along the grain in long, even strokes.

"She'll see me out, this one."

He dips the brush and taps it twice on the rim of the tin. "Forty-one years I took the Puffin over to the island. Rob has her now. He doesn't talk to the passengers. I talked to every one."

Down at the steps, Rob coils the same rope.

*Next time, Wilf:* 1. "How's she coming along?" · 2. "Can I help today?" · 3. "Tell me about the Puffin."

**Turn 1.2: inspect the clear corner of Tolley & Son's window.**
You stoop to it. The whitewash has been rubbed away in a rough oval, the glass behind it smeared clean. Through it: empty shelves going back into the dark, a coil of rope still on its hook, and on the far wall a calendar turned to March 1988. You try the door. It's locked.

**Turn 1.3: sketch Tolley & Son's window.**
You sit on the quay's edge with the sketchbook on your knee and draw the fascia, the tea-coloured gold of the letters, the blank white of the glass and the one clear corner. A gull lands on the bollard beside you to watch, decides you have no bread, and leaves.
*Sketch added: Tolley & Son's window.*

**Turn 1.4: the Kettle, and June Askew.**
The bell over the door rings. Warmth, steam, the radio low on a talk programme. June looks up from the window table with her cloth in her hand. "You'll be my lodger! Here, before I forget." She takes a key on a wooden fob from a hook behind the till and puts it in your palm. "Side door, up the stairs, the blue window."

You ask about the cardigan on the noticeboard. She nods at the hook by the door, where it hangs: grey, hand-knitted, darned at both elbows in a slightly different grey. "Been there since August. Must be a visitor's."

"Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house: you live here now." The cloth goes round the table again. "It was Mum's café, then mine. I've wiped this table every morning for thirty-one years."

*Next time, June:* 1. "Is my room ready?" · 2. "What's good here?" · 3. "What a lovely place."
