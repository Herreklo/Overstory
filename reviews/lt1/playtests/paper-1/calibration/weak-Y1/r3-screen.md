# Round 3

## Chapter One: Arrivals. The evening

The last of the cloud breaks up over the headland and the light goes long and gold, then blue. Somewhere up the hill, a door shuts and someone laughs.

**Turn 3.1: Dot and Ivy Hale.**
> You say: "You see everyone who goes up and down, and Dot, you darn. Those elbows were darned by someone who cared about it. Are you sure it's a visitor's?"

Dot smiles at her needles. "Oh, it'll be a visitor's, love."
Ivy: "They always are."

Dot: "We see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down."
Ivy: "And we wave at all of them."

Ivy folds the Gazette on her knee. "I was engaged to a sailor once. He wrote every week from Montevideo."
Dot: "And then he married a girl in Valparaíso."
They both laugh, the same laugh, at the same moment.
Ivy: "Best thing that ever happened to her."

*Next time, Dot and Ivy:* 1. "Good morning, ladies." · 2. "What are you knitting?" · 3. "Anything in the paper?"

**Turn 3.2: the house at step 20.**
You go down twenty steps. The house's paint is coming away in layers: white over green over the first ochre, each one curling back at the edges to show the one beneath. One ground-floor window is boarded. In the other, a card is propped against the glass: FOR SALE.

**Turn 3.3: up to Chapel Lane, for the lantern walk.**
By the time you reach the top, the jam jars on the chapel wall have candles in them and people are coming up the lane and the steps in ones and twos.

## The lantern walk

They come as the light goes. June, with her apron off for the first time today. Wilf, with varnish still on his knuckles. Teddy, the red bucket empty: Admiral was let go at teatime. Sal, out of uniform, in a big blue jumper. Dot and Ivy, arm in arm, a step at a time, a jar in Ivy's free hand. Rob Garrow. A man with binoculars round his neck, off the last Puffin, who nods to everyone and says nothing. Skipper, the old black dog, on a lead at somebody's heel and awake for once. And the brass band: a cornet, two horns and a euphonium, four players and no tuba.

Somebody presses a spare jar into your hands, a wire handle and a stub of candle, already lit.

**Turn 3.4: Rob Garrow.**
> You say: "Your dad says he talked to every passenger."

Rob looks at you, then nods. "Island?"

The band strikes up, and the lights start down Wren Steps: a long, slow line of them, bobbing, every jar swinging on its wire, the band playing at the front and missing the low notes. Past the new red roof at step 60. Past the cat at step 50, who watches the lights go by and doesn't move. Past step 40, where the geraniums are dark in the window box. Past the flaking house at step 20, where a jar's light goes over the layers of paint, white and green and ochre. Down to the Strand.

On the quay, everyone sets their jar down along the edge of the granite, one beside the next, until there's a row of small flames the length of the harbour. The tide has come in under them, still and black, and it holds a second row of lights, upside down, moving a little. Nobody makes a speech. The band plays one more tune. Wilf stands by the Marigold with his hands in his pockets. June says, to nobody in particular, "Every year."

You set your jar down in the gap someone left for it.
