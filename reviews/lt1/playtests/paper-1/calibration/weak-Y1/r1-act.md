# Round 1

## Pull
3 — beautifully written, but "nobody is expecting anything of you" and "no fail states, no wrong answers" are the exact opposite of why I play games. I'm here for the numbers it does have.

## Thinking aloud
Honestly, I'd never have bought this. If a friend had gifted it to me I'd be watching the Steam two-hour refund clock. The writing is good: mooring rings "polished on top where ropes have worked them" is the kind of detail I notice. But a lovely description isn't something to do. So I'm doing what I always do and looking for the system under it. I see three numbers: sketches out of 48, keepsakes out of 20, and Belonging as a percentage. And there's a hidden fourth that might matter more: the clock. It's 8:10, the ferry goes at 10:30, and the lantern walk is at dusk. If turns cost time, then the day is a budget, and "you can't miss anything" may not be quite true. The only things that look like problems to solve are the grey cardigan found on the steps and the whitewashed chandler's, with a peephole rubbed at a child's height. Those are where I'm going. I'm using my turns to learn what moves Belonging, not to admire the view.

## Prediction
Teddy Marsh rubbed that corner of the chandler's window clear. His "very big plans" involve the closed shop or something in it, and Tolley & Son has a missing son. The cardigan belongs to one of the Hale sisters, since it was found on their stair, and returning it is the key that gets them talking.

## Needs
- What raises Belonging, and by how much? Is it talking, sketching, keepsakes, time spent?
- Does each turn cost clock time? How much does a walk or a talk cost against the 10:30 ferry and dusk?
- What do sketches and keepsakes actually do? Do they unlock anything, or are they a tick list?
- How long is the game: how many chapters, and what ends a chapter?
- Is there any goal for the autumn at all, even a soft one?
