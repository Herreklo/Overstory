## 2. Compared with the real game
What it kept: the rule check, the bulletin's new rule as the trap of the day (Ostrova and her work pass), the detention bounty, the bribe, the guard who wants a favour, the journalist, the family ledger, and the citation printer as the voice of judgement. The prose is terse and physical, and it suits the game.

What it lost:
- **The squeeze on the eyes.** The real game is seconds per entrant, dragging papers around under a clock you feel. Here every discrepancy is typed out flat, so B31XW against B31XM or Lesrenadi under Obristan jumps off the page. The finding is trivial; only the deciding is left.
- **The money squeeze.** In the real game a good day early on still leaves you choosing between heat and medicine. Here a perfect day pays everything. The bills line was a formality, and I never had to choose who goes cold.
- **The clock.** It's accounting, and it's soft. Once the sixth entrant is lost, questions and detentions fit in the slack.
- **The question verb.** I used it three times and it never changed an outcome: no hidden paper, no lie that cracked. Detain was available without it, so asking was 15 minutes of flavour. In the real game, interrogating is what unlocks the next step.
- **Accumulation.** Papers, Please hurts across days. Your son dies on day 12 because of something you did on day 9. One day can only set threads up. Danik, Moravec's column and Volkov were all set-ups with no payoff.

What it gained: a game master who resolves off-menu acts honestly, like the bulletin. The real game can't do that.

## 3. Best and worst
**Best:** Danik's offer and the journalist's question about per-arrest pay arriving together. That was the one moment the stamp had a cost that wasn't arithmetic. Also Dubova walking "against the queue", the silent printer as a tell, and the bulletin resolution.

**Worst:** nothing bit.
- The two free warnings plus the savings meant the most profitable play (take Soler's 15, approve him) was also the kindest, and it cost nothing. When greed and mercy point the same way there's no dilemma, only a bonus.
- The colder play, taking his money and denying him anyway, was open and presumably free. That's a hole in the economy.
- The bribes came after I no longer needed money.
- Nothing I did showed a consequence: Danik walked past, and the column "names no one".
- The line between mismatch (deny) and forgery (detain) is never drawn. I guessed twice, it paid twice, and that felt like luck, not skill.
- I asked three times what each unpaid bill does to each person and never got an answer. I'd have been choosing blind if it had ever mattered.

**One line:** a faithful, well-written skeleton of Papers, Please with its two engines removed: the clock in your hands and the debt that grows across days. What's left is a polite puzzle where I could always afford to be good.
