# Round 3

## Turn 3.1: The grey cardigan

It hangs on the hook by the Kettle's door, under somebody's umbrella. Grey wool, hand-knitted in a plain rib, the cuffs stretched from being pushed up. Both elbows have been darned, carefully, in a grey just slightly different from the rest. No label, no name tape. It smells of the café: toast and steam.

You lift it off the hook. *You can't take that with you.* You hang it back.

June, wiping the window table: "Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house."

## Turn 3.2: Sal Brennan

She's still on the low wall at the foot of the steps, the bundle of letters on her knee, no smaller than this morning. The red bicycle has rain on its saddle.

You ask about the cardigan.
"The grey one on June's hook? That's been there since August. Some visitor's, I'd say."

"Up and up, and then you're there. That's Wrenmouth for you."

She squares the bundle against her knee. "Eighty-seven steps, twice a day, eleven years. Calves like a footballer."

## Turn 3.3: Dot and Ivy Hale

Forty steps up, the window opens before you reach it. Dot has a long blue scarf across her lap; Ivy has the *Wrenmouth Gazette* folded to the inside page.

You say: "Good morning, ladies."
"Good morning yourself!"

Dot: "We see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down." Ivy: "And we wave at all of them."

Ivy lowers the paper. "We were born in this house. Both of us, in the back bedroom."

## Turn 3.4: Up to the Seamen's Chapel

The last forty-seven steps, the handrail polished bright under your hand in the middle and rough at the top. A marmalade cat on a warm step lifts its head as you pass. At the top, Chapel Lane: a small white chapel with a bell, a garden plot of leeks and sweet peas with a scarecrow in an old oilskin, and the whole harbour below, going blue. Along the chapel wall, the row of jam jars, a candle stub in each.

## The lantern walk

They come up the lane as the light goes. June, with her apron off. Wilf, varnish still on his knuckles. Teddy, without his bucket: Admiral went back at teatime. Sal out of uniform, in a green anorak. Dot and Ivy arm in arm, a step at a time, Ivy counting them under her breath. Rob Garrow, who nods to you. A man with binoculars round his neck, off the last Puffin: **Cyril Bowe**. Skipper on a lead, awake for once. And the Wrenmouth Brass Band, four of them, no tuba.

The jars are lit one from another. You have none, and June presses a spare into your hands, warm already: "There you are, love."

The band plays something slow. The walk goes down Wren Steps, the lights bobbing between the houses, past the Hales' dark window, past step 50, down to the Strand. One by one the jars are set along the quay's edge, and the harbour holds two rows of lights, one on the stone and one on the water, moving a little when the Puffin rocks.

Nobody makes a speech. People stand about with their hands in their pockets. Somebody's radio, from an open window, gives the shipping forecast. When you go up to your room at last, the geranium and the cat are where you left them, and the town's lights are still along the quay.
