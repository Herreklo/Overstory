# Round 1

## Pull
4 — The writing is pleasant and I'll give it a few rounds, but the store page tells me up front that nothing I do can go wrong or cost me anything, and that is exactly what I come to games for.

## Thinking aloud
I like the first paragraph: the worn rings, the whitewashed window rubbed clear at a child's nose height. That tells me someone is looking at something, and I'd like to know what. But the page's list worries me: "No timers, no fail states, no wrong answers," "you can't miss anything." As someone who plays a game for the thing to master, that reads as "no game." What am I optimising? The only numbers I can see are Belonging (0%), sketches (0 of 48) and keepsakes (0 of 20). Those I will chase, because I always do, but a progress bar is not a challenge. If nothing is scarce I have no decisions, just a tour.

There is one tension that might be a real system: the ferry runs at 10.30, 13.00 and 15.30 and the Lantern Walk is tonight. A clock, and a "tonight" event. But the page says I can't miss anything, so I suspect those are decoration. I'll test it cheaply and see if the game tells the truth.

The first action is Wilf asking me a question. I'll offer him a hand rather than say a polite line; in any game, the option that offers a task is the one that may lead to a system. The noticeboard is the densest information on screen (a walk, a band that needs a tuba, a lost cardigan): three little hooks I'd love to see turn into a job. The chandler's window is the one mystery on the screen.

## Prediction
Teddy Marsh is the one who rubbed that corner of Tolley & Son's window clear, and what he's watching inside is part of his "very big plans." When I inspect the window, I'll find something he can see from that height and I can't, probably something to do with the shop being shut for a reason the town doesn't talk about.

## Needs
- What is the goal, or is there none? What makes me stop?
- How does Belonging grow: talking, inspecting, time spent, or choices I make?
- Do my dialogue choices change anything, or is "no wrong answers" a way of saying they don't?
- Does the clock move when I act? What happens if I ignore the ferry or the Lantern Walk?
- What can be sketched, and how will I know before I try?
- Any limit on my time or carrying capacity, anything scarce at all?

## Turns

2. Inspect the noticeboard — Expect: the three notices become entries in my journal, and some marker of the Lantern Walk and the tuba.
3. Inspect the cleared corner of Tolley & Son's window — Expect: something I can sketch, and a clue about the shop and Teddy.
4. If Wilf gave me a job, do it; otherwise talk to Teddy Marsh and ask what the plans are — Expect: a chatty boy with a big plan I can help with, the first sign of an objective.
