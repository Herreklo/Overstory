# Exit interview: X1 (Football Manager devotee), Saltlight, Chapter One

## 1. Marks, round 3
- Turn 3.1 (the grey cardigan): bored — the darned elbows are a lovely detail, then "You can't take that with you." The one loose objective I'd found hit a wall. Surprise: cheat — the noticeboard postcard said "ask at the Kettle" and the Kettle had nothing. I was offered a hook and then refused it.
- Turn 3.2 (Sal Brennan): bored — the same answer as June ("some visitor's"). Two dead ends in a row. "Eighty-seven steps, twice a day, eleven years" is a good line, but I didn't need it. Surprise: none
- Turn 3.3 (Dot and Ivy): curious — "We were born in this house. Both of us, in the back bedroom." Then the conversation ended. I wanted to ask about the cardigan (they knit, and Dot had a scarf on her lap) and the menu had no way to. No tea, no story. Surprise: none
- Turn 3.4 (the chapel and the Lantern Walk): engaged — the best scene in the game: June handing me a warm spare jar, Ivy counting steps, Teddy "without his bucket: Admiral went back at teatime", the band with no tuba, two rows of lights. Every ritual I met got a payoff. Surprise: delight — but I made no choice in it. It played to me.

## 2. Overall pull
5. Pleasant and well made, and I'd be sorry to lose the Lantern Walk, but I never once decided anything that mattered.

## 3. Best and worst
Best: the Lantern Walk, and the way small planted details came back: June's wiped table, Admiral, the jars on the Chapel wall that I'd seen lit up from the bench. The world also runs without me (rain, the Puffin, the jars), and the room rewards (geranium, cat, fern) are things I can see.

Worst, in order:
1. No stakes and no agency. There is nothing to win, lose or choose between. The chapter ended after three rounds as a fixed timeline: I turned up at the walk because I was told to.
2. Hooks that go nowhere. The cardigan was offered three times (postcard, June, Sal) and led nowhere.
3. The menus. Teddy's plans and the Hales' cardigan were not on the menu, and my own words were replaced by the nearest line.
4. Belonging is a black box: +13, +17, +26 with no breakdown, and Chapter One ended on 56% whatever I did.
5. The Wilf loop: "Can I help today?" gave the same drip as before.

## 4. Would I keep playing, would I buy
Chapter Two: yes, once, for the High Road, the Net Loft and to find out what a keepsake is. I have 0 of 20 and one sketch after three rounds, and the collector in me wants to know the rule. Buy at $14.99: not on what I've seen. I'd wishlist it and wait for a sale, or for someone to tell me something in the later chapters can be won or lost. For a player who wants a cosy evening it's probably fine. I'm not that player.

## 5. What I was trying to achieve, and what I risked
I was looking for the system: (1) what moves Belonging, (2) who owns the cardigan, (3) whether the clock and the Lantern Walk can be missed, (4) what Teddy's plans are. I got nothing on (1), (2) or (4). I never tested (3); I attended, so "you can't miss anything" is still a claim I haven't checked.

I risked nothing, because the game told me at the start there was nothing to risk, and it was right. The only cost was time, which I paid in turns I'd call neutral. That is my honest verdict: I enjoyed the walk, but I had nothing at stake while I watched it.
