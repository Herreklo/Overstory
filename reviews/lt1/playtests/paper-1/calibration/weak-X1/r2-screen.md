## Turn 2.1: Sketch, Tolley & Son

You sit on the quay's edge across from the chandler's and draw the window: the gold letters gone to tea, the whitewash, the one clear corner low down, and through it the coil of rope on its hook. It takes longer than you meant it to. The gulls lose interest in you.

## Turn 2.2: June Askew

The Kettle's bell goes over the door. Inside: six tables, cakes under glass domes, the radio murmuring, the windows running with steam. By the till, a photograph of a woman squinting into the sun outside this café, beside this same window table, 1962 written on the mount. A grey cardigan hangs on a hook by the door.

June looks up from the table she's wiping. "You'll be my lodger. Here you are, love." A key on a wooden fob with a 1 burnt into it. "Side door, up the stairs, the blue window. It's all aired."

You ask about the cardigan.
"Been there since August. Must be a visitor's."

You ask about Tolley & Son.
"Arthur Tolley? Retired to his daughter's, inland, in '88. Lovely man."

"Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house: you live here now."

She goes back to the table by the window. "It was Mum's café, then mine. I've wiped this table every morning for thirty-one years."

2. "What's good here?"
3. "What a lovely place."

## Turn 2.3: Teddy Marsh

The rain has started, soft and fine, more mist than rain. Teddy has put on a yellow oilskin two sizes too big and turned the hood up, and goes on fishing.

You say: "Caught anything?"

He tips the red bucket to show you. One crab, the size of a saucer, backed into a corner with its claws up.

"Just Admiral. He always comes back."

He lowers the line again, bacon rind turning on the string. "I catch him every morning and let him go at teatime. He doesn't mind."

**Teddy's menu, next time**
1. "Caught anything?"
2. "What's the bait?"
3. "Can I have a go?"

## Turn 2.4: The bench

The brass plaque: FOR MAY, WHO LIKED IT HERE. 1921–1990. The wood is dark with rain, and you sit on it anyway.

The Puffin goes out, Rob at the tiller and two figures in cagoules in the bow, her wake spreading across the harbour until it touches the quay below your feet. Under the tarpaulin he has rigged over the trestles, Wilf's brush goes along the plank and back. Two gulls argue over a crust on the cobbles until a third takes it. The light moves on the water, a paler patch sliding out towards the island and gone.

## The Strand, about five

The rain eased off a little after four. Now the sky is opening in the west, a long bright seam under the cloud, and the wet roofs going up the hill have turned gold and ox-blood and white again. Sal Brennan is back on her wall from under the café's awning. At the top of the town, along the wall of the Seamen's Chapel, a row of jam jars catches the light.
