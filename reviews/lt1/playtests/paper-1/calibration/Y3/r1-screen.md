# Round 1

**Turn 1. Water.** You start with the cauliflowers, as planned. The big pale heads sit tight in their leaves, nearly there, and the soil goes from tan to dark brown under the spout one tile at a time. The can runs dry somewhere in the third row; you walk back to the pond by the house, dunk it, and a frog plops off the bank. Then the beans, their thin tendrils just reaching the second wire of the trellis, and the kale, crinkled and blue-green, beads of water sitting on the leaves. A crow lands on the fence, thinks better of it, and goes. The last tile darkens at 7:20. Energy 180/270 (a little less than you feared: at Farming 3 each pour costs under 2).

**Turn 2. Harvest and ship.** Half an hour among the rows. The parsnips pull up pale and fat with soil on their shoulders; the potatoes come up in knobbly clumps of four and five. No stars on any of them this time: all normal quality. You keep the best-looking parsnip and potato in your pack for the Community Center and drop the rest into the shipping bin by the house. The lid closes with a wooden thunk: 14 parsnips, 19 potatoes. The bin pays overnight. 7:50.

**Turn 3. Pam's birthday.** The road south runs along the fence, and the salmonberry bushes are heavy with the last fruit of the season. Twenty minutes of picking and your fingers are stained red: 12 salmonberries. You cross the square, quiet this early (Pierre's shutters are still down), and follow the path down to the river, where Pam's trailer sits on its blocks with a folding chair outside and a television going behind the curtain. 8:35.

Pam opens the door with a mug in her hand and squints at you against the light. You hold out the leek.

"Well. Huh." She takes it and turns it over. "You knew it was my birthday? Nobody knows it's my birthday." She looks at the leek a moment longer than she needs to. "It'll go in a soup. Thanks, kid. Don't go telling everyone, now."

A heart rises over her head. **Pam: 2 hearts to 3.**

**Your prediction.** Pam was home, she was pleased, and she went up a heart: right. The cauliflowers come due tomorrow.
