# Round 3

**Turn 1. Clearing.** Twenty minutes down from the mountain. You load the furnace first, five ore and a lump of coal, and it starts to glow and tick by the porch.

Then the patch beside the house, where the coop will go. Weeds first, with the scythe: a few sweeps each, and they fall in green heaps (18 fibre, and two packets of mixed seeds caught in the stalks). The stones come up one swing at a time with a dull clack. The twigs snap underfoot. There are four trees on the patch itself, an oak and three maples, and each one takes ten swings before it tips with a long creak and hits the grass, then five more for the stump. When the patch is bare you carry on into the edge of the uncleared land, six more trees, while the furnace pings a bar out every half hour and you feed it again. Acorns and maple seeds roll out of the felled crowns. Whenever your arms go heavy you eat a salmonberry, sour-sweet and warm from your pocket.

At 3:20 pm you straighten up and count: **312 wood, 109 stone.** That's everything Robin asked for, with some to spare. Behind you there's a clean rectangle of earth next to the house, longer than it is wide, with the stumps gone and the ground raked flat by your boots. The furnace has cooled: **5 copper bars.** Energy 50/270, two salmonberries left.

**Turn 2. The Saloon.** The road to town goes past the bus stop, and Pam is on the bench there in the sun, arms folded, glaring at the bus. It has a flat tyre and moss in the wiper blades.

"Twelve years I drove that thing. Now look at it. Nobody in this town wants to fix anything." She notices you. "Hey, leek kid."

You reach the Saloon at 3:40, early. The room is nearly empty: the jukebox dark, sun on the pool table. Gus is polishing glasses.

"You're early! Sit down, sit down." When you ask about Shane, he leans on the bar. "Shane? Comes in every night after his shift at Joja. Beer. Always beer. And he'll never say no to a slice of my pizza, though he'll pretend he doesn't want it." He sets a glass of water in front of you without asking. "His birthday, is it? Hm. Good of you."

You stay into the evening. Emily comes on at four, humming, and tells Gus about a dream in which she sewed a coat out of moths. Pam pushes through the door at half past and takes her stool at the bar; she lifts her glass an inch at you and says nothing more, which you suspect is friendly. Shane comes in a little after six in his blue Joja hoodie and drops into the corner by the arcade machine with a beer. When you say hello, he looks at you for a long second.

"Do I know you? ...Look. I just want to drink this in peace."

Willy drifts in around seven, smelling of the sea, and plays a game of darts with nobody. Clint sits alone at a table near the bar and watches Emily over his mug, and every time she looks his way he studies the table. At 10:15 you say goodnight to Gus.

**Turn 3. Bed.** The walk home is dark and smells of rain coming. Bed at 10:35.

**Your predictions.** Wood still short tonight, around 230 to 250: wrong; you had 312 by mid-afternoon. Shane gruff, Gus telling you what he likes: right. The cauliflowers ready in the rain: see below.

## Friday, Spring 19, Year 1. 6:00 am.

**Rain.** It drums on the roof and runs in sheets down the window. **4,055g. Energy 270/270.**

Out on the farm, every tile is dark and shining; nothing needs watering today. **The 24 cauliflowers are ready**, big creamy heads sitting up in their leaves with the rain running off them. The beans have put out their first flowers; the kale has thickened. Thirty rows of parsnip seed show a green prick of a leaf each. The cleared patch by the house is a sheet of mud with rain pooling in the bootprints. The furnace sits cold under the eaves.
