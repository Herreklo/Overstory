# Exit interview

## 1. Marks for round 3

- Turn 3.1: engaged. Clearing went better than I predicted, and the writing made a chore feel like an afternoon: trees tipping with a long creak, acorns rolling out of the crowns, a salmonberry "warm from your pocket", the furnace pinging every half hour. Surprise: delight, mixed with a small doubt. I'd guessed 230 to 250 wood and got 312 by 3:20. The sums hold up when I check them (ten trees, the ore and coal add up, the berries cover the energy), so it isn't a cheat. It still went my way again, though, and the game master decided how many trees were on the patch, not me.
- Turn 3.2: engaged. "Hey, leek kid" is the best thing in the session. She remembered, and it changed how she talks to me. Pam glaring at the bus she drove for twelve years, Gus setting water down without asking, Emily's moth coat, Clint studying the table every time Emily looks over: a whole room of small lives. Shane's "Do I know you?" was right for 0 hearts. Surprise: delight, for "leek kid". One gripe: I sat in the Saloon for six and a half hours and it went by in one paragraph. I saw Clint mooning over Emily and couldn't do anything about it, because my turn was already written. I was watching through a window.
- Turn 3.3: engaged. Short and right: the dark walk home that smells of rain. Then the morning paid off. The bin came to exactly the 2,010 I had worked out, which felt like a promise kept. I woke at 4,055g in the rain with the cauliflowers ready and nothing to water, and a new letter from Jodi offering 350g for one cauliflower. Surprise: none. I'd expected all of it except the letter, and the letter is the first real choice in a while (one cauliflower for Jodi or for the bundle? I have 24, so both).

## 2. Overall pull

**8.** I'd come back tomorrow. The session stopped at the worst moment for me: I have the gold, the wood and the stone, it's raining, and I was about to walk up to Robin's, order the coop and choose where it goes. All of round 3 was building to that, and I didn't get to do it. That's a good hook, but it's also a little unkind.

## 3. Compared with the real game

Better than I feared, and better at people than the real game. In Stardew a villager gives you one line a day, picked from a list. Here they answer what I actually did. Pam remembering the leek, Evelyn's "George says I fuss", Gus handing me Shane's tastes with a bit of character: that's more warmth than the real game manages, and warmth is why I play.

What's lost is the hands. In the real game the walk up and down the rows, choosing which tree to chop and hearing the sound of each swing is half the calm. Here a turn is "clear land until 6 pm", and the story fills in the rest. Generous prose kept the feeling, but I made almost no small choices. It's also a lot of reading and sums: the first screen was a wall of numbers, and I worked out the bin's pay myself. Energy and time are stated rather than felt, so I never got that little thrill of "can I squeeze in one more tree before I'm wiped out?"

And nothing went wrong. In the real game small accidents happen: a crow takes a crop, an artifact spot turns up, it rains on a day you bought the wrong seed. Here every prediction I made came true except one, and that one was wrong in my favour. A cozy player wants low punishment, not none. I like the ease, but by round 3 I started to feel the world was agreeing with me instead of living alongside me.

## 4. Best and worst

**Best:** Pam. The birthday ("Nobody knows it's my birthday", and the way she looks at the leek a moment too long) and then "leek kid" that afternoon. A small kind act came back to me as a changed relationship. Close second: "the field is full again for the first time since you came." That one line is the place growing.

**Worst:** the evening in the Saloon was something I watched, not something I played. I planned my turns before I could see who was there, so the most alive room in the game was one I couldn't act in. Next worst: everything going exactly as I said it would. Then the opening wall of numbers, and the Flower Dance letter, which told me on day one that I'd already missed something I can't fix. Small confusion: I couldn't tell whether chatting with Pam and Gus that evening counted towards friendship; the hearts didn't move and the screen didn't say.
