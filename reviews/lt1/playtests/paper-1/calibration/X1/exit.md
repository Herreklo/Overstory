## 2. Overall pull
8 — I'd play the next round. Ainsley's open promise is a hook, and I want to see what he asks for against a £300 raise limit.

## 3. Compared with the real game
What it keeps: the fuzzy scout (knowledge 45% to 70%, ranges that narrow), the assistant's report, the injury-risk gamble on a key player, per-player opposition instructions, the morale and promises layer, and the planned substitution. The knowledge percentage is exactly the thing FM does well, and the half-time numbers (Pardoe 74%, Fenwick 72%) let me time the changes.

What it loses: the depth. I choose from three key attributes per player, not the whole sheet. There is no transfer market, finance, fixtures run or youth, and nothing I decide this week is a week-long consequence: the long-save feel is missing. In the real game I'd also make changes at any minute and watch the match unfold, not read nine highlights. I also lose training and the press conference; the assistant did both for me, which is realistic (FM lets you delegate) but meant two of my choices never got made.

What feels different: cause and effect is clearer than in FM, and so is the story. The goal went from a player I'd subbed, from an assist by a player I'd just fixed morale for. Real FM is messier and I'd trust it more.

## 4. Best and worst
Best: the chain from scouting to plan to match. Pete Dowd's report gave me Platt's knock and a flapping keeper; I built a plan around those; I could read the results in the numbers (Voss: 0 shots, Garvey pushed back). That is the reward of a Football Manager save.

Also good: the game let me take my own decision on Reid and carry it, with no prompt telling me what to do.

Worst:
1. "Risk: High" with no number. I can't price 86% against four to six weeks.
2. The match highlights are thin. Few events, no per-player stats until the end, and no way to see why 2 of 7 crosses failed.
3. My crossing plan did not work (11 crosses, 3 completed; Brandt 2 of 9). We won on a through ball over the top. The game never said my plan was wrong; I had to read it out of the table, and I want a prompt on that.
4. It was a bit too tidy. A late goal from the sub I brought on, assisted by the man I'd just spoken to, plus the board "pleased", plus the Echo headline. It felt authored. I enjoyed it, and I don't fully trust it.
5. A one-chance game decided by a 19-year-old with Composure 7: xG 0.55 to 0.37 means I was lucky. I'd not take the credit.

Would I quit? No. I'd want the next fixture, Ainsley's ask, and a view of the whole season.
