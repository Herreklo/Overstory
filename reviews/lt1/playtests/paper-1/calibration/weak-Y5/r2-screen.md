# Round 2

A little after one the rain comes in off the water, soft and fine, more a wetness in the air than drops. The Strand goes dark and shining. Nobody moves for it: Wilf has rigged a tarpaulin over the trestles and works on underneath it, Teddy has a yellow oilskin on over his jumper, and Sal has moved along the wall under the café's awning, her letters on her knee.

**Turn 2.1. Wilf Garrow.**
You say: "Who ran Tolley's, and why is its calendar stuck on March 1988?"
Wilf glances across at the whitewashed windows. "Arthur Tolley? Retired to his daughter's, inland, in '88. Lovely man."
The rain ticks on the tarpaulin. "Same as yesterday. Same as tomorrow, if the weather holds."
He lays the brush along the plank. "My father built her the winter I was born. Larch on oak, copper all through. You won't find a nail in her."

**Turn 2.2. June Askew, the Kettle.**
The bell over the door, then the warm: toast, wet coats, the radio low. Six tables, cakes under glass domes, and by the till a photograph of a woman squinting into the sun outside this same window in 1962. June looks up from the table by the glass and puts a key in your hand before you've said anything. "There you are, love. Side door, up the stairs, the blue window."
You ask about the cardigan from the noticeboard. She points with her cloth at a hook by the door, where it hangs: grey, hand-knitted, darned at both elbows in a slightly different grey. "Been there since August. Must be a visitor's."
"Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house: you live here now."
She goes back to the table. "It was Mum's café, then mine. I've wiped this table every morning for thirty-one years."

**Turn 2.3. Teddy Marsh.**
(No cardigan to carry: Teddy, then.)
You say: "Can I have a go?"
He hands you the line without a word, the wet string cold round your finger. You wait. A tug, then a slow weight, and you pull hand over hand until a crab comes up out of the green, hanging on to the bacon rind; over the bucket it lets go on its own and drops in with a clack.
"Just Admiral. He always comes back."
He takes the line back and drops it in again. Rain runs off the hood of his oilskin. "I'm going to be a diver. Or a marine biologist. Or a ferryman, like Wilf was."

**Turn 2.4. Rob Garrow, the Puffin.**
You say: "Is there a boat to the island at one?"
Rob looks up from the rope and nods. "Island?"
He waits, the rope in his hands. The Puffin knocks against the steps.

**Your prediction** (Wilf would say Tolley's shut when old Tolley died or retired and no one took it on; said kindly, then back to the brush; a sad fact with no handle): Arthur Tolley retired to his daughter's in '88. Wilf went back to the brush.

**About five. The Strand.** The rain has stopped. In the west the cloud has torn open over the headland and a low yellow light is coming in under it, laying the masts' shadows across the wet cobbles. Everything drips. High above the town, along the white wall of the Seamen's Chapel, a row of jam jars catches the light.

This evening: the lantern walk, from the Seamen's Chapel at dusk.
