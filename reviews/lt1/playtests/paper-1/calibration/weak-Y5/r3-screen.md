# Round 3

The evening comes in clear and still. The wet cobbles hold the last of the yellow light, and the gulls have gone quiet on the chimneys.

**Turn 3.1. June Askew, the Kettle.**
You say: "Could I take the cardigan round and ask whose it is?"
June glances at the hook. "That old thing? Been there since August. Must be a visitor's."
*You can't take that with you.*
"Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house: you live here now." She folds her cloth over the back of a chair. "You'll come to the lantern walk tonight? Everyone goes. You'll love it."

**Turn 3.2. Sal Brennan, the foot of Wren Steps.**
You say: "The grey cardigan at the Kettle. Any idea whose it is?"
Sal doesn't look up from the bundle. "The one on June's hook? Been there since August. A visitor's, I'd think."
She squares the letters against her knee. "Up and up, and then you're there. That's Wrenmouth for you." She nods at the stair. "Eighty-seven steps, twice a day, eleven years. Calves like a footballer."

**Turn 3.3. Dot and Ivy Hale, step 40.**
Up Wren Steps, the handrail cold and polished bright under your hand. At step 20 a house with its paint flaking in layers and a FOR SALE card in the window; at step 40 the geranium window opens before you reach it.
You say: "Did either of you see who left a grey cardigan on these steps in August?"
Dot, needles going: "The grey one? That's at June's."
Ivy, over the top of the Gazette: "Since August. A visitor's, we thought."
Dot: "We see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down."
Ivy: "And we wave at all of them."
They do; they wave at you. Then Dot, holding up the blue scarf to measure it against nothing: "We were born in this house. Both of us, in the back bedroom."

**Turn 3.4. Rob Garrow, the Puffin.**
Down again, eighty-seven steps. You say: "If I go over now, when do I get back?"
Rob nods. "Island?"
He waits, the rope in his hands.

**Your prediction** (Dot and Ivy would know whose the cardigan is at once and disagree about it; one would name someone, the other say it was too small; you'd be sent to Sal): they agreed. A visitor's, since August. Nobody was named.

**Dusk. The lantern walk.**

The bell on the Seamen's Chapel goes, a small flat note, and people start up the lanes towards it. By the time you reach the top the chapel wall is crowded. June is there with her apron off and you without a jar, so she presses a spare into your hands, a jam jar with a wire handle and a candle stub. Wilf has varnish on his knuckles. Teddy has an empty bucket; Admiral went back at teatime. Sal is out of uniform, in a green coat. Dot and Ivy come arm in arm, a step at a time. Rob stands at the back. A man with binoculars round his neck, off the last Puffin, lights his candle from Teddy's. Skipper is on a lead. The brass band is four players and no tuba.

Somebody lights the first jar from a taper and the flame goes along the wall from hand to hand, until yours is lit too and the glass warms in your fingers. Then the band starts, a slow hymn you nearly know, and the walk goes down Wren Steps, the lights bobbing between the houses, past the flaking house at step 20, past the geraniums, down and down to the Strand.

On the quay everyone sets their jar along the granite edge, one beside the next, and stands back. The tide is in and flat. The harbour holds two rows of lights: the jars on the quay and the jars in the water.

Nobody makes a speech. The band plays one more, and people stand about with their hands in their pockets until the candles burn low, and then they go home up the steps by ones and twos, saying goodnight to each other by name. Your jar is still burning at the end of the row when you go up to bed. In your room the wireless is on, low, reading the shipping forecast.
