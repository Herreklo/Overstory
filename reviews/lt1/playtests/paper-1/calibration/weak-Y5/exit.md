# Exit interview: Saltlight (Y5)

## Marks, round 3
- Turn 3.1: bored — "You can't take that with you." The cardigan was listed as a thing in view, pinned to a noticeboard postcard that begged for an owner, and it turns out to be wallpaper. June also repeated her own lines word for word: the hook, "since August", "must be a visitor's". Surprise: cheat — the game dangled the one task I could set myself, then took it back without a reason.
- Turn 3.2: bored — Sal gave me June's sentence, almost exactly: "Been there since August. A visitor's, I'd think." Nobody in this town has an opinion of their own about the cardigan. "Calves like a footballer" is a nice line and I'd have liked it in another game. Surprise: none
- Turn 3.3: bored — I predicted a quarrel and got a chorus. Two old women who "see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down" see nothing, because there is nothing to see. The answer wasn't wrong, it was empty. Surprise: delight — Dot "holding up the blue scarf to measure it against nothing" is a true detail, the best single line of the round.
- Turn 3.4: would quit — I asked Rob for a price and got "Island?" again, the same two beats as last time. Twice I've asked this boat a direct question about time, twice it ducked, and the afternoon got skipped around it. This is where I stop believing the timetable is real. Surprise: cheat — a ferry board is a promise, and it was never kept or broken.

The lantern walk itself (not a turn, it just happened): well written. Two rows of lights in the water, the band with no tuba, the jar still burning when I went up. It is the best passage in the game and I contributed nothing to it. It would have run the same if I had stayed in my room and spent the evening on the geranium.

## Answers

**1. Overall pull, 0 to 10.** 4. It is for the writing and the little rituals, not for anything I would do next.

**2. Best and worst.**
Best: hauling the crab line. It was the one time doing something, instead of choosing a numbered sentence, got a disclosure the menu never offered (Teddy saying "or a ferryman, like Wilf was"). The crack between Wilf and Rob ("He doesn't talk to the passengers. I talked to every one") is the only piece of the town with a tension in it. Close second: the darns at both elbows in a slightly different grey. That looked planted for a reader, and then nobody read it.
Worst: the cardigan chain. The game set up a small mystery with an owner, a postcard, a location and two witnesses who "see everyone", then answered it with the same sentence three times and a flat refusal. A dead end I could have borne. A dead end presented as a thread is a cheat, because it teaches me the clues aren't clues. Next worst: Belonging. It went +16, +17, +27 for rounds where I did different things, so it pays for the clock, not for me. And the menus steer. I wanted to ask Teddy about his plans and his menu had no such line.

**3. Would I keep playing into Chapter Two? Would I buy it at $14.99?**
Chapter Two: probably not, and a sceptic should say so plainly. The only reason I'd open it is to see whether the High Road or the Net Loft hold something that cannot be had by turning up. Nothing in Chapter One suggests they do. Chapter One has already shown me the shape: arrive, look, get a warm answer, get a bar tick, get a room item. I can see the next ten hours.
Buy at $14.99: no. At about $5 in a sale, as something to leave open on a quiet evening, yes, and I would not feel cheated by that, because the store page was honest about what it is. The trouble is that it asks more of me than it gives back. It says "get to know the people" and I got to know their opening hours. I'd say it is a screensaver with a very good writer.

**4. What was I trying to achieve, and what did I risk?**
Achieve: nothing the game gave me. I made up three goals on my own: find the cardigan's owner, find out why Tolley's stopped in March 1988, and test whether "you can't miss anything" is true. The first was struck down, the second was answered with a date and "lovely man", and the third was answered in the worst possible way: the lantern walk ran by itself. I did not go to it, it came to me. So "you can't miss anything" is true because I am not needed.
Risk: nothing, and that is the point of the store page. The only thing I spent was an evening, and I can't even say that was spent on anything, since the afternoon was cut from under me. Without a loss, a win doesn't mean anything. If I had one loss to put in this town, for instance the ferry leaves at 13:00 and the walk starts at dusk and you can't do both, the cardigan, the Puffin and the lantern walk would all start to matter at once. As it is, every question gets a kind answer and every answer is free.

**What would change my mind.** One thing that costs something. A choice between two evenings. A person who is upset if I ask the wrong question. A cardigan I could carry to the wrong door.
