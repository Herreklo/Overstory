# Round 1

**Turn 1.1. Wilf.** You say: "Do you need a hand with that?"

He hands you a rag without looking up. "Catch that drip. There. Good." A bead of varnish was creeping down the plank towards the keel; you take it off with the corner of the rag, and he nods. "She'll see me out, this one."

He dips the brush and draws it along the third plank, slow, the way you'd stroke a horse. "Forty-one years I took the Puffin over to the island. Rob has her now. He doesn't talk to the passengers. I talked to every one."

*Next time:* 1. "How's she coming along?" 2. "Can I help today?" 3. "Tell me about the Puffin."

**Turn 1.2. The Kettle.** The bell over the door. Warm air, the radio, six tables with oilcloth on them and cakes under glass domes on the counter: a lemon cake, a coffee and walnut, something with cherries. By the till, a photograph of a woman squinting into the sun outside this same window in 1962. June looks up from the table she is wiping.

You say: "Is my room ready?"

"All aired and ready, love." She puts a key on a loop of green ribbon into your hand. "Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house: you live here now." She goes back to the table. "It was Mum's café, then mine. I've wiped this table every morning for thirty-one years."

You ask about the cardigan on the noticeboard. She nods at a hook by the door, where it hangs: grey, hand-knitted, darned at both elbows in a slightly different grey. "Been there since August. Must be a visitor's."

*Next time:* 1. "Is my room ready?" 2. "What's good here?" 3. "What a lovely place."

**Turn 1.3. Teddy.** No cardigan and no name, so you go to the quay's edge. Teddy has the line wound round one finger and is staring into the water as if it owed him money.

You say: "What are you hoping to catch?"

He tips the red bucket to show you. One green-brown shore crab, the size of a saucer, raises a claw at you. "Just Admiral. He always comes back." He lowers the bucket carefully. "I catch him every morning and let him go at teatime. He doesn't mind."

*Next time:* 1. "Caught anything?" 2. "What's the bait?" 3. "Can I have a go?"

**Turn 1.4. The bench.** The brass plaque is small and well polished: FOR MAY, WHO LIKED IT HERE. 1921–1990.

You sit. The Puffin goes out with a cough of diesel and a long white crease behind her, and the gulls follow her to the harbour mouth and come back. Wilf's brush goes along the plank and back. Two gulls argue over a crust on the cobbles until a third takes it. The light moves on the water in long slow plates, grey, then silver, then grey. The tide is a little further out than it was.
