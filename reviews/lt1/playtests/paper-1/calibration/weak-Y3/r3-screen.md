# Round 3

**Turn 3.1. The Hales.** The window opens its crack again. You ask who lives in the house at step 20, the one with the boarded window.

Ivy: "Mrs Pennock went to her son's in the spring." Dot, peering down the steps: "Pretty, isn't it, all those colours coming through?"

You say: "Anything in the paper?"

Ivy shakes out the Gazette. "The council has repainted the railings on the Strand." Dot: "We see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down." Ivy: "And we wave at all of them." Then Ivy, smiling at her needles: "I was engaged to a sailor once. He wrote every week from Montevideo." Dot: "And then he married a girl in Valparaíso." They both laugh. Ivy: "Best thing that ever happened to her."

*Next time:* 1. "Good morning, ladies." 2. "What are you knitting?" 3. "Anything in the paper?"

**Turn 3.2. The Pennock house.** Twenty steps down. Up close the paint lies in layers, like the rings in a cut tree: white on top, flaking away to green, and under the green, in the cracks, the first ochre. One ground-floor window is boarded with weathered ply. In the other, propped against the glass, a card in red and white: **FOR SALE.** Behind it, a bare front room, a rectangle of brighter wallpaper where a picture used to hang.

**Turn 3.3. The lantern walk.** At the top of the steps the sky has cleared to a deep, clean blue, with one star over the island. The jam jars stand in their row along the chapel wall. You reach for one, and Sal, out of uniform in a green jumper, presses a spare into your hands before you can: a jar with a wire handle, a candle stub already lit inside.

Everyone comes. June with her apron off. Wilf with varnish on his knuckles. Teddy, Admiral let go at teatime, swinging his jar too hard. Dot and Ivy arm in arm, a step at a time. Rob, hands in his pockets. Cyril, off the last Puffin, a pair of binoculars still round his neck. Skipper on a lead, nose to every step. The brass band, four players and no tuba, strikes up something slow, and the walk sets off down Wren Steps.

**Turn 3.4. Wilf.** You fall in beside Wilf on the steps, the lights bobbing below you all the way to the water. You ask why Rob doesn't talk to the passengers.

You say: "Tell me about the Puffin."

Wilf watches his feet on the wet stone. "My father built her the winter I was born. Larch on oak, copper all through. You won't find a nail in her."

**The walk.** At the bottom, one by one, everyone sets their jar down on the quay's edge, and you set yours beside Wilf's. The row of little flames runs the length of the Strand, and the black water holds a second row beneath it, trembling. The band finishes, and for a moment nobody says anything. Then the gulls start up again on the chimneys, Teddy says "Wow," and June goes to put the kettle on.

*The lights stay on the water a long time after the band has packed up. One by one the windows on the hill go dark, the Hales' last of all, and the harbour keeps its two rows of candles until they gutter out, one at a time, towards morning.*
