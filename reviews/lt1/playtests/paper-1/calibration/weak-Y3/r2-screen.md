# Round 2

**Turn 2.1. Your room.** The side door of the Kettle, a narrow stair that smells of toast and floor polish, and the key on its green ribbon turns stiffly. The room is under the slope of the roof: a narrow bed with a patchwork quilt in faded reds and blues, a small desk with a lamp, a rag rug, a hook on the back of the door. On the windowsill, a little shelf, empty, and the geranium in its yoghurt pot, very red against the grey. Through the blue window, the whole harbour: the Puffin's wake drawing a line to the island, the Marigold on her trestles, Wilf a small shape beside her. While you watch, the first drops of rain tick on the glass and the water below goes from grey to pewter, stippled all over.

**Turn 2.2. June.** Downstairs the radio is playing something with violins. You reach for the grey cardigan on its hook.

*You can't take that with you.*

You tell June about the darning. She looks at it from where she stands. "Been there since August. Must be a visitor's."

You say: "What's good here?"

"The lemon cake. It's always the lemon cake." She flicks the cloth over her shoulder. "Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house." Then, looking out at the rain: "You'll come to the lantern walk tonight? Everyone goes. You'll love it."

*Next time:* 1. "Is my room ready?" 2. "What's good here?" 3. "What a lovely place."

**Turn 2.3. Sal.** The rain has brought Sal in under the café's awning, the bundle of letters on her knee, her red bicycle dripping beside her. You ask whether she's seen anyone in a grey cardigan, darned at the elbows. "The one at June's? Some visitor's, I'd say."

You say: "Busy morning?"

"Saturdays are all catalogues." She squares the bundle. "Up and up, and then you're there. That's Wrenmouth for you." She stretches one leg out in front of her and looks at it. "Eighty-seven steps, twice a day, eleven years. Calves like a footballer."

*Next time:* 1. "Anything for me?" 2. "Busy morning?" 3. "Which way's the chapel?"

**Turn 2.4. The Hales.** You climb Wren Steps in the rain, the iron handrail cold and wet, polished bright in the middle where the hands go. At step 20 you pass a house with its paint flaking in layers, one window boarded. At step 40, behind the geraniums, two white heads lift, and the window opens a cautious crack.

You say: "Good morning, ladies."

"Good morning yourself!" says Dot, needles going. You describe the cardigan; Ivy shakes her head. "The grey one at June's? Somebody's on their holidays, I expect." Dot: "We see everyone who goes up and everyone who comes down." Ivy: "And we wave at all of them." Then, together, as if they'd said it many times: "We were born in this house. Both of us, in the back bedroom."

*Next time:* 1. "Good morning, ladies." 2. "What are you knitting?" 3. "Anything in the paper?"

**Where you are:** step 40, Wren Steps, outside the Hales' window. About five o'clock. The rain has stopped; the steps are dark and shining, and in the west the cloud is breaking open over the headland in a long bright bar. Water drips off every gutter.
- **Dot and Ivy Hale**, at their window: Dot knitting a long blue scarf, Ivy with the Wrenmouth Gazette.
- **The handrail**, running up the middle of the steps.
- **The Pennock house**, below you at step 20: flaking paint, a boarded window, a card in another.
- **A marmalade cat**, further up at step 50, drying itself on the stone.
- **A house at step 60**, with a bright new red roof.
- At the top, **Chapel Lane** and **the Seamen's Chapel**, where a row of jam jars stands along the chapel wall.
- Below, **the Strand** and the harbour, the Puffin at the steps.
