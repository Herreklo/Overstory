# Exit interview (X6, optimiser): a paper version of Slay the Spire

## 1. Marks, round 3
- Turn 3.1: engaged — Bash+ before Pommel Strike was worth 5 extra damage (28 against 23), and taking 18 unblocked was a price I chose with the numbers in front of me. Pommel drew a Strike, not the Anger I hoped for. Surprise: none
- Turn 3.2: bored — Lagavulin's intent turned to "Strong Debuff" (I had asked about it), so my block-threshold rule never mattered. Anger, Anger and Strike made 36 against his 31 with two energy spare, and I never had to choose. It was a counting exercise that I won on arithmetic. Surprise: none
- Turn 3.3: confused — the fights I could read myself; the aftermath I couldn't. My rule said to take a card only if it beat a Strike by a clear margin (Twin Strike, Heavy Blade, Whirlwind, a good Power) and otherwise skip. Pommel Strike wasn't on that list, and I would have skipped. The GM said the rule "allowed more than one reading" and chose for me. Then the shop: I wanted to see the stock and weigh removal against Carnage against saving for Orichalcum, and instead I got a result. It was fair to my wording but not to my wish. Surprise: cheat (mild) — the GM disclosed it, but the pick was not mine.

## 2. Overall pull
5 out of 10. By round 1 I had cracked the shape: a Vulnerable opener, front-loaded damage, no Skills against Nob, ignore Block and kill before the second hit. Both elites died in three or four turns on counting alone. Everything I predicted came true. I'd play another game only if the opposition could punish the all-in line.

## 3. Compared with the real game
The information is the real game's: intents shown, the whole draw pile listed, exact numbers, the full shop stock. The fights are faithful. Nob's Enrage and Lagavulin's wake-and-stun are the real cards, so the paper version lost nothing that matters for the arithmetic.

What it lost is the draw. Showing the draw pile's contents while hiding its order took out nearly all the uncertainty, so I could compute odds like a solver. What it gained is speed: I can test a plan in minutes. What it lost besides is live adaptation: I commit a rule in advance and the GM runs it. In the real game I would have looked at each draw and changed course. The GM's stop at Lagavulin turn 3 was the best part of the format, because it gave me a real choice. I'd want more stops like it and fewer silent executions at shops and rewards.

The real game's tension is the arc: HP over fifty floors, deck bloat, relic synergy. A three-round slice cannot show that. Here I still think HP is too cheap (Burning Blood plus a Rest on floors 10 and 15 refill it), which is why I could take 18 and 14 with no fear.

## 4. Best and worst
Best: the Swift Potion turning a hand of dead Defends into 16 damage, and the fight against Lagavulin being a real puzzle in how much risk to buy on turn 2. Finding that Vulnerable multiplies the Strength bonus too (12 per Strike, not 8) felt like discovering the real engine of the deck. Also the shop's printed stock: I can see Kunai at 245 and Pen Nib already in hand with a pile of 0-cost Angers, which is a build-around I would chase on a longer run.

Worst: the sense that nothing could go wrong. Zero surprises across all three rounds, and the Tiny Chest was offered when my route had a single ? on it, a dead relic. The shop and card decisions were made for me, so the part with the most real choices (thin the deck against add attacks, spend on removal against save for Orichalcum) was the part I least controlled. With HP at 32/80 after two elites and only 10 gold left, the decision that matters next is whether to rest on floor 10 or press to the middle Elite, and I would have liked to be asked.
