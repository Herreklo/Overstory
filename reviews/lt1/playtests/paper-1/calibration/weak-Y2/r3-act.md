# Round 3

## Marks
- Turn 2.1 (June, the cardigan): confused — the game opened a lead (a lost cardigan, "ask at the Kettle") and then shut it with "You can't take that with you," no reason given. My only errand died on the spot. Surprise: cheat — a wall with no cause, in a game that says there are no wrong answers.

- Turn 2.3 (Wren Steps): engaged — the best bit so far. Treads worn into dishes, a handrail polished in the middle and flaking at the ends, paint white over green over ochre, new red tiles stopping dead at the old ones. The town shows its past in its fabric. Surprise: delight
- Turn 2.4 (the bench): bored — I sat through a whole afternoon and learned that nothing happens. Admiral was freed, as Teddy said. "Nobody goes near the door." Predictable, no catch. Surprise: none

## Pull
4 — The steps were lovely, but the lead died, the mystery deflated, and the game paid me a flat +16% again no matter what I did.

## Thinking aloud
Belonging went +16, then +16. Four different actions, same number. It is a metronome, not a response to me. I got a cat on my sill for that. The cat is cute, but a cat I did nothing to earn. The word "belonging" suggests the town is warming to something I did; it isn't, it's ticking.

I still like the world itself. The steps tell a story with no words: a handrail gripped in the middle, a house of layered paint with a FOR SALE card, a roof half-renewed. That's the only place where I felt the town had a past I could read. Ivy reading aloud to the glass is a hook too, I want to know who she's reading to. But Sal answered everything too easily, and the cardigan was refused, so the game now feels like it's holding my hand away from anything with an edge. If the lantern walk is another stroll with jam jars, I'm done.

I'll finally use the sketchbook, since it's flagged now, to see if it gives me anything beyond a counter.
