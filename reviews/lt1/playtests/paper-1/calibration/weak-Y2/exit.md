# Exit interview: Y2 (BG3 / RPG player), Saltlight

## 1. Marks, round 3
- Turn 3.1 (sketch the window): curious — a nice verb ("draw the chandler's front as the light goes"), and "the rope still waits on its hook" has a little weight. But the sketch only restated what I had already seen, so it paid me no new information. Surprise: none
- Turn 3.2 (June, the jam jar): confused — I asked for a jar and got "What's good here?", the lemon cake, and the very same "Tea's on the house: you live here now" she said an hour earlier. The jar I wanted came later from a stranger in the crowd. Surprise: cheat — my own words were dropped for a menu line.
- Turn 3.3 (Dot and Ivy): bored — I asked who Ivy reads to and what they watch for; I got "Anything in the paper?", a headline about repainted railings, and "we wave at all of them." The sisters I was promised ("watched the same stair all their lives") have nothing under them. Surprise: delight — Ivy waving at me while I stand right there. One real human beat.
- Turn 3.4 (the lantern walk): engaged, then shut out — "two rows of lights, one on the stone and one in the black water" is a lovely image, and a stranger handing me a lit jar is warm. But the whole evening was narrated to me, down to going upstairs to bed, and I got no say in a single beat. Teddy had a jar after all, so my prediction missed. Surprise: delight (the image), cheat (my agency).

## 2. Overall pull
4. If it ended there, fine. I would not have gone back by choice.

## 3. Best and worst
**Best:** Wren Steps. The dished treads, the handrail polished in the middle and flaking at the ends, the paint in layers, the new tiles stopping dead at the old ones. That's the one place where the town had a past I could read without being told. After that, the lantern walk's picture.

**Worst:** nothing could happen to me. The promise "no timers, no fail states, no wrong answers" is exactly what took the stakes away. Specifically:
- Belonging rose +16, +16, then +24 whatever I did. It isn't a response, it's a metronome.
- Every mystery dissolved in a line (Tolley retired to his daughter's; May was a woman on a bench). Teddy's "just rope" is the only tooth, and the sketch of it told me nothing new.
- My words were replaced by numbered lines three times, including the jar and the Hales.
- A lead (the cardigan) was blocked without a reason.
- The journal told me I'd like the town, twice. I don't think I will.
- The chapter's one event ran as a cutscene, with an auto-ending.

## 4. Chapter Two? $14.99?
No to both. I'd stop here. It's well written, and a reader who wants a gentle evening would be happy, but a reader like me is never asked a question. At $4.99 I'd try it once for the steps and the lights, and I'd tell a friend who likes Unpacking about it. "First Frost" with new places sounds like more of the same: more things to look at.

## 5. What I was trying to achieve, and what I risked
I was trying to solve something: why Tolley's is shut, what Teddy was hiding behind "just rope", who May was, and what the sisters had seen, with the cardigan as my way in. I wanted a thread I could pull so the town pulled back. I risked nothing, because there was nothing to risk, and I found that out in the second round. The nearest thing was spending a whole afternoon on a bench, and it cost nothing either. That's the whole answer: I never stood to lose, so I never stood to win.
