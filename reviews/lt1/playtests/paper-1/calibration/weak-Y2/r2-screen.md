# Round 2

## Saturday afternoon

The rain comes in soft from the west a little after one, so fine it hangs in the air more than falls. Nobody hurries. Wilf rigs a tarpaulin over the trestles and works on underneath it. Teddy pulls on a yellow oilskin two sizes too big. Sal moves under the Kettle's awning with her bundle.

**Turn 2.1. June Askew, in the Kettle**
Warm air, toast, the radio low. June looks up from the window table.
You say: "Is my room ready?"
"All aired and ready, love." She takes a key on a loop of red wool from the hook by the till and puts it in your palm. "Side door, up the stairs, mind the third one. Sit yourself down. Tea's on the house: you live here now."
She goes back to the table, wiping in slow circles. "It was Mum's café, then mine. I've wiped this table every morning for thirty-one years."
You ask about the grey cardigan on the hook by the door, and whether you could take it up to whoever owns it.
June glances at it. "Been there since August. Must be a visitor's."
*You can't take that with you.*
*Next time, with June:* 1. "Is my room ready?" 2. "What's good here?" 3. "What a lovely place."

**Turn 2.2. Sal Brennan, under the awning**
You say: "Anything for me?"
"Give me a minute. I'm in the right street, at least." "Up and up, and then you're there. That's Wrenmouth for you." She flexes one foot. "Eighty-seven steps, twice a day, eleven years. Calves like a footballer."
You ask who gets the post at Tolley & Son these days, and who May was.
"Arthur Tolley? Retired to his daughter's, inland, in '88. Lovely man." She nods across at the bench. "May Ord. She sat there every afternoon. Lovely woman."
*Next time, with Sal:* 1. "Anything for me?" 2. "Busy morning?" 3. "Which way's the chapel?"

**Turn 2.3. Wren Steps**
You climb in the rain. Each tread is worn into a shallow dish in the middle. The iron handrail is polished bright where hands have gripped it for a hundred years, and rough and flaking at both ends.
You pass the Pennock house at step 20, its paint coming away in layers, white over green over a first ochre; a FOR SALE card in one window. At step 40 the Hales' window is shut against the weather; behind the geraniums, Dot's needles are going and Ivy is reading aloud to the glass. At step 50 a marmalade cat sits tucked under an eave, dry, eyes half shut. At step 60, bright new red tiles on one roof stop dead at the party wall, next to the old mossy ones.

**Turn 2.4. The bench**
You go back down and sit. The slats are wet; the rain is already thinning.
The Puffin goes out at what might be three, Rob at the tiller, nobody aboard, two gulls following the wake. Under his tarpaulin, Wilf's brush moves along the third plank and back. Two gulls argue over a crust on the cobbles. The chandler's whitewashed windows stay white. Nobody goes near the door.
At teatime Teddy kneels at the edge and tips the red bucket over. Admiral drops into the green water and is gone. Teddy baits the line again, drops it in, and goes on waiting.
By five the rain has stopped. In the west the cloud lifts off the sea like a lid, and the light comes in low and gold.

## The Strand, about 5 pm

Everything is wet and shining. At the top of the hill, along the white wall of the Seamen's Chapel, a row of jam jars catches the light.

**In view**
- **The Kettle**: **June Askew**, wiping the table by the window.
- **Wilf Garrow**, under the tarpaulin, varnishing the third plank of the **Marigold**.
- **Teddy Marsh**, at the quay's edge in his yellow oilskin, the line in the water, the red bucket empty.
- **Sal Brennan**, under the Kettle's awning, sorting letters.
- **Tolley & Son**, whitewashed.
- **The Puffin**, back at the steps; **Rob Garrow** in the stern.
- **The bench**, FOR MAY. **The noticeboard.** **The phone box.** **Skipper**, asleep in the **brown Volvo**.
- **The foot of Wren Steps**; the **Hales' window** halfway up.

This evening: the lantern walk, from the Seamen's Chapel at dusk.
