# Round 1

## Pull
5 — The writing is good and a few details itch at me, but the store page tells me nothing can go wrong, and that is usually where I stop caring.

## Thinking aloud
I like the street. The mooring rings "polished on top where ropes have worked them" says this place is used, and the whitewashed chandler's window with one corner rubbed clear at child's-nose height is the kind of detail that makes me want to look. Somebody is peering in at a shut shop, and a boy with a crab line and "very big plans" is standing thirty feet away. I read that as a thread. The two sisters who have "watched the same stair from the same window all their lives" is another: people who have seen everything and say nothing, and I want to know what they've seen.

What bothers me is everything around the town. I'm told I can't fail, can't miss anything, and nothing has a timer. In my kind of game that means nothing I choose costs me anything, so I'm just a camera with legs. I also don't know who I am. No name, no class, no reason for coming, nobody travelling with me. The journal writes itself and has already decided how I feel ("I think I'm going to like it"). I'd like that to be mine. Forty-eight sketches and twenty keepsakes reads like a collection chore, and I know how I treat those: I tick them off and stop looking at the thing itself.

I'll give it a fair go and chase the mysteries, because the first screen has three I care about: the window, the sisters, the cardigan on the card. If those turn out to be nothing but flavour, I'm out.

## Prediction
Tolley & Son is shut for a reason the town doesn't say aloud (a death, an illness, a quarrel), and the cleared corner is Teddy's. He's been looking in at something for his big plan: a boat part, a map, a chart, something the old chandler promised him.

## Needs
- Who am I, and why did I come? Is there anything of my own in this story, or am I a blank?
- The lantern walk is "tonight at dusk." Do I miss it if I'm not there, or does it "still be here tomorrow"? The two seem to contradict.
- What does Belonging actually change in the room? What do I get for it, and for the sketches?
- Is there anything I can do that someone might not like?

3. Talk to Teddy Marsh, in my own words: ask what his plans are, and whether he's the one who cleared the window. — Expect: an eager kid who half-answers, who's guarding a secret, and who might ask me for a small favour.
4. Inspect the brass plaque on the bench. — Expect: a name (someone's memorial) that ties back to Wilf, the boat or the sisters.
