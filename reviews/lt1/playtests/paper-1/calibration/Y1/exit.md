# Exit interview

## 1. Overall pull
7. I'd play next week straight away: Reid comes back, and Ainsley's November deadline is now a real problem, with £300 of headroom against what he'll ask. I wouldn't play a whole season like this. One match week took three rounds, and a season is 46 weeks.

## 2. Compared with the real game
The micro-loop is faithful, and in one way better. Uncertain scouting fed training, training fed instructions, and the instructions produced highlights I could read as consequences: "had Platt on the turn twice," Kerr getting time once Garvey was closed down, the low cross beating Ridley, the back four no longer stepping up after 60. Real FM's match engine is often murkier than that. Even the sums added up: I checked the table after the match and the goal differences and points were all right.

What's missing is everything I play FM for over a long save. I got three key attributes instead of a full profile, and no data hub. The unscouted players stayed "unknown" through 90 minutes of watching them. Half the other results had no score ("Port Lanner won"), which a spreadsheet player notices at once. The three-turn cap fights the fantasy: in FM I can do every job on a Monday, and here I triage. That makes a sharper game, but it isn't the club-owning one.

It was also too obliging. Every one of my instructions got its own confirming highlight, and the winner came in the 90th minute after two shots off the woodwork. That's the most satisfying story available, and it makes me suspect drama was chosen over a roll. Real FM lets half your changes vanish into noise, and working out which ones mattered is the mastery. Here my reads were confirmed every time, so I learned I was right, never how right.

## 3. Best and worst
**Best:**
- The scouting ranges. "Pace maybe 8 to 12, might be carrying a knock" became Platt moving stiffly by the 62nd minute. The uncertainty paid off, and I'd have paid more to shrink it.
- The Ainsley promise. It became an object on his profile, with a date, a wage and a budget, from two lines of dialogue.
- The planned change at 60', with a condition, carried out with live condition numbers. That's the FM feature I want everywhere.
- Being told what the default would have been (Reid "would have stayed selectable").

**Worst:**
- Team talks are a black box. "Looks focused" and then a missed one-on-one taught me nothing, so I stopped spending turns on them. FM has the same flaw, and it was copied faithfully.
- The flattery described above. The game never pushed back on a read.
- Too little depth: no full attributes, knowledge didn't rise during the match, and half the results had no scores.
- Lowe on the left wing after 60' just ran it out of play, and the game never told me whether that was the role, the fatigue or the kid. The cause was visible when I was right and invisible when I wasn't.

## 4. Marks for round 3
- Turn 3.1: engaged — closing down Garvey showed up at once (46' forced long, 51' Kerr finally has time), and his rating fell from 7.1 to 6.7. Clear cause and effect. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.2: engaged — the low cross at 54' beat Ridley and Mensah hit the post. Exactly what I asked for. Surprise: delight — though by now each instruction was getting its own highlight, which I started to distrust.
- Turn 3.3: engaged — the trigger fired at 60' with the condition numbers I'd asked for, the high line dropped, Fenwick hit the bar at 66', then won it at 90'. Surprise: cheat (in my favour) — the winner came from Ince flapping, at the death, after two woodwork hits. It felt like story beats, not a match engine, and Rudd getting the "told you" was a nice sting, but it was scripted-feeling.
