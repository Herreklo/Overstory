# Round 1

## What happened

**1. "What's your proof?"**
Crane counts it off on his fingers, loud, for the crowd. "Weir mud on his boots to the ankle, at first light, a mile down the road and walking fast. He's cursed that mill since the weir went up; half of you heard him at the alehouse." He jerks his chin at the barn. "And ask him yourself. He won't deny it."
Through the gaps in the boards, Tobin lifts his head, looks at his mother, and says nothing.
No weapon, no tool, no one who saw him at the gate.

**2. Shadowheart watches the reeve**
Shadowheart touches two fingers to her brow (Guidance).
`[INSIGHT] Shadowheart: d20 7 + 5 + Guidance 1 = 13 vs DC 13. Success.`
She leans to your ear. "He's afraid. Not of the crowd: he's had his back to fifteen flails all afternoon and not blinked. Something else. Every time he says 'the mill', his eyes go to Vask. As if he's checking he said it right."
You watch for yourself. She's right about the eyes: on "the mill", Crane looks to the miller, not the other way round. Vask stares at the ground.

**3. "Hang no one until the apprentice wakes and says who hit him."**
`[PERSUASION] Tav: d20 19 + 7 = 26 vs DC 14. Success.`
You say it to Crane, but loud enough to carry: one boy's head is split and the only one who knows by whose hand is lying at the shrine; hang a man before the witness can speak and every farmer here will know the rope came first and the reason after.
Hedda stops shouting. Somebody near the back says "Aye." Crane's jaw works. He glances at Vask once more; Vask gives no sign.
"Fine," Crane says. "He stays tied in the barn till the 'prentice wakes and names who struck him. Then he hangs or he walks, and I'll hear no more about it from strangers."
Vask says something low to his crossbowman and walks back up the path towards the mill; the man goes with him. The other, the one with the cudgel, stays by the barn door and leans on the wall.

Karlach, grinning: "Words! Look at that. I'd have bet on the axe." Shadowheart: "Good. Now we find out what happened before anyone does anything stupid." Astarion examines his nails. "We've bought a farm boy a rope-free evening, for nothing. I'll treasure the memory."

**Your prediction.** Crane's proof was the mud and the law, and also Tobin's grudge against the mill and his silence. It was Crane who looked to Vask, not Vask to Crane. Whether money passes between them, nothing here has shown.

## Hob's Ford, outside the tithe barn. Late afternoon; the sun a hand above the hills.

The crowd loosens. A few farmers drift back to their cottages; most stay, sitting on the barn wall and the trough, flails across their knees. They are waiting to see what you do next.

**Hedda Brun** comes to you with her flail still in her fist. Close up she is grey with dust and hasn't slept. "You bought him a night. Nobody in this Ford would." She looks at the barn. "My Tobin wouldn't lay a finger on Pell. Whatever that man says." She spits towards the mill. "And you might ask who gave Vask leave to raise that weir. It weren't the moot."

**Reeve Crane** stays on the barn steps, the keys on his belt, his crossbow now unslung and leaning against the doorpost. He doesn't look at you.

**The barn.** Through the boards: Tobin, seventeen, tied to the centre post by the wrists, sitting in the straw. His boots are caked grey-brown to the ankle. Hay bales are stacked along the back wall. The door is locked; Crane has the key. The cudgel man (one of the farmers calls him "Gus") leans by the door.

**Up the stream:** the mill, its wheel turning slowly, and the new weir, a wall of fresh-cut stone a man's height above the old line, with the broken sluice gate hanging in the middle of it. Water spills through the gap. Vask and his crossbowman are going in at the mill door.

**Across the green:** the shrine, a small stone house with a lamp lit in the window. Sister Maud's broom leans by the door.

**Your party.** Tav HP 31/31; spell slots 4/4 and 3/3; Bardic Inspiration 3/3; Inspiration 2. Astarion 28/28, Karlach 41/41, Shadowheart 30/30. Gold 214. Camp supplies 46.
