# Round 1

## Pull
7 — Classic BG3 setup: a kid on a rope, a clock, and a crowd. I want to know whose story this really is, and I want the companions to argue.

## Thinking aloud
The reeve is hanging a kid on weir mud and a law that says anyone who touches the mill hangs. That alone is too thin, and Crane knows it, which is why he wants witnesses. The details I notice: the barley is dead from the dry, and the mill has a new, higher weir. Somebody raised the weir and the farmers downstream are starving. So the farmers have a motive to smash the sluice, and the miller's side has a motive to blame it on one of them, quickly and publicly. Pell got hit on the weir stones, but smashing a gate and splitting a head aren't necessarily the same night's work by the same hand. Pell is the only one who can say, which is why I don't want a rope tightened before he wakes. The miller stands there quiet with two hired men while the reeve does the talking. A man who benefits and keeps silent is the one I watch.

I won't open with threats. Karlach would love it, but two crossbows and fifteen farmers with flails makes a bloodbath, and I'm a bard: I'd rather turn the room than draw. I want information first, and I'll let Shadowheart read him since her Insight beats mine.

## Prediction
Orrin Vask stays quiet but his eyes go to Crane before Crane answers. The reeve answers "proof" with the mud on the boots and the law, and nothing else. I think the reeve is more eager than a man with a clear case should be, and the miller's pay is why.

## Needs
- Does walking between the shrine, the sluice, the mill and the barn cost time toward sundown, or only leaving the Ford or resting?
- What exactly did the reeve find on Tobin besides mud? Was there a weapon, a tool, a witness?
- Can I speak to Tobin before anything is decided, or is the barn guarded?
- How hurt is Pell, and could the wound match a person, a stone, or a tool? (Shadowheart's Medicine is +5.)
