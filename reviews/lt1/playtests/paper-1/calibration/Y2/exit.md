# Exit interview: Y2 (BG3 / RPG player), Hob's Ford

## 1. Marks, round 3
- Turn 3.1 (Tobin, the bar): engaged. It is short, and the bar is simply lost in the water, which is realistic, but "What are you going to do?" put the weight on me and my promise now binds me. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.2 (Hedda, the moot): curious, close to flat. Useful (ten households can ring the barn bell, the river is the moot's, a rumour that Vask paid Crane), but it came as one speech with no check and no cost. I asked, she told me everything. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.3 (the mill): engaged, the best beat of the session. Astarion lifting the ledger while Vask, sweating, offers me a hundred gold in the same minute is a proper BG3 moment. Surprise: delight. A small cheat on the edge: Astarion found the right page at once, with no check, and Vask's bribe landed at the exact second the ledger did. Neat, a bit too neat.

## 2. Overall pull
8. I'd keep playing. I have a bribe on the table, a boy's life, a secret that can hang an apprentice, and a ledger in my party's coat. I know what I want to do next (not the gold; the barn bell and the ledger, with Tobin's story kept out of it).

## 3. Compared with the real game
Better than the real thing in a few places: the knowledge was split between people ("only you know", "only you and Astarion know"), which BG3 almost never does so cleanly, and a clue (the shape of the wound) was later used to test a claim (Tobin's story). The dilemma was real: truth saves Tobin from the murder charge and still hangs him for the gate.

Worse than the real thing:
- **The rolls had no teeth.** Persuasion 26 vs DC 14, then 17 vs 15, Shadowheart 13 vs 13 (with Guidance), Astarion's one failure still handed me a lead. I never spent Inspiration or Bardic Inspiration, and none of my spells came out. A BG3 bard expects Charm Person, Hold Person, Dissonant Whispers and Calm Emotions to matter, and nothing made me reach for them. The resources were decoration.
- **The clock isn't a clock.** Walking is free and the hanging waits on Pell. So the pressure is a rule I choose not to break, not a thing pushing me. In BG3 the world moves whether I'm ready or not.
- **Companions only talk.** Karlach and Shadowheart approve, Astarion sneers. Nobody pushed back or argued over the secret, and nobody wanted a different outcome. Real BG3 companions have agendas and sometimes act without me.
- **Menus show the rails.** The options each round show where the author thinks the story goes (a "ledger" option appears just when I hold a ledger). I could name free actions, and I did, but the menu pulls toward the list.
- **No combat or movement.** I can't judge tactics here, and that is half of BG3.
- **Me.** Tav has barely had a say: no personality, no history with these companions, no origin hook. It's my plan and nobody else's story.

## 4. Best and worst
**Best:** Tobin's confession. It was not "he's innocent, find the villain": Pell fell, the gate was the crime, and the law hangs for the gate. Close second: the ledger beat, with the offer of gold at that moment.

**Worst:** the lack of risk. I won every roll of consequence, the clock never pressed, and a bard with a full spell list never cast a spell. The first villain pointed at (Vask, bribing Crane) was exactly who the screen pointed at, so my suspicion that it was a decoy only half paid off. The depth was in Tobin, not in Vask.

Quit points: none. Closest was Hedda's speech, because it handed me the moot as a free solution. If the next round let me ring the bell and Crane simply gave way, I'd start to lose interest.
