# Round 3

## What happened

**1. Tobin, at the gap above the hay**
You (reply 1): "I won't give Crane Pell's name. Say nothing to anyone, whatever he tells you. Where's the bar now, and did anyone else see you two?"
His shoulders come down an inch. "Thank you." Then: "The bar went when the gate went. In the water, or the reeds. I didn't look. I had Pell." He thinks. "Nobody saw. It was black dark, no moon. Nobody I saw."
He looks up at the gap. "What are you going to do?"

**2. Hedda, by the trough**
You say nothing of Tobin. You (reply 10): "Who gave Vask leave, if not the moot? And how does a moot get called here?"
"Crane gave it." She doesn't lower her voice. "Says Vask has the water rights, bought lawful. Bought from who, I'd like to know. The river's the moot's. Always was." She jabs the flail handle at the barn. "The moot's every household in the Ford. The reeve calls it, or any ten of us can, by ringing the barn bell. That's old law. We meant to ring it over the weir after harvest. Crane said wait." She spits. "Folk say Vask paid him for it. Folk say a lot. Nobody's seen a coin."

**3. The mill**
The crossbowman gets up off the step as you come. "Master's busy." From inside, Vask's voice: "Let them in, Wat."
Inside, the noise is the first thing: the wheel's thump through the floor, the stones grinding, flour hanging in the slanting light. Vask comes out of a door at the back and pulls it shut behind him. Up close he's a big soft man, fifty or so, with flour in the creases of his knuckles and sweat on his lip.
You ask about the weir, as a traveller would. He answers too readily. "Lawful. Every stone. I bought the water rights and I've the reeve's word on it. A mill wants a head of water or it's a shed with a wheel on." His eyes keep going to the door he came out of.
Behind the mill, Astarion has already looked through the back window: a desk, a lamp, a strongbox, a ledger bound in green. He waits for Vask's voice in the main room, then goes to the door.
`[SLEIGHT OF HAND] Astarion: d20 5 + 8 = 13 vs DC 12. Success.`
The lock gives with a click lost under the wheel's thump. He doesn't bother with the strongbox. He reads the ledger by the window, finds the page, and slips the book inside his coat.
In the main room, Vask has made up his mind about something. He steps closer and speaks low. "You've a good tongue on you, and you've done this village a kindness, stopping a thing in hot blood. But it's done now, and it's the reeve's to finish. I'll give you a hundred gold to walk on tonight and let the law work." He tries a smile. "Call it thanks for your trouble."
The front door creaks. Astarion leans in the frame behind Vask, out of his sight, and opens his coat an inch: green leather. Very softly: "To A.C., for the water: one hundred and twenty. Then ten every month after, like a tithe. Oh, darling. This is so much better than gold."
