## Thinking aloud
Store page says no timers, no fail states, no wrong answers. For me that means there is no optimisation target except the numbers the game shows: Belonging %, sketches (0 of 48), keepsakes (0 of 20). Those are the score. So the first question is what moves Belonging, and how fast, per turn. Everything else is flavour until I know that.

Candidates for the dominant line: talk to many people (more than 30 townsfolk, so Belonging is probably a per-person sum), or inspect-then-sketch loops (one inspect plus one sketch per object is a turn pair, so sketches are cheap points). I suspect the cheapest Belonging is "talk to everyone once, pick the warm line", and the cheapest sketches are the ones on this one screen. The "can't miss anything" line means I never need to plan, which also means I can't be punished for min-maxing. If that is true the game is solved by brute force.

There is one thing that doesn't fit: the Lantern Walk is "tonight" at dusk, and the clock reads 8:10 am. If it is a one-night event it contradicts "whatever you don't see today will still be here tomorrow". Worth testing whether a clock exists and whether turns spend it.

Wilf is a free probe. He is mid-sentence and offers three lines: the compliment, the offer of help, or the nod. I'll take the offer of help, since helping is the line most games reward with a bigger relationship jump. If it gives the same as the compliment, then dialogue choice doesn't matter and I can ignore it.

Sal the postwoman sits at a hub: she handles letters for every house, so she probably knows everyone and may gate the rest of the town. I want her second.
