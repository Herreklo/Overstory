# Exit interview: Saltlight (X6, the optimiser)

## 1. Marks, round 3

- Turn 3.1: confused. I asked for a jar and the cardigan in one free-text line. June handed me a room key I hadn't asked for, said the cardigan had hung there since August "must be a visitor's", and ignored the jar. The cardigan thread I had built a plan on (June, then the Hales) died in one line and the cardigan stays on its hook. Surprise: cheat. Free text was read for the cardigan and then answered with a deflection, and the jar request was dropped.
- Turn 3.2: curious. The clinker planks, copper rivets and the one glossy plank are good, specific detail. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.3: bored. Inspect plus sketch is two turns for 1 of 48, with no choice in it: no decision about what to draw or how well. At that price the full sketchbook is about 100 turns of the same click. The journal line was nice; the act was a vending machine. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.4: engaged, but not as a player. The climb was the best writing in the game: a house flaking white over green over ochre, a new red roof stopping dead at a mossy party wall. Those are building histories, I could read them, and nobody told me what they meant. Then the lantern walk ran as a cutscene. Surprise: delight at the climb and the two rows of lights; cheat in that the jar I tried to get was simply handed to me, so my turn 3.1 made no difference at all.

## 2. Overall pull

4 out of 10.

## 3. Best and worst

**Best:** the town reads as lived in and old, and the steps climb is the one place where the world showed me history I could work out myself. The rain, the tarpaulin and the rowlock came unprompted and were the only moment I felt I had earned something, and even that was a repeat of a line I had already used once. The loose threads are real hooks: Tolley's shop (rope still on its hook, whitewash rubbed clear at a child's height, a 1988 calendar), Rob who won't talk to passengers, Cyril with his binoculars, the gap where the tuba should be.

**Worst:** there is nothing to optimise, and I looked hard.
- I found no lever. Belonging went +16, +13, +23. I changed what I did each round (many people, one man four times, one man plus a walk), and the total stayed in the same band. It reads as a clock the game advances, not a score I earn. Room rewards (geranium at 16, cat at 29, fern at 52) arrive in the round report, not at the act, so I never saw a cause.
- The counters are decoupled from progress. I am at 52% with 3 of 30+ people, 1 of 48 sketches and 1 of 20 keepsakes. If the 48 and the 20 do not feed Belonging, the collection has no payoff I can see, and if Belonging finishes within a few chapters the whole thing is a short walk.
- "You can't miss anything" is true in the worst way. The 10.30, 13.00 and 15.30 ferries all passed unridden, the lantern walk happened anyway, the jar arrived without me. Nothing I chose changed the outcome. I took an enormous risk (the whole day on one man, no island) and it cost nothing, so it was no risk.
- The town is a set. The Puffin never leaves the quay although Wilf says it crosses "every day of the year", Wilf is on the same third plank nine hours later, Sal sorts the same bundle all day, and Wilf's "Same as yesterday. Same as tomorrow, if the weather holds" shows up on two different turns on day one. A player who tests for schedules finds none.
- Repeat conversation is a dead lever except where the game escalates deliberately (chore line gave a rag, then a keepsake). The other two lines were filler word for word.
- Free text is half-read. It caught names and dates (Arthur Tolley, '88) but never changed an outcome, and it was ignored by Sal and by June when the question did not fit the stock beat.

## 4. Keep playing? Buy?

I would play Chapter Two for one reason: to see whether any thread (Tolley's, the cardigan, Rob) is ever pulled by something I do, or whether each is answered with "lovely man, retired" and a return to the plank. If Chapter Two answers one of them, I stay. If it only adds places and sketches to the counters, I quit within a chapter.

I would not buy it at $14.99. For that price I would want at least one thing to be good at. As it stands it is an atmospheric short story with a counter attached. At a few pounds, as a mood piece, fine.

## 5. What I was trying to achieve, and what I risked

I was trying to learn what Belonging pays for, cheapest per turn, so I could grind the dominant line. I spent round 2 entirely on one man to test repeat talking; I tested free text; I tried to bundle two hooks into one stop; I tested the sketch economy. What I learned: repeats are dead except the escalating chore, the sketch is a fixed two turns for one point, and the Belonging meter does not respond to my choices in any way I could measure with one reading per round.

What I risked: time, and I now see that was an illusion. The game told me I could not fail and then proved it, which removes the one thing an optimiser needs, a cost. A min-maxer finds the game solved on the first screen: do anything, everything arrives.

What would give me something to play: a Belonging figure after each turn, a visible clock that spends time, one scarce thing (a ferry I can really miss, a favour that is owed), and people who are somewhere else at 5 pm than at noon.
