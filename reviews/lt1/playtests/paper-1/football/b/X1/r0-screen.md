# Round 0

**Store page.** *Harbour Seven.* A fishing town, 1989. Twenty-eight people between sixteen and fifty-five, and a seven-a-side league that starts in April. You manage the club the town founded last winter: pick the team, find the money, keep the committee patient. Your players work shifts at the sawmill and sail at dawn. They have babies due, grudges, and lives you don't see until they cost you a Saturday. Knock on doors. Buy a round at the Anchor. Read the noticeboard and the Courier, which is sometimes wrong. Win the league, or fold. Then come back five years on, to the first training of 1994, and see who still turns up.

**Tutorial.** Your desk shows the squad, the fixtures and 6,000 kr. A grey name is someone you haven't watched train: their ability is a range. Hover a name for job and household. Put seven on the sheet and three on the bench before Saturday; if you can't field seven, you forfeit. Ask "why?" of anything in your journal: the game answers only from what you have seen or been told.

## Monday 10 April 1989, 4 pm: the sawmill gate

The hooter goes at four. It rained at midday, and the yard behind the gate is wet sawdust and stacked boards under tin roofs. The day shift comes out in ones and twos, collars up.

Five of them are yours. Sid Tanner and Neil Lyons come first, then Vera Holmes, then Maisie Myers, in her second week here and still in new gloves. John Holmes, the foreman, comes last and locks the office behind him. Greta Brook, sixty-six, comes out with them. She passes Neil Lyons at the gate without a word.

Sid Tanner calls back to John: "Room went quiet for you on Saturday." John plays in the Night Shift, who were on at the Anchor that night. He nods, and stops by you at the gate. "You'll be the manager. Saturday, then."

Neil Lyons and Vera Holmes walk off up the road together, talking.

The other five of your ten are elsewhere at this hour: Sean Hale and Peter Scott work at the Anchor, Bert Kemp sails with the fleet, Toby Tanner works at the Courier, and Ian Myers, sixteen, is a pupil at the school.

## What you can do

A turn is one act, done in the order you write them; date them if you like.

- **The sheet** (a turn): one keeper, six outfield and three on the bench, in order, for April's three matches; you may vary it match by match. A keeper in the outfield, or an outfielder in goal, plays at −2. With no sheet, the first seven on the desk start. Under seven available is a forfeit: 0–3 and a 500 kr fine.
- **Shift cover,** bought on the sheet: 200 kr per worker per match. The sawmill's workers and the fleet's crew work shifts, and a shift can take one of them on any Saturday.
- **Train** at the Mudbowl (a turn and an evening). Whoever comes is watched and gets +1 form for the next match.
- **Visit** someone (a turn; an evening at their door, or none at their work by day) with an offer: time, an evening's talk; a lift to each match this month, 100 kr; cover, 200 kr a match (for someone not on shifts it buys his Saturday afternoon off); a promise, a place in the seven or a role; or a rest, left off a match or two with the place kept.
- **Recruit** anyone in town aged 16 to 55 who isn't in the squad (a turn and an evening). A recruit plays at −1 form in his first two matches.
- **Buy a round** at the Anchor (a turn, an evening and 400 kr). Squad members who come get +1 form next match, and you have two conversations.
- **Go and look or talk** (a turn): the sawmill gate at 4 pm, the fleet's berth at dawn, the school yard, the Anchor at 9 pm, the co-op, or someone's door.

Dropping a regular who started the last two matches, or breaking a promise, may cost you his goodwill.

Free, and not a turn: hover a name (age, job, household, position); read the desk, the noticeboard and the Courier; ask "why?" of any journal entry (answered on the next screen); and write in advance the half-time order for Saturday's featured match against Holm: sit deep, push, or a substitution. None means no change.
