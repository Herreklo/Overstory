# Round 2

## Marks
- Turn 1.1: confused — my hook was "Same name, that's all." Then the Courier gave me three more Holmeses, another Tanner and two Scotts, so the surnames are just a small name pool, and the opening scene put a Tanner and a Holmes in front of me on this case. Surprise: cheat — the scene seemed to pose a link that the name pool had made up.
- Turn 1.2: engaged — the 20 February piece is the first thing with grain in it: a 77-year-old who runs, swims every morning in February and wins quizzes, then dies at home on a Monday. Surprise: none.
- Turn 1.3: curious — Karen pinned the Friday and the band, and "ask me when it's done" is a nice tease. But she agreed with everyone, again. Surprise: none.
- Turn 1.4: bored — five hours to watch a man buy a loaf. It did answer my question: he lives there, alone, and nobody calls. Surprise: none.

## Pull
6 — Nobody has lied, misremembered or disagreed yet. That's Saltlight's problem coming back, and I've lost a turn's worth to a name collision. The one live thread left is Toby's piece.

## Thinking aloud
The notice I suspected looks sound, so I think the "obituary everyone believes" is Toby Tanner's piece, not the notice. Everyone so far has confirmed its pub and choir lines, which is just what a believed story looks like. Four lines rest on nobody who was there: the running, the swimming, the quiz wins and 1981. The quiz is the one a record can test, so I'm going to the bound volumes. Mark Holmes is the only person who was in the house, and he gives one question a visit, so I'll see him first, before my questions reach him and he closes. What bothers me most is that I have to write the hearing now, before I learn what my last three turns turn up. A deduction game where I commit to the solution blind is a real problem. I'm writing it as conditionals. I'm leaving the check unused: with three turns it costs more than it tells me.

## Prediction
The bound volumes will show that at least one of the 1989 and 1990 quiz nights was won by someone other than Mary, or by a team she was on. Toby will say he wrote the piece from talk at the Anchor, not from anything he saw.

## Needs

- Any record of the death itself: a doctor, a certificate, the register. The Courier is the only archive offered.
- Whether the check costs a turn.

## Turns
1. Monday 10 March, morning, the church hill. First, for free: inspect the house from the lane (a towel or a swimsuit on a line, running shoes, her coat still on its hook), and ask why of her death. Then I knock and ask Mark Holmes one question: "Tell me what happened on the Monday she died." Expect: she was taken ill at home that morning, perhaps after her swim, and he names a doctor or her heart. If he shuts the door, I don't press.
2. Monday 10 March, afternoon, the Courier. Read the 1989 and 1990 volumes for the Anchor's quiz nights (who won) and any piece naming Mary Tanner. Read the issues of 27 February and 6 March for letters, corrections, or anything on the Anchor or the Night Shift. Expect: a different winner one year, and no record of her running or swimming.
3. Monday 10 March, afternoon, straight after: talk to Toby Tanner. (a) "Are you kin to Mary Tanner?" (b) "Where did the running, the swimming and the quiz in your piece come from?" If the volumes contradict the quiz, I put the issue in front of him as I ask. (c) "Were you at the Anchor the night the Night Shift played, and did you see Mary?" Expect: not kin; it came from the Anchor's regulars or from Mark.
4. Friday 14 March, ten, the hearing. Each answer below is filed on my board with its source.
   1. **Of what did she die?** Whatever Mark Holmes told me on 10 March, in his words, since he was there. If he gave no cause or wasn't present: not established. I won't guess one.
   2. **Since when unwell?** Nobody saw her unwell outside her home up to the evening of Friday 7 February. She sang at choir on Thursday 6 February "same as ever" (Hannah Tanner, who was there), and she was at the Anchor with Mark on the 7th (Karen Holmes, who was there; the Courier of 13 February dates the night). So the illness began, or was kept private, in her last three days, unless Mark dates it earlier, in which case I give his date.
   3. **Was anyone answerable?** No one, on what I found. I name nobody unless Mark describes an act or a failure he saw himself.
   4. **Who stood by her and her household?** Mark Holmes, her husband, who lived with her. Karen saw them together on the 7th, and I saw him come home with his own key. I saw no one call on him after her death (7 March, noon to five).
   5. **Was there bad blood?** None found. If Toby (who was there) or the Courier records a quarrel at the Anchor on 7 February involving Mary, I report it with that source, and I say it did not bear on her death unless someone who was there ties it to her last three days.
   6. **Does the printed record stand?** The notice stands: she was 77 and the wife of Mark Holmes (the Courier, 20 February; seen together by Karen; he lives in her home). The 20 February piece stands in part. The two pints stand on Karen Holmes and the choir on Hannah Tanner. If the volumes give another quiz winner, that line does not stand (the issue and its date are the source). The running and the swimming are not proven.
