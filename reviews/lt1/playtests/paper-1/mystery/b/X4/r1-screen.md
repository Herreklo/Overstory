# Round 1: what happened

**Turn 1 · Monday 3 March, evening · the Gully Steps.** Andy Shaw's door is No. 6: green paint, a brass knocker and no name on it. You saw him pull it shut. Through Karen Holmes's open shed door you see a chair leg clamped in the vice, freshly glued, and a brush standing in a glue pot on the bench.

Across the steps at No. 5, Hannah Tanner comes to her kitchen door drying her hands. *Hover: Hannah Tanner, 81, retired; open.*
- "How were you and Mary kin?" — "Kin? No. Same name, that's all."
- "When did you last see her, and how was she?" — "At choir, the Thursday before she went. She sang, same as ever. She was there every Thursday."
- "Who looked after her in her last weeks?" — "She had Mark. Mark Holmes, her husband, up on the church hill."

**Turn 2 · Tuesday 4 March, morning · the Courier, Market Square.** Ken Scott is at the front counter and Toby Tanner is typing in the back room. *Hover: Ken Scott, 39, editor, the Courier; open. Toby Tanner, 39, reporter, the Courier; open.*

*The issue of Thursday 13 February,* four pages. Under Deaths there is only the notice you have, and there are no births or marriages that week. On the front page:
> The Night Shift played the Anchor on Friday to a packed room and were called back for more. Kate Holmes, Sam Brook, John Holmes and Ben Reed.

The rest is the tide table, the ferry times and advertisements for the Old Bank Café and the garage.

*The issue of Thursday 20 February,* page 2, by Toby Tanner:
> Mary Tanner died last week. She was 77. She married Mark Holmes in 1981. She had two pints at the Anchor most evenings, ran the coast road every other day, sang with the choir on Thursdays and swam in the harbour every morning, whatever the weather. She won the Anchor's quiz night in 1989 and again in 1990.

*The bound volumes,* June 1989 to December 1996: no marriage of Mary Tanner, and no notice naming Mark Holmes or Karen Holmes. Two notices carry the name Holmes:
> **HOLMES, Ellie.** Died aged 76. Mother of John Holmes. The funeral is on Friday at eleven. *(June 1992)*

> **SCOTT – HOLMES.** Jonah Scott and Vera Holmes were married on Saturday. *(September 1994)*

You ask who placed Mary's notice and paid for it. Ken: "We don't keep that. Notices come over the counter with the money."

**Turn 3 · Tuesday 4 March, evening · the Gully Steps.** You reach the steps at half past five. Andy Shaw comes out of No. 6 as he did on Monday, nods and goes down to the quay. Karen Holmes's shed at No. 8 is lit, and the same chair leg is in the vice. *Hover: Karen Holmes, 72, retired; open.* Nothing you have found ties her to Mark Holmes, so you ask your other three questions.
- "How did you know Mary?" — "From the Anchor. She was in most evenings for her two pints. I'm in Fridays, and Tuesdays for the cards."
- "When did you last see her?" — "The Friday before she went. She was in with Mark, same as any Friday. That was the night the Night Shift played. Packed. You had to shout to be heard."
- "What are you making?" — "Couldn't say yet. Ask me when it's done."

She looks at the clock over the bench, puts the brush back in the pot and turns off the lamp. "And I'm due at cards." She padlocks the shed and goes down the steps towards Harbour Row. At about seven, as you leave, Andy Shaw comes back up from the quay, one hand on the rail, and lets himself in at No. 6. A light goes on behind his front window.

**Turn 4 · Friday 7 March, afternoon · the church hill.** The file's address takes you to a plain, kept house on the lane up the church hill, below the churchyard wall. Its paint is some years old. You watch from noon to five. At about two Mark Holmes comes out, walks down to the shop at the bend and is back twenty minutes later with a loaf and a newspaper. He lets himself in with his own key. *Hover: Mark Holmes, 76, retired; guarded.* Nobody else comes to the door. Just before five the kitchen light goes on.

**Your prediction** (Karen Holmes no longer open by Tuesday evening): on Tuesday evening hover showed her open.

# Round 2 · Monday 10 to Friday 14 March 1997

## Your case file

**Mary Tanner, 77.** Died Monday 10 February 1997, at home on the church hill. Papers in the file: the notice of 13 February. Nothing else.

**The hearing:** Friday 14 March at ten, upstairs at the council office on Market Square. **This round:** three turns, then the hearing as your fourth turn.

Standing: 3 of 6. **Check: open,** once this case. Board: no claims filed.

**The coroner's questions:** (1) Of what did she die? (2) Since when was she unwell? (3) Was anyone answerable for her death? (4) Who stood by her and her household? (5) Was there bad blood, and did it bear on her death? (6) Does the printed record of her death and life stand?

## Where you are · the church hill · Friday 7 March, five o'clock

The lane is in shadow under the churchyard wall. Mark Holmes's kitchen light is on, and a figure moves past the window now and then. Below you the town is coming on in lights: Market Square, the council office's upper windows dark, the Anchor's windows lit along Harbour Row, and the lamps on the Gully Steps across the basin. The weekend passes. Your next turn can be any time from Monday 10 March.

## What you can do

As before: **talk** (three questions; one if guarded; none if closed), **watch** (unheard), **read**, **wait**, and now **check** (name three claims on your board; you learn only whether all three stand). Turn 4 is the hearing. **Give the account** there: answer the six questions from your board, each claim with its source. Write the account into turn 4 of your act file.

Free: walking between places within an act, inspecting, filing a claim with its source, and asking why of an event you have found.

Two acts may fall on the same day or the same time of day, in order. Acts can't go back in time.

**The coroner's rule on evidence.** A claim stands on what you saw yourself, on someone who was there (or, for anything not done in private, the partner or workmate of someone who was), on a dated record, or on two people who each heard it separately. Anything else is hearsay and is struck. Anyone your account names is summoned to the hearing.
