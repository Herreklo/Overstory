# Round 1

## Pull
7. This has what Saltlight lacked: a question to read against, wrong answers that cost, and a store page that says outright the record can be wrong. The opening scene itself is thin, though.

## Thinking aloud
I start with the notice, because it's the only record I have. It's oddly bare. It gives no cause, no "peacefully", no children and no address, only "wife of Mark Holmes". A 77-year-old woman in 1997 keeping her own name while married is unusual, and "wife of" rather than "widow of" suggests he's alive. Then the steps hand me a Tanner (Hannah, 81) and a Holmes (Karen, 72) living a house apart, on the far side of town from where Mary died. That's either the hook or a small name pool. If it turns out to be a coincidence from the generator, I'll feel cheated. For now I'm treating it as the hook. The store page's "an obituary everyone believes" points at question 6, and I think "wife of Mark Holmes" is the line that doesn't stand. I don't want to show that doubt yet, because every question is heard. So I'll ask Hannah about facts she saw herself and save the notice for the records. I'm also wary that hover tells me who is "open". I'd rather read willingness off a face. Andy Shaw nodding past me feels like set dressing so far, though I noted which door he shut.

## Prediction
Karen Holmes will no longer be open by Tuesday evening. Hannah lives across the steps and will tell her that the coroner's officer has been asking about Mary, and the Holmes side has the most to lose if the notice is wrong.

## Needs
- How each of these three, if at all, is kin to Mary or to Mark Holmes. Hover gives work and willingness, not kinship.
- Whether Mark Holmes is alive and where he lives.
- Who certified the death, and whether there's a register or certificate I can read. If hearsay is struck, question 1 can't rest on talk.
- Where exactly Mary's house is on the church hill.
- What the coroner counts as hearsay. Is a sister's first-hand account of a visit a source?
- Whether two acts can fall on the same evening.

## Turns
1. Monday 3 March, evening, now. First, for free: inspect the door Andy Shaw pulled shut (its number, and whether it's his), and from the lane look at what Karen has in her vice. Then I knock at Hannah Tanner's kitchen door and ask: (a) How were you and Mary kin? (b) When did you last see her, and how was she then? (c) Who looked after her in her last weeks? Expect: she's Mary's sister or sister-in-law. She puts the illness at about Christmas, names someone who saw to Mary, and is short with me by the third question.
2. Tuesday 4 March, morning: Read at the Courier. The whole issue of 13 February (every notice that week, any piece on Mary), then the bound volumes for any marriage of Mary Tanner and any notice naming Mark or Karen Holmes. I'll also ask whether the Courier keeps a record of who placed and paid for a notice. Expect: no marriage of Mary Tanner to Mark Holmes, or a marriage of Mark Holmes to Karen.
3. Tuesday 4 March, evening: Talk to Karen Holmes in her shed. If the records tie her to Mark: (a) How are you kin to Mark Holmes? (b) When did you last see Mary Tanner? (c) Where is Mark now? If they don't: (a) How did you know Mary? (b) When did you last see her? (c) What are you making? Expect: she's guarded and answers one question. If so, I ask (a).
4. Friday 7 March, afternoon: Watch Mary's house on the church hill. Expect: someone with a key is clearing it. If it's not Mark Holmes, he probably didn't live there.
