# Round 4

## Marks
- Turn 3.1: curious — "I couldn't tell you that" is the first time anyone hasn't simply had the answer. She watches his door from her sink, last saw him the Tuesday, and doesn't know who found him. Karen's vice is a tease again. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.2: engaged — the empty places were the information: no family, no Neil Lyons, no Oliver Brook, no Toby, no Olive Shaw. Sean Hale was the only bandmate at the grave. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.3: curious — the poster's "with 'Salt Radio'" beside Andy's "Salt Lanterns" and "Grey Radio" is the first thing that smells like the store page's wrongly credited song. But the room was a phone book: forty-odd names, nobody spoke, and there was nothing to sift. That's the Hesper's frozen-moment problem. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.4: engaged — the 24 September piece says "Friday 18 April". The poster and Toby both say September, Andy only joined in June, and 18 April 1998 was a Saturday. It's my first contradiction, and I found it myself. Surprise: delight — though "ran the coast road every other day" turning up word for word for Mary and for Andy is either Toby's habit or the game's template. If it's the template, that's a cheat.

## Pull
7 — at last there's a record that's wrong, a song with a smell, and absences to read.

## Thinking aloud
I file one claim now: the 24 September piece misdates the first night (sources: the poster, the 22 October piece, the 25 June piece). Toby told me himself that the running is talk, so it's the town's line, not a fact, and I want to know who started it. The real thread is the song. "Salt Radio" looks like Andy's two titles welded together, Neil announced it without credit, and Neil and Oliver stayed away from the grave. I won't ask anyone about a quarrel, though. Every question is heard, and I'd rather catch the bad blood from records and absences than be handed it. Sean and Ellie work at the Old Bank Café. If that's the corner café at the foot of the steps, they saw Andy pass at half past five every evening, and they'd know which evening he didn't.

## Prediction
The 1990 volumes will credit Andy with "Grey Radio". Sean will say "Salt Radio" grew from it, and that Neil now announces it as Pirate Radio's.

## Needs
- Kinship I can find. Olive Shaw is the only other Shaw, she was at the Anchor and not at the grave, and I can't tell whether that's a fact or the name pool.
- Whether the Old Bank Café is the café at the foot of the steps.
- Where Andy's shed is.
- Still: any medical record of either death.

## Turns
1. Monday 9 November, morning, the Courier. Read the 1990 volumes for Driftwood (its members, and who is credited with "Salt Lanterns", "Grey Radio" and "Cold Hours"), and every mention of "Salt Radio". Then the issues of 1 and 8 October 1998 (Andy's last weeks) and of 29 October and 5 November (any letter or correction). For free: note Colin Berry's last byline and whose desk is cleared. Expect: Andy is credited with "Grey Radio", "Salt Radio" appears nowhere before September, and Berry's bylines stop in the autumn.
2. Monday 9 November, late morning, the back room: Toby Tanner. (a) "Who wrote the unsigned piece of 24 September?" (b) "Where did the running and the Driftwood songs in your piece on Andy come from?" (c) "Who found Andy, and when?" Expect: Colin Berry wrote it; the songs come from the back numbers and the running is talk again; he doesn't know who found him.
3. Monday 9 November, afternoon: Sean Hale at the Old Bank Café. On the way, for free: inspect No. 6's yard and shed from wherever they can be seen. (a) "When did you last see Andy go by?" (b) "Who wrote 'Salt Radio'?" (c) "Who arranged his funeral and his notice?" Expect: he last saw him on Tuesday the 13th, the song was built on Andy's, and he or the band arranged the funeral.
4. Tuesday 10 November, evening: if the volumes or Sean tie "Salt Radio" to Andy, I talk to Neil Lyons at the Anchor or the sawmill and ask: "Who wrote 'Salt Radio'?" If they don't, I talk to Olive Shaw at the Anchor and ask: (a) "Were you kin to Andy?" (b) "Who did he sit with on Fridays?" (c) "When did you last see him?" Expect: Neil says it's his, or the band's. Otherwise Olive is no kin, and she names the Friday company.
