# Round 2: what happened

**Turn 1 · Monday 10 March, morning · the church hill.** From the lane you see the front of the house: a scrubbed step, net curtains, the kitchen window at the side. The yard is behind and out of sight. Nothing of hers is in view.

*Why: "Mary Tanner died, aged 77." February 1997. Nothing on record says why.*

Mark Holmes opens the door. *Hover: Mark Holmes, 76, retired; guarded.*
- "Tell me what happened on the Monday she died." — "She died here, at home. The Monday. I was here." He looks past you, down the hill. "We were at the Anchor the Friday, the night the band was on. She was right as rain."

He shuts the door.

**Turn 2 · Monday 10 March, afternoon · the Courier.** The bound volumes begin in June 1989. The Anchor's quiz nights appear in the paper's short "At the Anchor" lines:
- **1989:** June, Peter Lane and Agnes Fielding. August, Toby Tanner. November, Eddie Lane and Gary Myers. December, Mary Tanner.
- **1990:** February, Sean Hale and Peter Scott. March, Oliver Brook. November, Mary Tanner.

No other piece in either year names Mary Tanner.

The issues of 27 February and 6 March carry no letters and no corrections. On 27 February, "At the Anchor" has one line: *"The meat raffle on Friday went to Hannah Tanner."* Neither issue has anything on the Night Shift.

**Turn 3 · Monday 10 March, afternoon · the Courier's back room.** Toby Tanner stops typing. *Hover: Toby Tanner, 39, reporter, the Courier; open.*
- "Are you kin to Mary Tanner?" — "No relation."
- "Where did the running, the swimming and the quiz in your piece come from?" — "The swimming I saw myself. I'm in the harbour every morning, and so was she, winter and all. The quiz is in our own back numbers. The running's what everyone says of her."
- "Were you at the Anchor the night the Night Shift played, and did you see Mary?" — "I wasn't in that night."

**Your prediction** (another winner on her quiz nights; Toby wrote from talk): the volumes give Mary Tanner as the winner in 1989 and in 1990. Toby says he saw the swimming himself and took the quiz from the back numbers. The running, he says, is what everyone says of her.

**Turn 4 · Friday 14 March, ten · the hearing, upstairs at the council office.** The coroner reads your account into the record. Mark Holmes, Hannah Tanner and Karen Holmes have been summoned, because the account names them. The coroner puts the six questions.

1. **Of what did she die?** *Not established: Mark Holmes, who was there, gave no cause.* Mark: "She died at home. I was there." **Stands.**
2. **Since when unwell?** *Nobody saw her unwell up to the evening of Friday 7 February: choir on the 6th (Hannah Tanner), the Anchor with Mark on the 7th (Karen Holmes; the Courier of 13 February dates the night).* Hannah and Karen each confirm what they saw. **Stands.** *So the illness began, or was kept from others, in her last three days.* Asked, Mark Holmes says: "She wasn't ill. Not that I saw." **Struck** on his evidence: no witness or record shows an illness.
3. **Was anyone answerable?** *No one.* **Stands.**
4. **Who stood by her and her household?** *Mark Holmes, her husband, who lived with her (the Courier of 20 February; Karen saw them together on the 7th; I saw him come home with his own key). No one called on him on 7 March, noon to five.* **Stands.**
5. **Was there bad blood?** *None found.* **Stands.**
6. **Does the printed record stand?** *The notice stands. The 20 February piece stands in part: the two pints (Karen Holmes), the choir (Hannah Tanner), the quiz (the volumes give her the wins of 1989 and 1990). The running and the swimming are not proven.* **Stands** as your finding.

**Ruling: accepted.** One claim struck. The coroner records: *Mary Tanner, 77, died at home on the church hill on 10 February 1997. The cause is not established. Nobody saw her unwell. Nobody is answerable. Her husband, Mark Holmes, stood by her.*

Standing: 3 of 6. An account with nothing struck would have raised it. Your case-1 claims leave the board and stay in your journal. What the record shows you got right comes at the close, when your cases are done.

# Nineteen months later

## Your case file

**Andy Shaw, 85.** Died on Wednesday 14 October 1998, at home on the Gully Steps.

His notice, from the Courier of Thursday 22 October:
> **SHAW, Andy.** Died aged 85. Husband of the late Stella. The funeral is on Tuesday at two. Pirate Radio will play at the Anchor afterwards.

**The coroner's questions:** (1) Of what did he die? (2) Since when was he unwell? (3) Was anyone answerable for his death? (4) Who stood by him and his household? (5) Was there bad blood, did it bear on his death, and did anything settle it? (6) Does the printed record of his death and life stand?

**The hearing:** Friday 11 December at ten, upstairs at the council office on Market Square.
**Your days:** round 3 is Monday 26 October to Friday 6 November. Round 4 runs 9 to 20 November, round 5 from 23 November to 4 December. Round 6 is 7 to 11 December: three turns, then the hearing. Four turns a round.

Standing: 3 of 6. Check: one this case, from round 4. Board: no claims filed. Journal: case 1 kept.

## The Gully Steps · Monday 26 October 1998, half past five

The lamps on the steps have just come on. You stand on the half-landing where you stood nineteen months ago.

**What has changed, and what hasn't.**
- **No. 6,** Andy Shaw's door, is shut. Its windows are dark, front and back. The green paint has dulled a little and the brass knocker has gone brown. Nothing is pinned to the door.
- **No. 5,** across the steps, has had its door and window frames painted since you were last here, a dark blue. Hannah Tanner is at her kitchen sink, her back to the window. *Hover: Hannah Tanner, 83, retired; open.*
- **No. 8,** one house up: the shed is lit, its door open. Karen Holmes is at the bench under the lamp, something in the vice. *Hover: Karen Holmes, 74, retired; open.*
- Half past five comes and goes, and nobody comes down the steps.

At the foot of the steps the café's lights are on, as before. Over the roofs you see the boats in along the quay, the Anchor's lit windows on Harbour Row, Market Square at the head of the water and the church hill beyond.
