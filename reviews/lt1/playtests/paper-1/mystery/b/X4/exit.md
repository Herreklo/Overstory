# Exit interview: Answerable

## 1. Marks for round 6

- Turn 6.1: engaged — Oliver, who was in both bands, says "Cold Hours" was Andy's, and the poster on the loft door puts it on Driftwood's bill. But I had already written my account calling "Cold Hours" not shown, so the best testimony of the case arrived after my verdict. "Couldn't tell you why" about the split shut the one door I came for. Surprise: delight — the faded poster on the loft above his berth is the best found object in the game: a record hanging where the thing happened.
- Turn 6.2: confused — Sean claims "Salt Radio" outright: "It is. Mine." So when he said "Couldn't say" to the same question in round 4, that was a blank, not a lie. The one contradiction I had built a thread on was the game having no line for him. Surprise: cheat.
- Turn 6.3: engaged — I asked Karen what she could see from her bench, his lamp, and got a date: the Tuesday. Then came the first quarrel of the whole game: Sarah Lane had words with Andy at the Anchor. But it came secondhand, from Larry Baker at cards, on my last turn, with the account already written. Surprise: delight, and the worst-timed one I've had.
- Turn 6.4 (the hearing): confused — accepted, nothing struck, standing up to 4, and then 55 of 100 at the close. The hearing rewards caution, and the close scores me on what I didn't find. I can't tell what the 55 measures. Surprise: cheat — the close names things I had no path to. When I asked why Andy joined Pirate Radio I was told nothing on record says why, yet the close knows of a try-out against Sarah Lane and Alice Tanner. A 1995 poster credits "Salt Lanterns" to "Ash", and I never saw or heard of it. And my "1990 not shown", built on the paper's own dated items, is put down as "the paper's slips".

## 2. Would this tenth hour still hold you?

No. Nobody dies, no case comes, and nothing can be filed, checked or ruled on. Worse, the game plays me: "You keep after the song", and then it tells me how my month went. Saltlight's journal did the same, writing my feelings for me. What hurts is that the most interesting things in the game happen here, where I can't touch them. Oliver's new song is "Cold Lanterns", the third time Andy's titles have been welded into somebody else's song. Neil leaves the sawmill and stays guarded. Sean and Larry Baker fall out at the bar on Christmas Day. Every one of these is a thread with no means to pull it. It reads like a trailer for the game I wanted. Pull: 3.

## 3. Would you keep playing? Would you buy it? Would you come back after a week?

I'd play one more case if it were the next case and not an empty month. I wouldn't buy it as it stands; I'd wishlist it. I'd come back after a week only to find out about "Cold Lanterns", and since the game gives me no way to pursue that, I probably wouldn't come back.

## 4. What kept you?

- A question to read against. The six questions and the store page's promise that the record can be wrong gave every surface a job, which Saltlight never had.
- Case 2 was a man I had watched walk past me in case 1, unprompted. His walk became evidence that stood at the hearing.
- Toby Tanner: a witness who sorts what he saw, what is recorded and what is talk. Then I found he had founded Driftwood himself, so the obituary's author was inside the story.
- Karen's "He never stopped. May or any other month." It was the only time a witness's own eyes contradicted the printed record, and I got there by asking about what she could see.
- The pattern in the titles: "Salt Lanterns" and "Grey Radio" turning into "Salt Radio", and now "Cold Lanterns". I noticed it myself, and it still nags.

## 5. Where did you nearly quit?

At the end of case 1. Four turns and a hearing produced a death with no cause, no doctor and no lie. I wrote the account before my turns resolved and learned nothing about whether I'd been right. Second, the run of "couldn't say" answers in round 4 (Sean three times, then Karen). That was the moment I stopped being able to tell evasion from emptiness. Third, now.

## 6. What was hollow?

- **Questions 1 and 2 were dead letters in both cases.** No cause was ever on record, nobody could name who found Andy, and there was no doctor, register or certificate to read. A third of every account was "not established", and I spent turns finding that out twice.
- **"Couldn't say" from people who must know.** An "open" bandmate couldn't say who wrote his own band's song, then claimed it. Karen couldn't say when she last saw Andy, but could say when she last saw his lamp. That is guessing the phrasing, not reading people.
- **The records' slips.** Every Driftwood list disagreed a little with the next: Edward Baker "joins" after being listed, and Grace and Sylvia Waller appear where the lists have other people. When every record is a little wrong, no single error is a clue, yet the close scored me against those slips.
- **Writing the solution blind.** In both cases the hearing was written before my last three turns came back. Oliver's "Andy" and Karen's quarrel both arrived after my verdict.
- **Threats that never bit.** "Every question is heard" and "grief makes people short with you" never happened. Nobody turned on me until after the hearing. I spent the whole game being careful for nothing, like the Hesper's three-at-a-time rule.
- **The name pool.** Surnames mean nothing (a Tanner, a Holmes and a Shaw who are all "no relation"), so kinship, the most natural thing to deduce in a small town, is closed off. My first deduction of the game was spent on a coincidence.
- **Watches as roll calls.** Forty names in a room, nobody speaking, nothing to overlook.
- **Karen's vice:** "Ask me when it's done". It was mending, nothing more.
- **Voices.** Everyone speaks in the same short, flat register. Only Toby sounded like a person.

## 7. Which game does this better?

- **Paradise Killer:** you gather evidence, choose when to start the trial, and argue the case from what you hold, after you hold it.
- **Pentiment:** a small town of people with grudges and their own versions of events, where you must name someone and live with not knowing. Every person there has a voice.
- **Return of the Obra Dinn:** every clue is placed on purpose, the confirmation teaches you as you go, and there is no noise dressed up as evidence.
- **Shadows of Doubt:** the closest kin, a generated town with routines and records. It has the same hollowness of generated people, but its cases do have an answer at the end.

## 8. What would you change?

1. **Let me give the account when I choose,** after my turns have resolved, and never ask for the solution blind.
2. **Make every fact in the close reachable** by some act I could have taken. If asking why says "nothing on record", the close can't then tell me about a try-out.
3. **Separate "doesn't know" from "won't say"** in the text: a pause, a look away, a change of subject. And never let an open witness blank on something they must know.
4. **Make the slips mean something or not count.** Give them one hand with a habit I can learn, the way you learn a typesetter, or stop scoring against them.
5. **Make the cause of death reachable,** through a doctor, a register or whoever found him, or say at the start that it never is.
6. **Kinship:** a register of births, marriages and deaths, or a larger name pool, so that a shared surname means something.
7. **Make watches show exchanges,** not lists, and let me follow one person out of the room.
8. **Score each case right after its hearing,** with what I missed, so I can learn from case 1 before playing case 2.
9. **Make questions cost something** (people turning guarded, the town talking), or drop the threat.
10. **Let the threads continue between cases.** Mary argued with Andy in the street in 1996, and he was most often with her, but nothing in case 1 pointed me at the man who walked past me in its first scene. In the tenth hour, let me pursue the song on my own, with a stake, or end the game.

## 9. What would you lose if you stopped now?

The song. "Cold Lanterns" makes three welded titles, and I want to know whether Oliver is taking a dead man's work or honouring it, and who "Ash" is. Sean and Larry's quarrel too. But the game has no case and no tool for either, so honestly I'd lose a hunch, not a game.

## 10. What do you believe happened to Andy Shaw, Edward Baker and Sarah Lane, and why?

**Andy Shaw** died alone at No. 6 on the 13th or 14th of October 1998. His lamp was last lit on the Tuesday and his walk went as usual that evening. Nobody caused it, and at 85 I'd guess his heart or plain age, though nothing I found can say so. He kept to his routines to the end, mending in his shed most days, so the obituary's "until May" was a writer's flourish. He wrote "Salt Lanterns", "Grey Radio" and "Cold Hours". Since his death, his titles keep turning up in other men's songs. I don't think anyone stole a finished song. I think the band carries his phrases on and nobody credits him, and that is the record going wrong in slow motion. The "Ash" on the 1995 poster I would guess was a slip, or Oscar Ash, who crews in Oliver's fleet and came to the grave. I can't tell which.

**Edward Baker** played beside Andy for five years in Driftwood and was there to the split in September 1996. I never once saw him. He wasn't at the grave, the wake or any room I watched, and he didn't join Pirate Radio. I believe the split left him out, and his empty place at the funeral is the one absence I failed to read. I'd like to think he is kin to Larry Baker, who carried Sarah Lane's quarrel to the cards and fell out with Sean at Christmas, as if someone were keeping the band's grievances. But this town taught me that surnames are noise, so I don't trust that.

**Sarah Lane** joined Driftwood in January 1996, played its last night that March, and lost the band that September. She had words with Andy at the Anchor in July 1997. In June 1998 she tried out for Pirate Radio and lost the place to him, a man of 84. That is real bad blood: twice his band, twice not hers. Nothing she did touched his death. If anything settled it, his death did, and even then the place didn't go to her, because Pirate Radio go on as three. She was missing from the grave and the wake, and I believe that was deliberate.
