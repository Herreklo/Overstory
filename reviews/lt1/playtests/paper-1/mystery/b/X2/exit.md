# Exit: Answerable

## 1. Marks

- **Turn 6.1:** engaged. Olive Shaw was the first witness in either case to hand me something I hadn't asked for: "He had words with Sarah Lane in here, mind." She also put Andy, Edward and Alex Myers up in the loft writing. Both cut against the poster theory I'd already locked into my account. Surprise: delight, soured because it came on my last free evening with no turn left to chase it.
- **Turn 6.2:** confused. Oscar Ash said "Not me. I was never in that band." That should have stopped me, and it couldn't, because my hearing account was written in the same act. I had a branch for Oscar confirming and none for Oscar denying. Part of that is my fault. Surprise: none. It was a fair dead end.
- **Turn 6.3:** engaged. "All three stand." It was the first time in two cases the game told me I was right about anything. Surprise: none at the time, cheat an hour later.
- **Turn 6.4, the hearing:** engaged. At last I was in the room: I sat at the foot of the table, the witnesses were called in turn and the coroner ruled claim by claim. I'd asked for that scene since round 2. Surprise: cheat, then a fair sting. The operation my check had just confirmed was struck as hearsay, under a rule (only a wife or a workmate counts) that I was never taught. The Ash credit I struck was the actual wrong record, and I fell for it. That one I earned. Afterwards the close's table was the best screen in the game: "stands" set against what was true, line by line. 45 of 100.

## 2. Would this tenth hour still hold you?

No. Pull 2. The tenth hour shows that the town goes on without me: parties, a night out, Sean and Larry falling out. None of it touches anything. Sarah Lane gave me the tryout once there was no hearing to take it to. "Nobody turns", Neil stays guarded and my standing means nothing. The store page promised "a town that remembers you", and this town barely registered me. The one thing that stung was Oliver's new song, "Cold Lanterns". Either Oliver is starting to take Andy's titles, which would be the next wrong-man story, or the title generator only has about six words. I suspect the second, and that deflates "Salt Radio" in hindsight, which I'd read as a clue.

## 3. Would you keep playing? Buy it? Come back after a week?

No, no, and no. If a third case had opened with a death someone could actually answer for, I'd have played round 7. A sim running quietly in January is not a reason to return.

## 4. What kept you?

- Documents contradicting documents. The 1995 poster loose in the bound volume, set against Toby's piece, was a real deduction, Obra Dinn style.
- The world showing a death without saying it: half past five passes and nobody walks to the shingle. A new poster is pasted over the old one.
- The song. It was the one question in the game with sides.
- The close. At last, what was true, and what my "stands" had been worth.
- Fear of the wrong answer. Standing could fall, and it did.

## 5. Where did you nearly quit?

At the end of Mary's hearing. The account was written for me, everything stood, and I learned nothing. I'd have quit if Andy's case hadn't opened straight away. I nearly quit again at Sarah Lane's three "couldn't say" answers (5.2), and at Hannah being the fifth person who "couldn't tell you" who found a man on a lit set of steps. I'd quit here.

## 6. What was hollow?

- **Death has no body.** In both cases there was no doctor, no finder, no cause and no informant. Seven people "couldn't tell you" who found Andy. A coroner's officer who can't learn who found the body isn't doing the job.
- **Questions 3 and 5 always come out "no one" and "nothing follows."** The store page says "never name the innocent", but there was never anyone to name.
- **Dead answers.** "I couldn't say", "same as ever", "first I've heard of it". A refusal that points nowhere costs me a turn and gives the world nothing.
- **Template seams.** "Threw a party for their friends at the Anchor" came up four times, "had words" everywhere, and two obituaries share a running line. The running line turned out to be true, so the thing I flagged as a template was real, and I can't tell seam from fact any more.
- **Witnesses who don't notice being caught.** Oliver went from "Andy's" to "Sean brought it. I was there." I put it to his face and nothing happened. In BG3 or Disco that is a scene.
- **Nobody grieves and nobody wants anything from me.** The wake was a Tuesday card night. No one came to me, asked me to bury something or begged me to find something out. My officer has no name, no past and no reason to care.
- **The format hid the trap.** The hearing account is due before my last three turns resolve, so the last week's findings (Olive on Sarah, Oscar's denial) couldn't reach the coroner. The check tests truth while the hearing tests admissibility, and nobody told me they differ.
- **Name walls.** Seventeen names in a bar list, none of them reacting.

## 7. Which game does this better?

- **Return of the Obra Dinn:** fixed facts, a three-at-a-time check that you can learn from, and clear rules of evidence.
- **Pentiment:** a small town, an inquiry, people telling it their own way, a town that remembers you for years, and choices that cost.
- **Disco Elysium:** witnesses with wants, contradictions that become scenes, and your own character's story.
- **Paradise Killer:** presenting the case at the end.
- **Hob's Ford (my first game here):** its people wanted things, and its failures left evidence. Answerable beats Saltlight easily, because it has stakes, a fail state and a reckoning.

## 8. What would you change?

1. Give every death a finder and a doctor, even if the doctor says "old age". Make the finder a person I have to track down.
2. Make the hearing its own act, written after I've seen my last turns.
3. Teach the rules of evidence up front: who counts as first-hand, and whether a poster beats a witness. Say that a check tests truth, not proof.
4. Turn every "couldn't say" into a pointer ("ask Edward, he was in the loft") or a refusal that means something.
5. Make contradictions scenes. A caught witness explains, lies or goes closed, and I can tell which.
6. Give one death per pair someone who really was answerable, or nearly so, so that "never name the innocent" bites.
7. Give me a self: a name, a history in the town, and someone who wants a particular verdict from me. Standing should open and close doors I can see.
8. Show me the record after case 1, so I learn the rules before case 2.
9. Give me a clerk or the coroner to argue my board with. I missed a companion every round.

## 9. What would you lose if you stopped now?

Almost nothing. I'd never find out whether "Cold Lanterns" is Oliver lifting Andy's song. That's the only thread left.

## 10. What happened to Andy Shaw, Edward Baker and Sarah Lane, and why?

**Andy** died at home of old age, on an ordinary night, with nothing wrong that anyone saw. He ran, walked the shore and played until the end. He wrote "Salt Lanterns", "Grey Radio" and "Cold Hours". The poster's "(Ash)" was a printer's or a band member's slip, and I backed the slip. In June 1998 he won the Pirate Radio place over two young women. The record also puts him among the three people most often with Mary, so the "words" in August 1996 were between two old friends, in the month of his operation and of Driftwood's end. I think that month was one story, and the game never let me read it.

**Edward Baker** was Andy's real friend after Driftwood: a loft man, one of those most often with him, and still swimming at 83. He was the witness I flagged twice in my needs and never visited. He knew the song was Andy's and that there was never an Ash. That one is on me, though the game never put him in front of me either.

**Sarah Lane** joined Driftwood at 19, months before it ended. She had words with Andy in July 1997, then lost the Pirate Radio tryout to an 84-year-old. I think it was a young musician's resentment that never became anything. She said "couldn't say" to me until it no longer mattered, I'd guess because it looked worse than it was. Nothing follows from it, and that sums up the town.

**Overall: 4 of 10.** It's a real detective skeleton with no people on it.
