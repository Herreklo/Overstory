# Round 2

## Your turns

**1. Monday 10 March, nine · the council office, Market Square.** Lily Lyons takes you into her office while Sylvia Waller sorts the post at the front desk. She answers all three.

- *"Who registered Mary Tanner's death, and what cause is on the record?"*
  "We keep no register of deaths here. As for a cause, there's nothing written anywhere I know of that says what she died of. She was seventy-seven."
- *"Which doctor certified it, or was it sent to the coroner because none had seen her?"*
  "Every death in the district goes to the coroner, doctor or no doctor. It always has. Whether a doctor saw her, I couldn't tell you."
- *"Who was the informant: Mark Holmes, or a Toby Tanner?"*
  "Toby Tanner's a reporter at the Courier. Who told whom, I couldn't say."

**2. Tuesday 11 March, afternoon · the Gully Steps.** Karen Holmes is at the bench in her shed, the door open to the steps.

- *"Are you kin to Mark Holmes?"*
  "I couldn't say."
- *"When did you last see Mary out, and how did she seem?"*
  "At the Anchor, the week she died. Her two pints at the bar, same as any night. Same as ever."
- *"Who is Toby Tanner, and where do I find him?"*
  "He writes for the Courier. You'll find him there, days. No relation of Mary's that I know of. There's Tanners all over this town."

**Your prediction** (Karen is Mark's sister or first wife, and will say Mary had barely left the house): Karen did not say she was kin to Mark, and she saw Mary at the Anchor the week she died, "same as ever".

**3. Wednesday 12 March, evening · the Anchor.** Nobody had said Mary was unwell, so you went to the Anchor. Behind the bar: Evan Newman (56, manager; open), Olive Shaw (66, bar staff; open) and Peter Scott (45, bar staff; open). By the fire, Sid Tanner, Audrey Scott and Kate Holmes; along the bar, Molly Scott, Eric Lane, Tara Myers and Terry Sutton; by the dartboard, Steve Baker, Leo Lyons and Neil Lyons; by the window, Eve Vaughan, Tess Lane, Alex Myers and Lily Lyons. Evan keeps the bar, and answers all three while he pulls pints.

- *"When was Mary last in for her two pints?"*
  "The week she died. She was in most evenings, right up to then."
- *"Did she seem unwell this winter?"*
  "Not that I saw. No different. Mind, there's been talk. Mark and Kate Holmes had words in the street last month. And Sally Baker stopped him outside the shop."
- *"Any bad blood between her and anyone here?"*
  "Not that I know of."

## The hearing · Friday 14 March, ten · upstairs at the council office

*Your act gave the rule for the account but not the account, so it was built from your board and journal by your rule: your best first-hand claim on each question, nobody answerable without a first-hand harm, and the printed record struck only where a witness contradicts it.*

A long table under the first-floor windows, Market Square below. The coroner sits at its head with a clerk. Summoned, as named in your account: Mark Holmes, Karen Holmes, Evan Newman, Andy Shaw and Toby Tanner.

1. **Of what did she die?** "At home on Monday 10 February, aged 77. Nothing on record says of what." *The Courier's notice, 13 February; the Courier, 20 February.* Stands.
2. **Since when was she unwell?** "She was not. She kept her two pints at the Anchor, the coast road and the harbour to the end." *Mark Holmes, her husband; Karen Holmes and Evan Newman, who saw her at the Anchor the week she died; the Courier, 20 February.* Each confirms. Stands.
3. **Was anyone answerable?** "No one." Stands.
4. **Who stood by her and her household?** "Mark Holmes, her husband since 1981." *Seen at his door, 5 March; the Courier, 20 February.* Mark: "That's right." Stands.
5. **Was there bad blood, and did it bear on her death?** "Words with Andy Shaw in the street, August 1996. Nothing shows it bore on her death." *Andy Shaw.* Andy: "We had words, that August, in the street." Stands.
6. **Does the printed record stand?** "The notice and Toby Tanner's piece stand. No witness contradicts them." Toby Tanner: "That's what I wrote." Stands.

The coroner closes the file. **The account is accepted. No claim is struck.** Standing rises to **4 of 6**. You did not use the check.

## What you can do

A round is four turns. Write up to four acts, one a turn, in order, and say for each where you go, whom you see, and on which day and at what time of day.
- **Talk:** visit one person and ask up to three questions; write the questions in the act. Someone guarded answers one question a visit. Someone closed will not talk to you.
- **Watch:** one place for a morning, an afternoon or an evening. Nobody hears of a watch. You see who comes and goes, what happens there, and whose usual place is empty.
- **Read:** the Courier's bound volumes and this year's issues, at the Courier on Market Square, and any notice or poster where it hangs.
- **Wait:** let a day go by.
- **Check** (from round 4, once this case): name three claims on your board. You learn only whether all three stand.
- **Give the account** (the hearing, round 6's fourth turn): write your answers to the six questions in that act, each claim with its source.

Free at any time, and not a turn: walking between places within an act; inspecting a person or a thing (hover shows a name, age, work and willingness); filing a claim on your board with its source; asking why of an event you have found (the file gives a reason only where the record states one).

Your journal keeps every statement word for word: who said it, where and when.
