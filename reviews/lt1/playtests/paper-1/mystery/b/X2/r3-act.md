# Round 3

## Marks
- Turn 2.1: confused — "nothing written anywhere says what she died of." A death inquiry with no doctor, no certificate and no register is either the mystery or a hole in it, and I can't tell which. Surprise: cheat — the one office that should know, knows nothing.
- Turn 2.2: confused — Karen "couldn't say" whether she's kin to a man who shares her surname, then she repeated the obituary. She was open and told me nothing. Surprise: none.
- Turn 2.3: engaged — Evan gave me the first real gossip: Mark and Kate Holmes had words in the street last month, and Sally Baker stopped him outside the shop. It came with no turns left to follow it up. The seventeen names in the bar were a wall. Surprise: delight at the gossip.
- Turn 2.4: bored — I asked to write the account myself and it was written for me again. Everything "stood", so I won, but the store page promised that "the record shows what you got right" and it showed me nothing. I signed off an obituary and a shrug ("nothing on record says of what"). If that was the trap, nobody told me. If it wasn't, the case was empty. Surprise: cheat. If the next case hadn't opened straight after, I'd have quit here.

## Pull
6 — the Mary case ended flat, but this one opens better than anything so far.

## Thinking aloud
This quay screen is the best yet, because the world shows me the death without saying it. Half past five comes, and nobody walks to the shingle. A new poster is pasted over the old one, so an 85-year-old played a first night a month before he died. Driftwood played on Friday 15 March 1996 (that's the year the date falls on a Friday), and Andy had "words" with Mary that August. "Salt Lanterns" has become "Salt Radio": I'm nearly sure that's the store page's song "credited to the wrong man". Question 5 now adds "did anything settle it?", so this time there's a quarrel to find. The funeral is "Tuesday at two", which must be tomorrow, the 27th, with the band at the Anchor afterwards. The whole town will be in one room, and nobody hears of a watch. Fifteen turns feels like a real case at last. I'll script every question and write the account myself.

Filed free: died Wednesday 14 October 1998, aged 85, at home, husband of the late Stella (the Courier's notice, 22 October). Played Pirate Radio's first night on 18 September 1998, and Driftwood on 15 March 1996 (the posters on the loft door). Also free, I ask why of three things: Driftwood ending, Pirate Radio starting, and Andy's words with Mary.

## Prediction
At the wake, Pirate Radio will play "Salt Radio" as Andy's song, and someone old in the room (Olive Shaw behind the bar, or Karen) will know it began as someone else's "Salt Lanterns".

## Needs

- Who certifies a death here. Is there ever a doctor?
- Any sign that the town heard my questions, and what standing 4 changed.
- Who found Andy, and who placed his notice.
- Who I am: two cases in, I have no name, no past and no companion.

## Turns
1. **Monday 26 October, evening, the quay: Oliver Brook.** (a) "Andy walked past here to the shingle most evenings. When did he stop, and how was he at your first night in September?" (b) "Who wrote 'Salt Radio'? Is it the old 'Salt Lanterns', and whose name goes on it?" (c) "Who was with him at the end, and who found him?" — Expect: he's warm but vague about the song, and he names whoever found him.
2. **Tuesday 27 October, evening, the Anchor: I watch the wake.** Who comes from the funeral, who stands with whom, what the band plays and whom they credit, whose place is empty, and whether anyone leaves when "Salt Radio" starts. — Expect: someone walks out, or mutters at a credit.
3. **Wednesday 28 October, afternoon, the Gully Steps: Karen Holmes, his neighbour.** (a) "When did you last see Andy, and how was he this autumn?" (b) "Who came up the steps to him: family, a doctor, the band?" (c) "He had words with Mary Tanner in August '96. What about, and was it ever settled?" If Oliver named whoever found him, I visit that person instead and ask (a), (b) and "Who sent for help?" — Expect: the date he took to his bed, and a name.
4. **Friday 30 October, morning, the Courier: I read.** This year's issues up to 29 October: his obituary, if Toby wrote one, and anything on Pirate Radio's first night. Then the 1996 volume around March and August for Driftwood, the two songs and Stella Shaw's death. — Expect: the obituary credits Andy with a song that the 1996 pages give to someone else.
