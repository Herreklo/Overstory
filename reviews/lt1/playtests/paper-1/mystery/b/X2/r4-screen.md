## Your turns

**Asked why** (free): of "seldom out of his shed until May", and of the cleared desk at the Courier. To each, the file: "Ask someone who was there."

**1. Monday 9 November, half past five · the Gully Steps.** Hannah Tanner dries her hands and comes to her door. She answers all three.

- *"You look straight at Andy's door. Who found him, and who came to it in his last weeks?"*
  "Who found him, I couldn't tell you. I'd see him go down the steps of an evening, same as always, right to the week he died."
- *"The Courier says he was seldom out of his shed until May. What changed?"*
  "Nothing that I saw. He was in that shed most days, May or no May."
- *"Did you hear him play in the shed this summer, and anything like 'Salt Radio'?"*
  "He mended things in there. I never heard him play."

She goes back to her sink.

**2. Tuesday 10 November, morning · the Old Bank Café, Market Square.** Lisa Myers (47, owner; open) is at the till and Ellie Eden (44, the café; open) is clearing tables. Sean Hale (47, the café; open) comes out from behind the counter, a cloth over his shoulder. He answers all three.

- *"Neil named 'Salt Radio' as yours. Oliver says Andy brought it in September. When did you write it?"*
  "It's mine. I brought it to the band in September, a new song. Andy played it with us on the first night."
- *"Andy wrote 'Grey Radio' and 'Salt Lanterns' for Driftwood. Is 'Salt Radio' built from them?"*
  "It's just a new one."
- *"Was there anything between you and Andy that wanted settling?"*
  "Nothing. Neil and Andy threw a party for their friends at the Anchor, back in May."

**Your prediction** (Sean would claim "Salt Radio" and go short with you over "Grey Radio"; Hannah would have heard Andy play something like it in his shed before June): Sean said the song was his and answered all three; Hannah never heard Andy play in his shed.

**3. Wednesday 11 November, afternoon · the sawmill.** The saws are running in the shed; timber is stacked in the yard. At work: John Holmes (52, foreman), Sid Tanner (45), Leo Holmes (33), Alice Tanner (29) and Sarah Lane (21), all of the sawmill and open. Neil Lyons (50, the sawmill) shuts off his saw when you reach him. He is guarded: one question.

- *"Who told you 'Salt Radio' was Sean's, and did Andy ever hear you say so?"*
  "Nobody told me. Sean brought it to us in September. I was there."

He starts the saw again.

**4. Friday 13 November, morning · the Courier, Market Square.** Ken Scott waves you through. Toby Tanner turns from his typewriter. He answers all three.

- *"Who told you Andy wrote 'Salt Lanterns', 'Grey Radio' and 'Cold Hours'?"*
  "It's in our own pages. I wrote Driftwood up in March '95, when 'Salt Lanterns' had just gone into the set."
- *"Who told you he had died, and who found him?"*
  "He died on the Wednesday, the fourteenth. Who found him, I couldn't tell you."
- *"You wrote that Mary at 77 and Andy at 85 both ran the coast road every other day. Did you ever see either of them run?"*
  "Run? Not that I saw. Andy I'd pass on the shore of an evening. I walk it myself."

## What you can do

A round is four turns. Write up to four acts, one a turn, in order, and say for each where you go, whom you see, and on which day and at what time of day.
- **Talk:** visit one person and ask up to three questions; write the questions in the act. Someone guarded answers one question a visit. Someone closed will not talk to you.
- **Watch:** one place for a morning, an afternoon or an evening. Nobody hears of a watch. You see who comes and goes, what happens there, and whose usual place is empty.
- **Read:** the Courier's bound volumes and this year's issues, at the Courier on Market Square, and any notice or poster where it hangs.
- **Wait:** let a day go by.
- **Check** (open, once this case, as one turn): name three claims on your board. You learn only whether all three stand.
- **Give the account** (the hearing, round 6's fourth turn): write your answers to the six questions in that act, each claim with its source.

Free at any time, and not a turn: walking between places within an act; inspecting a person or a thing (hover shows a name, age, work and willingness); filing a claim on your board with its source; asking why of an event you have found (the file gives a reason only where the record states one).

Your journal keeps every statement word for word: who said it, where and when.
