# Round 6

**Asked why** (free): of the operation. The file: "Ask someone who was there."

**1. Monday 7 December, seven · the Anchor.** Olive Shaw (68, retired; open) has her pint by the fire. She answers all three.

- *"You share Andy's name. Were you kin, and did anyone go to his door when he stopped coming down the steps?"*
  "No kin of mine. Who went to his door, I couldn't tell you."
- *"Ten years behind this bar: who wrote 'Salt Lanterns', Andy or an Ash?"*
  "Andy's. 'Salt Lanterns' went straight into the set. They were up in that loft till all hours, him and Edward and Alex Myers."
- *"Andy had words with Mary Tanner in August '96, the month of his operation. What about, and was it ever settled?"*
  "Words with Mary? Not that I heard. He had words with Sarah Lane in here, mind. The summer before last."

**2. Tuesday 8 December, a quarter to six · the fleet's berth on the quay.** The boats are in. Oscar Ash (43, the harbour fleet; open) is stacking boxes on the stones. He answers all three.

- *"The band's poster for 10 March 1995 says 'Salt Lanterns' (Ash). Is that you, and is it your song?"*
  "Not me. I was never in that band."
- *"The Courier gave it to Andy in 1995 and again in his obituary. Was it ever put right, and was there bad feeling between you over it?"*
  "Andy and me? Nothing between us."
- *"Did Andy ever talk about his operation, or about Mary Tanner?"*
  "I couldn't say."

**3. Thursday 10 December, morning · the check.** Nobody named who found him or a doctor, so you checked claims 13, 1 and 2: the operation in the city in August 1996; died Wednesday 14 October 1998; aged 85. **All three stand.** The check is used.

## The hearing · Friday 11 December, ten · upstairs at the council office

A long table under the first-floor windows, Market Square below. The coroner sits at its head with a clerk; you sit at its foot with your file. Summoned, as named in your account: Ken Scott, Hannah Tanner, Oliver Brook, Karen Holmes, Neil Lyons, Sean Hale and Toby Tanner. Each is called in turn.

1. **Of what did he die?** "It is not known. Nothing on record says, and nothing reached the Courier." Ken Scott: "Nothing came to us on what he died of." **Stands.**
2. **Since when was he unwell?** "Nobody saw him unwell. He went down the steps and past the berth most evenings until the week he died, and played the first night on 18 September 'same as ever'." Hannah Tanner: "I'd see him go down the steps of an evening, right to the week he died." Oliver Brook: "Past the berth of an evening. He played the first night with us." Karen Holmes: "The week he died, in his shed with the door open. Same as ever." **Stands.**
   "He had an operation in the city in August 1996 and was home within the week." Oliver Brook: "He told me so himself, that August." The coroner: Mr Brook was not there and was neither his wife nor his workmate; one teller at second hand is not enough. **Struck.**
3. **Was anyone answerable?** "No one. Nothing shows a harm anyone could have prevented." **Stands.**
4. **Who stood by him and his household?** "No family since Stella. From June, his band: Neil Lyons, Sean Hale and Oliver Brook. Who found him is not known." Karen Holmes: "Family? Andy had none. Not since Stella." Neil Lyons, Sean Hale and Oliver Brook each confirm the band from June. Nobody summoned can say who found him. **Stands.**
5. **Was there bad blood, and did it bear on his death? And did anything settle it?** "Words with Mary Tanner in the street, August 1996. What about is not known, and nothing shows it bore on his death. No quarrel over 'Salt Radio': all three bandmates give it to Sean." The clerk reads Andy Shaw's words from your journal, 6 March 1997: "We did. August, in the street. Her and me." Sean Hale: "'Salt Radio' is mine. I brought it in September." Neil Lyons and Oliver Brook each confirm. **Stands.**
6. **Does the printed record stand?** "The notice stands." Ken Scott: "It ran on the twenty-second." **Stands.**
   "The obituary does not stand in full. It gives 'Salt Lanterns' to Andy, but the band's own poster for 10 March 1995 credits Ash." The coroner puts the poster to Oliver Brook: "'Salt Lanterns' was Andy's. He brought it to us that December, and it went straight into the set. I was there. There was never an Ash in Driftwood." Toby Tanner: "That's what I wrote, in '95 and in October." The coroner: a witness who was there outweighs a poster's credit. **Struck.**
   "'Grey Radio' was his." Oliver Brook: "His. July '94." **Stands.**
   "Its 'ran the coast road every other day' has no witness, and its writer never saw him run." Toby Tanner: "Not that I saw." **Stands** as you put it. Nobody here says he did or didn't run, so the obituary's line is not struck.

The coroner closes the file. **The account is adjourned: two claims are struck.** Standing falls to **3 of 6**. By evening, word of the adjournment is going round the town.

**Your prediction** (Oscar Ash would say "Salt Lanterns" was his and that nobody put the Courier right, and the coroner would strike the obituary's credit): Oscar said he was never in the band. The coroner struck the poster's credit, not the obituary's.

## The tenth hour

**Monday 14 December 1998 to the end of the record.** Those named at the hearing heard your account read, and the town knows it was adjourned. Nobody turns, and Neil Lyons stays guarded. The coroner sets no new date for the file.

You keep after the song. Edward Baker (83, retired; open) comes up the slip after his morning swim and answers all three. Who wrote "Salt Lanterns"? "Andy's, I think." Was there ever an Ash in Driftwood? "Never. Never an Ash." Why did Driftwood stop? "We finished in the September of '96."

At the sawmill you put Olive's words to Sarah Lane. What were they about? "I couldn't say." Was it settled? "Nothing came of it." Did anything else pass between you? "I tried out for Pirate Radio in the June. He got the place." There is no hearing left to take it to.

You watch the Anchor through December. Mona Sayer and Terry Sutton throw a party for their friends there. Tara Myers and Mona have a night out. One evening Sean Hale and Larry Baker fall out at the bar; asked why, the file says to ask someone who was there, and Sean says, "I couldn't say." None of it touches Andy. The Courier's last issue of the year, Thursday 31 December, prints the party.

In January, at the berth, Oliver Brook: "An Ash? Never in Driftwood. I've a new one for us myself, 'Cold Lanterns'." On Sarah and Andy: "Nothing came of it that I heard." Neil, he says, has left the sawmill. At the Anchor, Neil gives his one answer on why: "I couldn't say." January's issues carry nothing on any of it.

Nobody in the town dies. The record ends with January 1999, and no third file comes. There is nothing to file, check or rule on, and the turns after January find the same town and nothing new on record. Your standing stays at 3 of 6.
