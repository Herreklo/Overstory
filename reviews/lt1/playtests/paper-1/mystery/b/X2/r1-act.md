# Round 1

## Pull
6. The premise has teeth: my questions are heard, I can name the wrong person and lose standing, and the record judges me. But "nobody is murdered", I have no companion, and my officer has no name or past. I'm wary that this is Saltlight's harbour with a folder.

## Thinking aloud
The store page sells me reactivity. The first screen gives me a fish quay, six crew names and a poster, with no line to Mary Tanner, so I don't know why I'm standing here. The notice already smells wrong. She's "Tanner", yet she is the "wife of Mark Holmes", not his widow. Is he alive? Is the marriage real? Who placed the notice, and why does it name no children? That looks like question 6. The poster is faded and dated Friday 15 March, which falls on a Saturday this year, so it's a year old or more. An 83-year-old man is on a band's line-up, and he's just walked past me to the shingle without a word. The store page mentions "a song credited to the wrong man". I bet that's the other death, not this one, but I'll file the poster away. Oliver is offering himself, so I'll take the free lead and spend my real turns on the family and the paper. I'll file the notice's claims (died 10 February, aged 77, at home, wife of Mark Holmes) on the board now, with the Courier as their source.

## Prediction
The notice is wrong or self-serving about Mark Holmes. Either he died before her or they lived apart, and whoever really stood by her (a neighbour, a niece) isn't in it. Whoever placed it had a reason to leave someone out.

## Needs
- Why the case opens on the quay. Is anyone here tied to Mary?
- The basics a coroner's officer would hold: the doctor who signed, the next of kin, who found her, her exact house.
- Who placed the notice in the Courier.
- What standing does: what I lose at 2, what I gain at 6.
- Who *I* am, and why the town would remember me.

## Turns
1. **Monday 3 March, evening, the quay.** I wait my ten minutes and talk to Oliver Brook. (a) "Did you know Mary Tanner on the church hill? Who lived with her, and is Mark Holmes still living?" (b) "Who looked in on her this winter: family, neighbours, a doctor?" (c) "Who'd want to be at her hearing, and who wouldn't?" — Expect: he knew her a little. He names a household or a neighbour and tells me something about Holmes the notice doesn't. Word gets out that the coroner's officer is asking around the quay.
2. **Tuesday 4 March, morning, the Courier on Market Square.** I read this year's issues for anything on Tanner or Holmes beyond the notice (an obituary, letters), and the bound volumes for their marriage and for any death notice of Mark Holmes. I also ask the desk who paid for the 13 February notice, if they'll say. — Expect: the paper and the notice disagree on something, Holmes's status or her name.
3. **Wednesday 5 March, afternoon, the church hill.** I visit whoever Oliver or the Courier names as her household: Mark Holmes if he's alive, otherwise the next of kin. I ask, gently: (a) "When did you first see she was unwell?" (b) "Who helped you with her at the end?" (c) "Who wrote the notice?" If they're guarded, I ask only (a). — Expect: grief and shortness, but a date for question 2 and a name for question 4.
4. **Thursday 6 March.** If the household named a doctor, I visit the doctor in the morning: "Of what did she die, and since when were you seeing her?" If not, or if someone was named as left out or at odds, I visit that person instead. If I have nothing at all, I watch her house on the church hill all afternoon to see who comes and whose place is empty. — Expect: a cause of death, or the first sign of bad blood.
