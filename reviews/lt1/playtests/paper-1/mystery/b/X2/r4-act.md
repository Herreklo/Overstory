# Round 4

## Marks
- Turn 3.1: engaged — "Andy's. He brought it to us in September. A new one." At last a claim I can test. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.2: engaged — Neil, from the stage: "This one's Sean's." Oliver said Andy's. Two bandmates contradict each other about a dead man's song, a day apart. Surprise: delight — this is the first choice with teeth in the game. But the wake wasn't a wake: Tuesday cards as on any Tuesday, nobody spoke of Andy, and the gossip was about parties and dancing.
- Turn 3.3: bored — Karen is open, it's my third visit to her, and she's said "same as ever" twice and "first I've heard of it" once. She's his neighbour and knows nothing. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.4: engaged — the best page yet. The obituary gives Andy "Salt Lanterns", "Grey Radio" and "Cold Hours", but not "Salt Radio", which looks stitched together from the first two. Driftwood's line-up loses Edward Baker and gains Grace Waller. There's a cleared desk at the Courier. Surprise: delight. Then a cheat: he "ran the coast road every other day" at 85, word for word Mary's line at 77.

## Pull
6 — the song has hooked me. The town's one phrase, "same as ever", is wearing me down.

## Thinking aloud
At last a real case: whose song is it? Oliver says Andy's, Neil says Sean's, and Toby's obituary leaves it out. That's the bad blood under question 5, and question 6 probably turns on it. But two things worry me. Everyone says "same as ever" about everyone, Mary and Andy alike, and both of Toby's obituaries have the same running line. Either this town really does run at 85, or Toby writes one obituary for everybody and I signed off the first one at Mary's hearing. I still don't know who found Andy, there's still no doctor, and nobody has yet reacted to my asking. Hannah, at her sink across from his door, is the witness I've wanted since round 1. Free: I ask why he was "seldom out of his shed until May", and why the Courier desk is cleared.

Filed free: "Salt Radio" was Andy's, brought in September (Oliver Brook, the berth, 26 October). "Salt Radio" was Sean's (Neil Lyons, the Anchor stage, 27 October). No family since Stella (Karen Holmes, 28 October). Joined Pirate Radio in June 1998 (Toby Tanner, Courier, 25 June).

## Prediction
Sean Hale will claim "Salt Radio" as his own and go short with me when I bring up Andy's "Grey Radio". Hannah will have heard Andy playing something like it in his shed before June.

## Needs
- Who found Andy, and when.
- A cause of death, ever. Is there a doctor in this town?
- Whether "stands" at Mary's hearing meant true.
- Any sign my questions were heard. The store page promised it.
- Who Edward Baker and Grace Waller are.

## Turns
1. **Monday 9 November, half past five, the Gully Steps: Hannah Tanner, at her kitchen.** (a) "You look straight at Andy's door. Who found him, and who came to it in his last weeks?" (b) "The Courier says he was seldom out of his shed until May. What changed?" (c) "Did you hear him play in the shed this summer, and anything like 'Salt Radio'?" — Expect: a name for who found him, and the song heard before September.
2. **Tuesday 10 November, morning, the Old Bank Café: Sean Hale.** (a) "Neil named 'Salt Radio' as yours. Oliver says Andy brought it in September. When did you write it?" (b) "Andy wrote 'Grey Radio' and 'Salt Lanterns' for Driftwood. Is 'Salt Radio' built from them?" (c) "Was there anything between you and Andy that wanted settling?" — Expect: he claims it, turns guarded, and Lisa Myers hears.
3. **Wednesday 11 November, afternoon, the sawmill: Neil Lyons.** (a) "Who told you 'Salt Radio' was Sean's, and did Andy ever hear you say so?" (b) "Andy came out of his shed in May and joined you in June. Who brought him in?" (c) "Who found him?" If Sean admitted the song was Andy's, I replace (a) with: "Why did you name it Sean's at the funeral?" — Expect: he credits Sean on Sean's word alone.
4. **Friday 13 November, morning, the Courier: Toby Tanner.** (a) "Who told you Andy wrote 'Salt Lanterns', 'Grey Radio' and 'Cold Hours'?" (b) "Who told you he had died, and who found him?" (c) "You wrote that Mary at 77 and Andy at 85 both ran the coast road every other day. Did you ever see either of them run?" — Expect: his source is the band, and he never saw the running himself.
