# Round 1

## Your turns

**1. Monday 3 March, evening · the fleet's berth on the quay.** Oliver Brook turns the hose off, wipes his hands on his jersey and comes down onto the stones. Behind him the crew go on stacking; Eric Lane coils the last rope. Oliver answers all three.

- *"Did you know Mary Tanner on the church hill? Who lived with her, and is Mark Holmes still living?"*
  "To say good morning to. Mark's living, aye. Mark Holmes, her husband; it was the two of them up there. I'd see her in the water some mornings, off the slip. I swim there myself, first thing."
- *"Who looked in on her this winter: family, neighbours, a doctor?"*
  "Couldn't tell you who went up the hill. I never heard of a doctor going. Andy Shaw, now, the old fellow who went by just now: he said he'd had a few words with her in the street last summer. That's all he said of it."
- *"Who'd want to be at her hearing, and who wouldn't?"*
  "Mark, I'd think. He's the only family she had that I know of. Who'd not want to be there, I couldn't say."

He nods and climbs back onto his deck.

**2. Tuesday 4 March, morning · the Courier, Market Square.** Ken Scott lets you through to the back room. This year's issues are loose in a box file, the newest Thursday 27 February; the bound volumes run from June 1989 to the end of 1996. On Mary Tanner or Mark Holmes you find:

- **Thursday 13 February 1997:** the notice, as in your file.
- **Thursday 20 February 1997 · Mary Tanner · by Toby Tanner**
  > Mary Tanner died last week. She was 77. She married Mark Holmes in 1981. She had two pints at the Anchor most evenings, ran the coast road every other day, sang with the choir on Thursdays and swam in the harbour every morning, whatever the weather. She won the Anchor's quiz night in 1989 and again in 1990.
- **Thursday 29 June 1989,** Anchor notes: "Quiz night at the Anchor, won by Mary Tanner."
- **Thursday 29 November 1990,** Anchor notes: "Quiz night: Mary Tanner took the prize."
- **Thursday 31 October 1991:** "At the choir's concert Mark Holmes sang a solo."
- **Thursday 26 December 1991,** Anchor notes: "Mary Tanner and Mark Holmes threw a party for their friends at the Anchor."

No other notice for Mark Holmes, and no letters. Their marriage in 1981 is older than the volumes.
*Asking the desk who paid for the notice would be a talk, a turn of its own; this turn was the reading.*

**3. Wednesday 5 March, afternoon · Mark Holmes's house on the church hill.** Mark Holmes opens the door. "You'll be from the coroner. Come through." A plain kitchen: a table and two chairs, a range, a dresser, a garage calendar on this month. He stays standing. He is guarded: one question.

- *"When did you first see she was unwell?"*
  "She wasn't. Not that I ever saw. Down to the Anchor for her two pints most evenings, the coast road every other day, right up to the end. Ask any of them at the Anchor."

He sees you to the door.

**4. Thursday 6 March, morning · the Gully Steps.** Nobody named a doctor, and Oliver named Andy Shaw, so you go to him. His house is the one above the half-landing; he is in the shed behind it, a kitchen chair upside down on the bench. One house up, a light is on in another shed, a woman at the bench (Karen Holmes, 72, retired; open). Andy puts down his screwdriver.
*Your act named no questions for this visit, so you asked the one thing you had on him.*

- *"You had words with Mary Tanner in the street last summer?"*
  "We did. August, in the street. Her and me."

He picks the screwdriver up again.

**Your prediction** (Mark Holmes died before her, or they lived apart): Mark Holmes opened the door of the house on the church hill himself.

## Market Square · Monday 10 March 1997, a little before nine

The square sits at the head of the basin, cobbled, with the council office on its upper side. Lily Lyons (40, chief officer at the council office; open) is unlocking its front door, and Sylvia Waller (34, the council office; open) waits behind her with the post. The first-floor windows above them belong to the room where the hearing sits. A typed slip is pinned inside the glass of the door:
> Hearing into the death of Mary Tanner. Friday 14 March, ten o'clock, upstairs.

Across the square the Courier's lights are on, and Thursday's issue of 6 March is in its window. Next to it, at the Old Bank Café, Lisa Myers (45, owner; open) is setting out the board on the pavement. Down past the end of the square the basin is half empty: the fleet is out.

Harbour Row runs off along the water to the Anchor. The Gully Steps climb from the Row behind the warehouses. The church hill rises behind the town.

## What you can do

Round 2 is Monday 10 to Friday 14 March. Write up to three acts, one a turn, in order, and say for each where you go, whom you see, and on which day and at what time of day. Your fourth turn is the hearing on Friday at ten.
- **Talk:** visit one person and ask up to three questions. Someone guarded answers one question a visit. Someone closed will not talk to you.
- **Watch:** one place for a morning, an afternoon or an evening. Nobody hears of a watch. You see who comes and goes, what happens there, and whose usual place is empty.
- **Read:** the Courier's bound volumes and this year's issues, at the Courier on Market Square, and any notice or poster where it hangs.
- **Wait:** let a day go by.
- **Check** (open now, once this case, as one turn): name three claims on your board. You learn only whether all three stand.
- **Give the account** (turn 4, the hearing): answer the six questions from your board, each claim with its source.

Free at any time, and not a turn: walking between places within an act; inspecting a person or a thing (hover shows a name, age, work and willingness); filing a claim on your board with its source; asking why of an event you have found (the file gives a reason only where the record states one).

Your journal keeps every statement word for word: who said it, where and when.
