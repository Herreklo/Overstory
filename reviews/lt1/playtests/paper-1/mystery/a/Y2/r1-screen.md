# Round 1

## Your turns

**1. Monday 3 March, about six, the fleet's berth. Oliver Brook.**

Ten minutes on, Oliver coils the hose, steps down onto the quay and wipes his hands on his trousers. Behind him the crew carry the last boxes into the store.

You ask how he knew Mary Tanner.
"Same as everybody did, from the Anchor. She did Mona Sayer. Did her to the life, and the whole bar would be on at her to do it again." He thinks about it. "She gave up the Tuesday cards a couple of years back, more. Never said why. I always took it she'd not been right since she was poorly that winter, '89 into '90."

You ask when he last saw her, and how she seemed.
"Saturday before she went, taking her boat out. She'd had that boat since I was a boy, out in it every weekend, regular as the tide. Looked herself to me." He nods along the quay, the way the old man went. "If you want someone who saw more of her, ask Andy, the old boy who went by just now. She'd taken to walking the shore of an evening since last spring, about his time. He'd have seen her after I did."

You ask who was close to her, and who would know how she was in her last weeks.
"Couldn't tell you. I knew her across a bar and across a harbour, that's all."

He pulls on a jacket. "That do you? There's a pint with my name on it." He goes off along Harbour Row towards the Anchor's lit windows.

*Your prediction about Oliver came due.* He spoke of Mary as someone from the bar and the harbour. He said nothing about her heart, named nobody close to her and no quarrel, and didn't mention the band.

**2. Tuesday 4 March, morning, the Courier, Market Square.**

Ken Scott (39, editor, the Courier; open) looks up from the front desk. "If it's Mary Tanner, the notice was in the thirteenth and Toby's piece in the twentieth. The volumes are through the back." In the back room Toby Tanner (39, reporter, the Courier; open) is typing, and Colin Berry (48, reporter, the Courier; open) is laying out a page. The bound volumes run from Thursday 15 June 1989 to the end of 1996; this year's issues are loose in a box file, the newest Thursday 27 February.

> **Mary Tanner** · *Thursday 20 February 1997 · by Toby Tanner*
> Mary Tanner died suddenly last week of a heart attack. She was 77. She married Mark Holmes in 1981. She had her own boat from 1962 and was out on it every weekend, kept a shed she was seldom out of, played cards at the Anchor on Tuesdays for twenty-five years, and did an impression of Mona Sayer that the whole of the Anchor used to ask for. She had two strokes, in 1989 and 1991, and came through both.

Mary Tanner is not named anywhere in the volumes. Her marriage, in 1981, is older than the first of them. Mark Holmes is named twice:

> *Thursday 15 September 1994.* **Married.** Jonah Scott, 45, of the harbour fleet, and Vera Holmes, 46, daughter of Kate Holmes and Mark Holmes, were married on Saturday.

> *Thursday 18 April 1996.* **Twenty-nine years at the clinic.** Toby Waller, 67, has retired after twenty-nine years at the clinic. Jonah Scott has taken a job in the city, and Vera Holmes has gone with him.

The volumes hold two death notices, and with the first, Ken Scott's piece "Agnes Fielding, 1886 to 1989":

> *Thursday 21 December 1989.* **FIELDING, Agnes.** Died after a long illness, aged 103. Widow of Colin Eden. Born in the town in August 1886. The funeral is on Friday at eleven; friends are asked to come back to the Anchor afterwards.

> *Thursday 18 June 1992.* **HOLMES, Ellie.** Died suddenly, of a heart attack, aged 76. Mother of John. The funeral is on Monday at two. Afterwards at the Anchor.

February's four issues carry nothing more about Mary Tanner or Mark Holmes: tide tables, the council's meeting dates, advertisements.

**3. Wednesday 5 March, afternoon, the house on the church hill. Mark Holmes.**

Mark Holmes (76, retired; guarded) opens the door. "You'll be from the coroner. Come through." A plain kitchen: a table and two chairs, a range, a dresser, a garage calendar turned to March. From the window the hill falls away to the harbour and the shore. He sits across the table from you. He'll take one question today.

You ask who saw her doctor, and who was with her that day.
"It was her heart. All at once, on the Monday. That's what there is to say about that day." He stands. "I'll see you out."

**4. Thursday 6 March, morning. Nobody had named a doctor or a neighbour, so you watch the house on the church hill.**

From the lane above the house, from eight until noon. Smoke rises from the range chimney and the kitchen light is on. Nobody comes to the door, and Mark Holmes doesn't come out. At the side of the house stands a shed, its door shut.

*Watch notes:* Mark Holmes, in all morning. Mary Tanner's shed, not used since February.

Friday 7 March goes by. Your four turns for the week are spent.
