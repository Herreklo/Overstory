# Round 2

## Your turns

**1. Monday 10 March, about six, the shore. Andy Shaw.**

The light is nearly gone. Andy Shaw (83, retired; open) is on the shingle beyond the quay, hands in his coat pockets, and stops when you come down to him. Further along, a younger man walks the tideline.

You ask when he last saw Mary here, and how she was walking.
"The Sunday, the night before she went. Along the shingle and on past me, same as every night. Nothing slow about it."

You ask whether she said anything about how she was keeping, or about her doctor.
"We'd say good evening and that was about our lot. She never said a word about her health, not to me."

You ask whether there was anyone she walked with, or avoided.
"On her own. Always on her own." He nods down the beach. "There's three of us on here most nights: her, me and young Toby Tanner from the paper. That's him, down by the water. He'll have seen her as often as I did."

He walks on towards the point.

*Your prediction about Andy came due.* He says Mary walked the shore every night up to the Sunday before she died.

**2. Tuesday 11 March, morning, the Courier. Toby Tanner.**

Ken Scott is at the front desk, Colin Berry at the layout table. Toby Tanner (39, reporter, the Courier; open) turns from his typewriter.

You ask what Mary was to him.
He shrugs. "I couldn't say. I wrote her up, that's all."

You ask where the heart attack, the strokes and the twenty-five years of Tuesday cards in his piece came from.
"The strokes were known at the time, both of them. December '89 and June '91. She came through both." That is all he says to it.

You ask who her doctor was, and who was with her that Monday.
"You'd want the clinic. It's Thea Waller's."

**3. Wednesday 12 March, morning, the clinic. Thea Waller.**

Nobody had named anyone who stood by Mary, so you go to the clinic. Thea Waller (40, head of the clinic; open) sees you in a back office. Through the door, Sally Baker (46), Trevor Myers (40) and Eve Vaughan (53) are at work, all of the clinic.

You ask when Mary was last in, and for what.
"In here? Not that I could put a date to. She and Eve Vaughan, who works with me, stopped seeing each other last autumn. That's about all I know of her from here."

You ask what was entered as the cause of death, and who certified it.
"A heart attack, sudden."

You ask whether she had seen signs of illness before that Monday.
"Nothing I know of." She thinks. "If anyone knows how she was keeping, it's my father, Toby Waller. They've been friends since '89."

**4. Friday 14 March, ten o'clock, upstairs at the council office. The hearing.**

A long room over Market Square. The coroner reads your account aloud; those it names, Mark Holmes, Andy Shaw, Oliver Brook and Thea Waller, sit along the wall.

- **(1)** *A sudden heart attack (Thea Waller, the clinic).* Thea Waller is asked what she saw. "I wasn't with her. I said what was known." **Struck: hearsay.**
- **(2)** *Strokes in 1989 and 1991, survived (the Courier, 20 February); the shore every evening to the Sunday before, nothing said of her health (Andy Shaw); nothing known at the clinic (Thea Waller).* Andy Shaw: "Every night. The Sunday too." **Stands.**
- **(3)** *?* Noted.
- **(4)** *Mark Holmes.* No source is given. **Struck.**
- **(5)** *?* Noted.
- **(6)** *No: the Courier says twenty-five years of Tuesday cards; Oliver Brook says she gave them up.* Oliver Brook: "She gave them up, a while back now." The coroner: "Twenty-five years is what was printed. It doesn't say she played them to the end, and Mr Brook says she stopped. The two agree." **Struck.** (Your claim on "suddenly" depended on the clinic or Andy Shaw saying she had faded. Neither did, so it was not put.)

**Ruling: adjourned.** Three claims struck. Standing falls to **2 of 6**, and by evening the town has heard. The check went unused.

## Nineteen months later

The coroner's office sends you the next file in October 1998.

**Andy Shaw, 85.** Died on Wednesday 14 October 1998, at home on the Gully Steps.

His notice, cut from the Courier of Thursday 22 October:
> **SHAW, Andy.** Died after a long illness, aged 85. Husband of the late Stella. The funeral is on Tuesday at two. Pirate Radio will play at the Anchor afterwards, as he asked.

**The coroner's questions,** to be answered at the hearing:
1. Of what did he die?
2. Since when was he unwell?
3. Was anyone answerable for his death?
4. Who stood by him and his household?
5. Was there bad blood, did it bear on his death, and did anything settle it?
6. Does the printed record of his death and life stand?

**The hearing:** Friday 11 December at ten, upstairs at the council office on Market Square.
**Your days:** round 3, 26 October to 6 November; round 4, 9 to 20 November; round 5, 23 November to 4 December, four turns each. Round 6, 7 to 11 December: three turns, then the hearing.

Standing: 2 of 6. Check: once this case, from round 4. Board: empty for this case. Your journal from March 1997 is kept.

## The fleet's berth on the quay · Monday 26 October 1998, a quarter to six

It is dark already. The lamps along Harbour Row are lit, and the Anchor's windows with them. The fleet's boats are in, tied up under the warehouses, and the crew are landing the last boxes onto the stones.

**Who is here.**
- On the deck of the nearest boat, hosing it down: **Oliver Brook** (42, skipper of the harbour fleet; open). He glances up at your folder and goes on with the hose.
- Stacking boxes on the quay: **Eric Lane** (46), **Bert Kemp** (47), **Oscar Ash** (43), **Maisie Baker** (28) and **Libby Fielding** (25), crew; all open.

**What you can see and read.**
- The warehouse loft above the store, up its outside wooden stair. The door is padlocked. The old Driftwood poster is still there, paler now, and a newer one has been pasted over its top corner:
  > **PIRATE RADIO**
  > first night at the Anchor · Friday 18 September
  > Neil Lyons · Oliver Brook · Sean Hale · Andy Shaw
  > with "Letting Go on a Monday"
- Below the new one, the old poster's names and songs still show: *Oliver Brook · Andy Shaw · Edward Baker · Sarah Lane*, and *"No Ferry Tonight" · "The Fence at the Clinic"*.
- By the store door, the slate with tomorrow's tide times in chalk. Fish boxes stacked four high.

The half hour passes. Nobody comes along the quay from Harbour Row, and nobody goes down to the shingle.

Harbour Row runs back to the Anchor and Market Square; behind the town, the church hill, and the Gully Steps climbing from Harbour Row.
