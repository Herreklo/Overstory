# Round 2

## Marks
- Turn 1.1: engaged — Oliver gave texture and a lead (Andy), and was honest about not knowing who was close to her. Surprise: delight — he contradicted the Courier on the Tuesday cards without noticing.
- Turn 1.2: engaged — real material: two strokes, Toby Tanner's byline, and Ellie Holmes's 1992 notice in nearly the same words as Mary's. Surprise: delight — "died suddenly, of a heart attack" starts to look like a house phrase, not a finding.
- Turn 1.3: curious — Mark's "that's what there is to say about that day" is a rehearsed tell, but I crammed two questions into my one and got neither. Surprise: none.
- Turn 1.4: bored — four hours of chimney smoke and a shut door. Surprise: cheat (mild) — the notes know the shed has been unused "since February", which a morning on the lane can't show.

## Pull
6 — A hearing date, one check and a standing that can fall are a real clock. But a third of my week went on a stonewall and a quiet lane, and I still have no face, no companion and no choice that costs me.

## Thinking aloud
The case has a shape now. Everyone's Mary is a little different. The Courier gives a tidy obituary: cards every Tuesday for twenty-five years, the boat every weekend, two strokes survived, then "suddenly". Oliver says the cards stopped years ago. Two strokes plus "suddenly" plus Ellie's matching notice makes me doubt the heart attack. Mark's line sounds rehearsed, so something about that Monday is being kept from me. I won't press him again until I hold something he can't brush off, and I'll keep my questions open and accuse nobody: people hear, and I don't want to name the innocent. Toby Tanner wrote the piece and shares her name; he is the best source for where its details came from. The clinic is the only place that can say what she died of. I'd rather have the paper from the doctor than a guess.

## Prediction
Andy Shaw will say Mary stopped coming to the shore some weeks before 10 February, not days, which cracks "all at once" and the boat-every-weekend line. Everyone who knew her describes a fade, and only the paper and Mark say "sudden".

## Needs
- Why I don't hold the doctor's report. A coroner's officer would, so where does the cause come from?
- How Toby Tanner is tied to Mary, and where people live. There is no directory.
- Who her doctor was, and who is at the clinic now that Toby Waller has retired.
- How hearsay is judged for "Oliver said Andy saw her" against what Andy says himself.
- What moves my standing, and whether a failed check says which claim failed.

## Turns
1. Monday 10 March, evening (about six), the shore. Talk to Andy Shaw. (a) When did you last see Mary here, and how was she walking? (b) Did she say anything about how she was keeping, or about her doctor? (c) Was there anyone she walked with, or avoided? — Expect: free talk, a date for her last walk, maybe a stick.
2. Tuesday 11 March, morning, the Courier. Talk to Toby Tanner. (a) What was Mary to you? (b) In your piece of the twentieth, where did the heart attack, the strokes and the twenty-five years of Tuesday cards come from? (c) Who was her doctor, and who was with her that Monday? If he's short, I take (b) only. — Expect: grief, a name for the doctor, possibly his own notes as the source.
3. Wednesday 12 March, morning, the clinic. Talk to whoever attended Mary (Toby Waller or the present doctor). (a) When was she last in, and for what? (b) What was entered as the cause of death, and who certified it? (c) Had you seen signs of illness before that Monday? If Andy or Toby names someone who stood by her more directly, I see them instead. — Expect: a guarded answer, but one fact on the cause.
4. The hearing, Friday 14 March at ten: give the account. Q1 the clinic's cause, else "?". Q2 the strokes of 1989 and 1991 (Courier, 20 Feb), plus whatever Andy or the clinic add on her last weeks. Q3 "?" unless a witness gives a firsthand failure. Q4 Mark Holmes, plus anyone Andy or Toby name. Q5 "?" unless said firsthand. Q6 the record does not stand on the cards (Courier, 20 Feb against Oliver, 3 Mar), and on "suddenly" if the clinic or Andy says so.
