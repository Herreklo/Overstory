# Round 3

## Your turns

**1. Monday 26 October, about six, the fleet's berth. Oliver Brook.**

Oliver Brook (42, skipper of the harbour fleet; open) turns off the hose and comes to the edge of the deck.

You ask when and where he last saw Andy, and how he seemed.
"The eighteenth. Up at the Anchor with us, the whole night." He looks at the loft door. "He'd not have missed it. Not after the summer he'd had over that place in the band."

You ask whether he played on the 18th, and what he said that night about how he was keeping.
"Couldn't tell you what he said."

You ask who found him on the 14th, or was with him at the end.
"I couldn't say." He picks the hose up again.

*Your prediction about Oliver came due.* He was brief. He said Andy played the Anchor with them on the 18th, and said nothing of how Andy looked, how long he had been ill, or who found him.

**2. Tuesday 27 October, morning, the Courier.**

Ken Scott (40, editor; open) nods you through from the front desk. In the back room Toby Tanner (41, reporter; open) is at his typewriter; the layout table is bare. 1997 is bound now, and this year's issues are loose in the box file, the newest Thursday 22 October. The notice page of the 22nd holds Andy's notice, which you have, and, facing it:

> **Andy Shaw, 1913 to 1998** · *Thursday 22 October 1998 · by Toby Tanner*
> Andy Shaw died last week after a long illness. He was 85. Born in the town in 1913, he married Stella Shaw in 1957 and lost her in 1978. For twenty-six years he swam in the harbour every morning, whatever the weather, until illness kept him from the water. He sang with the choir on Thursdays for twenty-two years. In 1971 he tried to open a café in the old bank, and the council said no. In his late seventies he joined Driftwood and wrote some of the best songs this town has heard: "March Light", "The Fence at the Clinic", "No Ferry Tonight". In September he played the Anchor with Pirate Radio. Pirate Radio will go on without him.

The issue of Thursday 24 September says nothing of Pirate Radio's night: tide tables, the council's dates, advertisements. Andy is named twice more this year:

> *Thursday 25 June 1998, by Toby Tanner.* **The room went quiet.** At Friday's open night John Holmes, 52, of the Night Shift, played on his own, and the room went quiet. Oliver Brook did well after him. Pirate Radio have a new member: Andy Shaw, 84, last seen with Driftwood.

> *Thursday 26 March 1998, by Colin Berry.* **A washout, a new band and a retirement.** Saturday's festival on the quay, Ellie Eden's second, was a washout; Molly Scott never did her part. The council had said yes in December. On a better note, Neil Lyons, Oliver Brook and Sean Hale have started a band and call it Pirate Radio. And Olive Shaw, 67, has retired after ten years behind the bar at the Anchor.

The 1997 volume never names Andy Shaw. Stella Shaw appears only in the piece of the 22nd. No issue names "Letting Go on a Monday" or who wrote it.

**3. Wednesday 28 October, afternoon, Toby Waller's house.**

Oliver had named nobody, so you go to Toby Waller (69, retired; open). He takes you into his front room.

You ask what he saw of Andy's health, and since when.
"I swam off the harbour steps with him every morning for twenty-odd years. Then at New Year in '90 he stopped coming down, and he never took it up again. That's what I saw."

**4. Thursday 29 October, evening, the shore.**

Nobody had pointed you to anyone new, so you watch the shore from the end of the quay, from about five until seven. It is dark by half past five. People come down off Harbour Row in ones and twos, walk the shingle towards the point and back, and go up again.

*Watch notes:* walking the shore of an evening this month: Sid Tanner (45), Kate Holmes (77), Molly Scott (71), Steve Baker (72), Susie Lyons (74), Greta Brook (75), Sam Brook (72), Ben Reed (57), Eddie Lane (51), Lisa Myers (46), Toby Tanner (41), Lily Lyons (42), Agnes Tanner (31), Oscar Ash (43), Maisie Baker (28), Trevor Myers (42). Andy Shaw hasn't walked here since October. Mary Tanner hasn't walked here since February '97.

Friday 30 October to Friday 6 November go by. Your four turns are spent.
