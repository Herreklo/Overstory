# Round 6

**Filed:** claims 17 to 22 as you wrote them. Your board holds twenty-two.

## Your turns

**1. Monday 7 December, morning, the Courier.**

Ken Scott is at the front desk, Toby Tanner typing, Vera Holmes at the layout table. You go through the bound 1996 volume, March to June. The issue of Thursday 18 April has Toby Waller's retirement, which you have read. The rest is tide tables, the council's dates and advertisements. No issue from March to June names Driftwood, Andy Shaw, Sarah Lane or Edward Baker. The March 1997 issues have no Driftwood night in them.

**Asked why** (free). Driftwood's split: *"They wanted it to sound like different things. Oliver Brook, Edward Baker and Sarah Lane each needed to be the one who mattered."* (the record of the split)

*Your prediction about Colin Berry's spring 1996 piece was due.* No issue from March to June 1996 has a piece on Driftwood.

**2. Tuesday 8 December, the Courier, then the Anchor.**

In the morning Colin Berry's desk by the back window is cleared. Ken Scott, from the front desk: "Colin? Not with us since October."

In the evening it is cards night at the Anchor. Neither Sean Hale nor Sarah Lane is in. Colin Berry (50; guarded) is at a card table with John Holmes, Steve Baker and Leo Holmes. Your plan had Colin first, so you go to his table. He is guarded: one question.

You ask who told him, in the spring of '96, that Andy Shaw was unwell.
"It was going round that spring. Cancer." He looks back at his hand.

**3. Thursday 10 December, afternoon. The check.**

You name claims 17, 15 and 22. **All three stand.**

**4. Friday 11 December, ten, the hearing, upstairs at the council office on Market Square.**

The coroner reads your account into the record as you wrote it. Seven people are named and summoned: Hannah Tanner, Toby Waller, Alice Tanner (29, the sawmill), Sarah Lane (21, the sawmill), Agnes Tanner, Toby Tanner and Colin Berry.

1. **Of what did he die?** *?* Noted.
2. **Since when was he unwell?** *Nobody saw him ill: Hannah Tanner saw him go down for his walk the evening before; Toby Waller saw nothing up to April '96; only the paper dates it.* Hannah Tanner and Toby Waller each confirm what they saw. **Stands.**
3. **Was anyone answerable?** *?* Noted.
4. **Who stood by him and his household?** *Hannah Tanner, across the steps: she heard him practise and saw his lamp stay dark.* Hannah Tanner confirms what she heard and saw. **Stands.**
5. **Bad blood?** *Alice Tanner and Sarah Lane each went at him over his place in the band, in June (Agnes Tanner). Whether it bore on his death: ?. Whether anything settled it: ?.* Alice Tanner and Sarah Lane each confirm it, and so does Agnes Tanner. **Stands.**
6. **Does the printed record stand?** *No: the paper dates the illness to 1990 and to the spring of '96, and you saw him walk unaided in March 1997.* The court finds "until illness kept him from the water" in Toby Tanner's piece of 22 October, not in the notice. Toby Tanner and Toby Waller confirm that his swimming stopped at New Year 1990. Colin Berry confirms that "unwell since the spring" is his, in the Courier of 19 September 1996. **Stands.**

**Ruling: accepted.** Nothing struck. Your standing rises to **3 of 6**. The coroner records: *Andy Shaw, 85, died at home on the Gully Steps on 14 October 1998. The cause, and whether anyone was answerable, are not established. Those who saw him saw no illness. In June Alice Tanner and Sarah Lane went at him at the Anchor over his place in Pirate Radio. The piece of 22 October puts the end of his swimming down to illness; it ended at New Year 1990.*

# The tenth hour

**The close.** Your cases are done, and the record is opened to you.

*Mary Tanner:* a sudden heart attack at 77, her age weighing most; nobody answerable; strokes in 1989 and 1991 survived. On Friday 7 March the hat went round for Mark, and Greta Brook put in the most. The notice and Toby Tanner's piece stand.

*Andy Shaw:*
1. Cancer: found in April 1996, back in June 1997 and again in September 1998. *"?"*
2. Unwell from the spring of 1996; the Courier of 19 September 1996 had that right. *Not given.*
3. Nobody caused his death: his age weighed most, and his health had worn down. *"?"*
4. In July 1997, the cancer back, the hat went round: Oliver Brook and Eddie Lane put in the most, Edward Baker emptied his wallet, Grace Waller gave a note, and not a small one. Colin Berry gave the smallest coin he could find. *You named Hannah Tanner.*
5. In July 1998 Karen Holmes made peace with him, 27 years after the café. *Missed.*
6. In June 1998 he got the place in Pirate Radio as the better player. Alice Tanner and Sarah Lane, each wanting to be heard, went at him in front of everyone at the Anchor: Alice would not give ground, and Sarah had run out of patience. *Right.*
7. Nothing settled it: he took it and kept his head down, because he didn't think it was about him. *"?"*
8. "Christmas Fence" is his. Edward Baker let people think its best lines were his: it was worth claiming, and he thought nobody would notice. The Courier and the poster of March 1995 printed it his way. *Missed.*
9. Driftwood split over the sound, and because Oliver, Edward and Sarah each needed to be the one who mattered; not over Andy's health. *Missed.*
10. His swimming stopped at New Year 1990, more than six years before any illness; the piece of 22 October is wrong to blame illness. *Partly: you had the year and the clash, not which line was wrong.*

The paper's three errors about him: the swimming, the song's credit and the split. **Case 2: 15 of 100.** Nobody was named answerable.

**December.** The seven summoned heard your account; an accepted account turns nobody. You keep after who found him. Nobody can say, and no paper records it. At the sawmill, looking for Sarah Lane, you see Neil Lyons found out: a cover-up of his at work has come out. Sarah has been leaning on everyone lately. Alice Tanner says, not quite joking, that she could play the open night if she wanted to.

**January.** Neil Lyons walks out of the sawmill. Nobody in the town dies to the end of January 1999. No case comes, and there is nothing to file, check or rule on. Your standing stays at 3.
