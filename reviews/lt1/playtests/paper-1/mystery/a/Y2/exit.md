# Exit interview: Answerable (Y2)

- Turn 6.1: bored — A whole turn in the 1996 volume to learn the paper has nothing on Driftwood, Andy or Sarah, and my prediction missed. Surprise: delight (small) — the free "asked why" gave the split's reason in one line ("Oliver Brook, Edward Baker and Sarah Lane each needed to be the one who mattered"). It was the first time that free action paid, though I can't tell what "the record of the split" is. I had it on Monday morning, and my account was already frozen for Friday.
- Turn 6.2: engaged — Colin gone from the paper since October, and the one guarded man gave me the word "cancer", the first time anyone named the illness. Surprise: cheat (mild) — that answer should have rewritten my picture ("nobody saw him ill"), and the hearing read my account "as you wrote it" anyway.
- Turn 6.3: curious — All three stand. I picked three I was sure of, so it taught me nothing, and it came on Thursday, too late to change an account I'd already written. Surprise: none.
- Turn 6.4, the hearing: engaged, then deflated — Seven people called to confirm in front of a room is a real scene, and the coroner's summary line is good. But "accepted, nothing struck, standing 3" came from answering "?" to Q1 and Q3 and keeping the rest safe. Surprise: cheat (mild) — then the tenth hour scored the same account 15 of 100. Two scores that disagree, and the one I could see rewarded caution.

## 2. Would this tenth hour still hold you?

For one page. The reveal list is the best page in the game: Mary's case had nothing hidden, Andy's illness was real, the paper's errors are named, and I can see where my picture was wrong. As play it is dead. "No case comes, and there is nothing to file, check or rule on." It drops three live threads (Neil's cover-up at the sawmill, Sarah leaning on everyone, Alice's not-quite-joke about the open night) and gives me no verb to chase any of them. I read it and closed the game. The tenth hour should be a case, not a postscript.

## 3. Keep playing, buy, come back?

Keep playing: one more case, yes, if the account stopped being frozen. Buy: about $9.99 on a sale, as a mystery-fan game. Not at $19.99, and as a BG3 player I'm not the buyer. Come back after a week: only for a new case. The 15 of 100 makes me want a replay, because I now see where the points were, but only if the town stays put, and I doubt it does.

## 4. What kept me

- The deadline and the one check. "Hearing on Friday, three claims, yes or no" is a real clock.
- The source chain as the verb: who said it, where, when. Filing a board felt like a craft.
- The paper contradicting its own people without noticing: Oliver on the cards, the notice against Toby's line on the illness, "the paper cites the paper".
- Andy met alive on the shingle in case 1, then dead in the next file. That is the game's best structural trick, and it made his words weigh.
- The coroner catching my lazy "the two agree" logic in round 2.
- Hannah's dark lamp, a hand on the rail, the practising through the wall.

## 5. Where I nearly quit

After the first hearing. I wrote the whole account blind, was struck three times, and never learned what happened to Mary. "Adjourned" is a shrug. Second, the lane watch in round 1: four hours of chimney smoke. Third, the run of "I couldn't say" in round 5.

## 6. What was hollow

- I am a blank folder: no face, no name, no past, no companion. The only thing I can lose is a number.
- "I couldn't say" five or more times from different mouths. I can't tell a person who doesn't know from one who won't say. The willingness tags did not help. The "open" people (Oliver, Agnes, Olive, Hannah) stonewalled and the "guarded" one (Colin) gave me "cancer".
- The account is written before the findings and read as written. Colin's "cancer" and the split's reason changed nothing, and the check couldn't either.
- Standing rewards "?" and safety, and the hidden truth score rewards boldness. I optimised for not being struck, which is the wrong game.
- Q1 and Q3 (cause, answerable) stayed "?" in both cases, and the tenth hour says nobody can say who found him. A coroner's officer with no certificate is odd. If those questions can't be answered, don't ask them.
- "Never name the innocent" never came due. Nobody was ever tempting to name, so the best tension on the store page never played.
- I was scored on things I had no road to: the July 1997 hat round, and "Karen Holmes", a name I never met before the reveal (I met a Kate Holmes on the shore). Two misses were my own: the 1995 volume, where "Christmas Fence" was credited, and the 1971 café in Andy's own obituary, which I read as colour.

## 7. Which game does this better?

Return of the Obra Dinn: confirm in threes, and the whole timeline is a gift you piece together by looking. Disco Elysium gives the detective a voice and a body to examine. Her Story does testimony-hunting with no frozen account. Against my own earlier games: better than Saltlight (here I can lose something and the clock is real), thinner than the BG3 paper game, where I had a character, a secret and a dilemma.

## 8. What I would change

1. Give me a face, a name and one person at my side who argues with me.
2. Let me revise the account after my last turn, or put the check before I write it.
3. Show the real score as I go, or drop the second one. Don't let safety pay.
4. Make refusal readable: "doesn't know" and "won't say" should feel different, and the tags should predict them.
5. Let me ask one question at a time and react, instead of committing three before the first answer.
6. Give me a way at the cause: a certificate, a body, a person who found him. Or drop Q1 and Q3.
7. Let the tenth hour be a case: Sarah and the sawmill, with verbs.
8. Give a tempting wrong suspect, with a cost for naming them.

## 9. What I would lose if I stopped now

Almost nothing I own, which is the problem. Standing 3 of 6 does nothing, and there's no gear, no companion and no arc. I'd lose a 22-claim board and the answers to a few open threads: who found Andy, who Karen Holmes is, what happens to Sarah at the sawmill.

## 10. What I believe happened to Andy Shaw, Edward Baker and Sarah Lane

The tenth hour has told me part of this, so some of it is the reveal and not my deduction.

- **Andy Shaw:** a natural death, cancer from April 1996, with age weighing most. Nobody caused it. In hindsight, the stonewalling (Oliver, Olive, Hannah, Agnes, probably Thea and Toby Waller's careful "up to April '96") was the town shielding a private man's illness, not hiding a crime. I read it as guilt, and it was kindness. He took the band place as the better player and kept his head down.
- **Edward Baker:** he let people credit the best lines of Andy's song to him, a small vanity, not a crime. He later emptied his wallet in the hat round, and I read that as guilt.
- **Sarah Lane:** 21, impatient, shouted at Andy in the Anchor because she'd run out of patience. She's trying to be heard, not dangerous, and I believe nothing she did touched his body. The sawmill hints suggest she's trouble for other people.

All three are guilty of something small and social, and none of it killed him. That is the game's real answer, and it's a good one. I just didn't feel it until the list.
