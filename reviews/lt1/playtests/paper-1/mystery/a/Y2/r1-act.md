# Round 1

## Pull
6 — A hearing on a fixed date is a real clock, and "never name the innocent" sounds like a stake. But I am a blank folder with no face, no build and nobody at my side, so the pull is the case, not me.

## Thinking aloud
Okay. I'm the coroner's officer and I've got seven turns before I have to answer six questions in front of people. That part I like: a deadline, a limited check (one, and I only learn whether all three stand), and a standing score that can go down. That's a resource I'd spend carefully. What I don't have is any reason to be standing on this quay. Mary Tanner died on the church hill and I'm looking at a fishing crew and a gig poster. Nothing connects them yet, so either the game is dropping me in the town's busiest spot to meet people, or the poster matters. I'll treat it as a possible thread, not a fact.

The notice bothers me more. "Mary Tanner... wife of Mark Holmes." Different surnames. Could be a second marriage, could be she kept her name, could be the paper got it wrong, and question 6 asks if the printed record stands. "Died suddenly, of a heart attack" is also the line everyone believes, so I'll assume question 2 (since when was she unwell?) cuts against "suddenly". I need a doctor and the husband, but I'd rather read before I talk, since every question I ask is heard and hearsay is struck. Oliver offered me ten minutes, so I'll take him first. He's open, he's here, and he's in the poster. I'll keep my questions wide and listen for who he says to see.

I'm also unsure whether I write my own questions or pick from a list. If it's a list, say so, because that changes how much I can play this as my own detective.

## Prediction
Oliver will be warm, talk about Mary as a local fixture, and steer me towards the natural reading (old, weak heart, sudden). He will hold something back about the band or about who looked after her. He won't mention anyone's quarrel unless I ask a "who" question straight.

## Needs
- Who Mary's household is, and where on the church hill; I have only Mark Holmes's name.
- Why Tanner and Holmes differ; no map or town directory.
- Who the doctor was, and who signed for the heart attack.
- Whether I type my own questions or choose from a menu.
- What "hearsay" does to a claim in score terms, and what the standing is scored on.
- Why this case begins on the quay.

## Turns
1. Monday 3 March, quarter to six, the berth. Talk to Oliver Brook. (a) How did you know Mary Tanner? (b) When did you last see her, and how did she seem? (c) Who was close to her, and who would know how she was in her last weeks? — Expect: a friendly, partial answer and one or two names to follow.
2. Tuesday 4 March, morning, the Courier on Market Square. Read: the notice, back issues for Mary Tanner and Mark Holmes (marriage, earlier deaths, anything in her last months), and the Feb issues for the town's other news. — Expect: the Tanner/Holmes puzzle explained, or deepened. Nobody hears of a read.
3. Wednesday 5 March, afternoon, the Tanner/Holmes house on the church hill. Talk to Mark Holmes. (a) How was she in the weeks before she died? (b) Who saw her doctor, and who was with her that day? (c) Who stood by you both? If he's guarded I'll take only the one question about the day she died. — Expect: short answers, grief, maybe a flash of anger at being asked.
4. If the doctor or a neighbour is named and found, Thursday 6 March, I talk to them (cause of death, how long unwell). If not, I watch the church hill house on Thursday morning to see who comes and goes and whose usual place is empty.
