# Round 3

## What happened

**Before you cross the steps** (free). No. 13's door is shut. Nothing is pinned or chalked on it. The windows are dark and the curtains are open. At the foot of the steps the bakery's board is new paint over the window: BREAD, and below it the hours, six till two. It gives no name. The floors above it are a house, with its own door at the side.

**Asked why** (free). His death, 14 October: *"Age 85; his health had worn down."* (the record of his death). The notice's printing, 22 October: *Ask someone who was there.*

**Filed:** your act named no claims, so the board is still empty. Filing is free: name each claim and its source in your act.

**Turn 1 · Hannah Tanner's shed, the Gully Steps · Monday 26 October, a quarter to six**

She is at the bench under the lamp and doesn't get up. *Hannah Tanner, 83, retired; open.*

You: "When did you last see Andy Shaw, and how did he seem?"
Hannah: "The Tuesday, the thirteenth, half past five, going down for his walk. He was thin as a lath by then, with both hands on the rail, but he went. He'd not miss that walk for anything. I'd say it kept him on his feet longer than anyone would have given him."

You: "Who came and went at No. 13 in his last months?"
Hannah: "I don't keep a watch on his door. Him I can tell you about: down at half past five, back up by seven, the choir on a Thursday. And from the summer he'd play at night, once the steps had gone quiet. I'm up late myself, and I'd hear it from here."

You: "What did you see at No. 13 in the days around 14 October?"
Hannah: "The Tuesday night he was playing, late, same as ever. The Wednesday he never came down at half past five, and his window never lit. I heard on the Thursday he'd gone."

She goes back to the bench.

**Your prediction.** Hannah Tanner saw him go down for his walk on the Tuesday, the day before he died. She said nothing of anyone bringing him shopping.

**Turn 2 · The Courier, Market Square · Tuesday 27 October, morning**

*Ken Scott, 40, editor, the Courier; open*, at the counter: "Andy Shaw? Toby's piece and the notice were both last Thursday. The volumes are on the shelf, and this year's in the box file." At the back, *Toby Tanner, 41, reporter, the Courier; open*, is typing.

The bound volumes now run from June 1989 to the end of 1997. This year's issues are in the box file, the newest Thursday 22 October. You looked for every mention of Andy Shaw, Stella Shaw and Pirate Radio.

> **10 January 1991 · Ken Scott.** Oliver Brook, who skippers for the harbour fleet, and Peter Lane of the Old Bank Café have started a band. They call it Driftwood, and Andy Shaw, 77, has joined them.

> **23 May 1991 · Toby Tanner.** Driftwood played the Anchor for the first time on Friday and did well. Oliver Brook, Peter Lane and Andy Shaw kept the room for two sets. The best received was "Coming Home on a Sunday", the first song Peter Lane has ever written. They will be back.

> **16 June 1994 · Colin Berry.** Peter Lane, 36, has left the town for a job in the city. He has talked about the city for as long as anyone can remember, and now he has gone. Driftwood, the band he started with Oliver Brook, will go on without him, with Andy Shaw and Edward Baker.

> **16 March 1995 · Toby Tanner. Driftwood called back twice.** Friday's crowd at the Anchor would not let Driftwood go. "The Fence at the Clinic", Andy Shaw's song from last winter, had them called back twice, and the room sang the last chorus without them. Of the newer songs, "Christmas Fence" owes its best lines to Edward Baker, and it shows. Oliver Brook, Andy Shaw and Edward Baker have played together since Peter Lane left, and they have never sounded better.

> **21 September 1995 · Colin Berry.** Andy Shaw, 82, of Driftwood, took a spot and had a night to forget.

> **19 September 1996 · Colin Berry. Driftwood calls it a day.** Driftwood have split up after six years. People still talk about the night the room went quiet. It has been a hard year for Andy Shaw, who has been unwell since the spring, and the band has not been the same without him at his best. Sarah Lane, who joined in January, and Oliver Brook are expected to go on playing.

> **26 March 1998 · Colin Berry.** Neil Lyons, Oliver Brook and Sean Hale have started a band and call it Pirate Radio. And Olive Shaw, 67, has retired after ten years behind the bar at the Anchor.

> **25 June 1998 · Toby Tanner.** Pirate Radio have a new member: Andy Shaw, 84, last seen with Driftwood.

> **22 October 1998 · Toby Tanner. Andy Shaw, 1913 to 1998.** Andy Shaw died last week after a long illness. He was 85. Born in the town in 1913, he married Stella Shaw in 1957 and lost her in 1978. For twenty-six years he swam in the harbour every morning, whatever the weather, until illness kept him from the water. He sang with the choir on Thursdays for twenty-two years. In 1971 he tried to open a café in the old bank, and the council said no. In his late seventies he joined Driftwood and wrote some of the best songs this town has heard: "March Light", "The Fence at the Clinic", "No Ferry Tonight". In September he played the Anchor with Pirate Radio. Pirate Radio will go on without him.

The same issue carries his notice, the one in your file. Nothing in the volumes names Stella Shaw: they begin in 1989, and there is no notice of her death. Nothing else names Andy Shaw or Pirate Radio.

**Turn 3 · The Anchor, Harbour Row · Tuesday 27 October, half past three to six**

You have the stool at the end of the bar before they come. On the wall by the door:

> **PIRATE RADIO** · first night at the Anchor · Friday 18 September · Neil Lyons · Oliver Brook · Sean Hale · Andy Shaw · with "Letting Go on a Monday"

At half past three they come down from the church hill in twos and threes, in dark coats, and the room fills. At the long table under the window sit Karen Holmes, Trevor Lane, Ben Reed, Leo Lyons, Audrey Scott and Matt Sayer, with Olive Shaw beside Ben Reed. By the fire are Edward Baker, Toby and Grace Waller, and Hannah Tanner. Eddie Lane is at the bar, in garage overalls under his coat. Behind the bar are Evan Newman, Peter Scott and Agnes Tanner.

Under the poster, three men unpack two guitars and a drum: Neil Lyons, Oliver Brook and Sean Hale. Before they start, Neil comes to the bar for a glass of water and says to Evan Newman: "We'll do 'The Fence at the Clinic'. It had Driftwood called back twice in '95; I was in the room. That's Andy's song. Never mind what anybody's said about the other one."

They play "March Light" first, and the long table takes up the chorus in parts. Then they play "The Fence at the Clinic". Eddie Lane drinks up after it and goes back along the row towards the garage. Between songs, Agnes Tanner says to Peter Scott, drying glasses: "June, that was. Two of them went at him in here in one week, over his place in that band. Alice Tanner had it out with him, and then Sarah Lane shouted at him in front of everyone. Eighty-four, he was."

Then "No Ferry Tonight", and last "Christmas Fence". Olive Shaw comes to the bar for her second pint while they play it and says to Leo Lyons beside her: "Edward wrote the best of this one, whatever Andy said. He's earned a bit of credit, Edward."

All afternoon his name goes round the tables, mostly with the songs. At six the room is still full; nobody else has left. Nobody who keeps the afternoon here is missing. Olive Shaw drinks on this side of the bar now; she hasn't worked behind it since March.

*Hover* (all open): Karen Holmes, 74, retired · Trevor Lane, 71, retired · Ben Reed, 57, retired · Leo Lyons, 76, retired · Audrey Scott, 76, retired · Matt Sayer, 74, retired · Olive Shaw, 68, retired · Edward Baker, 83, retired · Toby Waller, 69, retired · Grace Waller, 72, retired · Hannah Tanner, 83, retired · Eddie Lane, 51, head mechanic, the garage · Evan Newman, 58, manager, the Anchor · Peter Scott, 46, bar staff, the Anchor · Agnes Tanner, 31, bar staff, the Anchor · Neil Lyons, 50, the sawmill · Oliver Brook, 42, skipper, the harbour fleet · Sean Hale, 47, the Old Bank Café.

**Turn 4 · The Anchor · Wednesday 28 October, afternoon**

The wake named nobody to you as close to him, so you waited for Olive Shaw, as your act said. She isn't in for the afternoon. At a quarter to six she comes in for her two pints and takes the table by the fire. *Olive Shaw, 68, retired; open.*

You: "When did you last see Andy Shaw, and how did he seem?"
Olive: "The night they played here, Friday the eighteenth of September. He'd had the cancer since the spring of '96. I heard that across the bar, when I was still working it. You could see it on him that night. They did well all the same, him too."

You: "Who was with him in his last weeks?"
Olive: "The band, I'd think: Neil Lyons, Oliver Brook and Sean Hale. He'd been with them since June."

You: "How were you related to him?"
Olive: "I couldn't say."

Your four acts were spent by Wednesday evening. Thursday 29 October to Friday 6 November go by.

## Round 4 · Monday 9 November 1998, eight in the morning · Your case file
