**Turn 1 · Trevor Lane's house · Monday 7 December, afternoon**

You ask along Harbour Row for Trevor Lane's house, and find him in the shed at the back with the door open, at a bench with a clock in pieces in front of him. *Trevor Lane, 71, retired; open.*

You: "How did you know Andy Shaw?"
Trevor: "I couldn't say."

You: "When did you last see him, and how did he seem?"
Trevor: "At choir, the Thursday before he went, the eighth. He sat for most of it, but he sang the lot, and he sang it well."

You: "What brings you to the Anchor on a Friday now?"
Trevor: "I've not missed a Friday since the start of November. Years, I'd not come in of a Friday. Andy would have laughed at me."

He picks up the screwdriver again.

**Your prediction.** Trevor Lane was open. How he knew Andy, he couldn't say. He didn't name the music when he spoke of his Fridays: he said Andy would have laughed at him. You didn't ask him who found Andy.

**Turn 2 · The Anchor, Harbour Row · Tuesday 8 December, evening**

Cards night. Toby Waller is at a table with Mona Sayer, Terry Sutton and Grace Waller, and turns his chair out between hands. *Toby Waller, 69, retired; open.*

You: "Why did Andy Shaw give up his morning swim?"
Toby: "He stopped coming down at New Year in '90. He never said why, not to me."

You: "How did he seem that winter?"
Toby: "Same as ever, right up to Christmas. In off the steps at seven, a towel round him, and up home."

You: "When did you last see him, and how did he seem?"
Toby: "The Friday they played here, in September. Thin. He played well, mind."

**Turn 3 · The Check · Wednesday 9 December**

You name claims 1 ("unwell since the spring of 1996"), 6 (Edward Baker wrote "The Fence at the Clinic") and 12 (he gave up his swim at New Year 1990).

**Not all three stand.**

As your act said, you drop all three from your board.

**Turn 4 · The hearing · Friday 11 December, ten o'clock, upstairs at the council office**

A long table, the coroner at its head, a clerk, a jug of water. Your act gave the account from the board, "not established" where you had no first-hand source, with claims 1, 6 and 12 dropped. So this is the account you gave:

1. Of what did he die? *Not established.*
2. Since when was he unwell? Claim (11), Neil Lyons: on 18 September he played every song, thin and tired by the end. Claim (5), Eve Vaughan: on 8 October he sat for most of choir, but sang. Claims (2) and (3), Hannah Tanner: on 13 October he went down for his walk, thin, both hands on the rail; on the 14th he didn't come down, and his window never lit.
3. Was anyone answerable? *Not established.*
4. Who stood by him and his household? Claim (10), Neil Lyons: he lived on his own, and Neil only saw him at the band.
5. Was there bad blood, did it bear on his death, and did anything settle it? Claim (8), Agnes Tanner saw both quarrels in June, from behind the bar. Claim (9), Sarah Lane: she shouted at him because Pirate Radio took him, not her, and nothing settled it.
6. Does the printed record stand? Claim (4), your own read: the Courier of 16 March 1995 gives the best lines of "Christmas Fence" to Edward Baker; the obituary names three songs as Andy's, and not that one. Claim (7), Ken Scott: the notice reached the Courier written out, and Toby's "long illness" came from it.

Those named in your account are summoned.

- Eve Vaughan: "At choir, the eighth. He sat for most of it." Claim (5) stands.
- Neil Lyons: "The eighteenth, every song. He lived on his own; I saw him at the band." Claims (10) and (11) stand.
- Ken Scott: "It came written out, and we set it as it came." Toby Tanner: "I had 'long illness' from the notice." Claim (7) stands.

- Agnes Tanner: "Both of them, from behind the bar." Claim (8) stands.
- Sarah Lane: "They took him, not me. Nothing settled it." Claim (9) stands.

The coroner: "Recorded. The account is accepted."

**Ruling: accepted.** No claim struck. Standing 5 of 6.

## After the hearing · 11 December 1998 to the end of January 1999

The close. Your account scored 25 of 100 on Andy Shaw: right on the June quarrels and that nothing settled them, partly right on the song credit, and open on the rest. What happened:
1. He died of cancer, found in April 1996, back in June 1997 and again in September 1998. The record of his death gives age 85 and his health worn down.
2. He was ill from the spring of 1996: the Courier of September 1996 had it right (your claim 1, dropped).
3. Nobody caused his death.
4. The hat went round for him at the Anchor on Friday 11 July 1997, when the cancer came back. Oliver Brook and Eddie Lane put in more than anyone, Edward Baker emptied his wallet, Grace Waller put in a note and not a small one, and Colin Berry the smallest coin he could find.
5. Karen Holmes and he made peace in July 1998, twenty-seven years after the café.
6. Alice Tanner and Sarah Lane each wanted the place in Pirate Radio he got in June 1998, and each went at him at the Anchor in front of everyone.
7. He took it and kept his head down; he didn't think it was about him. Nothing settled it.
8. "Christmas Fence" is his. Edward let people think its best lines were his own, and Andy found out. "The Fence at the Clinic" is Andy's too (your claim 6).
9. Driftwood split over its sound, with Oliver, Edward and Sarah each needing to be the one who mattered, not over his illness.
10. His swimming stopped in January 1990, six years before any illness (your claim 12, dropped). Nobody in the town could have told you why.

The obituary's three errors are the swim, the credit and the split. Your check held one wrong claim and two right ones.

Mary Tanner, as you asked: a sudden heart attack. She had survived strokes in December 1989 and June 1991, and had no illness before the death. Nobody was answerable, and her notice and the Courier stand. At the hat for Mark on 7 March 1997, Greta Brook put in most, Una Waller the smallest coin, and Leo Lyons had left his wallet at home.

Then twenty turns as you play them, on who found him, the songs and the swim. Oliver Brook talks about the songs, Toby Tanner about the swims of 1989. Nobody can say who found Andy. The town hears that your account was accepted; it named nobody, and nobody turns from you. Una Waller goes into hospital with pneumonia, and the hat goes round for her: Sam Brook and Lynn Newman put in most. Neil Lyons is found out at the sawmill, where he had covered up a mistake, and in January he walks out to start again somewhere else.

Nobody dies before the end of January 1999. No third case opens. Your notebook fills, and none of it goes to a hearing.
