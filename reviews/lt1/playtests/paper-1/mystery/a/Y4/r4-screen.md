**Turn 1 · The Courier, Market Square · Monday 9 November, morning**

*Ken Scott, 40, editor, the Courier; open*, comes to the counter.

You: "Who brought in Andy Shaw's notice of the 22nd, and on which day?"
Ken: "I couldn't say."

You: "Did they give you the words 'long illness' and 'as he asked', or did you write them?"
Ken: "It came written out, and we set it as it came. Neither of those is mine. Toby had his 'long illness' from it for his piece."

You: "Who told the Courier that Andy Shaw had died, and when?"
Ken: "Nobody told us as such. It was round the town by the Thursday. Toby said that morning he'd not seen him on the shore the night before."

**Your prediction.** Ken Scott couldn't say who brought the notice in. Nobody told the Courier he had died; the town had it by the Thursday.

**Turn 2 · The clinic · Monday 9 November, afternoon**

First, the fence (free): a white paling fence along the side of the clinic's yard, with a gate on a spring. Nothing is written on it.

*Eve Vaughan, 54, the clinic; open*, comes out to the front desk.

You: "When did you last see Andy Shaw, and how did he seem?"
Eve: "At choir, the Thursday before he went, the eighth. He sat down for most of it, but he sang."

You: "Do you know what he was ill with, and since when?"
Eve: "I couldn't say."

You: "Who looked after him at home, as far as you know?"
Eve: "It was said a year or two back that he leaned on people, and that they'd stopped minding, or stopped being there. Who, I couldn't tell you. My Neil played with him all summer; he'd know more than I would."

**Turn 3 · The Anchor, Harbour Row · Tuesday 10 November, evening**

Cards night. The poster is still by the door (free): no printer's line, and nothing says who put it up. "Letting Go on a Monday" stands under the four names with nobody's name against it.

At the table nearest the bar, Olive Shaw is dealing to Mark Holmes, Edward Baker and Leo Lyons. Between hands Edward turns his chair to you. *Edward Baker, 83, retired; open.*

You: "Who wrote 'The Fence at the Clinic'?"
Edward: "I wrote that one."

You: "Who wrote the words of 'Christmas Fence'?"
Edward: "Andy brought it to us. The best of the words are mine."

You: "When did you last see Andy Shaw, and how did he seem?"
Edward: "The night they played here, in September. He looked done in. He played well enough."

He turns back for the next hand.

**Turn 4 · The fleet's berths, then the Anchor · Wednesday 11 November**

In the morning the berths are empty; the boats went out at first light. So, as your act said, you went to the Anchor that evening. Oliver Brook is at the end of the bar where Agnes Tanner is working, with his first pint. *Oliver Brook, 42, skipper, the harbour fleet; open.*

You: "When did you last see Andy Shaw, and how did he seem?"
Oliver: "I couldn't say."

You: "How long had he been ill, and who looked after him?"
Oliver: "Since the spring of '96. It was the cancer."

You: "Who wrote 'March Light' and 'No Ferry Tonight'?"
Oliver: "I couldn't say."

Your four acts were spent by Wednesday evening. Thursday 12 to Friday 20 November go by.
