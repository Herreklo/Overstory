# Round 1

## Pull
7 — A real case with a small cast and a notice I already half-distrust. I want to see who talks and who doesn't.

## Thinking aloud
Three old people on one stair, and two of them share a name with the notice: Hannah Tanner (81) and Karen Holmes (72). Mary was a Tanner by name but "wife of Mark Holmes", so I guess Hannah is a sister or sister-in-law, and Karen is something on Mark's side. Mark himself is nowhere on this stair, which bothers me. The notice says "died suddenly", and then the coroner asks "since when was she unwell" and "does the printed record stand". That pair makes me suspect "suddenly" is the thing the town believes and the thing that's wrong. "No flowers; Mary would have said so herself" reads like someone else speaking for her, so I want to know who wrote it.

Andy Shaw interests me most, because he came out of a door above me and pulled it shut behind him like a man leaving an empty house, then went off to the quay. I can't talk to him now, but I'll inspect his door number for free and see which house it is. Hannah has something in a vice at 81, in a lit shed, three weeks after a death. I'll inspect that too before I speak to her, since looking is free and I'd rather spend my questions on what I can't see.

People hear of my questions, so I'll keep the first questions plain and not bring in names she hasn't given me. I won't ask anyone about each other yet.

## Prediction
Hannah Tanner will turn out to be Mary's older sister, short with me, and she'll say Mary hadn't been well for a good while before the "sudden" heart attack. Why: she's the only one of the three at work in the open with her door unlocked, and grief makes people short rather than silent.

## Needs
- Who Mark Holmes is, and where he is. Not on the steps, not named as present.
- Whether the house Andy Shaw left is occupied, and whose it is. The doors have only numbers.
- What is in Hannah's vice (inspecting it should be free).

## Turns
1. Talk to Hannah Tanner at her shed, Monday 3 March, evening (now). Ask: (a) How were you related to Mary Tanner? (b) When did you last see her, and how did she seem? (c) Who looked after her in her last months? — Expect: sister or sister-in-law, short answers, and "she wasn't herself for a while", with a name or two for who stood by her.
2. Talk to Karen Holmes at her kitchen, Monday 3 March, evening. Ask: (a) How did you know Mary? (b) When did you last see her? (c) What did you hear of how she died, and from whom? — Expect: her account will differ from Hannah's in who she says was close, and the third question gives me a source chain I can file with its source.
3. Read the Courier's bound volumes and this year's issues, Tuesday 4 March, morning, at Market Square. Look for Mary's notice and its date of printing, any earlier notice naming Mary Tanner and Mark Holmes (marriage, illness, a charity), and who gave the notice. — Expect: the record says "suddenly", and something earlier says otherwise; this one is silent, so it costs me no hearsay.
4. Talk to Andy Shaw, Tuesday 4 March, afternoon, wherever he is (the Anchor or the quay). Ask what the three of them tell me I should. If the Courier or the sisters name Mark Holmes' whereabouts, I'll go to him instead. — Expect: Andy is the guarded one; he'll give me one answer, so I'll spend it on where he was that Monday evening and whose house he left.
