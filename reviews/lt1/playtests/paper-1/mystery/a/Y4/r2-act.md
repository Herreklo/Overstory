# Round 2

## Marks
- Turn 1.1 (Hannah): engaged — she said flat out she was no kin, and volunteered "not her heart" unasked; my guess about her was wrong in a useful way. Surprise: delight — the first voice against the notice, by instinct only.
- Turn 1.2 (Karen): curious — she "couldn't say" she knew Mary, yet watched her boat go out in a gale; "same as everyone" shows where her news came from. Surprise: none.
- Turn 1.3 (Courier): engaged — the 1994 wedding notice (Kate Holmes, Vera, Jonah Scott) is free kinship. The volumes hold no trace of the strokes, though Hannah said "it was in the Courier". Surprise: delight.
- Turn 1.4 (Mark): confused, close to would-quit — I never wrote that question for him. I kept "where was he that Monday evening" for Andy Shaw, about the evening I saw him leave No. 13. Here it became an alibi demand on a widower at his door, and people hear of questions. Surprise: cheat — a question was put in my mouth, and a turn went on a shut door.

## Pull
7 — I still want whoever saw her die, but I will not be handed a question I didn't write again.

## Thinking aloud
Everything I hold about her death comes from the Courier. Hannah and Karen both say they barely knew her, then hand me the paper's own words back (the boat every weekend, the strokes, the heart attack). Yet she sat at the Anchor's card table every Tuesday for twenty-five years and the whole bar asked for her impression. Two women five minutes from the Anchor know her "to nod to"? Either grief makes them short, or they keep their distance for a reason. Nobody has given me a first-hand word on how she died or who found her. Karen's sighting puts her well enough to sail about 27 January, so "since when unwell" is a window of two weeks, and a gale followed by a "sudden" death makes me distrust "sudden". She "kept a shed she was seldom out of", and Hannah sits in a lit shed; I don't know whose it is. Karen is a Holmes and so is Mark's daughter; if they're kin, "not to talk to" is odd.

## Prediction
Toby Tanner will say he took the cause from the notice or from the family, not from a doctor. His piece repeats the notice's wording, so the printed record is one source printed twice.

## Needs
- Who placed the notice (the page doesn't say).
- Anyone who saw her die or found her; who her doctor was. I have no first-hand word on the cause.
- Whose shed Hannah works in, and where Mary's shed was.
- How Karen Holmes, Kate Holmes and Mark are related; who Mona Sayer is.
- The Courier of 6 March isn't in the box file. Is it on the counter?
- Does Check cost a turn? It sits among the acts.
- Please ask exactly the questions I write. For a guarded witness I'll number them in order and the first is asked.

## Turns
Free first. File on the board: (1) "Mary Tanner took her boat out past the harbour wall about 27 January, in heavy weather" — Karen Holmes, her own sighting, 3 March. (2) "The Courier's bound volumes 1989 to 1996 carry no notice of any illness of Mary Tanner" — my own read, 4 March. Ask why of: her death on 10 February, the notice's printing on 13 February, and Andy Shaw leaving No. 13.

1. Talk to Toby Tanner, the Courier, Monday 10 March, morning. (a) Where did you get the facts in your piece of the 20th, and who told you how she died? (b) Do you know who placed the notice of the 13th? (c) How did you know Mary? — Expect: "the family", no doctor named, and he doesn't know who placed the notice.
2. Watch the Anchor, Tuesday 11 March, evening (her card night). I look for who plays, whose chair is empty or taken, who says her name and how, who goes quiet, whether Hannah, Karen, Andy, Mark or Toby come in, and whether Mona Sayer is there. — Expect: a chair kept for her, one person who can't look at it, and a name for who really knew her.
3. Talk, Wednesday 12 March: whoever the Anchor points me to. If nothing better, Andy Shaw at No. 13 at half past five, when he left last time. (a) How long did you know Mary Tanner, and when did you last see her? (b) What did you see or hear of her in the last weeks? (c) Who was with her at the end? — Expect: open; he knew her and can name who was in the house.
4. The hearing, Friday 14 March: answer from the board. Where I have no first-hand source (the cause, so far) I will say "not established" rather than repeat the notice.
