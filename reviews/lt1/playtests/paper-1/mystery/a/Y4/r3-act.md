# Round 3

## Marks
- Turn 2.1 (Toby): engaged — he traced the notice to Mark Holmes, who brought it in himself. The cause of death now rests on one source, and Mark is the guarded man who shut the door on me. Surprise: delight — it is a thread I can follow, and nobody gave it to me whole.
- Turn 2.2 (the Anchor): engaged — the page says she played cards there on Tuesdays for twenty-five years. The Anchor says she has not sat in since December 1994. The printed record is stale, and I caught it by watching. Tara Myers stopped staying for her two pints in February 1997. That is grief, shown without a word. Surprise: delight.
- Turn 2.3 (Andy): curious — he and Toby both say she walked it brisk to the last week, which sits badly with "her health had worn down" from the record of her death. Nobody saw her die. Surprise: none.
- Turn 2.4 (the hearing): confused, nearly bored — I gave five "not established" and the coroner accepted it, and my standing went up. I am never told what killed her. If that passes, nothing I did counted, and I had nothing to be right against. Surprise: cheat — a rise for an empty account.

## Pull
8 — I walked down these steps with Andy Shaw and now he is dead. That is personal. The hearing left me cold, but the new case has a funeral date I can use.

## Thinking aloud
Free first: inspect No. 13's door and windows for any notice, and the new bakery's board. Ask why of his death on 14 October, and of the printing on 22 October.

The notice says the funeral is "Tuesday at two", so that is tomorrow, 27 October, thirteen days after he died. Mary's was six days. Why the wait? A wake at the Anchor with his song playing gathers the whole cast in one room, so I will watch it. Last case I filed two claims and gave the coroner nothing else. This time I file every first-hand sighting as it comes, with the speaker, because claims need a living witness to stand in the hearing. Hearsay gets struck.

I am not telling anyone he was ill. The notice says "long illness" and I want to hear what they volunteer. In March 1997 his coat hung loose on him. Today, nobody walks the quay.

## Prediction
Hannah Tanner will say Andy stopped coming down for his evening walk some weeks before he died, and that someone brought him shopping. Why: the screen takes care to say nobody turns along the quay, and Hannah, who notices things, works in a shed facing his door. She will not know who found him.

## Needs
- Who found him or was in the house on 14 October, and who his doctor was.
- Who placed this notice. When did Stella die?
- What "Pirate Radio" is, and who is credited with it.
- What the bakery is, and why the funeral came thirteen days after the death.
- What Mary Tanner actually died of. I would like that told after the case, or I will feel nothing counted.

## Turns
1. Talk to Hannah Tanner at her shed, Monday 26 October, evening, now. (a) When did you last see Andy Shaw, and how did he seem? (b) Who came and went at No. 13 in his last months? (c) What did you see at No. 13 in the days around 14 October? — Expect: she saw him thinner and indoors, a visitor with bags, nothing on the day.
2. Read the Courier, Market Square, Tuesday 27 October, morning. I look for every mention of Andy Shaw and Stella Shaw, the notice of Stella's death, anything about Pirate Radio (a song? a programme?), and the newest issue. — Expect: Stella died years ago, and the song has a credit that someone has been getting wrong.
3. Watch the Anchor, Harbour Row, Tuesday 27 October, afternoon, from the end of the funeral. I look for who comes, who stands near the radio, who leaves early, who is missing, and what is said about him. — Expect: a crowded room, one person who cannot stay for the song.
4. Talk to Olive Shaw at the Anchor, Wednesday 28 October, afternoon, unless the wake points to someone better (then that person). (a) When did you last see Andy Shaw, and how did he seem? (b) Who was with him in his last weeks? (c) How were you related to him? — Expect: kin, tired, with a name I do not yet have. If she is guarded, only (a) is asked.
