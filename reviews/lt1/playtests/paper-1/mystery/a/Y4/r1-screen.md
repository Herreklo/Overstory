# Round 1

## What happened

**Before you cross the steps** (free). Andy Shaw's door is No. 13: a plain painted door on the lane, shut. Its windows are dark. Nothing else is written on it.

**Turn 1 · Hannah Tanner's shed, the Gully Steps · Monday 3 March, a quarter to six**

The shed door stands open on the yard. In the vice is the works of a carriage clock, its brass case laid on a cloth beside it, and Hannah Tanner is easing out a screw with a jeweller's screwdriver. She looks up when your shadow crosses the bench and keeps the screwdriver where it is. *Hannah Tanner, 81, retired; open.*

You: "How were you related to Mary Tanner?"
Hannah: "I wasn't. There's Tanners all over this town, and half of us no kin to each other. I knew her to nod to, that's all."

You: "When did you last see her, and how did she seem?"
Hannah: "I couldn't give you a day. I'd see her about, and she looked well enough to me for her age. She'd come through two strokes, it was in the Courier, and she was still out in that boat of hers every weekend. I'd have said it would be another stroke that took her, if anything did. Not her heart."

You: "Who looked after her in her last months?"
Hannah: "Looked after? I never heard she needed looking after, not since the strokes, and that's years back. She had her husband. Mark Holmes, up on the church hill."

She goes back to the screw.

**Turn 2 · Karen Holmes's door, one house up · Monday 3 March, six o'clock**

Karen Holmes dries her hands, opens the door no wider than she needs and stays on the step, the kitchen light behind her. *Karen Holmes, 72, retired; open.*

You: "How did you know Mary?"
Karen: "I couldn't say I did. Not to talk to."

You: "When did you last see her?"
Karen: "Her boat going out past the harbour wall, a fortnight before or thereabouts, in weather I wouldn't hang a dishcloth out in. I said to myself, there's a woman who'll see us all out. Shows what I know."

You: "What did you hear of how she died, and from whom?"
Karen: "A heart attack, sudden. I read it in the Courier, the notice, same as everyone."

She waits to see if there is more, says goodnight and shuts the door.

**Turn 3 · The Courier, Market Square · Tuesday 4 March, morning**

A front office with a counter; a typewriter going in the back room. Behind the counter, *Ken Scott, 39, editor, the Courier; open*, is reading proofs; at the back, *Toby Tanner, 39, reporter, the Courier; open*, types and doesn't look up. When you say what you're after, Ken lifts the counter flap: "If it's Mary Tanner, the notice was in the thirteenth and Toby's piece in the twentieth. Volumes on the shelf; this year's in the box file."

The bound volumes run from Thursday 15 June 1989 to the end of 1996. The 1997 issues are loose in a box file, the newest Thursday 27 February. You looked for every mention of Mary Tanner and Mark Holmes.

> **Deaths · Thursday 13 February 1997.** TANNER, Mary. Died suddenly, of a heart attack, aged 77. Wife of Mark Holmes. The funeral is on Wednesday at eleven. No flowers; Mary would have said so herself.

The notice does not say who placed it.

> **Mary Tanner · Thursday 20 February 1997 · by Toby Tanner.** Mary Tanner died suddenly last week of a heart attack. She was 77. She married Mark Holmes in 1981. She had her own boat from 1962 and was out on it every weekend, kept a shed she was seldom out of, played cards at the Anchor on Tuesdays for twenty-five years, and did an impression of Mona Sayer that the whole of the Anchor used to ask for. She had two strokes, in 1989 and 1991, and came through both.

> **Married · Thursday 15 September 1994.** Jonah Scott, 45, of the harbour fleet, and Vera Holmes, 46, daughter of Kate Holmes and Mark Holmes, were married on Saturday.

Nothing else in the volumes names Mary Tanner or Mark Holmes: no notice of the 1981 marriage (the volumes start in 1989), no notice of an illness, no appeal.

**Turn 4 · Mark Holmes's door, the church hill · Tuesday 4 March, afternoon**

Hannah had said where Mark Holmes lives, so, as your act said, you went to him instead of Andy Shaw. Missing from your act: a question for Mark Holmes. You put the one you had kept for a guarded witness.

A house on the church hill, its front door on the street. Mark Holmes opens it himself. *Mark Holmes, 76, retired; guarded.* "You'll be from the coroner."

You: "Where were you that Monday evening?"
Mark: "Not today."

He shuts the door.

**Your prediction.** Hannah Tanner is no kin of Mary's, and she wasn't short with you. She said she never heard Mary needed looking after since the strokes, "years back".

Your four acts were spent by Tuesday afternoon. Wednesday 5 to Friday 7 March go by.

## Round 2 · Monday 10 March 1997, eight in the morning · Your case file

**Mary Tanner, 77.** Died Monday 10 February 1997, at home on the church hill.
**The hearing:** Friday 14 March at ten, upstairs at the council office on Market Square.
**Your days:** Monday 10 to Thursday 13 March, three turns. **Turn 4 is the hearing:** give your account there, answering the six questions, each claim with its source.
**Standing:** 3 of 6. **Check:** one this case, open now. **Board:** no claims filed.

**The coroner's questions:** (1) Of what did she die? (2) Since when was she unwell? (3) Was anyone answerable for her death? (4) Who stood by her and her household? (5) Was there bad blood, and did it bear on her death? (6) Does the printed record of her death and life stand?

**Your journal** keeps each statement word for word (under each turn above):
- Mon 3 March, about a quarter to six: Hannah Tanner, at her shed on the Gully Steps, three statements.
- Mon 3 March, about six: Karen Holmes, at her door on the Gully Steps, three statements.
- Tue 4 March, morning: Ken Scott, at the Courier, one statement.
- Tue 4 March, afternoon: Mark Holmes, at his door on the church hill, two statements.

**Seen by you.** Mon 3 March, half past five, the Gully Steps: Andy Shaw came out of No. 13, went down the steps past you, one hand on the rail, and turned along the quay towards the shore. His windows were dark after.

**People** (hover): Andy Shaw, 83, retired, open · Hannah Tanner, 81, retired, open · Karen Holmes, 72, retired, open · Mark Holmes, 76, retired, guarded · Ken Scott, 39, editor, the Courier, open · Toby Tanner, 39, reporter, the Courier, open.

**Places you know:** the Gully Steps (No. 13, Hannah Tanner's shed, Karen Holmes's house); Harbour Row (the café at the foot of the steps, the Anchor); the quay and the shore; Market Square (the Courier, the council office); Mark Holmes's house on the church hill.

## What you can do

Three turns, then the hearing. Say for each where you go, whom you see, the day and the time of day.
- **Talk** (up to three questions; guarded, one a visit; closed, none) · **Watch** (one place for a morning, an afternoon or an evening; nobody hears of it) · **Read** (the Courier's volumes and this year's issues; any notice or poster where it hangs) · **Wait** (a day).
- **Check** (once this case): name three claims on your board; you learn only whether all three stand.
- **Give the account** (turn 4, the hearing): answer the six questions from your board, each claim with its source.

Free, and not a turn: walking within an act; inspecting a person or a thing; filing a claim with its source; asking why of an event you have found.
