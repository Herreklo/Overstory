# Round 2

## What happened

**Filed** (free): (1) "Mary Tanner took her boat out past the harbour wall about 27 January, in heavy weather" — Karen Holmes, her own sighting, 3 March. (2) "The Courier's bound volumes 1989 to 1996 carry no notice of any illness of Mary Tanner" — your own read, 4 March.

**Asked why** (free). Her death, 10 February: *"Age 77; her health had worn down."* (the record of her death). The notice's printing, 13 February: *Ask someone who was there.* Andy Shaw leaving No. 13: *Ask someone who was there.*

**Turn 1 · The Courier, Market Square · Monday 10 March, morning**

Ken Scott is at the counter and waves you through. Toby Tanner turns his chair round from the typewriter. *Toby Tanner, 39, reporter, the Courier; open.*

You: "Where did you get the facts in your piece of the 20th, and who told you how she died?"
Toby: "The heart attack I had from the notice, same as everyone. The rest is what the town knows: the boat, the cards, the impression. The strokes I knew of at the time, '89 and '91. Two strokes and then the heart: I'd say the strokes took more out of her than she let on."

You: "Do you know who placed the notice of the 13th?"
Toby: "Mark Holmes brought it in himself, the day after. Ken took it at the counter."

You: "How did you know Mary?"
Toby: "From the shore. I walk it every evening, and this last year she did too; most evenings we'd pass. She was out there the week before she died, walking it like always. Whatever took her came quick."

**Your prediction.** Toby Tanner says he had the cause from the notice.

**Turn 2 · The Anchor, Harbour Row · Tuesday 11 March, evening**

At the tables: Olive Shaw dealing to Mark Holmes, Edward Baker and Leo Lyons, nearest the bar; Mona Sayer, Terry Sutton, Toby Waller and Grace Waller; Ivan Tanner, Molly Scott, Steve Baker and Eve Vaughan; Larry Baker, Tara Myers, Colin Berry and John Holmes; Sally Baker and Ellie Eden two-handed by the window. At the bar: Gary Myers, Oliver Brook, Lynn Newman. Behind it: Evan Newman, Peter Scott, Agnes Tanner.

Mark Holmes plays every hand, a pint at his elbow, and says little. Oliver Brook stays at the end of the bar where Agnes Tanner is working, and they talk between orders. When a glass goes over at the window, Agnes is there with a cloth before anyone calls.

At ten past seven an old man passes the front window, slowly: Andy Shaw, back along Harbour Row from the shore.

Later, at the far end of the bar, Evan Newman to Gary Myers: "The hat for Mark Holmes. Greta put in more than anyone. Una Waller found the smallest coin in her purse again. She does it every time."

No chair is kept for anyone. Whose usual place is empty: *Mary Tanner hasn't sat in on a Tuesday since December 1994. Tara Myers no longer stays for her two pints most evenings, since February 1997.*

Not there: Hannah Tanner, Karen Holmes, Andy Shaw (except passing), Toby Tanner. Mona Sayer is.

**Turn 3 · The Gully Steps, No. 13 · Wednesday 12 March, half past five**

The Anchor named nobody to you as close to her, so you waited for Andy Shaw. At half past five his door opens. His coat hangs loose on him. *Andy Shaw, 83, retired; open.* "I don't stop, but you can walk down with me."

You: "How long did you know Mary Tanner, and when did you last see her?"
Andy: "To nod to, all my life. To talk to, only this last year, on the shore. She took to walking it of an evening, the spring before last, the same hour as me. The last time was the week before she died."

You: "What did you see or hear of her in the last weeks?"
Andy: "Walking it brisk, every evening, whatever the weather. She'd be past me before I was at the slip. Never stopped for breath that I saw."

You: "Who was with her at the end?"
Andy: "I couldn't tell you that. She walked it on her own. I never saw anyone with her down there."

At the foot of the steps he turns along the quay, and you let him go.

**Turn 4 · The hearing · Friday 14 March, ten o'clock, upstairs at the council office**

A long table, the coroner at its head, a clerk, a jug of water. Your act gave the account "from the board"; the board held the two claims you filed, so those are what you gave.

1. Of what did she die? *Not established.*
2. Since when was she unwell? Claim (1), Karen Holmes's sighting; claim (2), your own read of the volumes.
3. Was anyone answerable? *Not established.*
4. Who stood by her and her household? *Not established.*
5. Was there bad blood, and did it bear on her death? *Not established.*
6. Does the printed record stand? *Not established.*

Karen Holmes is summoned, as named in your account. She stands, doesn't look round, and says: "Her boat going out, a fortnight before or thereabouts. That's all I saw." Claim (1) stands. Claim (2) stands.

The coroner: "Recorded. The account is accepted."

**Ruling: accepted.** No claim struck. Standing 4 of 6.

## Nineteen months later

## The Gully Steps · Monday 26 October 1998, half past five

It is dark already, and the lamps on the steps are lit. You stand on the half-landing where the steps turn.

## Your case file

**Andy Shaw, 85.** Died on Wednesday 14 October 1998, at home on the Gully Steps.

His notice, cut from the Courier of Thursday 22 October:
> **SHAW, Andy.** Died after a long illness, aged 85. Husband of the late Stella. The funeral is on Tuesday at two. Pirate Radio will play at the Anchor afterwards, as he asked.

**The coroner's questions:** (1) Of what did he die? (2) Since when was he unwell? (3) Was anyone answerable for his death? (4) Who stood by him and his household? (5) Was there bad blood, did it bear on his death, and did anything settle it? (6) Does the printed record of his death and life stand?

**The hearing:** Friday 11 December at ten, upstairs at the council office.
**Your days:** round 3 is Monday 26 October to Friday 6 November, four turns. Round 4 is 9 to 20 November; round 5, 23 November to 4 December; round 6, 7 to 11 December: three turns, then the hearing.
**Standing:** 4 of 6. **Check:** one this case, from round 4; it takes a turn. **Board:** no claims filed for this case. **Journal:** everything from the last case is kept.
