# Exit interview: *Answerable* (Y4)

## 1. Marks, round 6
- **Turn 6.1 (Trevor):** curious — "Andy would have laughed at me" is the first line in the whole game that sounds like grief, and the changed Friday habit is the one I predicted from the screens. He also gave me a second eyewitness to choir on the 8th. But "I couldn't say" how he knew a man he had sung beside for years is another wall. Surprise: delight — the Fridays. (The clock in pieces on a bench echoes Hannah's carriage clock. It reads as a reused prop, not a clue.)
- **Turn 6.2 (Toby Waller):** engaged — "Same as ever, right up to Christmas" puts the end of the swim six years before any illness, from a man who watched it. "He never said why, not to me" leaves a hole where a person should be. Surprise: delight — the obituary is wrong in a way a neighbour can prove.
- **Turn 6.3 (the Check):** confused — "Not all three stand" and nothing more. My act said to drop all three if it failed, so I threw away two sound claims on a gamble. I chose the triple badly: two hopeful claims and one contested one, so a no told me nothing. Surprise: cheat — not the game master, the rule: one bad claim costs the other two and I can't learn which.
- **Turn 6.4 (the hearing):** bored — accepted again. Every summoned witness read my filed line back to me and every claim stood, because I only filed what I was told first-hand. Standing went from 4 to 5. The close then scored the same account 25 of 100. Surprise: cheat — the ruling and the score disagree, so "accepted" means nothing.

## 2. Would the tenth hour still hold you?
Not as it is. The first hours are a good discovery: the Courier, the empty chairs, the overheard lines. By the tenth I would have learned that the loop is the same twice (read, watch, ask three things, wall, hearing), that "I couldn't say" is the commonest answer in town, and that the hearing pays nothing for deduction. My pull ran 7, 8, 8, 7, 7 in the second case, and it was sinking. A third case would hold me only if I knew my score mattered. None opens in the next three months, so my notebook "goes to no hearing".

## 3. Keep playing? Buy it? Come back in a week?
Keep playing: yes, for one more case, to see whether the score can be earned. Buy it: not yet, I would wishlist it. Come back after a week: probably, for the town and not for the cases. I want to see who dies next, and whether the Anchor's chairs have changed.

## 4. What kept me
- Walking down the steps with Andy Shaw in March 1997 and then finding him dead nineteen months on, with the quay empty at his hour. The best beat in the game.
- Dated empty places: Mary not sat in at the Anchor since December 1994, Tara Myers not staying for her pints since February 1997. Grief shown with no one saying it.
- The Courier's archive. I found a dispute by reading: Peter Lane's one song, Edward's credit, Driftwood's rise and fall, three songs the obituary names.
- Lines overheard unasked at the wake, and the hat for Mark (Una Waller's smallest coin "every time"), which the close paid off when the hat went round for her.
- From round 2 on, my questions were asked as I wrote them, in order.

## 5. Where I nearly quit
- Round 1, Mark Holmes. A question I had kept for Andy was put in my mouth, at a widower's door, and a turn went on a shut door.
- End of case 1. Five "not established" were accepted and my standing rose. If that passes, nothing I did counted.
- Round 4, Oliver Brook: "open", then "I couldn't say" twice from a bandmate.

## 6. What was hollow
- **Standing.** It rose for two near-empty accounts and the truth score (25 of 100) came only at the very end.
- **"I couldn't say."** I count nine, from eight people, on things they plainly know: how Trevor knew Andy, how Olive was kin, who wrote Andy's songs, what each quarrel was about. Agnes said the quarrel's reason at the wake, then "couldn't say" to me, so the wall is false. "Open" and "guarded" do not predict anything. Kinship is invisible: after two cases I have no family tree.
- **No one to ask the first and third questions.** No doctor, no one who found the body, no one who laid him out. "Of what did he die" and "was anyone answerable" cannot be worked in the field. "Nobody can say who found Andy" is the world declining to answer, not a puzzle.
- **The close is an answer key.** It is useful, but it is telling, a numbered list of what I was not allowed to find.
- **Ask why.** It gives the engine's own reasons for free (the Driftwood split, why he joined Pirate Radio), the truest lines in the game. I couldn't tell if they could be filed or heard, so I never used them.

## 7. Which game does this better?
Return of the Obra Dinn confirms in threes, but writing a fate is free and a wrong set costs nothing. Here the Check costs a turn and a loss. Pentiment does records written by fallible people, and years that carry consequences, better. Outer Wilds makes knowledge the only progress, and each find opens the next. Against Saltlight, this is better: it gives me something to be wrong against, even if late.

## 8. What I would change
1. Tie standing to the truth, show the per-question result after each hearing, and do not let "not established" ×5 pass.
2. The Check should say how many of the three stand, and a failed check should not strip my board.
3. Make "couldn't say" mean something: didn't know, won't say, wasn't there. A refusal with a reason is data.
4. Put someone in town who can speak to the first question (the doctor, the one who found him).
5. Tell me whether an "asked why" line can be filed and heard.
6. Show kin somewhere cheap: a family line on hover, or a stone in the churchyard.

## 9. What would I lose if I stopped now?
Little I could not re-read in an afternoon. The notebook, the standing of 5. What I'd miss is the wanting to know: whose song "Letting Go on a Monday" is, who wrote "March Light", what the 1971 café was between Karen and Andy (the close mentions it; I never asked), what Alice Tanner would say, and who dies next.

## 10. What I believe happened
**Andy Shaw.** He died of cancer, at home on the Gully Steps, alone, on or just after Tuesday 13–Wednesday 14 October, and nobody caused it. Oliver Brook told me plainly "Since the spring of '96. It was the cancer", and I did not file it, an error of mine; I read that turn as a wall and missed the one real line in it. Olive's "across the bar" version, the Courier of 1996 and Eve's choir sighting agree, and Hannah's sightings fit: walked on the 13th, played late, window dark on the 14th. He lived alone (Neil) and people "stopped being there" (Eve). The obituary is wrong where the town misremembers: the swim stopped in 1990 for a reason nobody knew, not illness, and the song credit.

**Edward Baker.** He took credit for Andy's songs. The 1995 Courier gave him the best lines of "Christmas Fence"; he told me to my face he wrote "The Fence at the Clinic" (the close says it is Andy's, so claim 6 was false); at the hearing he said "I let people think the best of it was mine." I think he is vain, not wicked, and Andy knew and kept quiet. Olive's "he's earned a bit of credit" puts her on Edward's side, which is a guess. The bad blood is real but did not bear on the death.

**Sarah Lane.** A 21-year-old at the sawmill who thought the Pirate Radio place was hers because she'd played with Oliver, and shouted at him in June in front of the room. She owns it plainly and says nothing settled it. She was the most honest witness in the game. I believe her, and I believe she had nothing to do with his death.
