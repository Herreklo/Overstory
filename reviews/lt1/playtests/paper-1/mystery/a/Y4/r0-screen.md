# Round 0

**Store page.** *Answerable.* A harbour town, 1997. Every death here is heard. You are the coroner's officer. Before each hearing you have days to find out what happened, and whether anyone was answerable. Nobody is murdered. But the dead leave people behind, each telling it their own way: a song credited to the wrong man, a quarrel at the pub, an obituary everyone believes. Every question you ask is heard. Grief makes people short with you. Give an account that holds, and never name the innocent. Then the record shows what you got right. Two deaths, a year apart and more, in a town that remembers you.

**Tutorial.** A turn is one act: visit one person and ask up to three questions, watch a place at a time, read records, or wait. Your journal keeps everything you are told, by whom, where and when. File claims on your board with their sources. People hear of your questions; the coroner strikes hearsay. Once a case you may check three claims, and you learn only whether all three stand. Your standing starts at 3.

## Your case file

You are the district's coroner's officer. By custom every death in the district is heard, and the coroner asks the same six questions each time. This is your next case.

**Mary Tanner, 77.** Died on Monday 10 February 1997, at home on the church hill.

Her notice, cut from the Courier of Thursday 13 February:
> **TANNER, Mary.** Died suddenly, of a heart attack, aged 77. Wife of Mark Holmes. The funeral is on Wednesday at eleven. No flowers; Mary would have said so herself.

**The coroner's questions,** to be answered at the hearing:
1. Of what did she die?
2. Since when was she unwell?
3. Was anyone answerable for her death?
4. Who stood by her and her household?
5. Was there bad blood, and did it bear on her death?
6. Does the printed record of her death and life stand?

**The hearing:** Friday 14 March at ten, upstairs at the council office on Market Square.
**Your days:** round 1 is Monday 3 to Friday 7 March, four turns. Round 2 is Monday 10 to Friday 14 March: three turns, then the hearing as the fourth.

Standing: 3 of 6. Check: one this case, from round 2. Board: no claims filed. Journal: empty.

## The Gully Steps · Monday 3 March 1997, half past five

The Gully Steps climb from Harbour Row up to the Landing: worn stone steps between houses built into the slope, an iron handrail on the uphill side, a lamp on a bracket at every second turn. The lamps have just come on. You stand on the half-landing where the steps turn.

**Who is here.**
- A door opens a few steps above you. An old man in a dark coat comes out, pulls it shut behind him and comes down past you, one hand on the rail. He nods to you and goes on down: **Andy Shaw** (83, retired; open).
- Across the steps, the shed at the back of a yard has its light on and its door open. **Hannah Tanner** (81, retired; open) is at the bench inside, under a lamp, with something held in a vice.
- One house up, a kitchen window is lit. **Karen Holmes** (72, retired; open) is at the sink, her back to the window.

**What you can see and read.**
- The houses on the steps have their doors on the lane and their yards behind. Nothing is written on them but their numbers.
- At the foot of the steps, where they meet Harbour Row, the corner house has a café on its ground floor, its lights on.
- From the half-landing you see over the roofs to the basin: the boats in along the quay, the lit windows of the Anchor on Harbour Row, Market Square at the head of the water. The church hill rises on the far side of the town.

Below you, Andy Shaw reaches Harbour Row and turns along the quay towards the shore.

## What you can do

A round is four turns. Write up to four acts, one a turn, in order, and say for each where you go, whom you see, and on which day and at what time of day.
- **Talk:** visit one person and ask up to three questions. Someone guarded answers one question a visit. Someone closed will not talk to you.
- **Watch:** one place for a morning, an afternoon or an evening. Nobody hears of a watch. You see who comes and goes, what happens there, and whose usual place is empty.
- **Read:** the Courier's bound volumes and this year's issues, at the Courier on Market Square, and any notice or poster where it hangs.
- **Wait:** let a day go by.
- **Check** (from round 2, once this case): name three claims on your board. You learn only whether all three stand.
- **Give the account** (at the hearing): answer the six questions from your board, each claim with its source.

Free at any time, and not a turn: walking between places within an act; inspecting a person or a thing (hover shows a name, age, work and willingness); filing a claim on your board with its source; asking why of an event you have found (the file gives a reason only where the record states one).

Your journal keeps every statement word for word: who said it, where and when.
