## From memory
- Hannah Tanner: 83, retired, a bench and a carriage clock in a vice. Said she was no kin to Mary and that it would not have been her heart. Saw Andy go down on the 13th, window dark on the 14th. Hears him play at night from her shed.
- Karen Holmes: 74, at her sink with her back to the window. "Couldn't say I knew" Mary, but saw her boat go out in a gale. Her news came from the Courier. At the wake, at the long table.
- Andy Shaw: 85, dead. No. 13, loose coat. Walked the shore with Mary on his own account, said she never stopped for breath. Choir, songs, Driftwood, Pirate Radio. A night to forget in 1995. Wife Stella, d. 1978.

## Turns
1. Talk to Ken Scott, the Courier, Monday 9 November, morning. (a) Who brought in Andy Shaw's notice of the 22nd, and on which day? (b) Did they give you the words "long illness" and "as he asked", or did you write them? (c) Who told the Courier that Andy Shaw had died, and when? — Expect: a bandmate, no family.
2. Talk to Eve Vaughan, the clinic, Monday 9 November, afternoon. First I look at the fence. (a) When did you last see Andy Shaw, and how did he seem? (b) Do you know what he was ill with, and since when? (c) Who looked after him at home, as far as you know? — Expect: careful with a patient's name; if guarded, (a) only.
3. Talk to Edward Baker, the Anchor, Tuesday 10 November, evening, at his cards. (a) Who wrote "The Fence at the Clinic"? (b) Who wrote the words of "Christmas Fence"? (c) When did you last see Andy Shaw, and how did he seem? — Expect: he wrote more than he was credited with, and says it quietly.
4. Talk to Oliver Brook, the quay by the fleet's berths, Wednesday 11 November, morning (if the fleet is out, the Anchor that evening). (a) When did you last see Andy Shaw, and how did he seem? (b) How long had he been ill, and who looked after him? (c) Who wrote "March Light" and "No Ferry Tonight"? — Expect: ill, but he played to the end; "March Light" is not simply Andy's.
