# Y2 memory

## A paper version of Baldur's Gate 3 (Act One, Hob's Ford)
Played as Tav, half-elf Bard, with Astarion, Karlach and Shadowheart. Three rounds plus the exit.

**Marks and pull.** Mostly engaged throughout, with delight at Maud's "shape of a step", Tobin's confession and the ledger beat. One cheap mark (the easy delay). Pull started at 7, rose to 8 and stayed there.

**What kept me.** Tobin's secret: the head wound was an accident, the gate was the crime, and the law hangs for the gate. Knowledge split across the party (only I know, only Astarion and I know). The clue (the wound's shape) tested a claim later. The bribe offered at the same moment as the ledger.

**What was hollow.** Rolls with no risk (26 vs DC 14; I never spent Inspiration or a spell). A clock that costs nothing because walking is free and the hanging waits on Pell. Companions only comment, never push back. The menu shows the rails. Hedda handed over the moot as a free solution. The villain was who the screen pointed at.

**Compared with other games.** This is the first game I have played, so nothing to compare yet.

## Saltlight (Chapter One: Arrivals, Wrenmouth, 1994)
Gentle exploration, $14.99. Three rounds plus the exit.

**Marks and pull.** Curious early, then bored and confused in the middle (the cardigan blocked, Sal's flat answers, an afternoon on the bench), delight at Wren Steps and the lantern walk's two rows of lights. Pull 5, 5, 4, ending at 4.

**What kept me.** The fabric of the town: worn treads, a handrail polished in the middle, layered paint, new tiles meeting old. Teddy's "just rope" and the stopped calendar. Admiral the crab.

**What was hollow.** Nothing at stake, so nothing to win. Belonging rose +16, +16, +24 whatever I did. Mysteries dissolved in a line. My own words were replaced by menu lines. The journal told me my feelings. The one event ran as a cutscene.

**Compared with other games.** Against the BG3 paper game it was thin: there I held a secret, a clock and a dilemma, even though the rolls were easy. Here I held nothing. Would not buy at $14.99; I'd try it at $4.99.

## Answerable (a coroner's officer in a harbour town, 1997 to 1998)
Two cases (Mary Tanner, Andy Shaw), six rounds plus the exit.

**Marks and pull.** Pull sat at 6 every round and never moved. Engaged by source chains (who said it, where, when), the paper contradicting its own people, Hannah's dark lamp, a hearing deadline and one check. Bored by a stonewalling widower, an empty lane watch and empty volumes. Nearly quit after the first hearing.

**What kept me.** Meeting Andy alive in case 1, then dead in case 2. The coroner catching my lazy logic.

**What was hollow.** I am a blank folder: no face, no companion, nothing to lose but a number. "I couldn't say" five times, and the open/guarded tags did not predict who talked. The account is frozen before the findings, so Colin's "cancer" changed nothing. Standing rewards "?"; the hidden score was 15 of 100. Cause of death was unanswerable. The tenth hour had threads and no verbs.

**Compared with other games.** Better than Saltlight (a clock, something to lose); thinner than the BG3 paper game on character and dilemma. Nearest to Obra Dinn's check-three, but with no face to look at. About $9.99 on sale.
