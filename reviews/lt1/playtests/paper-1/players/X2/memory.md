# X2's memory

## Game 1: a paper version of Baldur's Gate 3 (Act One, Hob's Ford)
A farm boy is to hang for smashing the mill's sluice, and the miller's apprentice lies hurt.

- **Marks:** mostly engaged. Bored at a dead Insight failure. Delight at "Tobin, *run*", at the Persuasion win that moved the problem instead of solving it, and at Karlach's camp talk. Cheat when the witness woke offscreen. Pull 7, 8, 7; overall 7, and 9 to play on.
- **What kept me:** companion voices and approval. Failures that turned into evidence. "Nobody hit him" overturned my whodunit and left a problem with the system itself (weir, moot, law). It ended on a dilemma where every choice costs someone.
- **Hollow:** three failures that gave nothing. Three turns for four people, with walking costing a turn. My bard sat idle. The clock went soft. The opening gave the motive away. Tav had no thread of their own. The climax was narrated to me afterwards.
- **Compared:** this is my first game, so it's the baseline. Strong on writing and companions, weak on turn economy and on letting me be present for the key scene.

## Game 2: Saltlight ("a gentle exploration game", Wrenmouth, autumn 1994)
You're a lodger in a harbour town, with no goals and no fail states.

- **Marks:** curious at first (Wilf's boat, the calendar stopped in 1988, the Pennock house's paint). Then bored, and cheat each time a hook was defused ("just rope", "a visitor's", "a catalogue"), and each time my own words were swapped for menu lines. Would quit at the Hales. Pull 4, 3, 1; overall 2. I'd have quit at round 2.
- **What kept me:** places that tell their history (worn steps, paint layers, a FOR SALE card), the Canada letters, and lovely prose.
- **Hollow:** nobody wants anything. No stakes, no choices, no consequences. Belonging and room gifts come whatever I do. No reason to be me.
- **Compared:** the opposite of Hob's Ford. The writing is as good, but BG3's people wanted things and its failures left evidence. Here every question closed a door. Lesson: a beautiful place with no one who needs anything isn't a game to me.
