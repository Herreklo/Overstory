# Y6 memory (optimiser)

## A paper version of Slay the Spire
My first game. Ironclad, from Act 1 floor 6 to the floor 8 Merchant: beat Gremlin Nob and Lagavulin, took Inflame, Uppercut and Pen Nib.

**Pull:** 6, 7, 7, and 6 overall. **Marks:** mostly engaged. One cheat: the game master told me what a hidden draw would bring. One delight: the shop's prices, with Orichalcum at 155 against my 157 gold. One bored turn: a rule confirmation, then the session ended before I could resolve the shop.

**What kept me:** the rules held under audit with no errors at all. Sums were tight (Nob died by 2 HP with the potion). Rule conflicts were named out loud. The shop made every line cost something.

**What was hollow:** on paper, fights turn into writing a policy and watching it run, so I made almost no decisions inside two elite fights. The game master also did my lookahead and leaked hidden information. Writing loose rules and letting the game master optimise is the dominant strategy of the paper format. Nob's Skill punishment wasn't shown up front, and I won on memory of the real game.

**Compared with others:** no other games yet. This is my baseline for a faithful paper port.

## Saltlight
"A gentle exploration game", Wrenmouth harbour, autumn 1994, $14.99. I played Chapter One, a single Saturday.

**Pull:** 4, 4, 3, and 2 overall. **Marks:** curious at first, then bored and confused. By the end it was "would quit" for the last three turns. Two cheats: June put a keepsake in my hands after I refused it, and sitting idle for a round paid the most.

**What kept me:** physical traces I could read history from (a handrail polished in the middle, layered paint on the FOR SALE house, a new roof against mossy ones, the darns in a different grey). One unpaid hook: Cyril Bowe, a visitor, when the cardigan is "a visitor's".

**What was hollow:** Belonging is a clock (+16 for a round of trying, +13 for a round of failing, +24 for doing nothing), so doing nothing is the dominant strategy. Deduction got the same canned line from three people. My refusal was overruled. Sketchable things were never shown (0 of 48). Nothing could be lost.

**Compared with others:** the opposite of the Slay the Spire port. That one had rules worth auditing and real loss. This had nothing to audit. I wouldn't buy it.
