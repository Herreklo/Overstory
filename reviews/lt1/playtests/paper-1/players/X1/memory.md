# X1 memory

## A paper version of Football Manager (Wexborough Town, League Two)

Played one match week: Wexborough Town v Halsey United, won 1-0 (Lowe 79', assist Ainsley).

Marks and pull: engaged on nearly every turn; confused once (Reid's injury risk given as "High", no number); delight twice (the scout's 45% to 70% update, and Mensah's dribble instruction visibly changing the match). Pull ended at 8.

What kept me: scouting led to a plan, and I could read the plan's effect in the numbers (Voss 0 shots, Garvey pushed back, Halsey fading at 72 to 78% condition). Having a promise open (Ainsley's contract talks, only £300 a week of wage room) made me want the next round.

What was hollow: training and the press conference were taken off me; the highlights were thin; the match came out a little too neat (a late goal from the sub I brought on, assisted by the man I'd just talked to). My crossing plan failed, and the win came through a ball over the top. The game never told me the plan was wrong.

Compared with other games: this is my first game, so nothing to compare yet. Against real Football Manager, it keeps the fuzzy scouting, injury gambles and planned substitutions, and loses the depth, the long save and the week-by-week run.

## Saltlight (Wrenmouth, autumn 1994)

Played Chapter One in three rounds; it ended on a fixed timeline at the Lantern Walk, Belonging 56%.

Marks and pull: mostly curious or bored, engaged four times; delight at the Lantern Walk and at small details paying off (June's wiped table, Admiral the crab); one cheat (the cardigan hook led nowhere). Pull started at 4, peaked at 6, ended at 5.

What kept me: the writing, the world running without me (weather, ferry, jars), and the room rewards (geranium, cat, fern). I'd play Chapter Two once, to learn what a keepsake is.

What was hollow: nothing to win, lose or choose. The cardigan was offered three times and went nowhere, and my own words were replaced by menu lines (Teddy's plans, the Hales). Belonging is a black box (+13, +17, +26 with no breakdown), and I never tested whether the walk could be missed. I would not pay $14.99 on what I saw.

Compared with the other games: Football Manager made me want the next round because I could read cause and effect and price a risk (Reid, Kerr's legs). Saltlight has the better prose and no stakes, and that is why it scored lower with me.
