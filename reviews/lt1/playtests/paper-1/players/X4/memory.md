# X4: my notes from games played

## Game 1: "A paper version of Return of the Obra Dinn" (the barque *Hesper*, ice off Uleåborg, 1812)

**Pull:** 8 every round; 8 overall. It ended at 3 of 14 fates, just as the hard part began.

**Marks:** mostly engaged and curious, never bored, never would quit. Delight three times: I was wrong about the man in irons (the split lip showed me why), J.H.'s untouched full chest, and Ned's cat drawing by the captain's bunk. No cheats.

**What kept me:** evidence I had to read, not hear. The neat versus clumsy stitches gave me the order of the deaths before the dates did. Then the hasp torn out with its padlock still shut, the forecastle chests, and choosing the order of the memories myself.

**What was hollow:** names were spoken in nearly every memory, so naming people was matching, not deduction. The frozen moments listed everyone, with nothing to overlook or sift. The revenge line went exactly the obvious way. The three-at-a-time rule never bit because I only wrote certainties. One memory (the cable tier) was beautiful but nearly empty.

**Compared with other games:** my first game, so there is nothing to compare yet. This is the bar the others meet.

## Game 2: "Saltlight" (a gentle exploration game, Wrenmouth harbour town, autumn 1994)

**Pull:** 5, 4, 3, then 2 overall. By the lantern walk, at the end of Chapter One, I would quit. I wouldn't play Chapter Two or pay $14.99.

**Marks:** mostly bored, a few curious, one would quit (the walk). There was one cheat: my own words were swapped for the menu line once. No delight.

**What kept me, briefly:** the surfaces. Rings polished by ropes, the calendar stopped at March 1988, the darns in nearly the right grey, and the Pennock house's white over green over ochre. The world can show time.

**What was hollow:** every clue ended in a kindly full stop (retired, moved to a son's, "a visitor's, we expect"). No one disagreed, lied or misremembered. The journal wrote my feelings and explained my clues away. Belonging rose for attendance, not attention. With no wrong answers and nothing to miss, I risked nothing.

**Compared with the Hesper:** it used the same kind of material, worn surfaces and dated objects, but there each was evidence with a fate on it and here each is a vignette. A surface that can be read needs a question to be read against.
