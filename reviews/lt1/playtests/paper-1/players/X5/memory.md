# X5's memory

## A paper version of Papers, Please (Day 7, Grestin, 29 November 1982)
- **Pull by round:** 6, 6, 7. **Overall:** 6. I never wanted to quit, but I was never at risk.
- **Marks:** mostly engaged, some curious. No bored marks, but no turn stung either. Delight came from three things: Halbert smiling as he read his numbers upside down, Danik leaning in, and the honest "no" when I tapped the bulletin.
- **What kept me:** a guard asking me to frame a journalist straight after she had asked about pay per arrest. Small physical lines, like Dubova walking out against the queue. The printer as the voice that judges you.
- **What was hollow:**
  - Every bill was paid by 14:35, so the bribes arrived after I no longer needed money.
  - The free warnings made greed and mercy the same move.
  - Questions never changed an outcome.
  - The clock had slack.
  - The line between mismatch and forgery was never drawn, so I was guessing.
  - Nothing I did had a consequence on screen.
- **Compared with others:** this was my first game. It's a faithful skeleton, but both of its engines are gone: the time pressure on your hands and the debt that grows across days. It's my baseline.

## Saltlight (a gentle exploration game, Wrenmouth, autumn 1994)
- **Pull by round:** 3, 2, 1. **Overall:** 2. I'd have quit at the bench, when the cat appeared.
- **Marks:** mostly bored, one would quit, and one cheat (I needled Rob, it bounced off, and Belonging still went up). Delight came from Wilf's rag and the gap in the band where the tuba should be.
- **What kept me:** first-rate observed detail (rings polished by ropes, a handrail bright in the middle). The lantern walk is beautiful, but as a cutscene.
- **What was hollow:**
  - Nothing was held back, so nothing could be found. Every question closed kindly.
  - Rewards came whatever I did: 51% Belonging in nine turns, and 0 sketches made.
  - Everyone is one gesture plus a number of years.
  - It trained me to stop looking, so I ignored its own best threads: the cardigan unclaimed since August, the house for sale, the sisters who see everyone on the stair.
- **Compared with others:** Papers, Please had stakes that melted by the afternoon. Saltlight never had any. It writes better than Papers, Please plays, but it isn't a game. Pull: 6 against 2.

## Thursday's Paper (reporter on the Courier, a harbour-town weekly, June 1990, then May to June 1991)
- **Pull by round:** 6, 7, 6, 7, 5, 4, 3. **Overall:** 5. Year one held me. Year two drifted.
- **Marks:** engaged and curious early, bored and confused late, and three cheats in round 6 alone. Delight came from Sutton knowing I'd asked Lily about him, Lily's "I hadn't forgotten", and Jonah's "since '88". "No correction" was the best line in the game.
- **What kept me:** the press-night deadline, a county paper scooping me, patience at 2, then 1,003 and the job. In year two, Ken's family rule finally bit, in a race between his partner and his brother.
- **What was hollow:**
  - Nobody said anything wrong for print, so the promised risk of a correction never existed.
  - Templates showed through ("I wouldn't know", "they're in my office", short-staffed N months).
  - Nobody decides the race.
  - Trust climbed to 10 on its own. Holding a story and disclosing a conflict both cost nothing.
  - Watch gave nothing, and there was no goal after the job.
- **Compared with others:** the best frame of the three. In year one it beat my paper Papers, Please in places. Its people are thinner than Saltlight's. It needs meters that answer you, as The Republia Times has.
