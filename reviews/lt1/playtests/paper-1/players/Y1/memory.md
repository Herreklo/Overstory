# Y1: my notes from games played

## A paper version of Football Manager (Wexborough Town, League Two)
One match week: preparing for Halsey, then the match, won 1–0 in the 90th minute.

**Marks:** mostly engaged. Confused once (the Lowe team talk did nothing I could see). Delight at the scouting payoff and the Ainsley promise. Cheat in my favour at the last-minute winner. **Pull** 7, 8, 8; overall 7.

**What kept me:** scouting ranges that paid off on the pitch (Platt "slow on the turn" turned out true). A two-line chat that became a dated promise with wage sums attached. A planned change at 60' with live condition numbers. Being told each default.

**What was hollow:** team talks were a black box. Every instruction got a confirming highlight, so the game never pushed back on me. The drama felt chosen over rolled. No depth: three attributes per player, unknowns that never updated, results without scores. The three-turn cap keeps me from running the whole club.

**Compared:** first game played. My benchmark from here: did my decisions show up as legible consequences, and did the game ever tell me I was wrong?

## Saltlight (a gentle exploration game, Wrenmouth, autumn 1994)
Chapter One, one day: arriving, the cardigan on its hook, the lantern walk.

**Marks:** I went from curious to bored and then to would quit. One delight: the elbows darned in a different grey. Then would quit when June and the Hales repeated their lines word for word, even after I pressed them. One cheat: the cardigan was a hook I wasn't allowed to pull. **Pull** 3, 4, 2; overall 2. I wouldn't play Chapter Two or pay $14.99.

**What kept me:** physical details that carry history and never repeat: paint layers at step 20, the calendar stuck on March 1988, a peephole at a child's height.

**What was hollow:** people on loops, Belonging as a progress bar unaffected by anything I did, sketches that do nothing, a clock that jumps to the next scene, and nothing at risk.

**Compared:** the opposite failure to the Football Manager session. There every read was confirmed; here no read was ever confirmed or denied. I want evidence that can be wrong, and a cost for being wrong.
