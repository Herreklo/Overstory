# Round 1

## Pull
6. Unlike my last two games, this one has numbers and a way to lose: sales, trust, patience, and the sack. But I can't see the exchange rate yet. How much does a "saw it" claim buy against a "heard" one? Until I know, I can't tell whether this is a system or a clock.

## Thinking aloud
The scoring is clear enough to plan against: hold 850, move trust from 6 to 7 within a month (three editions, 14, 21 and 28 June?), and don't spend Ken's 5 patience. The interesting piece is the conflict of interest. Ken sends me to "the tip", and the tip turns out to be a building staffed almost entirely by his family: Thea, Colin and Toby Waller. His partner and his brother are at the council. Only Eve and Terry are outsiders. The tip book has no story in it, only names, and "since April" is the one fact that isn't flat. The waiting room is empty at eleven on a weekday with five staff on, which smells like overstaffing paid for by someone. My working guess is that the council funds the clinic, and the people on both sides are related. The dominant strategy is probably to print only "saw it" and "record", never hearsay. I want to test whether that also sells, or whether the game makes me trade trust for sales. I'll also probe the edges: compound questions, reading both volumes in one turn, and what hover shows on a document.

The clinic: Eve Vaughan will tell me what changed in April, but only off the record. Her employer is the editor's partner's sister, so being quoted costs her more than talking does.
