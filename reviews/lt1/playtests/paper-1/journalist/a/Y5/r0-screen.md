# Round 0

**Store page.** *Thursday's Paper.* A harbour town, 1990. You are the new reporter on the Courier, the town's weekly. It goes to press on Wednesday night, ready or not. Each week you choose what to chase, whom to ask, and what to print or leave out. Everyone you ask has a reason to tell it their way, and the editor's own family is in the news. Print first and you sell. Print wrong and the correction stays in the bound volume. A year on, you'll open last June's volume and see what the town made of your pages.

**Tutorial.** A round is a week: three chase turns, then the edition. A chase turn is one act: visit someone and ask up to three questions, watch a place, read the back numbers, or wait. Answers are for print or not. Your notebook keeps who said what, where and when. In the edition, pick a front page and two inside stories, and mark how you know each claim. Sales, readers' trust and the editor's patience move every week.

## The Courier · Thursday 7 June 1990 · nine in the morning

The Courier is a front office and a back room on Market Square. The front office has a counter for callers and three desks; the back room has the make-up table, the files and a shelf of bound volumes. This morning's edition is stacked in bundles by the door. Its front page is Ian Myers leaving for university in the city.

Ken Scott, 32, the editor, is at the big desk with the planner open. Toby Tanner, 32, the other reporter, is on the telephone, writing as he listens. Susie Lyons, 66, is at the desk by the window with the notices.

Ken looks up. "You're the new one. Good. You're the second reporter, with Toby. You get three goes a week out in the town, and the paper goes on Wednesday night, ready or not. The front page is yours, and two inside. Notices are free; Susie does those. Toby takes whatever breaks.

"One rule. My partner, Sylvia Waller, works at the council office. Her mother and father are Grace and Toby Waller; Toby's at the clinic. Her sister Thea runs the clinic, and Thea's Colin works there too; they've a boy, Andy. My brother Jonah is at the council, and my mother is Audrey Scott. Anything that names any of them comes past me before it goes in.

"And don't ask me about the town. Get it from someone who was there. Sell me 850 a week and you stay. Get the readers' trust up to 7 by the end of the month and the job's yours for good. Run out my patience, or the readers', and you're gone."

## What you can do

Each chase turn is one act:

- **Talk:** visit one person and ask up to three questions. Each answer is for print or not, and your notebook says which.
- **Watch:** one place for a morning, an afternoon or an evening. You see who comes and goes, and what happens there.
- **Read:** the Courier's bound volumes (1989, and this year to date) at the office; the county paper at the co-op, out on Saturdays; any notice board.
- **Wait:** let a day go by.

The edition, on Wednesday, is your fourth turn. Choose a front page (500 words) and two inside stories (250 words each), and write them or say what each one says. Mark how you know every claim: saw it, a witness saw it, heard from someone named, or record. You may soften, hold or spike a story. You may write the edition as a plan, with "if" in it.

Free at any time, and not a turn: walking between places within a turn; inspecting a person or a thing (hover shows a name, age and work); noting a tip; asking why of a public event you have found (the file gives only what the record says).

Your notebook keeps every answer word for word: who said it, where and when, and whether it was for print.
