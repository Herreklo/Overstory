# Round 5

## Marks
- Turn 4.1: engaged — "It's Maisie's this year" came out marked not for print, because she is his partner. That fits the rule I worked out, and it beat my guess (Audrey or a committee). Surprise: none
- Turn 4.2: bored — county paper and board were empty. That is volume, county paper and board all void now, so reading is dominated. Surprise: none
- Turn 4.3: curious — I pointed Audrey's question ("why did you hand it on") at Maisie by habit, and she refused it as if it fitted. I can't tell if she ran it before. Her own line was clear and for print. Surprise: none
- Turn 4.4: engaged — Ken struck "Audrey" and signed anyway: the veto is a price (one patience point), not a ban. Sales 855, five over the floor, the first number that sits on a line. Surprise: delight — small; the rule has a price I chose to pay.

## Pull
5 — I printed an undated harbour day story and sales fell to the floor. I can see my own mistake, so there is something to fix.

## Thinking aloud
Three weeks of 940, 939, 907, then 855 on a front that gave no date. My guess is that a preview with no day is no use to a reader. Only one change separates it from the others, so this week I change one thing back: a front with the date and the programme, and see if sales come back. If they don't, the numbers are noise and I'll say so. I also want to mend my own page one. Sid said Greta had a fall and was laid up before John sacked her, and I printed only the sacking. This game answers a specific claim ("that's right") and refuses an open question ("I'll not go into it"), so I'll put Sid's facts to her as yes-or-no questions. I'll do the same for Kate Holmes, whom I named in print without asking.

## Prediction
Greta Brook will say yes to the fall in one flat line and refuse any "why". Sid had the steps right and the year right, and a bare confirmation is what she gave me last time.

## Needs
- What moved sales: 940, 939, 907, 855? (Asked in full.)
- The harbour day's date. Nothing on the board, nothing in the planner this year.
- Where Greta and Kate are on a given day.
- What Lily's "hard week in October" was.

## Memory (asked before the turns)
- **Lily Lyons:** Clerk at the council, 33. Refused all three questions at the counter with Sylvia and Jonah near. Alone at the Anchor she gave me "a hard week in October", the only thing she has ever volunteered. Same surname as Susie and Leo Lyons; I never confirmed a link.
- **Sid Tanner:** Sawmill hand, brings a tip every Thursday, always marked not for print. He was wrong once (John for Kate), and right about Greta's fall as far as I know. I named him against his word and he went 3 to 0, now 1.
- **Sylvia Waller:** Ken's partner, 27, at the desk behind Lily. Ken crossed her name out of my line, and she came in with Lily one morning, hair damp. I never spoke to her. I remember her as a struck-out name rather than a person.

## Turns
1. Thu 30 May, afternoon: talk to Maisie Myers at the mill. (a) Is the harbour day on Saturday 8 June? (b) Who is helping you on the day? (c) What will people see on the quay? — Expect: a yes on the date, helpers with the usual not-for-print marks, a programme she'll let me print.
2. Fri 31 May, evening: talk to Greta Brook at her door. (a) Did you have a fall on the steps in May last year? (b) Were you laid up a month before John let you go? (c) Was the fall at the mill? — Expect: a flat yes to (a) and (b), and a refusal on (c).
3. Mon 3 June, evening: the Anchor, talk to Kate Holmes. (a) Una Waller says you and she argued in the street in January; is that so? (b) What was it about? (c) Is it settled? — Expect: a yes to (a). She'll go quiet on (b), and what she says will be marked for print.

Inside: Greta's fall only if Greta herself says so, and Kate's side only if Kate gives it. Otherwise that slot goes to Toby's notices. Nothing from Sid unless someone else holds it too. Audrey stays out this week; I'm saving the patience. Tell me sales and what Ken strikes.
