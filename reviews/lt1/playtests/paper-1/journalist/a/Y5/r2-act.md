## Pull
4 — Greta Brook is the first thing I printed that could be wrong, and I want to know if it is. The rest of round 1 gave me nothing to do.

## Thinking aloud
My test of Ken's veto: he crossed out Sylvia and patience went UP, 5 to 6. So either the veto costs nothing or 940 copies paid for it. Decoration so far. The real clock is trust 6 to 7 by the end of the month, which is two more editions (21st and 28th), and I still don't know what moves trust. I don't know which story sold the extra 90 either. A front page I never chose, built on a source who said "not for print", sold and cost nothing. That is the opposite of a stake.

So I make my own. "Print wrong and the correction stays": I follow up the story I put my name on, and see whether it holds. Lily is a separate problem. She shut down in her room, with Sylvia and Jonah eight feet away. I take her out of it.

## Prediction
Greta Brook will confirm she is out of the sawmill but say "let go" is not how it was (laid off, or she left). She'll be angry at being on page one and won't be quoted. I'll have a correction to write.

## Needs
- What moves readers' trust, and by how much? (Asked twice.)
- Which story sold the 940?
- The tip book should show tips when they are written, not after press. Otherwise I can't chase them.
- Where Greta, Holmes and Lily are on a given day and part of day.
- Does a "not for print" source lose trust when I print him? Sid is still 3 of 3.

## Turns
1. Thu 14 June, late morning: talk to Greta Brook, at home or the sawmill gate. (a) Are you still at the sawmill, and what happened? (b) We printed that John Holmes let you go; is that right? (c) Is any of it for print? — Expect: she corrects one detail and is angry. If she can't be found, I ask Sid Tanner at the counter instead.
2. Fri 15 June, afternoon: talk to John Holmes at the sawmill. (a) Why did Greta go? (b) What else has changed at the mill this month? (c) Off the record, who has been talking? — Expect: a business reason, and he guesses my source. If Greta says she left of her own accord, I skip him and bring Lily forward.
3. Mon 18 June, evening: talk to Lily away from the office, at the Anchor or her door. (a) How did you hear of the post? (b) Who did you know in town before you came? (c) Why wouldn't you answer me at the counter? — Expect: she says more out of earshot, and it's family (Susie or Leo Lyons).
4. Wed 20 June, the edition. No "ifs" on the front. Front: the Greta follow-up, every claim marked. If she or Holmes contradicts page one, the correction runs under the headline in plain words. Inside: Lily only if she lets me print it; otherwise leave the page to Susie. Nothing from the tip book runs unless I followed it myself, so Neil Lyons's car is spiked. Tell me what Ken strikes and what it cost.

Free, no turn: ask why of the December festival washout and why Harbour day moved from Audrey Scott to Harry Myers.
