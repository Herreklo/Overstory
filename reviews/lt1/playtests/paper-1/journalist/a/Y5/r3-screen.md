# Round 3 · what happened

## Turn 1 · Thursday 21 June, afternoon · the back room

**October 1989,** all four issues, every page: tide tables, ferry times, advertisements. No news of anyone in the town that month. **January 1990:** the same. Nothing of Una Waller or John Holmes.

## Turn 2 · Thursday 21 June, evening · the Anchor

The choir comes in after practice: Terry Sutton, Eve Vaughan, Leo Lyons, Vera Holmes, and Sid Tanner with them. Sid takes his pint to the end of the bar, and you go over.

> **Sid Tanner, the Anchor, Thu 21 June, 9.30.** Asked what happened in the yard on the 7th: "Not the 7th. The week before. Greta's gone from the mill; John saw to it. Greta had a fall on the steps in May. Laid up a month." *(not for print: his coworkers)*
> **Sid Tanner, 9.31.** Asked what would put any of this on the record, named or not: "I'd sooner my name wasn't in it." *(for print)*
> **Sid Tanner, 9.33.** Asked what the words were between Una Waller and John Holmes: "They argued in the street, back in January. What about, I couldn't say. John and Harry Myers argued in the street too, in March." *(not for print: John is his coworker)*

## Turn 3 · Monday 25 June, afternoon · the school, after class

Una Waller is clearing a blackboard. Hover: *Una Waller, 23, teacher, the school.*

> **Una Waller, the school, Mon 25 June, 3.50.** Asked what she and John Holmes fell out over in January: "That wasn't John. That was Kate Holmes. Kate and I argued in the street, in January." *(for print)*
> **Una Waller, 3.51.** Asked if she knows Greta Brook: "Only what's in your paper." *(for print)*
> **Una Waller, 3.52.** Asked if any of it is for print: "Put it in if you want." *(for print)*

## Turn 4 · Wednesday 27 June · the edition

You held one story, so by your plan the week was thin: Neil Lyons's car went inside with Sid named.

- **Front · A row in the street.** Una Waller, of the school, says she and Kate Holmes argued in the street in January. "Put it in if you want," she told the Courier. *(heard from someone named: Una Waller)*
- **Inside · A car for Neil Lyons.** "Neil Lyons has gone and splashed out on a car. Second-hand, mind," says Sid Tanner, of the sawmill. *(heard from someone named: Sid Tanner, named against his word)*
- **The other inside slot:** Susie's notices.

Ken reads the dummy. Nothing in it names his family. He strikes nothing. Sales: **907.** Sid Tanner's trust: 3 before, **0** after.

**Your prediction:** Sid talked, and gave two things you hadn't asked for. The yard came out softer than "let go", not bigger. His yard answers came marked not for print again; his answer about himself did not.

# A year on

## The Courier · Thursday 23 May 1991 · nine in the morning

The front office is as it was. Susie Lyons's desk by the window is bare; she retired in January. Ken Scott is at the big desk. Toby Tanner is out. On the shelf, the 1990 volume has been bound beside the 1989 one.

Ken looks up when you come in. "Morning." He kept you on at the end of last June: you have been on the staff for eleven months.

This morning's edition is in bundles by the door:
> **23 May 1991 · Driftwood's first night** · *By Toby Tanner.* Driftwood played the Anchor for the first time on Friday, and it went down well. Oliver Brook, Peter Lane and Andy Shaw kept the room for two sets. Among them was "Winter Boats", a new song of Peter Lane's. They will be back.
