# Exit interview · Thursday's Paper · Y5

## 1. Round 6 marks and pull

- Turn 6.1 (Hannah Tanner, her garden): confused. Her tip arrived marked for print, unasked, on the day. Then she refused all three questions, including saying it again under her name. A for-print tip nobody will stand behind. Surprise: cheat (small).
- Turn 6.2 (Sally Baker, the garage): curious. One flat yes and no cause, as I predicted. But "Sylvia Waller and I had a dance there last month" came unasked, and it is the first line in the game that joins two threads. I can't print it (Ken's family) and can't follow it. Surprise: delight (small).
- Turn 6.3 (Greta Brook, her shed): engaged. She gave the order in her own words, fall in May, sacked in June, which mends my first front. But "off sick a few days last month" is May 1991, not May 1990. A second fall, or the year slipping? I can't tell. Surprise: none.
- Turn 6.4 (the edition): engaged. 925 against 848, on a front where two people say "we argued" and give no cause. It is the one time the game answered a question I put to it. Trust did not move, so I still can't say what moves that. Surprise: none.

**Pull: 3.** My own guess about sales was confirmed, and then the game summarised the rest. I'm not hungry for a seventh week.

## 2. Would the tenth hour, the five weeks, still hold me?

No. I chose nothing in those five weeks. They read as a table, which is what a loop looks like when it has run out. Two weeks of "nothing came into the tip book". Four sources on "no comment" for good. Both dials at 10 with nothing left to lose. Sales ran 925, 938, 872, 920, 849, 853: conflict on the front sold, notices did not, exactly as I guessed in round 6. Once I know that, every week is the same decision. The tenth hour is three questions, one refusal, and printing the printable.

## 3. Keep playing? Buy? Come back after a week?

- Keep playing: for one more session, only to learn whether Greta is all right. Not for the paper.
- Buy: no. A free browser toy, perhaps.
- Back after a week: no. I'd have lost the names, and the notebook is a list of refusals with nothing to resume.

## 4. What kept me playing

- Una Waller catching Sid's mistake (John for Kate). The first error I found myself, and a second source caught it.
- Lily alone at the Anchor, volunteering the "hard week in October". The place changed what she would say. Her counter, with Sylvia and Jonah eight feet away, did not.
- Greta's phone message on 29 June, the one time a person reached for me. Then "I'll not go into it" when I called back. That stung in a good way.
- Working out two hidden rules myself: "not for print" follows whom the speaker is talking about, and conflict sells while notices don't. Nobody told me either.

## 5. Where I nearly quit

- Round 3: Lily, Greta and Holmes all shut the same door in one line, and October 1989 turned out to be tide tables. Reading was dominated from then on.
- "A year on": every target met, no correction, nobody wrote. I read that as "my pages caused nothing", and for a round I was only playing to make a stake.
- Round 5: knocking twice at Greta's door and getting nothing, no neighbour, no hint where she was. The first real cost, and it taught me nothing.

## 6. What was hollow

- **Trust** is a ratchet: 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 whatever I did. I asked five times what moves it. It never fell, so failure is impossible.
- **Ken's veto** cost one patience point once. Patience then rose to 10.
- **Answers.** A specific claim gets "that's right". An open question gets "I'll not go into it". Quarrels (Una and Kate, John and Harry, Trevor and Audrey, Mark and Sally, Hannah and Sally, Ken and Toby) are one sentence with the names swapped, none with a cause. The unasked asides ("We played here in April") link to nothing.
- **Reading, watching, the free "why"** all returned a void. I would never choose them again.
- **Tips** reach me after press, so I can't chase them. Sid's was wrong. Hannah's will not stand behind hers.
- **The promise.** The store page says a year on I'd see what the town made of my pages. The town made nothing of them. The game planted a promise and then broke it.
- **The edition** is resolved by my own rule, so a cautious plan prints a story with no content.

## 7. Which game does this do better

None of the ones it resembles.

- Papers, Please has the squeeze. This has a dial I filled in week two.
- Return of the Obra Dinn tells you when a deduction is right. Here nothing confirms or refutes my reading; Una caught Sid by accident.
- Against my own two earlier games, this one is the best. Naming Sid against his word cost me a source and showed on a number, and my choices did sell or not sell. That is more than Saltlight gave me, and a cost that Papers, Please made slack when it mattered.

## 8. What I would change

1. Show tips when they arrive, with a day and a time, so I can chase them before press.
2. Let trust fall. A correction, a letter from Greta's family, a source who cuts me dead and a reason on the page. Tell me what moved each bar, once, in a line.
3. Give a way past "I'll not go into it": a trade, a document, a second witness. Right now the only unlock is a different room.
4. Put something in the record where the game points. "A hard week in October" led to tide tables, which makes every pointer suspect.
5. Show me what my page did to a person. Greta was sacked on the front I chose, and I never learned what that cost her. That is the stake.
6. Don't summarise weeks. If a week can be summarised, it should not be a week.
7. Tell me when I've got something right.

## 9. What would I lose if I stopped now

Almost nothing. The answer to whether Greta is all right, and two or three loose threads (Terry's "something done", Lily's October, the dance). I'd lose no skill, no standing and no collection I'd miss.

## 10. What I believe happened

- **Terry Sutton.** Probably an operation in the city in August 1990 ("home within the week", from Jonah, a coworker, so not for print). He came back quickly and is putting on another festival on the quay (Lily, December 1990). The first, in December 1989, was a washout and was his. He drinks at the Anchor with Leo Lyons and sits apart from Lily. I think he is a man who recovers fast and wants to redeem the washout. Confidence: low. Three thin lines.
- **Greta Brook.** She fell on steps in May 1990, was laid up, and John Holmes sacked her in June, at 66, in front of the yard. She won't say whether the fall was at the mill. I think she was dismissed after being hurt and has no claim she wants to press. She is frail and alone. The "few days off" in May 1991 and the "bad fall yesterday" in June look like more falls. She rang me because the paper is the only place she can say it. My first front, "John let me go", turned her injury into a verdict. Confidence: sequence high, cause low.
- **Lily Lyons.** I suspect she is kin to Leo or Susie Lyons and came through Terry's friendship with Leo, which is why she won't say whom she knew. The "hard week in October" 1989 was private, perhaps a loss or a break. She keeps to herself because the people beside her are the editor's partner and brother. Confidence: very low. The surname is weak evidence, since the town seems to share a short list of them.
