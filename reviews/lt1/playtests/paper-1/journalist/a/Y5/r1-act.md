# Round 1

## Pull
4 — The editor's family sitting in the news is a real hook, but my first job is a "a year on" puff piece and sales are already at the 850 I'm meant to reach.

## Thinking aloud
Ken tells me to sell 850 a week. The page says I sell 850 now. So "stay" means "don't fall", and the real target is trust 6 to 7 by the end of the month. That's one point, and I don't know what moves it. If I can't see what a good story is worth against a bad one, there's no squeeze, and I'll quit by round 3. I've seen this before: the pressure gets solved on day one.

The tip is Ken's own make-work. Lily Lyons is the clerk, Susie Lyons wrote her welcome piece, and the surname matches. Are they related? If so, last year's paper was a family favour. Sylvia (Ken's partner) and Jonah (Ken's brother) sit eight feet behind the counter, and Terry Sutton's door is open. Nobody will say anything real with those two listening. So the room is the obstacle, and I'll ask the question that matters off the record.

What I actually want to know: why does Ken send me to the one office where he's compromised? Is it a test of whether I'll write about his family? I'd like to find out what a refusal costs, since Ken's veto is the only hard rule.

## Prediction
Lily will give me a polished, safe answer to the first two questions. Sylvia will pretend not to listen and Jonah will drift to the counter to help. The off-record answer is the one worth having. She'll name someone who isn't in this room, probably someone who came to the counter and got a no.

## Needs
- What moves readers' trust, and by how much per edition (a good scoop, a correction, a story Ken kills)?
- Is Lily related to Susie Lyons? (I can look at that myself; I'll note it.)
- What does Ken do if I go around his veto, and does he find out?

## Turns
1. Thu 7 June, late morning: talk to Lily at the counter, three questions. (a) What crosses this counter in a week, and what do people come in angry about? (b) What's different in the office since you started a year ago, and who decided it? (c) Off the record: who in town would tell me something about this office that you wouldn't? — Expect: (a) and (b) are for print and bland; (c) is not for print and gets one name, with a glance at Sylvia.
2. Fri 8 June, morning: read the 1989 volume, the council items and the weeks around Lily's arrival (22 June). If Lily named someone, I visit them instead and read in the afternoon. — Expect: the record shows something the office has quietly dropped or changed, so I can mark it "record" and not lean on anyone's word.
3. Mon 11 June, morning: watch the council counter for the morning, noting who comes and what they leave with. If the name from turn 1 turns up, talk to them in the afternoon in place of this. — Expect: Sylvia and Jonah handle most callers; one customer leaves unhappy.
4. Wed 13 June, the edition: a plan with "if" in it. Front: whatever I can mark as record or a named witness. If I have only the anniversary, it goes inside, short, and the front goes to the unhappy caller's story. Nothing naming the Wallers, the Scotts or Audrey goes in unless I hold it cold. And I'll put Sylvia or Jonah's name in one draft line on purpose, to see what Ken's veto costs me. — Expect: Ken cuts it and my patience drops a point. If it doesn't drop, the rule is decoration.
