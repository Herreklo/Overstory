# Exit interview: The Courier (Y2)

## 1. Marks, round 6

- Turn 6.1 (Sally Baker, the garage): bored. For "where" and "who else" I got my own sentence handed back ("argued in the street yesterday"), and "Gary left in July" gave me a date and no destination. The free "why" of the 1989 festival got "Nothing on record says why", which is at least honest about the limit. Surprise: none.
- Turn 6.2 (Watch the quay, Saturday 8 June): confused. No harbour day. I had guessed the date because the screen never gave it, so a go went on a coin flip, and the tenth hour then says the harbour day ran on 22 June. Sylvia's washing is one more stray with nowhere to go. Surprise: cheat (mild): the one date I needed was hidden, and the miss counted against me.
- Turn 6.3 (Hannah Tanner): bored. The "conflict" between her and Sally is one word: "argued" against "shouting match". I printed "the two put it differently" over a synonym. Her "made head mechanic that June" matches the June 1990 tip, and I liked that the dates agree. Surprise: none.
- Turn 6.4 (the edition): engaged, briefly. Ken's rule fired for the first time and took four of his kin out of the swim list. But I had planned to put them in as a test, and the game decided I would back down, so I never got to argue. 899 on a one-word difference. Surprise: cheat (mild): I was folded without choosing.

## 2. Would the tenth hour still hold me?

No. It plays my habits as policy. Five editions land between 861 and 924, trust stays at 10 and patience at 9, and I run Maisie's harbour day twice because the tip book is "quiet". A third arrival (Gary Newman, after Gary Hughes) makes a new face, not a new problem. The one line with teeth is the last: the oldest entries in the tip book are the ones I held back. But holding back was free, so they carry no weight. Five weeks in a paragraph also tells me the first six rounds were the whole game.

## 3. Keep playing, buy, come back?

Keep playing: a round or two out of habit. Pull is 3. Buy: not at $9.99. At $4.99, yes, for the structure. Come back after a week: no. Nothing is waiting that would be worse for my leaving it.

## 4. What kept me

- The claim marks (saw it, a witness saw it, heard from someone named, record). Every sentence I write has to say how I know it. That is the best idea in the game.
- The opening: Susie handing me a story about her own son's workplace and writing "not for print: Neil's my son".
- The mill office having a worn sill and no steps, when three people said "the steps". A wrong guess that reframed the case.
- The daily swim at seven, fourteen names, then the boats going out.
- Una's "if it's worth the ink", the only line with a voice of its own.
- 938 when Greta was the front, and 879 on a bare fact: the only sales numbers that told me anything about my choices.

## 5. Where I nearly quit

Round 3, the county paper: "Nothing from the town this week", a whole go, and again five more times in the tenth hour. Round 4, when "I couldn't tell you. It's done" came out of Una, then Toby, then Ken. Round 6, when trust hit its 10 of 10 ceiling over a harbour-day announcement and I saw no number could hurt me.

## 6. What was hollow

- Every why is walled by one stock sentence ("I couldn't tell you. It's done", "I couldn't say"), in five mouths. Who, where and when were answered; cause never was. "Nothing on record says why" suggests the world has events and no reasons.
- The strays never joined up. Gulls in January, gulls in April, a wave in December, a bus, a car, a washing line. I spent six rounds building a pattern (the Mays, the quay, the festival). I now think the connections were mine, and the game drew those lines from the same pool of dated events. A mystery with no answer behind it is hollow.
- Nothing punished me. The empty, careful week was rewarded (913, 938). Trust went 6 to 10, patience to 9, a permanent job arrived across a year skip, and I was never shown why. I could not lose.
- I am a blank reporter again: no face, no past, no beat. The notebook reused another turn's three questions word for word twice.
- The tips are repeated and never escalate. Susie and Maisie are 3 of 3 sources and I may print nothing they say. Toby "beat me" to stories in round 1 and then lost two harbour days in a tenth-hour line.
- The steps were never found, so the Greta thread ended in nothing.

## 7. Which game does this better?

Answerable, a game I have already played, does the same harbour-town source chain better: it had a hearing and a case, and I met a person alive and then dead. It also had the paper contradicting its own witnesses, which gave me a lie to catch. The BG3 paper game does stakes better: a secret, a clock, a dilemma, and I held something there. Saltlight did the fabric of the town about as well and asked nothing of me, so this is above it. For the grammar of evidence, Obra Dinn. For a small week with a real cost, Papers, Please.

## 8. What I would change

- Let a why be answerable at a price: a favour, a trade of a not-for-print for a named source, a witness who talks if I protect them.
- Make a wrong or lazy week cost something: a correction, a source dropping to 1, a libel scare. Cap trust lower or give it sinks. Show me why a number moved.
- Show a calendar of public events (harbour day), so a guessed date is not a lost go. Tell me before I walk to the co-op when the county paper has nothing.
- Give each person their own refusal. Una's line should be the model.
- Make strays real: if a gull line is a clue, let me ask "why" of it free; if it is filler, tell me.
- Let an ignored tip rot, or let Toby run it first and cost me trust. Let me play the tenth hour week by week.
- Give me a face and a beat at the start.

## 9. What would I lose if I stopped now?

Very little. Two small curiosities: where the steps are, and why Ken and Gary took the bus in June 1990. A permanent job and a trust of 10 that cannot go down. The thing I'd miss in the BG3 paper game was a man's secret and a hanging; here, nothing is waiting that I will mourn.

## 10. Terry Sutton, Greta Brook, Gary Hughes

All of this is a guess from dates, with low confidence.

**Terry Sutton.** I never met him. I have one December 1989 piece: the council officer, 62, who first spoke of a quayside festival in May, with Ken, Sylvia Waller, Sam Brook, Eddie Lane, Peter Scott, John Holmes and Harry Myers all "doing their bit". It was a washout, and the file says nothing on record says why. He never appears again: not in the swim, not in any notice. No obituary ran, though Agnes Fielding's did. I believe he is alive and still at the council office, and that the failed festival left bad blood. The washout is the only thing before John's January row with Una and his March row with Harry, but I have no evidence tying them.

**Greta Brook.** I believe the fall and the sacking are linked in the plainest way. She was off her feet a month in May, and she was let go on 1 June, about when she would have come back. At 66 she was cheap to lose, and "parted company" is how the foreman describes that without saying it. The fall was probably not at the mill (the office has a worn sill and no steps); I don't know where it was. She may simply have gone home to feed gulls.

**Gary Hughes.** He came to the garage in February 1990, left it in July, took the bus to the city with Ken in June, and started at the mill in May 1991, eleven months after Greta went. So he did not replace her. I believe he drifted: out of the garage after a quarrel or an offer, a spell in the city, then the mill. Why Ken went along I cannot say, and Ken said "it's done".
