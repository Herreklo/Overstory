# Round 4: what happened

## Turn 1 · Thursday 23 May, late morning · the council office: Jonah Scott

Lily Lyons is at the counter. Sylvia Waller is at her desk. Terry Sutton's door is shut. Jonah Scott comes over and leans on the end of the counter. Hover: *Jonah Scott, 42, the council office.*

- *Mr Sutton steps down next year: will you put your name forward for chief officer?* "If the job's going, I'd not say no. I've had my eye on it a good while, since before Terry said anything."
- *Is anyone else standing, as far as you know?* "Sylvia's said she wants it."
- *May I print that?* "You can print what I said about me. Not Sylvia. I've told her myself it ought to be hers."

Notebook: three entries, Jonah Scott, the council office, Thursday 23 May, late morning. The first is **for print** (about himself). The second and third are **not for print** (about a coworker).

## Turn 2 · Thursday 23 May, afternoon · the council office: Sylvia Waller

Jonah is back at his desk. Sylvia Waller takes you to the end of the counter. Hover: *Sylvia Waller, 28, the council office.*

- *Mr Sutton steps down next year: will you put your name forward for chief officer?* "I want it. I've said so, and I'll say it to you."
- *Is anyone else standing, as far as you know?* "Jonah wants it too. That's no secret in here. He told me this month it should go to me."
- *May I print that?* "Print that I want it. Leave Jonah out of anything. Terry's said it himself: he's sixty-four, and tired."

Notebook: three entries, Sylvia Waller, the council office, Thursday 23 May, afternoon. The first is **for print** (about herself). The second and third are **not for print** (about her coworkers).

Your prediction was that Jonah would say yes on the record and defend Terry Sutton, and that Sylvia would hedge: "if the council asks". Neither denied it, but it went the other way round: Jonah hedged and said nothing about Terry, and Sylvia said it plainly.

## Turn 3 · Friday 24 May, morning · the council office: Terry Sutton

Terry Sutton's door is open and he waves you in. Hover: *Terry Sutton, 64, chief officer, the council office.*

- *You step down next year: your decision, and why?* "My own decision. I'll be sixty-five in January. Thirty-odd years is long enough for anyone, and it's time someone else had it."
- *Who should follow you?* "That's for the council to say, not me. Jonah's said he wants it, and so has Sylvia."
- *May I quote you?* "You can put that it's my own decision. Nothing about the others."

Notebook: three entries, Terry Sutton, Friday 24 May, morning. The first is **for print** (about himself). The second is **not for print** (about his staff).

## Turn 4 · Wednesday 29 May · making up the edition of 30 May

In the tip book since Friday:
> *Monday, in Toby's hand:* Jonah Scott rang. Lily Lyons has let it be known she wants to be chief officer. Not for print: she's at the office with him.
> *Tuesday, in Toby's hand:* Jonah Scott rang. Lily Lyons and Sylvia Waller are both after the job, and it shows. Not for print: they work with him.

Your front named Jonah and Sylvia Waller, so it went to Ken. He read it and said, "Take their names out." You insisted, as your plan said: both had said it to you for print, about a public post. Ken read it again, said nothing, and sent it down as you wrote it. The editor's patience falls to 6.
