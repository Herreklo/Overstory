# Round 2: what happened

A correction to last round's screen: its prediction line gave what happened, not what you predicted. You predicted answers on the first two questions and silence on the third; it went the other way round.

## Turn 1 · Thursday 14 June, morning · Greta Brook's door

Greta Brook, 66, comes to the door and talks to you on the step. She doesn't ask you in.

- *Are you no longer at the sawmill, and what reason were you given?* "I'm not at the sawmill now, no. John and I didn't see eye to eye. That's all."
- *May that be printed with your name?* "Print what you like. I've said what I've said."
- *Was anyone else let go, or only you?* "Just me. Ask any of them up there: John's been telling me how to do every last thing since the winter."

Notebook: three entries, Greta Brook, at her door, Thursday 14 June, morning. All three **for print** (about herself, and John Holmes, who isn't her workmate now).

Why, of Saturday's boats: *Nothing on record. Ask someone who was there.*

Your prediction was that Greta would confirm she was out, let herself be named, and give a reason softer or odder than "let go". She did: "didn't see eye to eye", and she never said "let go".

## Turn 2 · Saturday 16 June, morning · the co-op: the county paper

The county paper is on the rack by the till. It has the town this week, a short piece on an inside page:

> **Sawmill parts with Greta Brook.** Greta Brook, 66, is no longer at the town's sawmill. "John Holmes has let Greta go," said Sam Brook, the father of her children. "After all those years."

Nothing else from the town. Nothing about the council office.

## Turn 3 · Monday 18 June, morning · the council office: Terry Sutton

Lily Lyons is at the counter. Sylvia Waller and Jonah Scott are at their desks. Terry Sutton, 63, the chief officer, is in his room with the door open, and he waves you in.

- *Have your expense claims been questioned, and by whom?* "You've heard, then. Yes. I was found out, taking from the expense claims. Who found it is the council's business."
- *May I quote you?* "I've nothing more to say to the Courier."
- *Who signs council expenses, and are the papers open?* "The council's papers are the council's."

Notebook: three entries, Terry Sutton, the council office, Monday 18 June, morning. The first is **for print** (about himself). Nobody at the desks looks up while you're in there.

## Turn 4 · Wednesday 20 June · making up the edition of 21 June

Ken has the county paper on his desk, folded open at the sawmill piece. Nothing new has come into the tip book.

**Your page, as it went to press:**
- **Front: "Chief officer found out over expense claims."** Terry Sutton, 63, chief officer at the council office, has been found out taking from the expense claims, he told the Courier on Monday: "I was found out, taking from the expense claims." He would not say who found it, and had nothing more to say. *Marks: heard from Terry Sutton, for print.*
- **Inside: "Greta Brook leaves the sawmill."** Greta Brook, 66, is no longer at the sawmill. The county paper reported on Saturday that John Holmes, the foreman, had let her go. Mrs Brook told the Courier: "John and I didn't see eye to eye. That's all." Nobody else was let go, she said; John Holmes had been telling her how to do every last thing since the winter. *Marks: heard from Greta Brook, for print; record (county paper, 16 June).*
- **Inside: "At the garage since February."** Gary Hughes came to the town in February for a job at the garage. *Marks: record (February notices).*

None of it names Ken's people. Ken reads it through twice and sends it down.

**The edition sold 942.** The county paper's sawmill piece cost the editor's patience a point on Saturday; the sales gave it back.
