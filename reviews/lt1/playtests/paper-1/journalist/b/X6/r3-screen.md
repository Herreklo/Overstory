# Round 3: what happened

## Turn 1 · Friday 22 June, morning · the council office counter: Lily Lyons

Lily Lyons, 33, is at the counter. Terry Sutton's door is shut today; Sylvia Waller and Jonah Scott are at their desks. Hover: *Lily Lyons, 33, the council office.*

- *With Mr Sutton found out, who runs the office now?* "Terry does. Nothing's changed."
- *What happens to the money: repaid, or passed on?* "Nobody's said a word about the money, not to me, and it's not my place to ask. When my first year was out he made sure the whole office knew I'd carried it. You don't forget a thing like that."
- *May I quote you?* "Not about the office. It's where I work."

Notebook: three entries, Lily Lyons, the council office, Friday 22 June, morning. All three **not for print** (about her workplace and its people).

Why, of Terry Sutton being found out: *Not on the public record. Ask someone who was there.*

## Turn 3 · Monday 25 June, morning · the clinic, from before opening

A little before eight Toby Waller, 61, comes up from the harbour with his hair still wet, unlocks the door and goes straight through to the dispensary hatch. Eve Vaughan comes next, then Terry Baker, then Colin Berry. Thea Waller comes in after the others, her hair wet too. From nine the waiting room fills and empties in a steady trickle. Nothing else happens that you see.

Your prediction was that Toby would be in first and that Thea would come later with Colin, who'd drop the boy first. Toby was first and Thea came later, but she came alone, and you didn't see the boy.

## Turn 4 · Wednesday 27 June · making up the edition of 28 June

Lily's answers were all not for print, so your plan's other front ran. The clinic piece named Toby and Thea Waller, so it went to Ken. He read it and said, "Leave the names out." Your plan didn't say whether to insist, so it ran as he asked.

**Your page, as it went to press:**
- **Front: "Eleven festivals on the quay."** Terry Sutton's festival on the quay last December was, in the words of more than one person walking home, the best night the town had had in years. It was his eleventh since 1963. *Marks: record (14 December 1989).*
- **Inside: "First in."** At the clinic, the first key in the door each morning belongs to a man who has already swum in the harbour. *Marks: saw it.*
- **Inside: "The Night Shift at the Anchor."** The Night Shift played the Anchor in April last year and the room went quiet; in September, tighter than the time before; and again in March. *Marks: record (notices).*

**The edition sold 919.** The editor's patience rises to 7. Readers' trust rises to 7: last week's front and the sawmill piece have stood.

### The year since, in the volume

**10 January 1991, by Ken Scott. "Susie Lyons says goodbye; a new band at the Anchor."** Susie Lyons, 66, has retired after five years at the Courier. Readers will know her name from the harbour days, the festivals and a good many notices. We shall miss her at the desk by the window. *Driftwood.* Oliver Brook, who skippers for the harbour fleet, and Peter Lane of the Old Bank Café have started a band. They call it Driftwood, and Andy Shaw, 77, has joined them.

**14 February 1991. "Married."** Thea Waller, 33, who runs the clinic, and Colin Berry, 42, also of the clinic, were married on Saturday.

**23 May 1991 (this morning), by Toby Tanner. "Driftwood's first night."** Driftwood played the Anchor for the first time on Friday and did well. Oliver Brook, Peter Lane and Andy Shaw kept the room for two sets. The best received was "Coming Home on a Sunday", the first song Peter Lane has ever written. They will be back.

**Notices.** *1990.* August: Maisie Myers bought the big trip abroad; Toby Tanner bought the big trip abroad. October: the Night Shift played the Anchor, and "Your Father's Boat" got them called back twice. December: Oliver Brook and Peter Lane started a band, Driftwood; Andy Shaw joined Driftwood; Ivan Baker born to Sally Baker and Terry Baker. *1991.* March: Peter Lane will step down as owner at the Old Bank Café next year. April: the Night Shift played the Anchor, tighter than last time; Terry Sutton will step down as chief officer at the council office next year. May: Gary Hughes started work at the sawmill.
