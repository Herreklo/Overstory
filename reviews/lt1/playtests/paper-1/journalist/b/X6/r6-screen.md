# Round 6: what happened

## Turn 1 · Thursday 6 June, afternoon · the sawmill: Gary Hughes

John Holmes is at the office door. Sid Tanner and Neil Lyons are at the saw, Maisie Myers and Vera Holmes stacking. Gary Hughes is at the far end of the yard with a barrow of offcuts, and he puts it down to talk. Hover: *Gary Hughes, 39, the sawmill.*

- *You left the garage last July and started here in May: why?* "I left to start again. The way it went there had shaken me, and it happened at work. The sawmill was taking on in May, and I was the only one looking."
- *Did you go for head mechanic when Trevor Lane retired?* "I'd been doing the better work. Make of it what you like. Who got it was in your own paper."
- *May I print that?* "Print it."

Notebook: three entries, Gary Hughes, the sawmill, Thursday 6 June, afternoon. All three **for print** (about himself, and Sally Baker, who isn't his workmate now).

Why, of Sally Baker made head mechanic: *The strongest record of the two.*

Your prediction was that Gary would say for print that he left when Sally got the head job and the better work, and say nothing against Sally herself. He did.

## Turn 2 · Friday 7 June, morning · the council office: Lily Lyons

Lily Lyons is at the counter; Jonah Scott and Sylvia Waller are at their desks, and Terry Sutton's door is shut.

- *Were you the one who raised Mr Sutton's claims last June?* "No comment."
- *Did you raise them again with the office this February?* "I said it the once, in front of the office. I hadn't forgotten, that's all. Somebody had to say it."
- *May I print that, with your name?* "Not that. It's the office."

Notebook: three entries, Lily Lyons, Friday 7 June, morning. The second is **not for print** (about her chief officer).

## Turn 3 · Monday 10 June, morning · the garage: Sally Baker

Sally Baker is under a bonnet in the bay. She straightens up and wipes her hands. Hover: *Sally Baker, 40, head mechanic, the garage.*

- *How did you get the head mechanic's job?* "No comment."
- *Did Gary Hughes apply?* "Gary was in for it as well. They gave it to me."
- *May I print that?* "No comment."

Notebook: three entries, Sally Baker, the garage, Monday 10 June, morning. The second is **for print** (about Gary Hughes, who isn't her workmate now).

## Turn 4 · Wednesday 12 June · making up the edition of 13 June

Nothing new has come into the tip book. Lily's answer was not for print, so your first inside piece fell back to the Night Shift. There was no second Driftwood night, so the desk made your round-up from the notices of the year you hadn't yet printed that name none of Ken's people: the two trips abroad. None of the page names Ken's people. He reads it and sends it down.

**Your page, as it went to press:**
- **Front: "Who got the garage?"** Sally Baker, 40, has been head mechanic at the garage since Trevor Lane retired last June. Gary Hughes was in for it as well, she told the Courier: "They gave it to me." Mr Hughes left the garage last July and is now at the sawmill. "I left to start again," he said. "The way it went there had shaken me. I'd been doing the better work. Make of it what you like." Peter Lane of the Old Bank Café: "He'd been doing the better work there, and they gave it to Sally." *Marks: heard from Sally Baker, Gary Hughes and Peter Lane, each for print; record (notices, June 1990 and May 1991).*
- **Inside: "Your Father's Boat."** The Night Shift played the Anchor in October, and "Your Father's Boat" got them called back twice. *Marks: record (notices).*
- **Inside: "Notices of the year."** Maisie Myers and Toby Tanner each bought the big trip abroad last August. *Marks: record (notices).*

**The edition sold 953.** The editor's patience rises to 8. Readers' trust rises to 9: last week's front has stood.

# The next five weeks

You play on as you have: principals about themselves, on the record; the volumes for the rest; Ken's people written around; nothing on one not-for-print source.

**20 June.** Nothing reaches the tip book. Nothing new has happened in the race: Lily, Jonah and Sylvia all still want the job, and nobody has given way since May. You run it on the front again, with Terry Sutton's money still unanswered, and fill the inside from the volumes. Sold 937. Readers' trust rises to 10: the garage story has stood. Patience 9.

**27 June.** The harbour day on Saturday 22 June goes off well enough, with Maisie Myers running it. You watch it and front it, marked saw it. Sold 869.

**4 July.** Gary Newman has come to the town for a job at the garage, with Lynn Newman; the counter has it. You talk to him about himself and front it. Sold 894.

**11 July.** A thin week. Nothing reaches the tip book, and the front comes from the volumes again. Sold 906. Patience 10.

**18 July.** A line in the tip book, Thursday, in Toby's hand: *Terry Baker called in. Colin Berry walked out of the clinic yesterday, to start again somewhere else. Not for print: they work together.* It is the one story of the summer that names Ken's family, and its only source is not for print, so by your rules it is held. Nobody else prints it. The front comes from the volumes. Sold 908.

By 18 July readers' trust is 10 and the editor's patience 10. Your six editions from June last year to this June sold 933 on average, and you are on the staff for good. No complaint has come in against anything you printed. In all the summer, Ken has had nothing of yours to read past him. Nobody has told you who found Terry Sutton out.
