# Exit interview: Thursday's Paper (X6, the optimiser)

## 1. Marks for round 6

- Turn 6.1: engaged — Gary spoke for himself on the record and said nothing against Sally, as I predicted; "Make of it what you like" is a good line. Surprise: delight — the free why on Sally's promotion returned "The strongest record of the two", which cuts across his grievance. A record that contradicts a source is the best thing the game did with a check.
- Turn 6.2: engaged — Lily gave me "I said it the once, in front of the office... Somebody had to say it", the best line of the year, then walled it as not for print. I guessed yes on the second question and for print; half right. Surprise: none. It is the pattern: the good part is always the part I can't use.
- Turn 6.3: curious — Sally stonewalled the main question and gave one for-print line about Gary. I predicted "on merit", wrong. Surprise: none.
- Turn 6.4: confused — 953 sold, trust 9, patience 8. Surprise: cheat — my front ran Gary's grievance, Peter's line and Sally's two sentences, but left out "the strongest record of the two", which was in my own notebook, because my plan didn't say to include it. The desk printed my plan to the letter. A front that lets readers think Sally got a job over a better worker, with the one fact against that left out, earned +1 trust. Nothing told me it was lopsided. Trust here measures the absence of a correction, not fairness. The "Notices of the year" filler (two trips abroad) was dead weight, and I'd named it as a fallback.

## 2. Would the tenth hour, the five weeks above, still hold you?

No. Those five weeks were a table, not a game: I made no decisions, trust and patience went to 10, and sales wandered between 869 and 937. The win line was met in week four. The one line that woke me was 18 July: Colin Berry walked out of the clinic. By my own rules it is held, Ken has nothing of mine to read past him, and I have no way to pull it. That is the story the game opened with, and my strategy carried me clean past it.

## 3. Would you keep playing? Would you buy it? Would you come back after a week?

Keep playing as it stands: no. Pull is 5, and it was 5 or 6 for most of the game. I only stayed in rounds 4 to 6 to test Ken's rule and the "permanent" trust, and both tests are done. Buy it: not at a full price, and not on this form. The cast and the volumes are worth reading, but I can solve the loop, and once solved there is no tension. Come back after a week: probably once, to see what became of Colin, Greta and the job race. I'd be coming back to read, not to play.

## 4. What kept me

Building a model of a hidden machine: what moves trust, what moves sales, what Ken's rule fires on. The answer for trust, a front with something at stake that stands a week, was a real lever and I was pleased to find it. Also the first-hand finds that no one handed me: Toby and Thea Waller with wet hair after a harbour swim, Terry Baker's one aside, and Lily's grievance in June paying off eight months later when Jonah says she raised the claims in front of everyone. The town remembers, and that is its best feature.

## 5. Where did you nearly quit?

Three places. Round 3, turn 2: a whole turn to read an empty county paper and an empty notice board. Round 4, when trust turned "permanent" and the win line was already met, so I had nothing to lose. And now, at the five-week summary, where my rules played themselves. The Watch verb nearly lost me twice: an ordinary morning at the council office, nothing passed between them.

## 6. What was hollow?

- **No stakes once the line is crossed.** Trust is a ratchet, patience only moved when I insisted against Ken, and sales never came near 850 (lowest 869). Nothing could be lost after week four.
- **Risk was optimised out of existence.** The dominant line is to print only people's statements about themselves, on the record, plus record notices. It cannot be wrong, no correction ever arrived in eleven editions, and the brief's line "print wrong and the correction stays in the bound volume" never got tested.
- **Not for print is a wall around the good material.** Greta's cancer, Lily and Sutton's quarrel, Colin and Thea's row, Jonah's and Sylvia's ambitions: all held, all unusable by my rule. Two independent not-for-print sources agreed on Jonah backing Sylvia and it was still held. I asked three times whether corroboration clears a held item and never learned.
- **Ken's rule is a spelling test.** "Two colleagues" went past him untouched, and "leave the names out" cost nothing when I obeyed. The only cost came from insisting, once (patience 7 to 6, and sales fell with no stated cause). Meanwhile the rule steered me away from the Wallers, the Berrys and the Scotts, which is the story the game is about.
- **Conversations all have one shape.** Three questions, one for print about the speaker, the rest refused. After five I could predict them. A source at 3 (Sid, Sylvia) gave me nothing a source at 2 didn't.
- **Verbs.** Talk pays; Watch and the county paper paid almost nothing. The best check, the free "why" on public events, is rare.
- **Unanswered all game:** who found Sutton out. Sales causes are hidden, so I couldn't optimise them.

## 7. Which game does this better?

Against Slay the Spire I found the dominant line just as fast, and once found it became a rule I ran, as before. But the Spire shows its numbers and their causes, so I could attack them. Here the three numbers were visible and their causes mostly hidden. Against Saltlight this is better: it has a real lever, a lose condition and a town that remembers, and Saltlight had none of those. Papers, Please does the squeeze I wanted better: the rule against the human in front of you, with the cost landing at once. Here the rule had no teeth.

## 8. What would you change?

- Let held items rot or rise: a rival prints what I held, a second source clears it, a leak costs me a source. Make holding a decision with a price each week.
- Make a wrong or lopsided front able to cost something, and let trust fall. Drop "permanent" or make it earn a harder bar.
- Make Ken's rule fire on anyone identifiable and make clearing a story a bargain, not a spelling test.
- Print a one-line cause with the sales figure.
- Let a source at 3 give something a 2 won't (a document, a name, the finder of Sutton).
- Make Watch and the co-op worth a turn, or cut one.
- Ask me at the edition when my plan leaves a fact out ("you hold the record that Sally had the strongest record; include it?").
- Play the last five weeks as rounds, or cut them. As a summary they were the weakest page in the game.

## 9. What would you lose if you stopped now?

Little of play: the win line is met and the numbers are capped. I'd lose the answers to threads I've opened but can't pull: Colin's leaving, who wins the chief officer's job, whether Greta is alive, whether Sutton repays, and who found him out. I'd lose seeing what Ken does when a story finally names his family.

## 10. What do I believe happened to Terry Sutton, Greta Brook and Colin Berry, and why?

These are my reads from evidence. I hold none of them as fact.

- **Terry Sutton:** he took money from the expense claims and was found out in June 1990 (his own words, for print). I think it was not repaid: two "No comment" answers and Lily saying nobody mentioned it. His going is "his own decision" and, I suspect, a quiet exit with the claims behind it. As to who found him out, my best guess is someone in his own office who saw the paperwork, and Lily is the likeliest, because she is the clerk, she "had to say it", and she refused to answer when I asked. Low confidence.
- **Greta Brook:** let go by John Holmes (the county paper, quoting Sam Brook), "didn't see eye to eye" in her words. Sid says she has had cancer since February 1990. I think her illness and the foreman's oversight since "the winter" are one story, and I can't tell whether he pushed her out or was clumsily easing her load. I believe she is dying or dead, because the year's volume has no word of her and she took a boat out alone on harbour day, but that is a feeling, and nothing says it. I did right not to print the cancer.
- **Colin Berry:** an in-law in a Waller clinic, working under his wife and her father, with a baby at home, who in April argued with Thea in front of the Anchor and walked out of the clinic in July "to start again". Olive saw one scene and I don't read his character from one scene; I never spoke to him, although he was the first person I met. My belief is that the strain between work and family broke him. I'd want his words and Thea's.
