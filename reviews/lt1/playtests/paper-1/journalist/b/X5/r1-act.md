# Round 1

## Pull
6 — there's a deadline, three meters and a boss who can block me, which is already more game than Saltlight. But my first lead is an anniversary puff piece, and I don't know what moves any number.

## Thinking aloud
What am I trying to win? Hold 850 (I'm already on it, so really it's "don't lose any"), get trust from 6 to 7 in three editions, and don't burn Ken's patience (5, and nobody says what spends it). What do I risk? Printing wrong, and his family. Ken's family rule reads like a Papers, Please bulletin: eleven names in one breath, and two Tobys. I'll be checking it against every story. The tip itself is soft. A clerk a year on is a notice, not a front page. Two things in the clip itch, though. Susie Lyons wrote up the arrival of a Lily Lyons. And Sutton said the office had waited a long time "for her", not for a clerk. Why was the post empty for eleven months, and who sat there before? I also don't like questioning Lily at the counter with the editor's partner and brother at their desks behind her. If the game doesn't model who's listening, that's hollow. If it does, it's my first real cost.

## Prediction
Lily gives me something pleasant for print and stops when she glances back at Sylvia and Jonah. Whatever is under the eleven months leads to Sylvia or Jonah, so Ken's rule bites in week one. And Susie turns out to be Lily's mother: the Courier wrote up its own family.

## Needs
- What moves sales, trust and patience, and roughly by how much.
- Whether Lily and Susie are related (the hover gives name, age and work, not family).
- Whether it matters who can hear an answer.
- A list of places in town I can go.
- Whether today, Thursday at eleven, counts as my first turn (I'm assuming it does).

## Turns
Free first: I hover Lily, Sylvia, Jonah and Terry. I ask why of the public event "the council office was a clerk short for eleven months". I keep my eyes on Sylvia and Jonah while Lily answers.

1. **Thursday 7 June, late morning, council office: talk to Lily at the counter.** (a) "Who had your desk before you, and why did it sit empty eleven months?" (b) "Mr Sutton said the office had waited a long time for you. For you, or for anyone? Did you know him before you applied?" (c) "Are you related to Susie Lyons at the Courier?" I ask for print each time. If she won't talk here, (c) becomes "Where and when can we talk instead?" — Expect: (a) in a careful voice, (b) off the record or dodged, and a glance back at the desks.
2. **Friday 8 June, morning.** If Lily named her predecessor, I visit them: why did you leave, who covered your work, who wanted the post filled and who didn't. If Lily offered somewhere else to talk, I go there. If neither, I talk to Terry Sutton: what was he waiting for, who did the work in those eleven months, and when does he retire and who follows him? — Expect: the predecessor is bitter, or Sutton is smooth and the real story is who succeeds him.
3. **Saturday 9 June.** If I still have no name for the predecessor, I read the 1989 bound volume in the morning for anything on the council office. If I do have one, I watch the quay on harbour day in the afternoon: who Lily, Susie, Sylvia, Jonah and Terry talk to, and who they avoid. — Expect: a record that dates the gap, or a social map of the town.
4. **Edition, Wednesday 13 June.**
   - **Front:** if I have a named on-record source and a second source or a record behind it, I run the eleven months, with every claim marked. If it names Sylvia or Jonah, I take it to Ken on Tuesday, not on press night. If he wants it gone, I hold it rather than spike it. If I can't stand it up, the front is "A year at the counter": Lily's own on-record words plus the 1989 clip. It's soft and true.
   - **Inside 1:** harbour day as I saw it, if I watched, with named people only.
   - **Inside 2:** the second-best marked thread. If there's nothing, I run less. I'd rather run thin than wrong. Nothing off the record goes in.
