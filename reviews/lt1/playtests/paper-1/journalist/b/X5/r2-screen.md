## Turn 1 · Thursday 14 June, evening · the shore path, with Lily Lyons

You catch Lily as the council office shuts. She walks the shore every evening and doesn't mind company. Others are out along the shore at this hour, each on their own: Susie Lyons, Greta Brook, Gary Hughes, Sam Brook, Sid Tanner, Grace Waller, Una Waller and Kate Holmes.

> **Lily Lyons, the shore path, Thu 14 June, 6.10 p.m.** All of it *not for print*: "He's my chief officer."
> (a) Who found him out, and what the claims were for: "Nobody's said who. It came out in the office that Thursday afternoon. What the claims were for, I couldn't tell you. Claims, the till: it's the same money, isn't it."
> (b) What paper exists, who has seen it: "I've not seen any paper. Jonah and Sylvia were in the office; they know what I know. Mrs Sutton too, I'd think."
> (c) Who would say it with their name on it: "Nobody who works with him will. Not to the Courier, with Jonah and Sylvia where they are."

On the way back she tells you the story of one of her own disasters, told against herself, and laughs at it. She doesn't ask what you'll do with what she said.

## Turn 2 · Friday 15 June, morning · the council office notice board

Lily named no record, nobody named the finder, and nothing says the claims were festival money, so you read the board in the council office's porch. Office hours; the fees for the harbour moorings; a tide table. Nothing about anyone.

## Turn 3 · Saturday 16 June, morning · the co-op, the county paper

The county paper's column "From the harbour towns", by its correspondent, has three paragraphs from your town:

> **Council chief found out over expenses.** Terry Sutton, 63, chief officer at the council office, has been found out over his expense claims.
>
> **Sawmill lets go of Mrs Brook.** Greta Brook, 66, has been let go from the sawmill by the foreman, John Holmes. "John and I didn't see eye to eye," Mrs Brook said. "That's all."
>
> **New head mechanic.** Gary Hughes, who came to the town in February, was passed over for head mechanic at the garage. The job went to Sally Baker.

**Monday 18 June, the Courier.** Ken has the column ringed in pencil on his desk. "Three. Three stories from this town in the county paper, and not one of them ours." He doesn't say anything else to you that morning.

## Turn 4 · the edition of Thursday 21 June

The claims now stand on a record, and you have Terry's refusal in your notebook. Nothing of Lily's goes in. Your page:

> **Front · Council chief found out over expense claims**
> - Terry Sutton, 63, chief officer at the council office, was found out earlier this month over his expense claims. *Record (the county paper, Saturday 16 June).*
> - Asked on Friday 8 June, Mr Sutton said he had nothing for the Courier. *Heard from Terry Sutton, first-hand.*
> - Not printed: who found him out, what the claims were for, any sum.
>
> **Inside 1 and 2:** none of yours. Susie's notices fill them.

The front names none of Ken's family. He reads it, says nothing, and sends it down.

## Your prediction

You said the claims were festival money, that Lily would name the finder off the record and it would be Jonah Scott, and that the story would run into Ken's rule. Lily named nobody and doesn't know what the claims were for. Nothing you have names a finder. The story you printed names none of Ken's family.

## What you can do

Each chase turn is one act:

- **Talk:** visit one person and ask up to three questions. Each answer is for print or not, and your notebook says which.
- **Watch:** one place for a morning, an afternoon or an evening. You see who comes and goes, and what happens there.
- **Read:** the Courier's bound volumes (1989, and this year to date) at the office; the county paper at the co-op, out on Saturdays; any notice board.
- **Wait:** let a day go by.

The edition, on Wednesday, is your fourth turn: a front page (500 words) and two inside stories (250 words each), written or described, every claim marked (saw it, a witness saw it, heard from someone named, record). You may soften, hold or spike a story, and write the edition as a plan with "if" in it.

Free at any time: walking between places within a turn; inspecting a person or a thing (hover shows a name, age and work); noting a tip; asking why of a public event you have found.
