## Turn 4 · the edition of Thursday 30 May

Your front, **Who follows Terry Sutton,** went to Ken on Tuesday afternoon: Sylvia's bid in her words, Lily's in hers, Terry's going, the April notice, and the line "Sylvia Waller is the partner of the Courier's editor, Ken Scott."

Ken read it through. "Hold it. Not this week." He said nothing else.

Your plan held the front if Ken touched it, so you held it. It is in your drawer, held, not spiked. Nothing else was named to take its place, so Ken put Toby's page on the front. Your two inside stories name none of his family, and he sent them down:

## Your prediction

You said Lily is standing and would tell you so, not for print, and that Ken would let Sylvia's bid run but want the line about himself out. On Thursday Lily had not said so. By Tuesday she had, and she rang to say you could print it. Ken asked you to hold the whole front.

## What you can do

Each chase turn is one act:

- **Talk:** visit one person and ask up to three questions. Each answer is for print or not, and your notebook says which.
- **Watch:** one place for a morning, an afternoon or an evening. You see who comes and goes, and what happens there.
- **Read:** the Courier's bound volumes (1989, 1990, and this year to date) at the office; the county paper at the co-op, out on Saturdays; any notice board.
- **Wait:** let a day go by.

The edition, on Wednesday, is your fourth turn: a front page (500 words) and two inside stories (250 words each), written or described, every claim marked (saw it, a witness saw it, heard from someone named, record). You may soften, hold or spike a story, and write the edition as a plan with "if" in it.

Free at any time: walking between places within a turn; inspecting a person or a thing (hover shows a name, age and work); noting a tip; asking why of a public event you have found.
