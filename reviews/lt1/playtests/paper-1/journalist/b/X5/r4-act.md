# Round 4

## What I remember
- **Lily Lyons:** the clerk who came in June 1989, no relation of Susie. Sutton said she "carried the office". She rang the expense tip in to Toby the morning after I asked her about him, and never gave me anything with an edge for print. I've half suspected all year that she brought him down.
- **Terry Sutton:** 63 then, chief officer, eleven festivals since 1963. Found out over his claims last June. He refused me once, then went on the record with only the headline. He's stepping down next year.
- **Sylvia Waller:** Ken's partner, a Waller, at a desk behind Lily. She never looked up, and I never spoke to her. Until this morning she was a name on Ken's list.

## Marks
- Turn 3.1: bored — Sutton went on the record only to read the headline back to me: "You've printed that." Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.2: confused — Gary talks like a record ("Sally Baker got it. I didn't"). His "fifteen months short-handed" is the council's eleven months again. Clue or template? Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.3: engaged — the shore has ears, like the counter. Now it's a rule I can play: talk to the subject before I ask anyone about them. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.4: curious — my thinnest page, three quotes that say nothing, sold 1,003 and won me the job. The lesson seems to be named voices the county doesn't have. It paid for having the voices, not for anything they said. Surprise: cheat. Then "No correction" landed, and that's the best line in the game so far.

## Pull
7 — Ken's rule finally has teeth. His partner and his brother are circling the same job, and his paper is where it gets told. But I'm on the staff with no stated target, so all I can still lose is patience.

## Thinking aloud
The jump promised "what the town made of your pages" and gave me "no correction" and a list. Sutton is going and Gary has gone, but nothing ties either to my pages. The year also handed me four not-for-print lines I never asked for: the game did the legwork. The story now is who follows Sutton. Sylvia's bid is for print. Terry says Jonah wants the job, and Jonah says he's backing Sylvia. Both lines are off the record, and they can't both be the whole truth. Lily isn't named, so she's who I want. The risk is the Courier turning into Sylvia's poster. A fair page runs every candidate who'll speak and says in print whose partner she is. If Ken cuts that line, I hold the story. That could cost me patience at 3. I want to see whether the game knows the difference. I'm not touching Tara Brook's stroke, or Colin and Thea at the Anchor: one is private, and the other is a row, not news.

## Prediction
Lily is standing and tells me so, not for print. Ken lets Sylvia's bid run but wants the line about himself out.

## Needs
- What I'm playing for now I'm on the staff. Can patience still sack me?
- What my pages did in the town, if anything.
- Who appoints a chief officer, and when.
- Whether a for-print line Toby took at the counter counts as first-hand for me.
