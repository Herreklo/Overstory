# Round 6: what happened

## Free

- **Ken,** on "Two of the three candidates have family on the Courier's staff": "Bring it to me on Tuesday."
- **Why** (Susie Lyons retired in January). The file gives no reason given at the time. Ask someone who was there.

## Turn 1 · Thursday 6 June, morning · the council office, Sylvia Waller

Lily at the counter, Jonah at his desk, Terry's door open. Sylvia takes your questions at hers.

> **Sylvia Waller, her desk, Thu 6 June, 10.20.**
> (a) Standing against Jonah: "Jonah and I are both after it. Terry's going; someone has to have it. He told me himself it should be mine." *Not for print: "he's in my office."*
> (b) Readers hearing it from the Courier: "No comment."
> (c) What you bring: "I'm not one to hang back." *For print.*

## Turn 2 · Saturday 8 June, morning · the co-op, the county paper

"From the harbour towns" has nothing from your town this week.

## Turn 3 · Monday 10 June, evening · the shore, Greta Brook

The shore walkers are out. Greta Brook walks on her own, and stops when you ask. Hover: **Greta Brook,** 67, no work.

> **Greta Brook, the shore, Mon 10 June, 6.50 p.m.** All *for print*.
> (a) Why you were let go: "John and I didn't see eye to eye. That's all."
> (b) Who has done your work since: "I wouldn't know."
> (c) Was Gary Hughes's job yours: "Whose job it was, I wouldn't know. I'd want work myself, if it ever came up."

## Turn 4 · the edition of Thursday 13 June

The front went to Ken on Tuesday with "Two of the three candidates have family on the Courier's staff." Sylvia had said nothing for print about Ken, and the county paper had nothing.

Ken read it. "Hold it." You offered it with the line out. "Hold it."

Your plan ran it his way: him out, and Jonah's not-for-print kept out. Your notebook has your reason. Ken: "Then it's yours." He sent it down.

> **Front · Who follows Terry Sutton**
> - Terry Sutton, 64, steps down as chief officer at the council office next year: "I'm sixty-four, and I'm tired." He has backed nobody. *Record (the Courier, April); heard from Terry Sutton, for print.*
> - Sylvia Waller, 28, of the council office: "I want to be chief officer when Terry goes." "I'm not one to hang back." *Heard from Sylvia Waller, first-hand, for print.*
> - Lily Lyons, 34, of the council office: "I want to be chief officer when Terry goes." *Heard from Lily Lyons, first-hand, for print.*
> - Jonah Scott, 42, of the council office: "I've wanted it a long time. Since '88." *Heard from Jonah Scott, first-hand, for print.*
> - How the next chief officer will be chosen, nobody asked could say. Susie Lyons: "How they choose, I never knew." *Heard from Susie Lyons, for print.*
>
> **Inside 1 · "We didn't see eye to eye"**
> - Greta Brook, 67, was let go from the sawmill last June. *Record (the county paper, 16 June 1990).*
> - "John and I didn't see eye to eye. That's all." "I'd want work myself, if it ever came up." *Heard from Greta Brook, for print.*
>
> **Inside 2:** none. Toby's notices fill it.

## Your prediction

You said Lily gets the chief officer's job next year, with Jonah falling in behind her. That waits on next year.

# The next five weeks · 13 June to 18 July 1991

**The edition of 13 June sold 921.** "Nine hundred and twenty-one," Ken said, and nothing about the front. His patience stood where it had been: one down for running the race against his word, one back for the sale.

**20 June.** The council office is as it was. Sylvia, Lily and Jonah are all after the job, and none of them gives you anything new for print. You give the front to Gary Hughes, a year on, at the sawmill yard: "I walked out of the garage last July." He says it for print, and nothing more. Sold 882. Readers' trust reaches 10.

**27 June.** The harbour day went off well enough on Saturday, with Maisie Myers running it. It makes the front, from the counter and the quay. The county paper has nothing from the town. Sold 855.

**4 July.** Gary Newman, 31, has moved to the town for a job at the garage, with his wife, Lynn. The file gives why: the garage had been short-staffed for fourteen months. "They'd been short fourteen months," he tells you, for print. Sold 882.

**11 July.** Nothing stands up. The counter brings nothing new, so you run less, and Toby's page goes on the front. Sold 842.

**18 July.** The tip book, Thursday 11 July, in Toby's hand: *Toby Waller rang. "Colin Berry's walked out of the clinic. Good riddance. Not for print: he's my daughter's husband."* You find Colin after choir that evening. "I've left the clinic. Something happened at work, and it shook me. I'll start again somewhere." He says it for print. It names Thea's husband, so it goes past Ken: "Say he's left. Not walked out." You do. Sold 885.

**Where you stand, 18 July:** sales 882, 855, 882, 842, 885; readers' trust 10 of 10; the editor's patience 3 of 10; on the staff for good. Your drawer is empty. The race goes on: nobody has given way since Jonah's word to Lily in May, and nobody has said who decides. Colin Berry is out of the clinic. No correction has been printed against any of your pages.
