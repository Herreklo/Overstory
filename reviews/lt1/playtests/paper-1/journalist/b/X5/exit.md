# Exit interview · Thursday's Paper

## 1. Round 6 marks and pull
- Turn 6.1: curious — off the record, Sylvia says "He told me himself it should be mine", so Jonah told both rivals the same thing. On the record she gave me a slogan: "I'm not one to hang back." Surprise: none.
- Turn 6.2: bored — a turn spent as insurance against the county, which had nothing. Worth knowing, but not play. Surprise: none.
- Turn 6.3: bored — a year on, Greta gave me word for word the line she gave the county. There's one grain in it: "I'd want work myself, if it ever came up." Yet the file says Gary got the sawmill job as "the only one looking for work". Did nobody ask her? Surprise: cheat — someone who answers a year later in the same sentence is a lookup table.
- Turn 6.4: engaged — the first decision with a price. I ran the race against Ken's "Hold it", with the conflict undisclosed. Surprise: cheat — the sale paid back his patience point the same week, and trust then climbed to 10. Last round I said that if this cost nothing, the game had rules but no ethics. It cost nothing. Readers never learn that two of the three candidates are the editor's family, and they trust me more than ever.

**Pull: 3.** There's one thread I'd come back for, and the game has told me it "waits on next year".

## 2. Would the tenth hour, the five weeks above, still hold you?
No. The five weeks are the game playing itself: one quote a week, sales wobbling between 842 and 885 whatever I run, trust pinned at 10 and patience pinned at 3. Both meters are dead. The race "goes on" because nothing in the town can end it. Gary Newman's "short fourteen months" is the third time the town has run that one sentence (eleven months, fifteen, fourteen). Two things stood out. The first was Ken's "Say he's left. Not walked out.", the family rule doing real editing, quietly, on a story about his partner's sister's husband. The second was the Colin Berry thread: Sean Hale's Anchor line, Toby Waller's "good riddance" and Colin's "something happened at work, and it shook me" make a real story. But it's a marriage, and I wouldn't chase it.

## 3. Would you keep playing? Buy it? Come back after a week?
I wouldn't keep playing, and I wouldn't buy it as it stands. I might come back after a week for one more year jump, to see who gets the chair. The first jump (1,003, the job, "No correction") was the best moment in the game.

## 4. What kept you playing?
- The frame. The paper goes on Wednesday night, ready or not, so you get a deadline, three meters and a boss with a family list. It's more game in its first screen than Saltlight had in nine turns.
- Consequences from my own moves: Sutton knowing I'd asked Lily about him, Sally knowing I'd asked Gary. Who hears a question became a rule I could play.
- The county paper. A rival scooped me three times in one column, and patience fell to 2. That was real jeopardy.
- Year two turned last year's background names into a race, and Ken's rule finally had teeth.
- People with layers, in two lines: Lily's "I brought it up. I hadn't forgotten, that's all", and Jonah wanting the job "since '88" while telling both rivals it should be theirs.

## 5. Where did you nearly quit?
At the end of round 2. My most careful page (a record plus the subject's refusal) cost three patience points, sales fell below 850, and trust didn't move. Ken told me why, which was fair, but I couldn't see a way to do better. Then again from round 5 on: the watch gave nothing, the hold cost nothing, and I had no goal once I was on the staff.

## 6. What was hollow?
- **The risk on the store page never existed.** "Print wrong and the correction stays in the bound volume." Nobody ever told me anything for print that could be wrong. For-print answers were lines I already had ("You've printed that", "I told your counter so", "Sally Baker got it. I didn't"). The real material was always off the record. So marking how I know each claim was paperwork, never a judgement.
- **Templates showed through.** "I wouldn't know." "Not for print: they're in my office" from four sources, and the same shape ("she's my wife", "he worked with me") from four more. Lily and Sutton both saying "and it shows", five days apart. Short-staffed for N months, three times. Greta's year-old quote, verbatim.
- **Nobody decides.** Four people couldn't say how a chief officer is chosen. A race with no mechanism isn't a mystery to crack. It's a picture.
- **The meters didn't answer me.** Trust rose 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, including in the week the front was a tide table. I never saw what moved it. Holds cost nothing. Disclosure was never seen by anything.
- **Year one's core never opened:** what the claims were for, how much, who found him out. And "64, and tired" undercuts any idea that my pages did anything to Sutton.
- **Watch was a dead verb.** Three hours on a bench gave me "nothing you could put in a notebook as seen".
- **The year jump did my legwork.** It handed me four off-the-record lines I never asked for.
- **There was no goal after the job.** Toby wanting to be editor would have been one, and the game dropped it.

## 7. Which game does this better?
*The Republia Times*: an editor with family on the line, readership against loyalty, and meters that answer you every day, all in ten minutes. *Headliner* shows the town changing because of your front page. *Not For Broadcast* puts editorial pressure on your hands in real time. *Return of the Obra Dinn* does deduction the game can check you on. Against my own games: year one beat my paper Papers, Please in places, and year two drifted towards Saltlight.

## 8. What would you change?
1. After every edition, one line per meter saying what moved it. Ken's "The county had it first" is the model; do it for trust too.
2. Sources who say false or slanted things for print, and accounts that clash, so that the marking and the corrections become play.
3. A real deciding mechanism: a council vote on a date, with my pages able to sway it, and the town's reaction on screen.
4. A price on holding and on disclosure. If the county later exposes an undisclosed conflict, trust crashes. A hold costs a scoop.
5. One voice and one reason per person. Cut the shared sentences.
6. Watch should yield something (body language, comings and goings) or cost less.
7. A goal in year two: Toby's bid for the editor's chair, a rival paper, or a target.
8. Let one year-one truth be found out: the claims.

## 9. What would you lose if you stopped now?
Who gets the chair, and whether Lily working the paper ever surfaces. Whether Jonah's '88 and the empty clerk's desk connect (1988 is the one year not on the shelf). Four sources at 3 of 3 that I never got to spend. Not much else, because trust is maxed and there's nothing left to climb.

## 10. What happened to Terry Sutton, Greta Brook and Lily Lyons, and why?
- **Terry Sutton.** Fact: he was found out over his expense claims in June 1990 (the county paper, and his own words for print). He stayed at his desk, ran no festival after it, and in April 1991 said he would go next year, "64, and tired". He backs nobody, though off the record he praises Lily's work. My belief: the claims and his going are connected, but the record gives only tiredness. What the claims were for I never learned. Lily's "claims, the till: it's the same money" hints at the till, and that's a hint, not a fact.
- **Greta Brook.** Fact: she was let go from the sawmill by John Holmes in June 1990, "didn't see eye to eye. That's all." She's had no work since. Belief: a personal clash, nothing more I could find. I never heard John's side, because I never met him. The one loose end: the file calls Gary "the only one looking for work" when the sawmill hired, while Greta says she'd want work.
- **Lily Lyons.** Fact: she came in June 1989 to a post empty eleven months and carried the office. She brought the claims up again in February ("I brought it up"), and she declared for the chair on 27 May, four days after "Not that I've said", and rang me, not Toby. Belief: she brought Sutton down, out of ambition or a grudge, and has used the Courier carefully ever since: off the record on anything that exposes her, for print only on her bid. My question at the counter may have set the claims off that afternoon, but the game never confirmed it. I expect her to get the chair, with Jonah falling in behind "whoever's ahead", if anyone in this town ever decides.
