# Round 6: what happened

## Turn 1 · Thursday 6 June, afternoon · Susie Lyons, at home

Susie Lyons, 67, opens the door and stays in it. You ask (1) and (2) off the record, (3) for print.

> **Susie Lyons, her door, Thu 6 June, 2.50.**
> (1) Kin to Lily, Neil or Leo Lyons: "Neil's my son, and Leo's his father." *Not for print: that's my family.*
> (2) The county paper last June: "Not through me." *Not for print, as you asked.*
> (3) Where she looked for work last August: "I thought about somewhere else for a while last summer. In the end I retired." *For print.*

## Turn 2 · Friday 7 June, morning · the sawmill yard, Neil Lyons

Susie named no one, so you go to her son. Neil Lyons, 42, is at the saw, and doesn't stop it for long.

> **Neil Lyons, the yard, Fri 7 June, 9.10.**
> (1) Was he "one of the men at the mill": "No comment."
> (2) Is Lily Lyons his kin: "No comment."
> (3) Did the council item go through him: "No comment."

## Monday 10 June, at nine

Ken has Saturday's county paper on his desk:

> **Second stroke.** Mary Tanner, 71, has had another stroke, her husband Mark Holmes told us.

"They had Mary Tanner," Ken says. He puts it on the spike. The editor's patience falls to 3.

## Turn 3 · Monday 10 June, morning · the Old Bank Café, Peter Lane

Peter Lane, 33, the owner, talks across the counter.

> **Peter Lane, the café, Mon 10 June, 10.30.**
> (1) Who takes the café on: "Lisa Myers and Eddie Lane both want it, and it shows." *Not for print: they work for me.*
> (2) Why next year: "I'm stepping down as owner next year." *For print.*
> (3) Does Driftwood go on: "We've only just started." *For print.*

## Turn 4 · the edition of Thursday 13 June

Peter gave you something for print, so the front is the café.

> **Front · The Old Bank Café to change hands**
> - "I'm stepping down as owner next year." *Heard from Peter Lane, named.*
> - Peter Lane will step down as owner at the Old Bank Café next year. *Record (the March notice).*
> - Driftwood played the Anchor for the first time on 17 May. *Record (23 May).*
>
> **Inside 1 · Driftwood**
> - "We've only just started." *Heard from Peter Lane, named.*
>
> **Inside 2:** held. Nothing on the county paper's source.

Ken reads it. None of it names his family; he sends it down. On Thursday: "Eight hundred and fifty-four." Readers' trust rises to 9: last week's front has stood.

## Your prediction

Peter named Lisa Myers and Eddie Lane as wanting the café, not for print. He goes next year; who follows him isn't known yet.

# The next five weeks

You play on as you have: principals about themselves, on the record; the volumes for the rest; soften when Ken asks, run it when he can name nothing wrong; nothing printed from a not-for-print answer.

**20 June.** Nothing reaches the tip book. Lily, Jonah and Sylvia all still want the job, and nobody has given way since May. You front the race again, each in their own words. Ken asks you to take out the line naming them as his people, and you do. Inside, Lisa Myers and Eddie Lane each tell you, for print, that they want the café. Sold 919. Patience 4.

**27 June.** The harbour day on Saturday went off well enough, with Maisie Myers running it; the counter has it. Maisie tells you, for print, that she ran it, and the front is her and the notice. Sold 848. Readers' trust rises to 10: the race has stood.

**4 July.** Gary Newman has come to the town for a job at the garage, with Lynn Newman; the counter has it. Gary Newman talks to you for print about coming for the job. Sold 899.

**11 July.** A thin week; nothing reaches the tip book. The race goes on the front again, and again Ken asks you to soften it, and you do. Sold 881.

**18 July.** A line in the tip book, Thursday, in Toby's hand: *Thea Waller rang. Colin Berry has left the clinic, to start again somewhere else. He needed a fresh start. Not for print: he's my husband.* You go to Colin, and he says it himself, for print: he walked out, and he's starting again somewhere else. He is Ken's family; Ken asks you to take out "walked out", and you do. Colin is your front. Sold 896.

By 18 July readers' trust is 10 and the editor's patience 4. Your six editions from June last year to this June sold 903 on average, and you are on the staff for good. Nothing you printed has drawn a complaint. Nobody has told you who spoke to the county paper last June, or who found Terry Sutton out.
