## Turn 1 · Thursday 21 June, afternoon · the council office, Terry Sutton

Nothing current on the counter's notice board. Terry Sutton, 63, chief officer, comes out himself.

> **Terry Sutton, the council counter, Thu 21 June, 3.10.**
> (1) What the claims were for, what's been found: "There was a mistake in the claims. That's all I'll say." *For print.*
> (2) Who looked into them, has the council decided: "Nobody's decided anything. I've put on eleven festivals for this town." *For print.*
> (3) Is he staying on: "I'm chief officer." *For print.*

He goes back in.

## Turn 2 · Friday 22 June, morning · Greta Brook, at home

Greta Brook, 66, answers her own door. She doesn't ask you in.

> **Greta Brook, her door, Fri 22 June, 10.20.**
> (1) What she says to John's words: "John let me go." *For print.*
> (2) How long at the mill, and at what: "I've been in this town all my life." *For print.*
> (3) Who does it now: "I wouldn't know."

## Turn 3 · Saturday 23 June, morning · the co-op

Saturday's county paper: nothing from the town this week.

## Turn 4 · the edition of Thursday 28 June

Terry spoke for print, so your front is (a); the sawmill and the garage go inside, under Ken's headlines.

> **Front · Council chief answers expenses query**
> - Terry Sutton, chief officer at the council office, has been found out over his expense claims, the county paper reported on 16 June. *The county paper, credited.*
> - "There was a mistake in the claims. That's all I'll say." "Nobody's decided anything. I've put on eleven festivals for this town." "I'm chief officer." *Heard from Terry Sutton, named.*
>
> **Inside 1 · Let go from the sawmill**
> - "Her work had been poor for months. I'd no choice." *Heard from John Holmes, foreman, named.*
> - "John let me go." *Heard from Greta Brook, named.*
>
> **Inside 2 · The garage's head mechanic**
> - Sally Baker became head mechanic at the garage on 9 June. *Record (the counter's notice).*
> - "I'd been doing the better work. Make of it what you like." *Heard from Gary Hughes, named.*

Held: John's "snapped at me"; everything given not for print.

Ken reads them on Wednesday night. Nothing names his family, and he sends them down without a word. On Thursday: "Nine hundred and ninety-nine." The editor's patience rises to 4.

## Your prediction

Terry gave you three answers for print and is still chief officer. Nobody has told you who at the office spoke to the county paper. Ken sent the council front down without a word.

# Round 4 · Thursday 23 May 1991 · the Courier, nine in the morning

Eleven months on. Toby is on the telephone; the desk by the window is empty: Susie retired in January. Ken looks up. "You're on the staff for good. Trust's at seven; the council front stood." Then, as on every first morning: "Same rule. Sylvia, her mother and father, Thea, Colin and Andy, Jonah, my mother. Anything that names any of them comes past me."

### The year since, in the volume

**10 January 1991, by Ken Scott. "Susie Lyons says goodbye; a new band at the Anchor."** Susie Lyons, 66, has retired after five years at the Courier. "We shall miss her at the desk by the window." **14 February 1991. "Married."** Thea Waller, 33, who runs the clinic, and Colin Berry, 42, also of the clinic, were married on Saturday.

**This morning, by Toby Tanner. "Driftwood's first night."** Driftwood played the Anchor on Friday and did well. The best received was "Coming Home on a Sunday", the first song Peter Lane has ever written.
