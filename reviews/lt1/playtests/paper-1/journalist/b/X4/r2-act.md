# Round 2

## Marks
- Turn 1.1: curious — Trevor's off-record "Gary's been doing the better work" only became interesting when Sally got the job. But "It goes on the record, whoever's is the stronger" reads like a rule talking, not a man. He retired that same day while telling me "nothing for the paper". Was he hiding it, or did he just roll a refusal? I can't tell. Surprise: none.
- Turn 1.2: engaged — the back numbers gave me threads to cross myself: Lyons at the garage and the council, two Brooks, two Bakers, a clerk short eleven months, a garage short seventeen. The festival helpers were a roll call. Surprise: none.
- Turn 1.3: bored — Sally dodged where she'd been before and gave me nothing for print. The news (retired, promoted) reached me through the counter. I could have stayed at my desk. Surprise: none.
- Turn 1.4: confused — my inside story was built from Toby's counter notes, and his piece was spiked for mine. Sales rose 30 and I don't know whether that was Susie's front or me. Surprise: none.

## Pull
5 — there's a real contradiction and a sacking, but people answer in one line, and so far the news has come in whether or not I chased it.

## Thinking aloud
There may be a pattern in the refusals: Trevor won't speak for print about "mine", Sally not about her husband, Maisie not about people "at the mill with me". If so, a source won't go on record about people they're tied to, and to print anything I need the person themselves or someone outside their circle. That would be a rule I can learn, and it's the first thing here that feels like mastery. So with Greta Brook I go to Greta, not Maisie, and never mention the tip. The garage still nags me: the man I was told did the better work was passed over. "Whoever's is the stronger" could mean whoever has the longer record, so I'll ask Gary how long and where. Ken's "Short" stung a bit. This week I want a front I found myself.

## Prediction
Greta Brook will go on record, since she has nothing left to lose at the mill, and John Holmes will decline or give a one-line reason. Her work will have gone to someone with a name from the town's families.

## Needs
- Who owns the garage and who chose Sally.
- What "whoever's is the stronger" means: stronger what?
- Where people are when they're not at work. The place list doesn't say who's where.
- Whether Susie is related to Lily Lyons (she wrote that piece) and to Leo Lyons.
- What moved sales, the front or the inside.
- Whether an off-record answer can be used as a lead to ask someone else.

## Turns
1. **Thursday 14 June, afternoon, the garage: talk to Gary Hughes.** I don't mention Trevor's words. (1) "Sally was made head mechanic on Saturday. When were you told, and by whom?" (2) "Who owns the garage, and who made the choice?" (3) "How long have you been a mechanic, and where were you before February?" I ask for print on all three. — Expect: told late and second-hand, a name for the owner, and fewer years than Sally.
2. **Friday 15 June, evening, the Anchor: talk to Greta Brook** (I find her by inspecting who's there). (1) "How long were you at the sawmill, and are you still there?" (2) "What were you told, and by whom?" (3) "Who's doing your work now?" For print. *If she isn't there, I talk to John Holmes if he is; if neither is, I count the turn as watching the Anchor.* — Expect: years and a date, a reason she disputes, a named replacement.
3. **Monday 18 June, morning, the sawmill yard.** *If Greta spoke for print:* talk to John Holmes. (1) "Greta Brook has told the Courier she's finished here. Why?" (2) "Who has taken her place?" (3) "Who else has the mill taken on or lost this year?" *If she didn't:* watch the yard for the morning instead. — Expect: a short reason, and a replacement who confirms or breaks my prediction.
4. **Wednesday, the edition (a plan).**
   - *Front:* if Greta and Holmes are both on record, "Sawmill lets go of worker after N years", with both sides marked heard from named sources. If only Greta is, her account, with "Mr Holmes was asked" or his decline. If I only have Maisie's tip, nothing on the sawmill.
   - *Inside 1:* the garage, only if Gary gives for print who decided. No "passed over" from a sore man alone.
   - *Inside 2:* "Short of hands": the council clerk post empty eleven months (record) and the garage short seventeen months (Sally, heard, named). If Gary adds his own months, those too.
