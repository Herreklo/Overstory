# Round 2: what happened

## Turn 1 · Thursday 14 June, afternoon · the garage, Gary Hughes

Sally Baker is at the bench. Gary is under a car on the forecourt and slides out when you say his name. He has a face like thunder, and keeps it while he answers.

> **Gary Hughes, the forecourt, Thu 14 June, 2.30.**
> (1) When he was told, and by whom: "I'd been doing the better work. Make of it what you like." *For print.*
> (2) Who owns it, who chose: "Her record was the stronger, they say." *Not for print: I work with her.*
> (3) How long, and where before: "I came in February. They'd been short fifteen months." Where he was before, he doesn't say. *For print.*

He slides back under the car.

## Turn 2 · Friday 15 June, evening · the Anchor

The bar is full for a Friday. Hovering, you find: Evan Newman, Peter Scott, Olive Shaw and Sean Hale behind the bar; Terry Sutton, Sylvia Waller, Toby Waller, Sally Baker, Sam Brook, Neil Lyons, Mark Holmes, Vera Holmes, Leo Lyons, Larry Baker and Maisie Myers in the room; Oliver Brook at the end of the bar; John Holmes, 44, foreman at the sawmill, at a table with his two pints.

Greta Brook is not here. You go to John, and put him your three questions about her.

> **John Holmes, the Anchor, Fri 15 June, 8.40.**
> (1) How long she was there, and is she still: "She's not. Her work had been poor for months. I'd no choice." *For print.*
> (2) What she was told, and by whom: "I told her myself, and she snapped at me for it." *For print.*
> (3) Who's doing her work now: "No comment." *No comment.*

He goes back to his pints. Nobody near you talks about the sawmill or anything else you can use.

## Turn 3 · Monday 18 June, morning · the sawmill yard

**At nine, Ken has Saturday's county paper open on his desk.** Two pieces from the town:

> **Sawmill lets a worker go.** Greta Brook has been let go from the sawmill by the foreman, John Holmes. "John won't have done it lightly," one of the men at the mill told us.
>
> **Council officer in expenses query.** Terry Sutton, chief officer at the council office, has been found out over his expense claims, we understand from someone at the office.

"They had the sawmill and the council on Saturday," Ken says. "We had neither." He folds it and puts it on the spike.

Greta didn't speak for print, so you watch the yard for the morning. John Holmes is at the office door with a clipboard. Sid Tanner and Neil Lyons work the saw; Maisie Myers stacks. Nobody new comes, nobody leaves, and Greta Brook is not there. Nothing happens that you could print.

## Turn 4 · the edition of Thursday 21 June

Every sawmill branch of your plan needed Greta on record, and you didn't reach her, so the sawmill stays out. Gary named no one who decided, and not for print, so inside 1 stays out. With no front of yours, Ken ran Susie's notices on the front.

> **Inside 2 · Short of hands**
> - The council office had been a clerk short for eleven months when Lily Lyons came last June. *Record (C-02).*
> - The garage had been short fifteen months when Gary Hughes came in February. *Heard from Gary Hughes, named.*
> - It had been short seventeen months when Sally Baker came in April. *Heard from Sally Baker, named.*

Held: Gary's and Trevor's answers on who decided (not for print); John Holmes on Greta Brook; Maisie's tip.

Ken reads it on Wednesday night and sends it down without a word.

## Your prediction

You said Greta would go on record and John would decline or give a one-line reason, and that her work had gone to someone from a town family. You didn't reach Greta. John gave you a reason, for print, and wouldn't say who does her work. Nobody new was at the yard.
