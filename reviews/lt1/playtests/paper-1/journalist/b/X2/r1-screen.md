# Round 1: what happened

## Turn 1 · Thursday 7 June, eleven · the sawmill yard, John Holmes

John Holmes hears you out at the office door, papers under his arm. The saw doesn't stop.

> **John Holmes, the sawmill office door, Thu 7 June, 11.10.**
> (a) The mill's year, orders, the crew: "Nothing for the paper on that." *No comment.*
> (b) Anyone taken on, or anyone gone: "Greta Brook. I let her go last week. Her work had been poor for months. Gone to pieces, if you want it plain. I'd no choice." *For print.*
> (c) Why she went, and may you quote him: "I've told you. I let her go, and you can put my name to it." *For print.*

On your way out you ask Maisie Myers, at the lean-to, where Greta Brook lives (free). "Up the shore road. Last house before the turn." She goes back to her boards.

## Turn 2 · Thursday 7 June, evening · Greta Brook's house, the shore road

Nobody answers, and the windows are dark. Towards nine Greta Brook comes up the road from the shore and finds you at her gate.

Hover: **Greta Brook,** 66. Work: none.

> **Greta Brook, her gate, Thu 7 June, 21.00.**
> (a) Left, or let go: "I've nothing to say to the paper." *No comment.*
> (b) The reason she was given, and whether she believes it: she shakes her head. *No comment.*
> (c) Would she say it in print, under her name: "He let me go. Put that, if you want. Nothing else." *For print.*

She goes in. After a while a lamp comes on inside.

## Turn 3 · Friday 8 June · the tip book, then the back room

**At nine, the tip book has two new lines:**

> *In Toby Tanner's hand, Fri 8 June, 8.45:* Jonah Scott (council) rang. There's been some bother at the office over Terry Sutton's expense claims. Terry's given that office thirty-odd years. Not for print: he's my chief officer.
>
> *From the counter, Fri 8 June:* Garage. Trevor Lane retired yesterday after 34 years at the garage. (Toby's.)

Greta gave no account to set against John's, so your second branch ran: Friday afternoon at the volumes.

**The back room.** The 1989 volume runs from January (no earlier ones on the shelf). On the sawmill it has one line: *April 1989. Maisie Myers left the school for a start at the sawmill.* No owner of the mill is named, and no lay-offs or accidents. This year's issues have nothing on the mill.

The year's four long pieces say nothing of the mill: the harbour day under Audrey Scott (C-01), Lily Lyons's arrival at the council office (C-02), Agnes Fielding's death at 103 (C-04), and Terry Sutton's eleventh festival on the quay (C-03, "the best night in years"), with his helpers Larry Baker, Toby Waller, Jonah Scott, Kate Holmes, Molly Scott and Mona Sayer: "every one of them pulled their weight".

**Notices, 1989:** houses bought by Alex Myers and Eddie Lane; Matt Sayer and Olive Shaw split; Sam Brook joined the Night Shift and retired from the clinic; Leo Lyons retired from the garage; Trevor Lane to step down as head mechanic next year; Una Waller came for the school; born, Colin Myers, and Andy Waller to Thea Waller and Colin Berry. **1990:** Gary Hughes came for the garage (February); the Night Shift at the Anchor (March); Sally Baker for the garage, with Terry Baker, now at the clinic (April); Ian Myers to university (May).

## Turn 4 · the edition of Thursday 14 June

By Monday the counter adds: *Garage. Sally Baker became head mechanic on Saturday.* (Toby's.) *Quay. The harbour day ran like clockwork; Harry Myers had a list for everything.* (Susie's.)

Your front ran, with Greta and John on record. John gave nothing on the mill's year, so Susie filled inside 1 with notices.

> **Front · Sawmill lets worker go**
> - John Holmes, the foreman, has let Greta Brook go from the sawmill. *Heard from John Holmes, for print; heard from Greta Brook, for print.*
> - "Her work had been poor for months. Gone to pieces, if you want it plain. I'd no choice." *Heard from John Holmes, for print.*
> - Greta Brook would give no reason. "He let me go. Put that, if you want. Nothing else." *Heard from Greta Brook, for print.*
>
> **Inside 2 · A year at the saw**
> - In April last year Maisie Myers left the school for a start at the sawmill. *Record (the Courier, April 1989).*

Ken reads it on Wednesday night and sends it down.

## Your prediction

You said John wouldn't mention Greta unless you named her, and that his reason wouldn't match hers. Asked whether anyone had gone, he named her and gave his reason. Greta gave none.

## What you can do

A chase turn is one act: **talk** (one person, up to three questions, each answer for print or not), **watch** (a place for a part of the day), **read** (the volumes, the county paper, a notice board) or **wait** (a day). The edition is the fourth turn: a front (500 words) and two inside (250 each), each claim marked saw it, a witness saw it, heard from someone named, or record; soften, hold or spike; plan with "if". Free: walking within a turn, hover, noting a tip, asking why of a public event you have found.
