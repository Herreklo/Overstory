# Round 4

## From memory
- **Greta Brook:** 66. Holmes let her go from the sawmill. She was diagnosed with cancer in February 1990, she walks the shore at night and she gives nothing away. I printed "gone to pieces" and then her illness, without asking her. In January she said she'd come through it, and that the real trouble was fifteen months of bad terms with Holmes.
- **John Holmes:** 44, the foreman. He put his name to "gone to pieces" without being pushed, and drinks his two pints at the end of the Anchor bar. I never got his answer on whether he knew she was ill. He's on speaking terms again with a Hannah Tanner I've never met.
- **Jonah Scott:** 41, Ken's brother, at the council under Terry. He tipped Toby about Terry's claims, not for print, then stonewalled me when I came asking. I've thought from the start that he wants Terry's chair.

## Marks
- Turn 3.1: bored. A whole turn spent to learn the office hours, the dead Insight roll all over again. Surprise: none.
- Turn 3.2: engaged. I came to apologise and got "It's in the paper now. Nothing to be done about it." It's the first answer I've had as a person rather than as a source, and it stung. Her trust didn't move either way, so the apology counted for nothing in the rules. Surprise: delight, the line.
- Turn 3.3: engaged. Gary is the first person in town to react to my paper ("not a word on me till the county paper had it"). That's the reactivity I've wanted since round 1. I named no questions, which was my fault. Surprise: delight.
- Turn 3.4: curious, and robbed. Ken says "Fine", then the game jumps eleven months and gives me the job in half a sentence. The win I played three weeks for happened offscreen. But the year page is the best thing in the game: Gary walked out of the garage and turned up at the sawmill, Greta came through it, Terry is going. It's history I can read, and none of it was me. Surprise: cheat, the job.

## Pull
7. At last the boss-versus-readers trap I asked for is here, with teeth, and both sides are Ken's own.

## Thinking aloud
Greta's January line turns my June fronts round. "Gone to pieces" was Holmes's side of a fifteen-month feud, and I printed it twice. No correction stands, but I know.

Now Ken's partner and Ken's brother both want Terry's chair, and both came to our counter. Both tips are in Toby's hand, and Toby wants Ken's job. Everyone at this paper has an angle except me. Sylvia wants print. Jonah's "it should go to her" is either a real step back or a line given in private so that it gets round. And Lily "dragged up what Terry did", so the claims never went away.

I won't touch Peter Scott's line on Colin and Thea. A couple's row overheard in a pub isn't news, whoever says it's for print. Molly's Ivan stays out too.

## Prediction
Ken will cut Sylvia from the front. He won't be protecting her. He'll cut her because printing his partner's bid in his own paper makes him look as if he's campaigning for her.

## Needs
- How did trust go from 7 to 9? Did holding Terry's lone line count, or nothing I did?
- Why did Jonah's trust go from 0 to 1, when I never spoke to him?
- Who appoints the chief officer, and when?
- Who is Hannah Tanner? Is she kin to Toby or Sid?
- Did my garage piece push Gary out?
- I'm asking a fourth time: does talking to Toby or Ken cost a turn? And who am I?
- Two Ivans, two Tobys, two Terrys, two Colins. Is that on purpose?

## Turns

   Q1: "Terry told me last June there was a mistake in his claims. Was it ever put right?" Q2: "Is that why he's stepping down?" Q3: "Will you put your name to either?" If she isn't there, I watch the shore and note who walks with whom.
   Expect: she knows more than she'll say, and gives one line.

   Q1: "You're stepping down next year. Why?" Q2: "Who should follow you, and who decides?" Q3: "Is the summer festival still on, for print?"
   Expect: the long look, and one line.
3. **Monday 27 May, afternoon, the council office: talk to Sylvia Waller.**
   Q1: "You've told us you're after Terry's job. Why should it be you?" Q2: "Ken will read this before it runs. Do you still want it in his paper?" Q3: "This winter Lily raised something Terry did. What was it, for print?"
   Expect: eager on Q1, careful on Q3.
4. **Wednesday 29 May: the edition, as a plan.**
   - **Front: "Who follows Terry Sutton?"** Terry's going is on record (the Courier, April). His reason, Sylvia's bid and Lily on the claims go in only if they're for print, each marked heard from someone named. Jonah isn't named as a candidate. The front names Sylvia, so I take it to Ken on Wednesday morning and stay while he reads. If he cuts her, I argue that she asked to be printed and that the whole office knows. If he pulls rank, I offer one line at the foot, from the record: the editor is Sylvia Waller's partner. If he refuses that too, the front is Terry's going and his festival, and the race is held.
   - **Inside 1: "I've come through it."** Greta's January words, for print. I'm torn on the Holmes sentence. It's the truest thing anyone has said about why she went, but he's had no reply in a year. So I run her first and last sentences and hold the middle until I've put it to him.
   - **Inside 2:** Terry's summer festival, if it's still on. Otherwise Gary's move to the sawmill, from the record only.
