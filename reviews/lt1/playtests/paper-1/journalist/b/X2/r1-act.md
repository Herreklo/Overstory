# Round 1

## Pull
6 — there's a boss with a conflict of interest, a job on the line and a tip I mustn't burn. That's already more teeth than Saltlight. But I nearly bounced off Ken's family tree.

## Thinking aloud
Ken's speech is a wall of names. Two Tobys (Tanner the reporter, Waller at the clinic), a Sylvia, a Thea, a Colin, an Andy, a Jonah and an Audrey, all in one breath. I didn't take it in as a story. I took it in as a list to memorise. What I did take in is that the editor's family runs the council and the clinic, and he wants to vet anything that names them. That's the hook for me. A compromised boss is a companion with an agenda, and I want to find the story that makes me choose between him and the readers.

The tip is a nice small dilemma. Neil gave it to Susie, Neil is her son, and he's standing right there at the saw. If I march up to Holmes and say "why did you sack Greta?", Holmes will know where it came from. So I'll come at it sideways and protect Neil. If the game notices that I did, I'll be delighted.

I also notice that Sid Tanner shares a surname with Toby. And I still have no self: "you're the new one" is all I'm given. Who am I, and why did I come here?

## Prediction
John Holmes won't mention Greta unless I name her. When I do, his reason for her going won't match hers. That gap between his account and hers is the story.

## Needs
- Who owns the sawmill? Holmes is only the foreman. Is the owner anyone on Ken's list?
- Where does Greta Brook live, and what was her job at the mill?
- Is Toby Tanner kin to Sid Tanner? Does that matter for who covers the mill?
- What does a source's trust do at 0 and at 3? And what moves sales as opposed to readers' trust?
- Who my character is: a name, where I came from, why this paper.

## Turns
1. **Thursday 7 June, morning, sawmill: Talk to John Holmes.**
   Q1: "I'm new at the Courier. How's the mill doing this year: orders, the crew?"
   Q2: "Are you taking anyone on, or has anyone left lately?" (This gives him the chance to raise Greta himself, so Neil isn't exposed.)
   Q3: If he names Greta: "Why did she go, and may I quote you?" If he doesn't: "Who owns the mill, and who do you answer to?"
   — Expect: a guarded "steady year". Either he leaves Greta out, which is a lie of omission I can use, or he gives a tidy reason. Either way I get an inside piece on the mill.

2. **Thursday 7 June, evening: Talk to Greta Brook at home** (I'll ask where she lives as I leave the yard).
   Q1: "Did you leave the mill, or were you let go?"
   Q2: "What reason were you given, and do you believe it?"
   Q3: "Would you say that in print, under your name?"
   — Expect: she was let go, for a reason she disputes. Maybe it touches the clinic or the council, which puts it on Ken's list.

3. **Friday 8 June, afternoon. Conditional.**
   If Greta's account and Holmes's disagree: Talk to Maisie Myers after her shift. Ask what she saw of Greta's going, whether anyone else is worried for their job, and whether she'll go on record.
   If Greta won't talk: Read the 1989 bound volume and this year's issues for anything on the mill (its owner, past lay-offs, accidents).
   — Expect: a third account that tips the balance, or a record that gives me the owner.

4. **Wednesday 13 June: the edition, as a plan.**
   - **Front:** If I have Greta on record plus either Holmes or Maisie: "Sawmill lets worker go." It gives both reasons side by side, each marked as heard from someone named. Neil and Susie are never named, and the tip book is never a source. If the story names anyone on Ken's list, I take it to him on Wednesday morning, not that night, and I watch what he does. If I don't have two named sources, I hold the story and run the mill's year (Holmes, on record) as the front.
   - **Inside 1:** The mill's year, orders and crew (Holmes, on record).
   - **Inside 2:** Whatever else Greta or Maisie gives me for print. If they give me nothing, a short piece from the bound volume on what the mill was a year ago, marked as record.
