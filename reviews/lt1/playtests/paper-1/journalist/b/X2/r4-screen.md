# Round 4: what happened

## Turn 1 · Thursday 23 May, evening · the shore

A still, grey evening. Among the walkers (hover): Greta Brook, Gary Hughes, Susie Lyons, Sam Brook, Grace Waller, Una Waller and Kate Holmes. Lily Lyons is walking on her own.

Hover: **Lily Lyons,** 34, the council office.

> **Lily Lyons, the shore, Thu 23 May, 20.30.**
> (a) Was Terry's mistake in the claims ever put right: "I've nothing to say about that." *No comment.*
> (b) Is that why he's stepping down: she doesn't answer. *No comment.*
> (c) Will she put her name to either: "No. Not about Terry. He's my chief officer." *Not for print.*

## Turn 2 · Friday 24 May, morning · the council office, Terry Sutton

Lily Lyons is at the counter; Sylvia Waller and Jonah Scott are at their desks. Terry Sutton comes to his door.

> **Terry Sutton, his door, Fri 24 May, 10.00.** "You've been asking about me. Nothing for the paper." He gives the same to each of your three questions. *No comment.*

## Turn 3 · Monday 27 May, afternoon · the council office, Sylvia Waller

Lily Lyons is at the counter. While you are there, neither Sylvia nor Jonah says a word to her, nor she to them.

Hover: **Sylvia Waller,** 28, the council office.

> **Sylvia Waller, her desk, Mon 27 May, 14.30.**
> (a) Why it should be her: "I want to be chief officer when Terry goes. That's all there is to it." *For print.*
> (b) Does she still want it in Ken's paper: "I told your counter you could print it, and I meant it." *For print.*
> (c) What Lily raised this winter: "I've nothing to say about that." *No comment.*

## Tuesday 28 May · the tip book

> *In Toby's hand, Tue 28 May, 9.10:* Sylvia Waller rang. "Lily's after Terry's job now too, and she and Jonah are at it. Not for print: they're both in my office."
>
> *In Toby's hand, Tue 28 May, 11.40:* Jonah Scott rang. "Lily wants it as well. She and Sylvia are both after it, and it shows. Not for print: they're in my office."

## Turn 4 · the edition of Thursday 30 May

Ken reads the front on Wednesday morning with you standing at his desk. "Take Sylvia's name out of it." You tell him she asked to be printed and the whole office knows. He reads it again. "Run it, then." It goes down as written.

Terry gave you nothing, so the front runs without his reason. Nobody confirmed the festival, so inside 2 is Gary's move, from the record.

> **Front · Who follows Terry Sutton?**
> - Terry Sutton, 64, will step down as chief officer at the council office next year. *Record (the Courier, April).*
> - Sylvia Waller, 28, of the council office, wants the job. "I want to be chief officer when Terry goes." *Heard from Sylvia Waller, for print.*
>
> **Inside 1 · "I've come through it"**
> - Greta Brook, 67, let go from the sawmill last June, told the Courier in January: "I've come through it. It's behind me." *Heard from Greta Brook, for print.*
>
> **Inside 2 · At the sawmill**
> - Gary Hughes started work at the sawmill this month. *Record (the Courier, May).*

## Your prediction

You said Ken would cut Sylvia from the front, so as not to look as if he were campaigning for her. He asked you to take her name out. He gave no reason beyond his rule.

## What you can do

A chase turn is one act: **talk** (one person, up to three questions, each answer for print or not), **watch** (a place for a part of the day), **read** (the volumes, the county paper, a notice board) or **wait** (a day). The edition is the fourth turn: a front (500 words) and two inside (250 each), each claim marked saw it, a witness saw it, heard from someone named, or record; soften, hold or spike; plan with "if". Free: walking within a turn, hover, noting a tip, asking why of a public event you have found.
