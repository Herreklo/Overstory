# Round 6: what happened

## Free · asking why

- Terry Sutton stepping down (April): the notice gives his reason, "64, and tired."
- Gary Hughes at the sawmill (May): the notice gives why. He was "the only one looking for work."

## Turn 1 · Thursday 6 June, afternoon · Mary Tanner's house

Mary Tanner doesn't work at the clinic. Her house is on the church hill. You find her in the shed at the side, at the bench, with something half mended in front of her.

Hover: **Mary Tanner,** 71, retired.

> **Mary Tanner, her shed, Thu 6 June, 15.00.**
> (a) Why she wanted the paper to know: she doesn't say why. "It's the second. The first was the December before last." *For print.* Then: "Mark had one last December. Not for the paper: he's my husband." *Not for print.*
> (b) Who looks after her now: she turns back to the bench. *No comment.*
> (c) In her own words: "Another one, on Sunday. I'm managing. You can put that." *For print.*

## Turn 2 · Friday 7 June, evening · the Anchor, Toby Waller

The Friday room is full. Evan Newman and Olive Shaw are behind the bar; Peter Scott and Sean Hale carry glasses. Hover gives the regulars: Terry Sutton, Toby Waller, Sylvia Waller, Sally Baker, Sam Brook, Neil Lyons, Mark Holmes, Vera Holmes, Leo Lyons, Larry Baker and Oliver Brook. John Holmes has his two pints at the end of the bar.

Toby Waller (hover: 62, the clinic) is at the bar.

> **Toby Waller, the bar, Fri 7 June, 21.00.**
> (a) How many pounds, and who was making a lot of it: "Terry was found out over the claims. A few pounds, that's all it was. How many, I couldn't tell you. Thirty-odd years he's given that office." *For print.*
> (b) What Lily raised this winter: "Lily Lyons brought the claims up again in February, in front of the whole office. Over a few pounds." *For print.*
> (c) His name to it: "Put my name to it." *For print.*

## Turn 3 · Monday 10 June, morning · the council office, Terry Sutton

Toby Waller was on record, so you went to Terry. Lily Lyons is at the counter; Sylvia Waller and Jonah Scott are at their desks. Terry comes to his door.

> **Terry Sutton, his door, Mon 10 June, 10.00.** Toby Waller says it was a few pounds; is that right: "There was a mistake in the claims. That's all I'll say." *For print.*

## Turn 4 · the edition of Thursday 13 June

Ken reads the front on Wednesday morning with you at his desk. It names Toby Waller, Sylvia's father. "Hold it." Your plan said that if he pulled rank you'd hold the whole story rather than run Terry's line alone, so you held it. Mary Tanner takes the front. Your plan had nothing else for inside 1, so Toby filled it with notices.

> **Front · "I'm managing"**
> - Mary Tanner, 71, had another stroke on Sunday 2 June, her second; the first was in December 1989. *Heard from Mary Tanner, for print.*
> - "Another one, on Sunday. I'm managing." *Heard from Mary Tanner, for print.*
>
> **Inside 2 · A summer festival?**
> - Terry Sutton told the Courier in December: "I mean to put on a summer festival on the quay." *Heard from Terry Sutton, for print.*
> - In May he would not say whether it is still on. *Heard from Terry Sutton.*

## Your prediction

You said Mary Tanner was Toby Tanner's kin and would give you a line for print about being on her own. She named her husband, Mark Holmes; nothing she said ties her to Toby. She gave you "I'm managing", and no comment on who looks after her.

# The next five weeks · 13 June to 18 July 1991

**13 June** sold 925. Ken: "Nine hundred and twenty-five." The editor's patience goes to 9.

**20 June.** You put the claims piece back in front of Ken, with Terry's answer in it. "I said hold it." You hold it. Lily Lyons at the counter: "You were asking Toby Waller about me. Nothing for the paper." Nothing else stands up, and Toby's page goes on the front. Sold 850.

**27 June.** Lily, on the shore of an evening: "I want to be chief officer when Terry goes. I've said as much in the office." She says it for print. The front is "Two in the race", her words beside Sylvia's of 27 May. Ken: "Take Sylvia out of it." You tell him Lily alone would be unfair the other way. "Run it, then." On the claims piece: "Hold it." Inside, from the counter: the harbour day went off well enough on Saturday, with Maisie Myers running it. Sold 877. Patience 8.

**4 July.** From the counter: Gary Newman moved to the town for a job at the garage, with Lynn Newman. He is your front, from the record. As to why, the file says only "ask someone who was there". The claims piece is held again. Sold 893.

**11 July.** Ken reads the claims piece a fourth time. "Take Toby's name out." You tell him Toby said it to a full bar and put his name to it. He reads it again. "Run it." The front is "A few pounds": Terry's line of June 1990 and his line of June 1991, word for word the same; Toby Waller's three answers; and Lily in February, from Toby. Nobody complains. Sold 932. Patience 8: one down for insisting, one back for the sale.

**18 July.** The tip book is empty all week. The county paper has had nothing from the town in all five weeks. Toby's page goes on the front. Sold 859.

**Where you stand, 18 July:**
- Sales: 925, 850, 877, 893, 932, 859.
- Readers' trust: 10 of 10.
- The editor's patience: 8 of 10.
- On the staff for good, and no correction stands against your pages.

The race goes on. Sylvia and Lily are on record, Jonah is at 0 in your notebook, nobody has given way, and nobody has said who decides.
