# r03: cold critic

**Verdict: far.** At a glance: "three clean kit models on a grey table, one with planks nailed across its windows."

## The read at a glance
- **Eye level:** three new houses. 107 reads *boarded*, not neglected; 108's gable top melts into the sky; the row is one value. No era, no age.
- **Street stop:** the best view. 108 reads Swiss-style and kept; 109 a dark store with a hoist; 107 a shed with trellises. Three ages: none; every arris equally crisp, every line dead straight.
- **12 m:** the roofs take over: 107 a blotched grey, 108 and 109 two black slabs.
- **High:** a kit on a table; in low sun the cast shadows are the only weight in the sheet.

## Faults, worst first
1. **Windows (every view, both lights).** Ref 10: pane 24 L*, wall 87, a gap of 63. r03 street stop overcast: 108 panes 50–52, wall 66, gap 15; close-glass pane 45 vs wall 73; low sun 56–59 vs 80. The glass is milky paper over a grey room: no dark depth, no sky flash, no sheer as a pale edge in a dark hole. The dark eyes are grey eyes. r03 moved nothing: r01 panes 51/49, r03 52/50.
2. **Value structure, overcast.** Sky 71–72, 108's white 66–72: house and sky are one value, the apex a bargeboard line. No one white (the 91 max is a frame). Many equal darks: 109 wall 12, 109 roof 16, 108 roof p10 19. Corners: 108 gable 70 vs side 66, a 4 L* turn; 107 front 44 vs its end wall 59, inverted (r01: 41, the right way).
3. **Value structure, low sun.** The roofs are the darkest mass (109 roof 4–7, 108 roof 7–11), bigger and blacker than any window (panes 17–59): the eye lands on two black slabs, not on the lit gable or the windows.
4. **Roofs.** 107's pantiles are a pale lichen mottle (overcast p25 19, p75 72): blotches, not courses. The wave along the courses, the one mark that says pantile, is absent on all three at every distance.
5. **Three ages: not shown.** 1828, 1850 and 1901 share one crispness and one white (107 front p95 77, 108 p95 82). 107's neglect is four identical airbrushed smudges under its sills and planed battens at L 75, lighter than its wall, across open glass: a trellis. Ref 09 is whole plywood sheets darker than the wall, black behind. Three winters means chalked paint, a slipped board, a sagging gutter, moss on the shaded slope: none drawn.
6. **108's plinth** lit to 80 in low sun, as white as its wall; ref 10's is 59–67, darker and dirtier.
7. **Ground** 52–54 everywhere; no street, no wet, no contact beyond a soft occlusion.

## Good
Massing and proportion hold against refs 10, 01 and 04: 108's gable, band and sills; 109's hoist dormer and casements; 107's storey-and-a-half and downpipe. The low-sun turn on 108 (gable 83, side 42). 109's dark against 108's white is the one strong read.

## Against r01
The same within 1–3 L* on every surface measured; the one visible move (107's end wall lighter than its front, overcast eye level) goes the wrong way. Not better.

## The colour still
Not stunning. Ground a khaki card (hue 78, chroma 8), sky a flat pale card (chroma 9). The ox-red is mud: 109 front hue 36–57, chroma 10–16, L 25–40, a dark brown where ox-red sits near hue 35 at chroma 30 and up. 108's white has a yellow-green cast (hue 100, chroma 6). Three paint pots on grey kit models: no air, no wet, no accent, nothing warm in a cool hour.

## The one change
Make the glass a dark eye. At the street stop in overcast the pane's median sits 50–60 L* below its wall: wall 66, pane 10–18. Build it as a dark room behind the glass (L 8–12 through it), one soft sky flash in the top third of each pane only at the reflected angle (L 55–65, Fresnel: strong grazing, near zero face-on), the sheer a pale band of L 35–45 along head and jambs inside the frame, never a fill, white muntins over the dark. Pass when 108's first-floor pane median is 18 or under with p90 65 or under in overcast, and the close-glass pane median is 20 or under against its wall at 73. Then 108's front reads as ref 10.
