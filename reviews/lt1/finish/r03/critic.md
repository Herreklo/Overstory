# Round 3: the cold critic (Fable, blind to our verdicts, 28 October)

**Verdict: far.** Beside photo 09 it reads as a clean new model with a peel preset on it, not a derelict house.

**Faults, worst first:**
- **The boarding sheet.** It is the story's anchor, and it fails. The sheet sits at nearly the wall's value, while in 09 the sheets are the darkest things on the facade. Nothing says plywood: no face grain, checking, edge lift, nail heads or join lip. The stains are airbrushed clouds with no source, and the lower streaks read as frosted glass. At 20 m the window reads as glazing with two white bars.
- **The battens.** Planed, pale, clean and parallel: "nailed last week". In 09 they are rough sawn, grey, skewed and ragged-ended.
- **The paint loss.** Stamped lobed flakes, all one scale, with a hard dark rim and a flat grey fill, spread evenly. At 20 m they turn into speckle. Real loss sits at drip edges, board ends, sill ends and the lowest courses, with a chalky halo. 09's wall body hardly flakes.
- **The wall tone and the laps.** Boards are flat paper-white with no gradient to the underside, and the lap shadow is a ruled ink line. The lighter rectangles read as texture seams.
- **The dirt runs** cross five laps unbroken. Real runs restart at each lap, start at the sill ends, and are narrow with dark cores.
- **The trims** are crisp and new. The plinth reads as stage concrete. The overcast light is a stop too bright.

**Good:**
- The mesh: chamfers, lap thickness, the sections of the battens and trims, and the lap scale, which matches 09.
- The sun's shadows.
- The restraint in how much paint is lost.
- Nothing sparkles at distance.

**Fixability:** the method is sound. Most faults can be fixed within it: the battens, the laps and board tone, the runs, the plinth and the trims. Two need authored marks under the procedural layer, placed only where the baked masks say: a real plywood face (a scanned or hand-painted tile) and a flake atlas in which bare wood has its own value and grain, with a chalky halo and no rim. Plain procedural noise is at its limit for paint loss. No sculpting is needed.

**The one change:** make the sheet read as weathered ply, the darkest value on the wall, with a real ply face and every stain sourced from a batten end, a nail or a join.
