# Round 4: the cold critic (Fable, blind to our verdicts, 28 October)

**Verdict: far.** Beside photo 09 it reads as "a freshly clad white shed with a boarded window, not a house empty for three winters". It would pass as a new build, not as a derelict.

**Faults, worst first:**
- **The wall paint.** One flat near-white over every board. The laps are a uniform dark line, which reads as an extruded profile. There is no grime under the laps, no rain streaks and no breathing from board to board, and in overcast it blows out to paper. This alone makes it read as new at every distance.
  - Our measure disagrees on the board values: neighbour |dL\*| is 1.81 and 1.62, against 09's 0.37–1.7.
- **The paint loss.** Small grey confetti, all at one scale, in rows along the board edges, with a visible repeat. The bare wood is flat mid-grey, not dark, silvered and grained. 09's loss sits in big ragged patches on the ledges where water sits.
- **The sheet's stains.** A hard-edged island that reads as a decal. The top panel should be the dirtiest and is the cleanest.
- **The battens** are lighter than the wall in sun, so they read as painted or fresh.
- **The trims** are a clean machined box, with no drip stain under the sill, and a seam at the head cap.
- **The plinth** has a panel repeat and reads as precast. **The roof** is uniform tiles with a white verge, like a toy.
- **One fine speckle** sits on every material at the same scale, which gives away the procedural method.
- **At 20 m nothing says derelict.** At 12 m and from high, it is a white box with a grey lid.

**Good:**
- The lap profile and chamfers in low sun.
- The battens' placement and skew.
- The depth of the reveal.
- The ply's base value and sheen.
- The sunlit window crop: the one passage near the photo.

**Fixability:** mostly within the method. The critic names:
- per-board value, grime under the laps and streaks;
- a flake atlas at three scales with dark grained bare wood;
- a directional wicking gradient.

A scanned peeling-paint tile and real scanned plinth and roof materials would get further.

**The one change:** the wall paint: a value per board, with a gradient of grime and drips under every lap and rain streaks down the face.

**Against round 3's critic:** the details differ (round 3 said 09's wall body hardly flakes and asked for the sheet first). The whole agrees: both read a clean new model, not a derelict house. That is the fourth round in a row whose list of fixes did not move that read.
