# Painted finish p2: the cold critic (Fable, blind to our verdicts, 28 October)

Judged against photos 09 and 01, Hopper, Stålenhag, Dredge, Sohlberg and the art-direction page's light rows.

**Verdict: far.** "A clean grey kiosk on a concrete podium, one window boarded over, shown in a material viewer."

**The read at a glance.** New shed, one storey, boarded window. "Boarded" lands at every distance; old, 1828, a loft, three winters and failing paint do not: nothing is worn, and the sill mark reads as a shadow.

**Faults, worst first:**
- The paint failure (every still): a soft airbrushed cloud centred under the sill, where water does not go. Water leaves a sill at its ends in narrow lines that darken each lap's lower edge and end in flakes with bare wood inside. The cloud crosses the laps unchanged, its "peel" a jagged outline round the same value; at 20 m it is a smudge (wall 63, cloud 55). The "silvered slivers" are two-pixel specks.
- The overcast street still has no value structure: street face 63, side face 60 (page: ~15 apart), plinth 49 on ground 49; the foot dissolves.
- Massing reads as a kiosk: a bunker podium of 1 m blocks, a shed's pitch, pantiles twice too big and scalloped at the eave like a canopy; nothing says 1828 or a loft.
- Clapboard reads as vinyl (eye): every lap identical, no board ends, joints, nail lines or a split board; photo 09 has them all.
- The ply reads as marbled slate: a soft swirl, no lifting veneer, no dark foot; the bright slit above it reads as a gap, and a dotted black line runs along the eave (eye, sun).

**Good:** the boarding as one dark shape on the pale wall, legible at 20 m and from above; the cast shadows read as light; lap lines gone by 12 m; the rust bleed under the nail heads, the one mark both fact and designed.

**The colour still:** not stunning and not its own: a URP sample of beige vinyl siding on a sunny afternoon. Lit wall 72–75 L* at chroma 5–7, shade 32–51 and grey, nothing above chroma 10, against the page's 88, 30 in the sky's blue and one accent. Light: the cast shadows. Paint: the cloud, the ply swirl and the beige itself, a tint over clay.

**The one change:** replace the cloud with run-off that obeys the wood: from each sill and batten end, one streak 2–4 cm wide, 10–15 L* below the wall, darkening the lower 2 cm of each lap it crosses for two to four boards, ending in a hard-edged flaked patch of bare wood a step darker than the paint.
