# How masters design frames like ours

LT1 corner, 27 October 2026. Text sources only. Rules for our three frames: up the granite steps, the house front at eye level, 12 m over the roofs. Brackets name the source.

## In short

Masters decide the picture before rendering it. Four or five masses, valued by each plane's angle to the light; one small darkest dark near the viewer; the white on the focus; air lifting and cooling each layer behind. Colour comes from the sky, one key, one accent; white changes temperature, not value. Shapes are flat and nameable, detail sits only where it means something, edges are hard only at the focus. Outstanding frames have one idea of light, simplified over many tries, in a real place made exact.

## 1. Value plan

1. Sort surfaces into four planes before lighting, valued by their angle to the sky. Drizzle, lightest first: sky, wet treads, roofs, walls. [Carlson]
2. Local value moves a plane, not across groups: a white wall in drizzle sits just under sky and wet steps, above all else built. [Carlson; Gurney, overcast]
3. Darkest dark small and near: an open door, the nearest eave's shadow, a trunk at the frame edge. Lighten every other dark. [Gurney, smog]
4. In fog or against the light, two groups only: sky with lit whites, and the rest. No inner detail in dark silhouettes. [Gurney, smog]
5. Step layers back lighter, cooler, nearer the sky, through an artist-painted ramp, not one fog colour. [Firewatch: Ng; Calahan]
6. From 12 m the roofs are the big slant mass; walls thin light strips that lead, chimneys the small darks. [Carlson]

## 2. Colour script

1. One key per light, one accent from across the wheel ("mood and accent"). Mix the family heads first; use nothing outside them. [Gurney, gamut]
2. Lean the key blue-violet, the accent lamp yellow in a window or lantern; avoid sepia. [Stålenhag] Take the key from the sky. [Firewatch: Ng]
3. Low sun: sunlit white with almost no yellow [Hopper, Second Story Sunlight]. Shaded white takes what it faces: sky blue turned up, granite lower, pink beside red tiles. [Gurney, white building]
4. Drizzle: fewest colours, warm and cool greys, white trim the lightest thing; red tiles carry the chroma. [Hammershøi; Gurney, overcast]
5. Blue hour: close blues, lights as small gold points; white walls pale blue-violet, still the lightest built mass. [Whistler; Sohlberg]
6. Turn white by temperature: folds told by temperature, grounded by a few dark accents. [Sorolla]

## 3. Shape language

1. Choose each view for its lines, "the architectural stance", then its light. [Hammershøi]
2. Big forms as flat shapes, strong silhouettes, simplified insides. A shape the team nicknames (Pride Rock) is working. [Firewatch: Ng]
3. Trees by far silhouette; detail only the lower branches; simplify outlines with distance. In soft light foliage is lightest at its outer, upper edge. [Firewatch: Ng; Gurney, foliage]
4. Granite as a few angular "rock words" combined into big shapes, little texture. [Firewatch: Ng]
5. Houses reduced to essentials; stretch or compress a front for the picture. [Hopper, Early Sunday Morning] Pick, choose and group; nature rarely composes. [Whistler]

## 4. Edges

1. Crisp edges only where silhouette meets sky, or lit form meets dark, at the focus. Lose them in shade; the eye fills them in. [Gurney, edge induction]
2. Soften and cool edges with distance; judge squinting. Mostly soft, one loud passage, like musical dynamics. [Schmid]
3. Veil the frame slightly so the few found edges, a white door frame, a lit window, carry the focus. [Hammershøi]

## 5. Detail hierarchy

1. Detail is the signal: plain shapes everywhere, rich texture only where the story is; for us, wear and repair at the focus. [Firewatch: Ng]
2. Suppress the rest: illegible signs, loose ornament, people implied by curtains; doors and windows clear only on the brightest house. [Hopper, Early Sunday Morning; Gurney, edge induction]
3. Leave much loose; make the few things that matter exact. [Oga] Keep rest areas empty: plain wall, sky, fog. [Hammershøi]

## 6. Focus and composition

1. Frame with near dark masses like a stage curtain: house corners, a trunk at the steps' foot. Keep that foreground calm; it holds the mood. [Sohlberg]
2. Focus where the white, the hardest edge, the accent and the detail meet. [Gurney; Hopper]
3. Depth in three: near frame, lit middle (the landing house), glimpse beyond (harbour, a door through a door), one landmark fixed to steer by. [Hammershøi; Firewatch, IGN]
4. Let steps and walls funnel the eye, so players "naturally frame the shot well themselves". [Firewatch: Ng; Moss]
5. Hopper invented light for its shadow shapes; our sun is fact, so choose camera and moment for them. [Hopper, Early Sunday Morning]
6. At eye level the front is one plane: eave shadow the dark, one lit window the event. [Hopper; Gurney]

## 7. Outstanding, not merely correct

1. One idea of light. Hopper: "What I wanted to do was to paint sunlight on the side of a house."
2. Distil over years: fifteen years of Rondane, people in few versions, the last leaving the mountains towering. [Sohlberg] The same view again, only the light changing. [Hammershøi]
3. A real place, walked and photographed; the sketch fixes design, composition and palette before rendering. [Stålenhag]
4. A colour gradient owned so fully a slice of it names the work. [Firewatch: Ng] Colour rising with the emotion. [Eggleston]
5. How a place felt, not only how it looked; cameras miss colour. [Calahan] A place you would feel at ease in alone, keeping its age. [Oga]

## Reference candidates (private study, owner's permission first)

1. Second Story Sunlight, Edward Hopper, 1960, Whitney: https://whitney.org/collection/works/873. White house in sun, white kept white.
2. Cape Cod Evening, Edward Hopper, 1939, National Gallery of Art: https://www.nga.gov/research/publications/online-editions/american-paintings-1900-1945-cape-cod-evening-1939. White house at evening against dark trees.
3. Early Sunday Morning, Edward Hopper, 1930, Whitney: https://whitney.org/collection/works/46345. Low light as subject; a street reduced to essentials.
4. Fisherman's Cottage, Harald Sohlberg, 1906, Art Institute of Chicago: https://www.artic.edu/artworks/152437/fisherman-s-cottage. Lit white cottage at twilight framed by dark trees: our blue hour.
5. Winter Night in the Mountains, Harald Sohlberg, 1914, Nasjonalmuseet: https://www.nasjonalmuseet.no/en/collection/object/NG.M.01185. Norwegian blue night; foreground as stage curtain.
6. Street in Røros in Winter, Harald Sohlberg, 1903, Nasjonalmuseet: https://www.nasjonalmuseet.no/en/collection/object/NG.M.00882. A Norwegian timber-house street as one design.
7. Sunshine in the Drawing Room III, Vilhelm Hammershøi, 1903, Nationalmuseum Stockholm: https://collection.nationalmuseum.se/en/collection/item/41485/. Warm and cool greys, white trim as the light.
8. View of Heath Street by Night, Atkinson Grimshaw, 1882, Tate: https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artworks/grimshaw-view-of-heath-street-by-night-t00626. A lamplit night street.
9. Nocturne: Blue and Gold, Old Battersea Bridge, J. M. Whistler, c.1872–5, Tate: https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artworks/whistler-nocturne-blue-and-gold-old-battersea-bridge-n01959. Close blues, gold points: sea-fog blue hour.
10. New Ōhashi Bridge, Kawase Hasui, 1926, LACMA: https://collections.lacma.org/object/88120. Night rain, lanterns in wet ground, rain as designed marks.
11. The Temple Kankaiji, Beppu, Kawase Hasui, c.1927, LACMA: https://collections.lacma.org/object/87818. Rain over a hillside village: drizzle on a stepped town.
12. Sewing the Sail, Joaquín Sorolla, 1896, Ca' Pesaro (Google Arts & Culture): https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/sewing-the-sail-joaquin-sorolla-y-bastida/ggHMkbvZk1yLaw. White in sun and shade, told by temperature.
13. Tales from the Loop and Things from the Flood, Simon Stålenhag, 2014 and 2016: https://www.simonstalenhag.se/tftl.html, https://www.simonstalenhag.se/tftf.html. Ordinary Swedish places in grey light; the lead style.
14. Firewatch, Campo Santo (Olly Moss, Jane Ng), 2016: https://www.firewatchgame.com/. Colour layers by distance, designed tree silhouettes.
