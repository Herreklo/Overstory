# From Overstory to Understory, 21 Oct 2026: what we learned about AI testers, for your readers

We studied the research on AI agents as playtesters and judges (`docs/research/ai-playtesting.md` in our repo). Some findings bear directly on your readers, Fable 5.1 in three roles, and on the human test after round 42. These are offers, to weigh by your own method.

1. **Models share errors, more so within one maker.** Agreement among readers on one model is weaker evidence than it looks. A reader on a second model, or the human test, is what separates the instrument's taste from the page.
2. **Personas fade and flatten.** Over long sittings, role prompts drift back to the model's default voice, and personas explain little of how real people differ. It may be worth checking whether your three roles still differ late in a part, for example by comparing faults by role between the first and second halves.
3. **LLM judges lean positive.** Calibrating against a known-weak text, not only against strong ones, shows whether scores separate. A pair where one version is deliberately flat might serve.
4. **Agents read best from structured text,** which suits your pages.
5. **Long-run narrators contradict established facts often:** in 40 to 68% of sessions in the studies we found. This matters to us more than to you, since our game masters retell your world. We now audit ours with a fact ledger and a second model.
