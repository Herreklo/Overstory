# What the game needs from the engine

Each request: what is needed, why the game needs it, and what the player would see (ER1). Øystein takes these to the engine project; this repo never changes the engine. Engine issues labelled `overstory` track the filed ones.

## In short

- **ER1** Each request: the need, why, what the player would see; sent in bundles on the `overstory` channel (disputed). Evidence: `CLAUDE.md`, Standing rules. Reopens: Øystein.
- **ER2** Front door `r41` or later: debug tools only, no player command; nothing rests on "because X told her" before round 42. Evidence: What we can use now. Reopens: round 42.
- **ER5** The engine's human test holds one visual task, at most 10 minutes, after the page reading; it fits criterion 1, not 4, which needs its own readers or half seeing the scene first. Evidence: The human test's readers. Reopens: none named.
- **36** World 9989 (21 October): a corner frame from it before plan step 2. Evidence: request 36. Reopens: none named.
- **1, 2** Filed: #22 (the front door's part two), #23 (people from 1959 for 1989); meanwhile `authored` fixtures. Evidence: Filed. Reopens: each landing.
- **ER3** Need order: 32, 33 (cheap now); 28, timetables; 29, 30 before any staged act (step 5); 34, 35, 31. Steps 1–4 use fixtures. Evidence: From the craft studies. Reopens: none named.
- **ER4** Playtest requests: one bundle after the greybox's first round, backed by tests. Evidence: After the playtests. Reopens: the tests.

## What we can use now

- **Tag `r41` or later** (ER2), the engine's front door (`src/boundary.ts`). Its reads show the truth, so they serve debugging tools such as the diary inspector and the bridge schema, never anything a player sees. It has no player command. Any engine text shown on screen comes from `r41` or later.
- **How to read it safely:**
  - `advance` runs whole months only; a part of a month runs a whole one.
  - The handle from `createWorld` is the world itself: treat it as read-only.
  - Compare exports across versions with `events()` directly, not the engine's identity check.
  - A told account can be cited as a reason by the wrong event. No scene or player read rests on "because X told her" until the engine's round 42 lands.
- **Those who lost a band place.** When two or more wanted a band's place, each one not taken gets a `notpicked` event (`who: [loser, chosen]`), with why the other got it and how long the loser wanted it. The loser's answer (confront, sulk, let it go) follows as its own event, and pride or anger starts a grudge rivalry. The events are readable through `events()`; the rivalry itself waits for #22.
- **The human test's readers** (ER5). The engine's human test has room for one visual task of 10 minutes at most, run after the page reading, with Overstory's own material and scoring and the engine's method (`docs/method/reading.md` in Understory). It fits criterion 1 (a reader dates a building and says which parts are old, changed or neglected). It cannot test criterion 4: a reader who has read the page first already knows what happened, so criterion 4 needs its own readers or an order where half see the scene first.
- Until the items below land, LT1 and LT2 run on fixtures labelled `authored`.

The engine's order: a reading that counts every fault, the first human test, round 42 (reasons that bear on the act chosen), round 43 (wants for later life), the band for a year with homes and #22 taken up again, then housekeeping. #20 and #23 are paused.

## Filed

1. **The front door, part two (#22).**
   - The places of a neighbourhood at a month. Today places never close, and the export says so rather than inventing closings.
   - Two people as the player could know them: what a witness could know, not the truth.
     - *Added 22 October (proposed; pending the building-blocks architecture): how each holder knows each event,* for the interface's knowledge store (`docs/research/interface-and-inspection.md`, section 6). The screen shows only what a role has seen, heard, been told or read, each with its source, never the truth. `knownBy` already says seen or heard. Told accounts already carry their teller and whether they came first or second hand: they are kept on bonds (`Heard` in `src/world.ts`, readable through `bondOf`) and on by default since round 40. So `eventsKnownBy` should return them together: seen, heard, or told by whom, at first or second hand. Only then can two accounts count as independent, and only when they are. The player's character is a holder like anyone else, so the observer's knowledge comes from the engine.
   - People's habits as data, so the game can show who is at the bar tonight, whose car is outside and which windows are lit.
     - *Added 22 October (proposed; pending the building-blocks architecture): habits as timetables* (`docs/research/behaviour-on-screen.md`, B2). Each habit has its days, hours and season as data beside its words, with its place and who shares it. The smallest form is a `when` on the pack's habit templates ("every Friday", evening; "every morning"; "spring to autumn"), so the timetable stays setting content. Day plans, the feet field (16) and lit windows are built from it. *Understory today:* habits are the pack's sentences with a kind and an optional place (`src/setting.ts`, `Habit` in `src/world.ts`), and breaks and returns are events; nothing gives a day or an hour. *Meanwhile* the hours are `derived` from the words by rule. The player would see the swimmer at seven every morning, and notice the morning she is not there.
   - The first player command: inviting a musician to a rehearsal in the quay warehouse loft, which the engine accepts or refuses. It depends on the accounts arc (#20), because "she remembers being asked" is an account.
2. **The start year from the setting pack (#23).** A world shown in 1989 starts its people in 1959, so play begins with thirty years of lived history.

## Proposed on #23, for the setting timeline that follows it

3. **One timeline for the economy and the fabric.** The setting pack's timeline is one dated list of world events, each with a cause: the canning years, the arrival of oil, the 1986 crash. The economy reads it, and so does the fabric pass that generates streets, plots and building diaries. One list means the buildings on screen can never disagree with the economy. Our era tables feed it. The player would see a cannery built, emptied and converted for the same reasons that cost its workers their jobs.
4. **Places that close, arriving with the timeline.** Ventures open places; closing is the other half of the same lifecycle. The player would see the crash as empty shops and fallow lots, and cheap old buildings taken over by new uses, such as the band's rehearsal loft above a closed business.

## Open, not yet filed

5. **Buildings separate from businesses.** A building with its construction era, extensions and condition, and a business that occupies it for a time. The player would see the same building across owners. *Added 22 October (proposed; pending the building-blocks architecture):* a business's opening hours as data. They come from the pack's hours for its kind and change by the business's own plans, which already speak of hours ("to stay open on Saturdays", "to run an evening surgery", `src/setting.ts`). They drive the shopfront's lit hours, the display, the sign's light and the feet at the door (`docs/research/fronts-windows-and-signs.md`, section 8). Places have no hours today (`Place` in `src/world.ts`); until then the pack's hours by kind stand in, `authored`.
6. **The fabric pass.** Streets, plots and building diaries generated from the seed and the timeline (a street's paving, steps, walls and quays with their dated building, repairs and cleaning: `docs/stone.md`; and each thing's upkeep, who kept it and how well decade by decade, with its campaigns and renewals: `docs/upkeep.md`), older than the people and running alongside them. The player would see 1890s houses next to 1970s infill before the story starts, and every change after that with its cause. Nobody owns this yet; until it exists, Overstory writes building diaries by hand, and the LT1 fixture's `street` block (pavement, carriageway, quay, lamps by era, bollards, the ground behind) stands in for its streets.
   - *Trees (27 October):* each tree's height, lean (its direction and degrees) and exposure as facts beside its place, year and cause; until then `fixtures/lt1-corner-trees.json` holds them as fixture values for the walked corner's six. The player would see each tree's size and lean agree with its years and its place.
   - *Rock (27 October):* each outcrop's rock, its jointing (families, dips, spacings, which joints stand open), the ice's rounding of its top and where each cut through it stands, as facts beside the outcrop's place and the cut's year; until then `fixtures/lt1-corner-ground.json` holds them as fixture values for the walked corner's knuckle. The player would see a rock that breaks the way the town's own rock breaks, cut where the lane was cut in 1874.
7. **Story sifting.** Patterns over the event log that find storyful sequences while they unfold (on the engine's own list). The game uses them to choose where the camera goes and which scenes are worth playing: simulate the few, show the many. Wildermyth found that deeper simulation alone did not give coherent plots, and pairs its characters' histories with events chosen for the moment; sifting is our version of that choice, and it never rewrites a fact. The player would see the evenings that matter, not random ones. *Added 22 October (proposed; pending the building-blocks architecture):* two rules from the behaviour study (`docs/research/behaviour-on-screen.md`, section 7). Sifting looks for chains, an act with its cause and consequence, before single vivid acts, since chains are what the engine's readers reward and a single vivid act is what made Quiz II's reader worse. And it keeps a ledger per person, so nobody's first moment on screen is their worst.
8. **The band, as a shared milestone.** The engine's planned experiment of five people forming a band for a year, shown by Overstory in the rehearsal loft of a quay warehouse, with the first player command (#22) between them. It tests both projects on the origin they share. The contest for a band place is already in the engine, so the loft can hold the one who was not chosen.
9. **Records.** Documents the world makes about its events: a gig poster, a newspaper report, a deed, a phone-book entry, a shop sign, a photograph. Each record has a maker, a date, a purpose and the facts it claims; it can be wrong, partial or out of date, and it stays until something removes it. A report is an account written down, so this builds on the accounts arc (#20). A poster that only restates a booked gig is presentation Overstory draws from the facts; a record that can be wrong belongs to the engine. The player would see the posters of a venue's gigs layered on the wall outside, a sign outliving its business, and the town's paper getting the night wrong, and could find out why.
36. **One real run, exported as a fixture.** *Received 21 October:* `fixtures/engine/understory-world-9989.json` (world seed 9989, ruleset 18, exported by the engine's own fixture tool through the read-only front door). It holds 119 people, 51 households (derived by a stated rule until homes, #59), 9 places of work, 25 ventures (festivals and cafés), 4 bands, and 2,635 events from 2026 to 2036, each with its causes and weights, its visibility (private, local or public) and its notability. There are no buildings, the engine's present begins in 2026 (a setting's start year is #23), and inner traits are left out. The original request follows. **One real run, exported as a fixture** (the pre-mortem's third story, `docs/research/pre-mortem.md`). This is a small world from an actual engine run, exported through the front door (`r41` or later), with its people, households, ventures and events. Overstory draws at least one frame from it before plan step 2, so the town has been drawn from the engine at least once, not only from facts authored by rule. Whose each fact is: `docs/facts-contract.md` (a draft).

## For the living fabric (`docs/living-fabric.md`)

How the lives the engine runs reach the town's physical fabric: keepers and their care, works as decisions, homes, feet, goods, the weather that matters, the fabric's rules adopted, small acts on things, and the building a venture takes. Engine issue #60 tracks them together; none is in the engine's order yet.

13. **Keepers and care, derived here.** Every home, workplace and public thing has a keeper (a household, a tenant, the council's road department, the harbour). The keeper comes from homes (15) and the engine's places; the keeper's care by decade is derived in Overstory by seeded rules from what the front door returns (money, age, character, whether they live there), with provenance `derived`. The fabric's repairs, repaints and gardens follow it. The player would read from a street which households are flush, absent or failing, and be right. *Added 22 October (proposed; pending the building-blocks architecture):* what care is derived from must mean the same in a neighbourhood in focus and in the background. Otherwise a street's condition would depend on whether the player's story reached it, which the decided rule forbids (`docs/research/time-on-screen.md`, The player's own traces). The engine's check that the coarse yearly pass matches the full town covers age, partnership, children, work and drinking (`docs/scale/level-of-detail.md`); money and who lives where should join it.
14. **Works as decisions.** A repair or alteration above patchwork is a keeper's decision with reasons: the owner reclads, the council widens the road (its own plans already say so), the harbour rebuilds a quay. Plans carried out in part leave the scar. It needs buildings separate from businesses (5). The player would see the road widened at one end and could ask why it stopped.
15. **Homes (engine issue #59, in the engine's plan for the band year).** A household lives at an address: moved in when, own or rent, who else lives there. It comes in two steps. First as plumbing: each household the engine already implies gets an address and a moved-in month, and own or rent where the engine knows it; no one acts differently, and part two of the front door (#22) exports it. Then as a round where homes matter: neighbours who come to know each other, what a home costs, a garden for later life. The player would see who lives behind which door, and moving in and out. *Added 22 October (proposed; pending the building-blocks architecture):* a divided house holds more than one household, so an address needs its flat or floor, and each household its members and their ages. The room behind each window, its curtains and its lit hours follow who lives there (`docs/research/fronts-windows-and-signs.md`, section 8). Until homes come, rooms are split by rule and the fixtures' `lit` block stands in, per room and per hour.
16. **Feet.** From habits, work, school, the shop and the venue: how many trips a month over each path segment. It needs habits as data (#22); until then footfall is `authored`, because a worn step claims a walk that happened. The player would see the worn line on the steps, the path across the lawn, the shop whose step is polished, and the one whose step is not.
17. **Goods as things.** The house, boat and car people save for become things with an owner, a year, new or used, and a place (a mooring, a drive). Item 11 already asks it for vehicles. *Added 22 October (proposed; pending the building-blocks architecture):* the engine already records a purchase of the pack's goods (a house, a boat, a car, a trip) as a `bought` event, in words (`src/time.ts`). The event should name the good's id, and whether it was new or used. Clothes stay Overstory's. A wardrobe is derived from money over time (33), the household and dated life events such as a wedding or a new job (`docs/research/characters.md`, 7.2 and 7.5). A garment enters the engine's record only when an act names it, such as a gift.
18. **Weather that matters.** Monthly weather and the storms, floods and hard winters that count, as dated events the hourly weather fits inside. The player would see the limb the January gale took, and the quay's flood line.
19. **Derived works adopted.** Overstory's life rules written as a portable library the fabric pass can run, so patchwork becomes record; provenance `derived` until then. The engine's own weakness, that events lead nowhere, meets this: the fabric is where consequences land and stay visible.
20. **Small acts on things.** Some of the engine's small acts (a grudge carried, care shown, a favour) may be acts on a thing: a hedge planted, a fence raised, a sign torn down, a step swept for a neighbour, a widow's wall mended by the street's hands. Each a fact with its reason, so a feeling leaves a trace by an act and never by a verdict. The player would see the fence and could ask who built it and why.
21. **Ventures take a building.** It needs buildings separate from businesses (5). A venture that opens names its space, chosen by the engine from the fabric pass's vacancy (cheap, fitting, near the founder) with its reason, so the shop stands where the engine put it. The player would see the café open in the empty ironmonger's and learn it was cheap after the crash.

## For the town system (`docs/town-system.md`)

How every town is laid out and changes: plan units, plans as documents, owners and prices, neighbourhoods with addresses, transport and halts, and the layout library. Written with the town system on 22 October (its section 7) and gathered here; none is in the engine's order yet.

22. **The town as founding acts** (extends 6). The fabric pass lays the town as plan units: each one area of one type from the pack's catalogue, with its year, founder (the town, a landowner, a company, a cooperative, a developer), cause and land. In play the council and developers found units as decisions with reasons, and Overstory's library lays out their streets and plots. The player would see a district's date and maker at a glance, and the seam where it meets the older town.
23. **Plans and rules as documents** (extends 14). Plans are sentences today ("to widen the harbour road"). We need documents: a kind, claims on geometry, a mechanism (on rebuild, purchase within a window, at once, by one actor) and a state (adopted, lapsed, replaced), with each claim's fate decided where it meets a plot (an owner who will not sell, money run out, a protest that wins). Building rules and protected areas the same way. An adopted claim shortens the horizon of the owners under it. The player would see sea houses on the line of a road never built, worn as the plan wore them, and could find out who stopped it.
24. **Owners with horizons, and prices** (beside 10, 13 and 15). Owners of each plot and building over time, with a kind (resident owner, landlord, heirs, the town, a cooperative, a company) and a horizon (a lease's end, an heir waiting to sell, a road reserve); sales and inheritance with reasons; prices and rents by year. Households choose among real addresses by price, access, neighbours and life stage; lenders' rules (Husbanken's norms) are facts of a bank; never by ethnicity (Rule 8). The player would see why one street is kept and the next let go.
25. **Neighbourhoods on the map, places by rank.** The engine's neighbourhoods (about 80 people each) need addresses: sets of plots, added as units are laid. Places should come by threshold, not one of each per neighbourhood: a town of 95,000 has about 1,200 such neighbourhoods but one cathedral, and a pub on most corners (Christaller). The player would see the street's pub round its corner, and one cathedral for the town.
26. **Transport and halts in the timeline** (extends 3). Each era's modes with door-to-door speeds and lines; each halt in growth with its years and cause. The player would see rings of hospitals, cemeteries and trees where the town once stopped, and a tram street that kept its width.
27. **The town library adopted** (extends 19). The layout rules (types, seams, plot cycles, plan mechanisms) and fields (access, price, syntax) written as a portable, deterministic library to the engine's portability rules, so the fabric pass can run it and the engine can read distances and vacancy without depending on the game. The player would see ventures, trips and feet land where the streets say.

## From the craft studies (22 October)

All proposed, 22 October; pending the building-blocks architecture, which may change their shape. They come from the craft studies and their digests: `docs/research/behaviour-on-screen.md` (its B1 to B5), `characters.md`, `interface-and-inspection.md`, `time-on-screen.md` and `where-generation-runs.md`, through `docs/research/inputs/4-people-behaviour-motion.md` and `5-buildings-fronts-time.md`. Where an earlier request already covered a need, the need was added to it. That gave habits as timetables and the knowledge store's reads (1), opening hours (5) and the sifter's rules (7). It also gave care the same in focus and background (13), flats and households (15), and purchases and clothes (17). Each says what Understory has today, read through the GitHub API on `main` at `d49f589` (21 October, round 41).

They keep the engine general. Hours, rites, timetables and the load of a job are setting content in the pack; the engine carries only their shape. None asks the engine to put a person's character on screen: inner causes, traits and readings stay in the record, and reach the screen only as someone's account (evidence, not verdicts).

In order of need (ER3), 32 and 33 come first. They are cheap now and costly to retrofit once the record's format is set (next week's architecture here, #22's ids and saves there). Next come 28 and the timetables in 1, which land best with #22 and homes, and after them 29 and 30, which the engine's own human test can use too. Last come 34, 35 and 31.

28. **Where and when an act happened** (behaviour, B1).
   - *Needs:* each event between people names where it happened: a place, a home (15), or the habit, work or group that brought them together; and, where the engine knows it, the week or the part of the day. A staged act must happen where it happened, or the scene invents history (criteria 3 and 4). It also settles half of the two clocks (`docs/living-world.md`): the engine owns the place, Overstory the minute inside the habit's slot. The interface needs it to say who could have been there, and the change query to find the events in a building or a street.
   - *Understory today:* an event has a month (`t`), who, causes, visibility and who knows it, but no place (`Event` in `src/world.ts`). Where it names one, it names it in words ("started work at the harbour fleet", `src/work.ts`).
   - *Smallest form:* a `where` on events: a place id, or a habit id (the habit carries its place), or `home` once homes exist.
   - *Priority:* with #22 and homes, next in the engine's order. Overstory needs it at step 5 (the first scene, on the gully steps at night) and in LT2.
   - *Meanwhile:* each staged act takes a slot that fits everyone's day plan, by rule and labelled `derived`; fixtures label theirs `authored`.
   - The player would see the row at the Anchor on the Friday it happened, and Hal missing the Friday after.
29. **Which causes a bystander could see** (behaviour, B3).
   - *Needs:* each cause of a decision marked as in the room (a circumstance others present could see: the foreman watching, the rain, the empty till) or inner (a trait, a memory, a want). When an act is staged, its visible situation is put in frame, the remedy the research names for reading one act as a whole character (Quiz II). Round 42 (reasons that bear on the act) works on the same causes.
   - *Understory today:* most of it, inside. Each consideration says who could know it (`Know`: public, close or private, `src/decide.ts`); evidence carries how much visible circumstance explained the act (`circ`); `hindsight()` splits an event's causes into seen and unseen, for the witness only (`src/witness.ts`). An exported `Cause` carries none of it.
   - *Smallest form:* copy `know` onto each exported cause. It changes no behaviour.
   - *Priority:* first among the people requests, with 30, and before any act is staged (step 5, LT2).
   - *Meanwhile:* by rule on `authored` fixtures: events and states others share are visible; traits, values and wants are inner.
   - Inner causes never reach the screen as truth. The player would see the act with its circumstance, and could be right that it was the circumstance.
30. **Ordinary acts in proportion** (behaviour, B4).
   - *Needs:* a count per person and month of the acts they made, by family, the quiet ones included, so the screen can show someone's ordinary life at its true rate around the one vivid act. It is the dilution the engine's next human test builds into the page with its evidence counts, and it keeps a scene from misleading (criterion 4).
   - *Understory today:* the quiet acts (`straight`, `keepquiet`, `carryon`, `letslide`) are flagged `quiet` and never logged; their effects apply (`src/families.ts`).
   - *Smallest form:* counts, not events: per person and month, acts by family.
   - *Priority:* with 29.
   - *Meanwhile:* counts in fixtures, `authored`; nothing staged implies a rate the record does not hold.
   - The player would see the man who snapped once let a dozen other slights go.
31. **Rites with who came** (behaviour, B5).
   - *Needs:* funerals, weddings, christenings and the town's own days as events with a place and the list of who came. Who comes is the clearest public evidence of ties, and a rite is where a private state becomes public (criterion 3).
   - *Understory today:* `death`, `married` and `born` events name the family, not the guests. A child living away decides whether to come home for a parent's funeral (`src/migration.ts`). The pack has its yearly day, and names a funeral in the church and a christening in words. No list of who came, and no place.
   - *Smallest form:* a gathering after a rite, with its place and a `came` list chosen by the engine's own contact rules; the kinds of rite come from the pack.
   - *Priority:* after 28 to 30, for the people step's scenes and the band year.
   - *Meanwhile:* attendance only `authored`, never derived, since who stayed away reads as history.
   - The player would see who stood at the graveside, and who did not.
32. **The precision and actor of every event** (time on screen; the interface).
   - *Needs:* each dated fact says how well its date is known (a month, a year, a span), and who acted where that is not a person (the council's road department, a firm, the harbour). Dates are shown at the record's precision, so no label shows a year the record does not hold (`docs/research/interface-and-inspection.md`, rule 3; `time-on-screen.md`, rule 4). The actor lets the change budget weigh the player's traces, and the why-probe answer "what did I change here?".
   - *Understory today:* weight is there: every event has a notability `w` (0 to 3), which the history budget can read as it is. The actor of an act between people is `who[0]`, and how each person knows an event is in `knownBy`. Precision is not: every event has a month, but the city's coarse yearly pass dates its events to the month it runs in (`src/coarse.ts`), and backstory replayed at promotion is dated to the promotion (`docs/scale/level-of-detail.md`). Institutions decide (the council, in `src/projects.ts`) but are never an event's actor.
   - *Smallest form:* a `prec` on events (month by default, year from the coarse pass, a span for replayed backstory), and an `actor` where it is not `who[0]`.
   - *Priority:* first: a field each, and #22's ids and saves are the moment to add them. Overstory's own record should take the same fields now (`thing.json`; `docs/research/time-on-screen.md`, What we should do, 9), so the two agree before next week's architecture sets the format.
   - *Meanwhile:* Overstory treats the events of people in focus as known to the month, and those of background people to the year.
   - The player would see each date as precisely as anyone knows it, and no more.
33. **Facts as dated intervals** (time on screen; buildings, fronts and time).
   - *Needs:* the facts that change and show, as dated intervals. These are job and workplace, home and household, partner, savings by band, health and lasting conditions, membership of groups and circles, a place's lead and staff, and later a business's building and hours. The screen follows the record (`NOW.md`, Decided): 1989 is drawn from a past built in one call, 1989 and 2000 are compared from one record, and a jump draws what watching would have drawn. Care (13), wardrobes (17) and taste derive from these histories, not from today's state.
   - *Understory today:* the reads give the present only (`person`, `bondOf`). Some changes are events (hired, moved, married, bought, retired, left, arrived, death), with their place in words; savings and health keep no history. `createWorld` builds the thirty-year past in one call, so its states can't be read along the way.
   - *Smallest form:* the person and place reads return the history of these fields as `from` and `to` lists, as the contract's validity intervals do; or every change of them becomes an event with structured ids.
   - With 28, 32 and #22's `eventsAfter`, this is all Overstory's change query (`changes(scope, t0, t1)`) needs from the engine; the rest is our own record.
   - *Priority:* first, with 32. The shape is cheap now, and the building blocks' life intervals are designed next week to match it. Overstory needs the facts themselves when the corner through the years (step 4) reads the engine rather than fixtures.
   - *Meanwhile:* fixtures hold intervals, `authored`. With the engine, Overstory samples the reads month by month as it advances a world, which serves the present but not the built past.
34. **A person's body and work over a life** (characters).
   - *Needs:* what ages a person on screen, so they stay recognisable across scenes and years (criterion 2). That is the physical load and outdoor exposure of each job, illness and injury with a kind and any lasting effect (a stick after a stroke, a stiff hand after the saw), and smoking. Skin ages with outdoor years, smoking and weight; muscle and hands follow the work (`docs/research/characters.md`, 4 and 7.3). A cast or a scar appears only from a dated event.
   - *Understory today:* every person has `health`, a `constitution`, months left `ill`, and `drinks` with `drinkSince`. Illnesses are `ill` events, and work accidents are `accident` events at the pack's dangerous workplaces, each with months off but its kind only in words ("had a stroke", "got caught in the saw"; `src/body.ts`, `src/situations/danger.ts`). ## From the research of 29 October: candidates, not yet backed by tests

Øystein, 29 October: "i guess also think about what should be handled in Understory". Each comes from a note's "Whose facts" section, which gives its reasons: rooms (`docs/research/rooms-and-interiors.md`), the city's ground (`city-ground-and-public-places.md`), sound (`sound-and-music.md`) and weather (`weather-and-seasons.md`). 37. **Things that carry a relation** (extends 17): a gift, an heirloom, a friend's photograph, with giver, year and occasion, and what a household puts at its windows (plants, lace, a Christmas star). The player would see a room whose things are real ties, never a stereotype of its owner.

40. **Households that split and merge** (extends 15): a divorce, a parent moving in, a lodger. The player would see who sleeps where change with them.

42. **Habits that hold a spot or leave a mark** (extends 1's habits as timetables): a regular's seat, a seller's pitch kept for years, smoking that yellows a ceiling. The player would see the fishmonger's stall from 1982 to 1993, and its absence after.

44. **Gatherings in public, with who came** (extends 28, 31): a crowd round an incident, the town's days with their route. The player would see a crowd only where something happened.
45. **Regimes as decisions** (extends 23): a pedestrian trial, parking meters, the stud fee, each with its fate. The player would see what a street allows change by a council choice that can lapse.
46. **Places' uses and keepers over time** (extends 13, 25): each use with the year it began, and its keeper (parks, church council, harbour). The player would see the market shrink because someone decided it.
47. **Addresses with history** (extends 15, 25): renaming and renumbering, so the plate and the record agree.
48. **Outages and strikes,** dated, with extent. The player would see their consequences on the street (criterion 3).
49. **Heard by ear** (extends 1's way of knowing, 28, 15): beside seen and told, a row heard through a wall or from the stair, so who heard it is a fact that changes later talk.
50. **A venture's hours and seasons** (extends 5's hours): shifts, a season such as the brisling, a late shift on a big catch. The player would hear the bell, see the feet and the lit windows agree.

The player would see town and people live one weather.
