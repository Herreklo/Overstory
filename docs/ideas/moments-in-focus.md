# Idea: moments in focus

Status: idea for the engine, not a decision. It is the model of time that Inquest, the band and any game with drama rest on. The request is item 17 in `docs/engine-requests.md`.

## The idea

Pressure is slow; the rupture is an instant. A relationship wears down over months, but it breaks in a moment: one sentence at a dinner, a letter found in a drawer, a night someone does not come home. Some instants have no build-up at all: a slip on a wet pier.

So the world needs clocks at several speeds, each for its own job:

| Clock | What it carries |
| --- | --- |
| Decades and years | The fabric and the economy: streets, buildings, booms and busts (`research/generating-the-past.md`) |
| Months | Pressure building across the whole town: liking, trust, resentment, wants, habits |
| Hours | The moments where pressure meets a situation, resolved for real |

## How it works

1. **Each month, find the moments at risk.** A moment is pressure meeting a situation: two people with strained accounts of each other who will be at the same funeral, gig or dinner; a man who drinks on Fridays and a storm that cuts the power.
2. **Expand those moments to hours, from habits.** "Arne drinks on Fridays" becomes "Arne at Sjøbua from 18:00". The same seed always gives the same schedule.
3. **Resolve them as events with causes.** Who said what, who was in the room, what nearly went differently.
4. **Commit what matters back to the month.** A rupture, a fall or a promise becomes an event in the record. Everything else evaporates.

The situations are what Versu called social practices: a dinner, a greeting, a rehearsal, an argument, each with roles and expectations (`research/scenes-and-sound.md`).

## The rule that keeps it honest

**An outcome never depends on whether the player is watching.** If a marriage breaks only when the camera is on it, the world is lying. Moments that can change a life are resolved whether anyone looks or not. Watching is optional; simulating is not.

## Why it matters

- It is how events start leading somewhere: a rupture in a moment has a cause, a place and witnesses, so it can have consequences.
- Detective play needs it. Inquest's case turns on 20:30, 21:05 and 21:12.
- Coincidence needs it. At monthly resolution, two people cannot be in the same place at the same time, and most stories start there.
- It keeps the cost bounded: only the moments at risk run at fine resolution, never the whole town every hour. Simulate the few, show the many, applied to time.

## Open questions

- How many moments per month can a neighbourhood afford, and a city?
- Who owns the hourly schedules: the engine, or a scene layer the engine checks?
- How is a moment chosen fairly, so the town does not only break at gigs and funerals?
