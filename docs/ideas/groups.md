# Building a group or a place

Ideas, not decisions: no game is chosen (`NOW.md`). Every idea is catalogued in `README.md`; this page holds the full detail of this family's ideas.

## In short

- Games about assembling people, growing and succeeding: manager, strategy and role-playing. **The band** (option 2 of `first-game-options.md`) and **the club** (option 1) are the fullest; **the crew**, **the venture** (with the café owner) and **the scene builder** are lighter. A festival and a hotel are named only in the needs table (`game-layer-ideas.md`).
- What sets them apart here: **you never see a stat. You see people, and they remember.** Decisions are taken monthly, and the month's key evenings are watched as scenes.
- The family across a century, a sixth group concept, is in `lives.md`.

Costs are rough weeks of the weekly usage limit, the plan's own unit, before the architecture. The criteria are the five in `CLAUDE.md` (What it is for). Claims about other games are sourced in `first-game-options.md` (Sources).

## What every group game here shares

### The ceiling of the genre

The best group games make people stat bundles. Football Manager hides personality (ambition, loyalty, temperament, how a player takes pressure) and reveals it only through scouts' and coaches' remarks, the closest the genre comes to reading people. XCOM and Battle Brothers make soldiers with names and permadeath, Darkest Dungeon gives them stress and quirks, Wildermyth lets heroes' stories and scars accumulate, Midwinter (1989) lets relationships decide who can recruit whom, Mewgenics and Crusader Kings pass traits down generations. Rock Star Ate My Hamster (1988) was already a band manager: four gold discs in a year or bust.

Everywhere the late game turns into a spreadsheet. Chemistry is a hidden number, nobody remembers what you did, and replacing a person replaces numbers.

### Our edge

**You never see a stat. You see people, and they remember.**

- **People are read, not shown.** No attribute screens. You learn a guitarist from how she plays tonight, what the drummer says about her, how she took it when the van broke down. Every remark comes from a named person and can be wrong.
- **The group's history lives in its members.** Each keeps their own account of what happened (the accounts arc, #20). Two members remember the same night differently and act on it.
- **"What if" can be shown.** The engine can run the world again, so at the end it can show what would have happened if you had kept the drummer.

### Decide monthly, watch the evenings

A group game needs the player's decisions to reach the engine, which `docs/research/reality-check.md` rates Hard. The way through: one set of decisions per month (who to sign, which gig to take, what to promise, whom to talk to), and the month's key evenings, a rehearsal or a gig, played as scenes the player watches (`moments-in-focus.md`). The engine's first planned player command, inviting a musician to a rehearsal (#22), is exactly this kind of decision.

The mechanics these games draw on are in `game-layer-ideas.md` (promises, chemistry, the rehearsal room, legendary without successful) and `talent-discovery.md`.

### The risk

The engine reports that living barely changes anyone: thirty years move how someone chooses by about 3% of the distance between two people. Ten years in a band needs people who visibly change, harden, sober up or lose faith. The accounts arc's third step, accounts becoming lessons, is aimed at this, and it is paused.

## The band

### The game (option 2)

**The game.** You manage a band formed in 1989 in the loft on Harbour Row, for as long as it lasts: Understory's origin game and the shared milestone (request 8).

**Core loop.** Find people where music leaks out (a choir, a wedding, the pub; `talent-discovery.md`) and bring them together. Then shape conditions without controlling anyone: book the loft and the gigs, spend the money, make promises that are remembered (`game-layer-ideas.md`). The members' lives decide the rest.

**Dials.** Control: the manager's own acts (bookings, money, the van). Influence: each member's choices, starting with the first command, an invitation to a rehearsal, which the engine accepts or refuses. Knowledge: the band deeply but as evidence; the scene thinly.

**Time.** Weeks inside an album cycle of about eighteen months, then jumps of years back to the same venue. A career may run to a reunion in 2020, or end in 1991.

**The game in it.**
- *Goal:* the band's own ambition, set with its members: the big hall, a record deal, the crowd it draws (the engine's own measure), or lasting at all. Success and legend are counted apart.
- *Resources:* money (the loft's rent, the van, studio days), bookings, your evenings, and promises that are remembered.
- *Opponents and pressure:* rival bands for gigs and players, venues that close, landlords, money running out, the members' jobs, partners and children.
- *Fail states:* the band splits; debts; the loft lost; a member's life damaged by the push.
- *Mastery:* reading chemistry before it shows as rows; booking the right room at the right time; knowing when to step in and when to stay out.

**Needs.**
- *World:* the loft and the Anchor (both on the corner's street), a venue ladder, poster walls as coat stacks of gigs, the van, the walk home at three.
- *Engine:* the band as a group kind, contests for a place with the loser's answer, and shows exist; the real run holds four bands and 32 shows in ten years. Planned: the band for a year, homes, the first command. Later: cheap space (10), places that close (4), records (9).
- *Interface:* commands with their reasons, clocks for plans and debts, no trait labels even for your own band.
- *Music:* agents can't make the songs (`reality-check.md`), and there is no commission budget.

**Only here.** A band's history written on the town. The loft was cheap after the crash and is lost when flats go up. The one who wasn't picked turns up in the front row, or in a rival band. The reunion plays the same address, now another business.

**Risks.** Borrowed-sounding songs leave a hollow heart; the ways out are Øystein and local musicians, free licences, or music shown only through rooms, crowds and accounts (untested). The engine's band year has not run, and most of its events still lead nowhere (Understory's summary).

**Cost on top of the plan.** 12 to 20 weeks, plus the music: venues and gigs as scenes, the business (deals, radio, records), posters.

**Criteria.** 1 some; 2 strong; 3 strong (who wasn't chosen; the loft lost); 4 strong; 5 some (gig crowds).

**Greybox.** Invite a musician to the loft; the fixture accepts or refuses, with its reason. A week later, is the trace readable: who comes late, who leaves first, whose window is lit? Does a refusal feel like a person deciding or a dice roll?

### As a group game

**Pitch.** Find musicians in a small town, keep them together for ten years, climb from a pub back room to the big hall. Find a band in a small town, keep it together and make the promises that will be tested years later.

**The moment.** In 2019, a documentary built from the members' own accounts: the singer blames the drummer, the drummer blames you, and you remember the night it started. Or a retrospective, in the style of Behind the Music, generated from what happened, and you recognise the night it started to fall apart.

**Why it works.** Moneyball scouting, scene histories (the Bergen Wave) and remembered promises.

**Engine.** The band for a year (request 8), accounts (#20), player commands.

**Weakness.** The music needs people. You rarely hear a whole song: you hear the room, the rehearsal through a wall, the crowd.

**Cheapest test.** The first player command (#22): an invitation the engine accepts or refuses, and whether the refusal reads as a person.

**The groups round's recommendation** (recorded, not a decision): **the band, with the venture as its home base.** Your rehearsal loft in an old warehouse is the building that changes with you, and other bands form around it (`docs/research/world-that-remembers.md`, principle 10). It joins talent discovery (`talent-discovery.md`), chemistry and promises (`game-layer-ideas.md`), the venue ladder (`docs/research/scenes-and-sound.md`) and the engine's own milestone of a band for a year. The mark: **"Ten years. Five people. Everyone remembers it differently."**

### A first playable slice: the first gig

From the game layer's ideas, collected from two external reviews of the Person Model Lab (October 2026) and discussion (`game-layer-ideas.md`). One band, four musicians, one manager. A rehearsal room, a small venue, a studio. One album cycle (about eighteen months). Preparation, performance, immediate consequences, lasting memories. Then a jump of several years back to the same venue, to see whether earlier choices made a history the player recognises.

### Multiplayer: a band is a hidden-role game

Four friends each play one member. Everyone wants the band to succeed, and everyone has a private ambition: a solo career, leaving town, someone at home who wants them back. Diplomacy and The Traitors, inside something built together.

### The first test

Read the engine's band for a year as a story when it runs. If that year is not interesting to read, no game on top of it will be.

## The club

### The game (option 1)

**The game.** You run a harbour town's football club in the lower leagues from 1989. The players are amateurs with day jobs at the cannery, the harbour and the oil base. The week is long and the match short.

**Core loop.** Two training evenings, the Saturday match, the Anchor after. Between them you find players where talent is unmeasured (school sports, the estate's sports hall), keep a squad together round shifts, families and injuries, and deal with the board and a sponsor.

**Dials.** Control: the team sheet, training, tactics, the club's money. Influence: players' choices (to sign, stay, train after a night shift), the board, the sponsor. Knowledge: your own squad well, as evidence; other clubs and the town thinly.

**Time.** Weeks inside a season that runs spring to autumn; the dark winter is the off-season, a natural jump. Seasons for decades, as in a long Football Manager save.

**The game in it.**
- *Goal:* the board's target each season, then promotion; a club that survives and grows.
- *Resources:* a small budget, two training evenings, your own evenings (one spent at a player's door is one not spent scouting), and promises: a job found, a place kept.
- *Opponents and pressure:* other clubs, the fixture list, the board's patience, a sponsor tied to the town's firms, and players' lives: the cannery closes, a striker takes the oil base's shifts.
- *Fail states:* relegation, the sack, debts, the club folding; a promise broken and remembered.
- *Mastery:* seeing who will hold up (shifts, a new baby, drink) before form shows it; finding unmeasured talent; tactics. Early seasons are survival, later ones building a squad over years. Reading the town is the scout's edge.

**Needs.**
- *World:* the Mudbowl in the railway's valley (planned, not built), a clubhouse, players' homes and workplaces, match-day streets and crowds.
- *Engine:* aptitudes, work, accidents, groups and contests for a place exist. Needed: a club as a group kind in the pack (the shape exists), sporting skills that rise and fall with age, and a match model. A result is a fact, so the model must be the engine's or a portable library it adopts (as request 19 proposes for derived works). Homes (#59) for the lives round the squad.
- *Interface:* players as acts with counts, never rating words; a team sheet; a match view.

**Only here.** A squad that is the town. The striker's week at the cannery shows on Saturday. The 1989 captain's son trains with the club in 2010, and you know him. The ground keeps the club's history: a stand built with oil money, sponsor boards that change with the town's firms.

**Risks.** The core decisions don't need the town on screen, and Football Manager proves the page carries the genre. Twenty-two people moving well is work after the people step.

**Cost on top of the plan.** 16 to 26 weeks: the match model, the match as staged moments (floodlights, the crowd as one organism), the league, the ground, scouting.

**Criteria.** 1 some; 2 strong (players across seasons, fathers and sons); 3 some; 4 some; 5 strong (match day across the town).

**Greybox.** The corner has no pitch, so test the town side. A boy kicks a ball against D's boarded wall every evening; three men's shifts change after the cannery news (fixtures, `authored`). After three evenings, can a player say who is worth approaching and who will miss Saturday, and be right?

### As a group game

A small town's football club across thirty years: the striker's marriage, the youth player's father, the cannery that sponsors the shirts and closes. **The moment:** the centenary book, full of wrong memories. **Weakness:** it needs a match engine; every comparison is with Football Manager.

### The youth team

A connection of the club, talent discovery and the town's schools and stadium (`docs/town-plan.md`, TP6). You coach the town's under-15s in 1989, kids from the Heights and from Tin Town. You find talent where nobody looks. Over twenty years you see who made it, who didn't, and who never forgave you for dropping them.

## The crew

A fishing boat's crew, 1960s to 1980s: seasons at sea, families ashore. **The moment:** the empty bunk. **Weakness:** most of the play is at sea, away from the town.

## The venture

**As a group game.** Open a place, a café in the old bank or a rehearsal loft in a closed cannery; hire people, gather regulars, watch the building and street change. **The moment:** the building, years later, showing what you did to it. **Weakness:** tycoon loops drift towards spreadsheets.

**The café owner.**
- *The loop.* Your café is the stage. Regulars' lives pass through it, and the street outside changes around you.
- *What it uses.* Ventures (6 cafés in the run), regulars' habits, the street's fabric.
- *Fit today (world 9989).* Strong: cafés are engine ventures.
- *Risks.* A cozy loop can go flat. It needs stakes (rent, rivals, regulars leaving).
- *Nearest games.* Coffee Talk, Stardew Valley.

The acorn, a small place run from 1989 to 2030, is in `lives.md`. The loft's landlord, a panel's hybrid, is in `README.md` (Combinations). What the town must give a venture (empty shops after the crash, rents by street, foot traffic by route, rivals, the market) is in `docs/town-plan.md` (A city to play in).

## The scene builder

One of the ideas that pass the spreadsheet test in Overstory's reading of the open notebook (20 October 2026; `game-layer-ideas.md`, What each idea asks of Overstory). The music game's sibling: not one band but the venues, rehearsal rooms, posters, local paper and audiences that make a town known for a sound. It runs on the rule that cheap old space breeds new life (`docs/research/world-that-remembers.md`, principle 10).
