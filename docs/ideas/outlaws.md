# On the wrong side of the record

Ideas, not decisions: no game is chosen (`NOW.md`). Every idea is catalogued in `README.md`; this page holds the full detail of this family's ideas.

## In short

- Three games where the player breaks a rule and the town is the one watching: **casing** houses, **the smuggler** on the prohibition coast, and **the forger** of records.
- Each turns the reading skill round: the player must read the town to stay unread by it.
- None is violent. Crime sandboxes built on violence are not a fit (`README.md`, Not a fit); these work on routines, money and documents.
- The player whose secret others must uncover, a resident hunted by the casebook, is in `multiplayer.md` (One of them is a player).

## Casing *(old game)*

From *Thief* (1998) and *Hitman*. Stealth needs routines, but those cities were stages. Here you read from the street which houses are empty, what is worth taking and who would notice. The town remembers: a new lock, a dog, a neighbourhood-watch sticker. Multiplayer: a crew, or one side plays the police.

The details of the old games are from memory and are checked before the idea is built on.

## The smuggler on the prohibition coast

**Pitch.** Norway's prohibition years, 1916 to 1927. Run spirits along the fjords while the town watches: who suddenly has money, whose boat is out at night, which grave is too deep.

**The moment.** Your neighbour's new car is what gives you away, not anything you did.

**Why it works.** Wealth and routine already read from the street (`docs/research/what-moves.md`). Here they become suspicion.

**Engine.** A 1920s setting pack and timeline (#23), habits (#22), the liar's ledger (who could contradict a claim: not yet requested; `game-layer-ideas.md`), player commands.

**Weakness.** A second era pack, off the 1989 path.

**Cheapest test.** One street's habits printed for one month; can a player spot the smuggler from traces alone?

It was fourth of the seven games built on reading a world (`first-game-options.md`, section 7).

## The forger

**Pitch.** You fake records: a will, a deed, a letter of recommendation. The town checks them against what people remember. Every lie must survive a world that knows the truth.

**The moment.** A forged alibi holds for two years, until a widow remembers that her husband was at sea that week.

**Why it works.** It turns "who could contradict this?" into the whole game. Papers, Please inverted: you make the documents, the town inspects them.

**Engine.** The liar's ledger (who could contradict a claim: not yet requested), records as accounts (request 9), and player commands back to the engine. Both are in `game-layer-ideas.md` (What the reading games need from the engine).

**Weakness.** Needs the two-way channel, and lies must be checked fairly, or failures feel arbitrary.

**Cheapest test.** On paper: one forged document against a printed list of who knew what. Can a player predict who will catch it?

It was third of the seven games built on reading a world (`first-game-options.md`, section 7).
