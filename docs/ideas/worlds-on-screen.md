# Idea: games where reading the world is the play

Status: idea, not a decision. Overstory's reading of the Understory × Overstory open notebook (20 October 2026), a shared bank of game ideas for both projects. This note keeps what Overstory carries.

## The test

A game idea uses Overstory when the player's main act is reading the world: a street, a room, a person, a record. If the idea would play as well as a spreadsheet, the world on screen is decoration, and the idea belongs to the engine's side.

## Ideas that pass

- **The restoration architect.** You take on an old building or a block. You work out why it changed, which traces are original and which were later, and what to keep. The building system becomes the game: the coat stack is the evidence, the era shelves are the vocabulary, and a careless 1970s window is a clue and a choice.
- **The town archivist.** A photograph, a deed, an old newspaper, a family name. You rebuild lives across generations from records that disagree, with the houses as one source among the papers. Traces cannot lie; records can, and the game is in the gap.
- **Social archaeology.** You arrive decades after a place changed and work out what it used to be: closed businesses, faded posters, renovated houses, a street widened halfway. The fabric pass's output is the level.
- **The many-lives anthology.** Short chapters as different people in the same town, years apart. A choice made as one person becomes another's background. The street you left is the street you return to: the return to the first rung, at the scale of a whole town.
- **The scene builder.** The music game's sibling: not one band but the venues, rehearsal rooms, posters, local paper and audiences that make a town known for a sound. It runs on the rule that cheap old space breeds new life (`docs/research/world-that-remembers.md`, principle 10).
- **The counterfactual observatory.** One saved history, one changed decision, two versions of the same street side by side. The diff between the two towns' plans becomes a view: what changed, and what the town resisted.

The detective in the forest is already in the research (`docs/research/nature.md`).

Read across: the fabric and records are needed by nearly every idea; crowds and stage light by few. The building and history system is the most reusable thing either project makes, which is why it comes first. Records are the cheapest next shared system: mostly type, paper, ink and layout by era, and on every street they double as set dressing (posters, signs, notices in windows).

## Records follow the world's rules

- Each record is a dated object with a maker and a purpose, and it claims facts that may be wrong (`docs/living-world.md`, What the player can know).
- Records age like surfaces: sun-faded, rain-streaked, pasted over. A venue's wall is a coat stack of its gigs.
- Their words come last, from a grammar, and never invent a cause.
- A record that only restates a fact (a poster for a booked gig) is drawn by Overstory. A record that can be wrong (the paper's report) is an account, so it comes from the engine (`docs/engine-requests.md`, Records).
