# Game ideas

The catalogue of every game idea for Overstory: one card per idea, grouped by family, each linking to the page that holds its full detail. Ideas, not decisions.

## In short

- **What is here.** Twenty-four cards in six families: mysteries and investigation; lives and families across time; building a group or a place; a role or a trade in the town; on the wrong side of the record; played together. After the cards: the mechanics several games share, ideas for the world that are not games, combinations and connections, what is not a fit, and where other ideas sit.
- **No game is chosen.** Which game comes first waits for Øystein: "nothing is decided" (`NOW.md`). Several rounds of ideas recorded their own rankings and recommendations. They disagree, and they are kept as recorded, never as decisions (`first-game-options.md`, sections 5 and 7). The cards are in no order of merit.
- **How ideas are judged.** By the four tests and the rule that we make a game, not a walking simulator (`CLAUDE.md`), and by the exit criteria in `CLAUDE.md` (What it is for). Below, in How ideas are judged.
- **Status** on a card means only what is recorded: Øystein's own words, verbatim with their date, and the outcomes of the paper playtests. A card with neither says so.
- **Each idea's full detail lives in one place.** Where an idea appears in several rounds, its card merges them and its page keeps every distinct point.

## How ideas are judged

**The four tests** (`CLAUDE.md`, Øystein, 22 October). Everything we build looks amazing, in our style with clear references; is economical (60 fps at 1440p on an ordinary M4 Mac mini); had its subject and craft researched before it was built; and fits the living world (it can wear, break, be repaired, carry a history and be affected).

**A game, not a walking simulator** (`CLAUDE.md`, Øystein, 21 October). Every game built on Overstory has goals, challenge, stakes, mastery, failure, and decisions that cost something. Reading the world is a skill that pays off in play, as deduction does in Return of the Obra Dinn or scouting in Football Manager, never the whole of the play. A concept that would only be looked at, however beautiful, is not a game. Its question, how it is judged and what would change our minds are written before it is built. Game-ness is judged with Jesper Juul's classic game model (`first-game-options.md`, What this is for).

**The exit criteria** (`CLAUDE.md`, What it is for; a draft to agree with Øystein). The six options are rated against them (`first-game-options.md`): a building's history read at a glance; a person recognised across scenes and years; a consequence the page does not make obvious; the scene preferred to the page without misleading; a street at city scale holding its look and frame rate.

**The spreadsheet test.** A game idea uses Overstory when the player's main act is reading the world. If it would play as well as a spreadsheet, the world on screen is decoration (`game-layer-ideas.md`, What each idea asks of Overstory).

**What the world has that others don't.** A game type is strong here when its loop feeds on these four, and weak when it would play the same without them:
1. **Lives with causes and witnesses.** Every event has its reasons, its weights, and who could have seen it (private, local or public).
2. **A town that records history truthfully:** wear, repairs, care and coats, signs and ghost signs.
3. **Time that can jump,** with the screen always following the record.
4. **Evidence, not verdicts:** accounts and records can be wrong.

**Before any idea is chosen,** gameplay needs much more research: balance, difficulty and engagement (written for the old games' ideas; Øystein, 22 October: "we can do it later"). `docs/research/fun.md`, `play-craft.md` and `playtesting.md` cover fun, feel and testing. Balance and long-term engagement depend on the game, so they are studied once one is picked.

**Claims about other games** come from press, wikis and interviews, sourced in `first-game-options.md` (Sources) and `docs/research/game-precedents.md`. An idea marked *(old game)* starts from an older game that reached for something its tech or its time could not give. A town of developing people whose history stays is the missing part in each. Those details of old games are from memory and are checked before any idea is built on them.

## The cards

Each card gives the pitch; the loop; precedents; what it uses from the town and the engine; multiplayer; status; and where its detail is.

### Mysteries and investigation

#### A death in the town

*Someone has died, and what happened is unclear; you read the town to find out, and certainty may never come.*
- **Forms.** The death on today's engine, where the question is what happened and who is answerable (option 6); the casebook, Sherlock Holmes Consulting Detective with the town as map and directory; the long inquest, where choosing when to look is the decision; Columbo, where you watch the death and then prove it; and Inquest, the ghost who closes one hole of many (its own card below).
- **Loop.** Walk, watch, ask and read; each question is an act others hear of; give an account to the inquest, the family or the paper; the record scores it fact by fact.
- **Precedents.** Bad News, Pentiment, Paradise Killer, Shadows of Doubt, Return of the Obra Dinn, Sherlock Holmes Consulting Detective, Columbo, The Past Within.
- **Uses.** Natural deaths, secrets, lies and accounts the engine has now; who knew what (1), sifting (7), records (9), where and when (28); murder would need new requests, within Rule 8.
- **Multiplayer.** Co-op and rival reporters on one case; two investigators in different decades; the culprit as a player and the daily case (`multiplayer.md`).
- **Status.** Øystein, 22 October, on the casebook: "actual a good idea. this could work very similar as well. The big thing is the story and mystery though, but maybe would could do this in a very clever generative way?"; "something fun for multiplayer stuff would also be nice." Paper playtest (*Answerable*, 4 runs, no verdict): lives on predicted better, 33% and 25% right against 8% and 10%, but arm and model are confounded; lives on solved the case worse, 15 and 25 of 100 against 45 and 55, a gap made wholly by three facts whose lives-off answer was "nothing on record" (the other seven tied); all four players called the hearing's rules a cheat; all four runs asked who found and certified the dead, and the engine records neither. The hypothesis that lives make the mystery solvable leans against.
- **Detail.** `mysteries.md` (A death in the town).

#### Inquest: the ghost who closes a hole

*You are a ghost with one very specific death, your own: find every hole that lined up to kill you, close one, and see what the town became.*
- **Loop.** An inquiry from records, the Afterward (the town thirty years on) and rationed returns; then one change, run forward, with its ripples marked. There is no right answer: every reachable change saves the victim, and they differ in what else they do.
- **Forms.** The board of inquiry, for any accident; the hand-over; Fate against the inquiry; the ghost in the cemetery; the ghost who cannot act and only nudges.
- **Precedents.** Return of the Obra Dinn, The Case of the Golden Idol, The Sexy Brutale, Ghost Trick, Outer Wilds, Twelve Minutes (as a warning), Spoon River Anthology, Reason's Swiss cheese model.
- **Uses.** A deterministic engine that can prove each hole by running the world again; records as accounts (9); deaths with a moment, branching and things in events (not yet requested; `game-layer-ideas.md`). Every change is precomputed, so it needs no live player commands.
- **Multiplayer.** Split archives, the Tide as Traitor, shared returns, share codes.
- **Status.** None recorded. A hand-built case is ready for a paper test (`inquest-case-1.md`).
- **Detail.** `inquest.md`.

#### The Week *(old game)*

*One week in 1989 repeats, and something terrible happens on Sunday; each loop you learn more routines and change the ending.*
- **Loop.** Live the week, learn who is where, use it next loop.
- **Precedents.** The Last Express, Majora's Mask, Deathloop.
- **Uses.** Understory's world copy and run-on (`clone`, `whatif`), so loops are exact; habits as timetables.
- **Multiplayer.** The Week, together: two to four residents pool the notebook; optionally one player causes Sunday.
- **Status.** None recorded.
- **Detail.** `mysteries.md` (The Week).

#### Shenmue, fulfilled *(old game)*

*You arrive in the harbour town looking for someone who vanished years ago; the town is the plot.*
- **Loop.** Odd jobs at the harbour, asking around, people who remember you and talk to each other.
- **Precedents.** Shenmue.
- **Uses.** Routines for everyone; people who remember and tell.
- **Multiplayer.** None written.
- **Status.** None recorded.
- **Detail.** `mysteries.md` (Shenmue, fulfilled).

#### The round: the postman and the paper boy

*A daily route through the town, where you notice routines and their breaks.*
- **Forms.** The postal carrier (letters and parcels as records); the paper round, a 13-year-old in 1989 who sees everything, with a mystery only a kid would notice.
- **Precedents.** Lake, Death Stranding, Papers, Please, Paperboy.
- **Uses.** Habits and their breaks, visibility, records, the outdoor town; no interiors.
- **Multiplayer.** None written.
- **Status.** Øystein, 21 October, on the postal carrier (with the restorer and the photographer): "sounds a bit boring, but ok". It is tested as the way into the murder mystery (Combinations, below).
- **Detail.** `mysteries.md` (The round).

### Lives and families across time

#### A family across generations

*One family across decades: you live a life in acts years apart, then go on as your child.*
- **Forms.** A life and its heirs, the role-playing game (option 4); the family saga followed from outside; the family as a group across a century; the acorn, a small place run from 1989 to 2030; a child growing up; Up the Hill, the ambitious Jones, with Class of '89 and the living board; the anthology, Relay and Heirs, where friends or chapters pass the town on.
- **Loop.** A season of evenings spent on people and places, each remembered by whoever saw it; a jump; the return to what your choices and other lives made.
- **Precedents.** Persona, Pentiment, Crusader Kings, The Sims, Fable's acorn, Harvest Moon: A Wonderful Life, Jones in the Fast Lane, Diplomacy, Risk Legacy, Pandemic Legacy, Boatmurdered, Night in the Woods.
- **Uses.** Homes (15), facts as dated intervals (33), body and work over a life (34), rites (31), player commands; Up the Hill also needs family background, class and schools, which the engine lacks.
- **Multiplayer.** Up the Hill's two to four families with simultaneous orders; Class of '89; the living board as a legacy campaign; Relay and Heirs passed between friends.
- **Status.** Øystein, 22 October, which led to Up the Hill: "is there a jones in the fast lane varioation, but much more ambitous". Paper playtest: *The Family Table* card, not played.
- **Detail.** `lives.md` (A family across generations).

#### The house itself

### Building a group or a place

#### The band

*Find musicians in a small town, keep them together for years, and make the promises that will be tested later.*
- **Loop.** Find people where music leaks out; book the loft and the gigs; decide monthly and watch the key evenings; the members' lives decide the rest.
- **Precedents.** Rock Star Ate My Hamster, Rock Manager, Football Manager's hidden personality, Behind the Music, the Bergen Wave, Moneyball.
- **Uses.** The band as a group kind, contests for a place and shows (in the engine now); the band for a year (8); the first command (#22); accounts (#20).
- **Multiplayer.** A hidden-role game: each friend plays a member with a private ambition.
- **Status.** Proposed as a shared milestone with the engine, waiting for Øystein (`NOW.md`). Paper playtest: *The Loft* card, not played.
- **Detail.** `groups.md` (The band).

#### The club

*A harbour town's lower-league football club from 1989, whose players are amateurs with day jobs.*
- **Forms.** The club (option 1); the club across thirty years, with its centenary book; the youth team.
- **Loop.** Two training evenings, the Saturday match, the Anchor after; scouting unmeasured talent; keeping a squad together round shifts and families.
- **Precedents.** Football Manager, Motorsport Manager.
- **Uses.** Aptitudes, work, accidents, groups and contests now; a club kind, sporting skills over age and a match model needed.
- **Multiplayer.** None written.
- **Status.** Paper playtest: *Harbour Seven* card; its 8 runs were among the 13 left when the round stopped.
- **Detail.** `groups.md` (The club).

### A role or a trade in the town

#### The paper

*The reporter, or the editor, of the town's weekly: what you chase, whom you ask, what you print and what you leave out.*
- **Forms.** The reporter (option 5); the Courier's editor or the Anchor's landlord, deciding what to print or pass on; the journalist the real run supports.
- **Loop.** Leads from the town; watch, ask and read; write, choosing each claim and its source; the printed piece is a record in the world.
- **Precedents.** Bad News, Prom Week, Papers, Please, Suzerain.
- **Uses.** The engine's `tell` events (112 in the run), visibility, the Courier and the Anchor; records (9), who told whom (1), plans as documents (23), publication as a command.
- **Multiplayer.** Rival reporters racing on one case (`mysteries.md`, The casebook).
- **Status.** Paper playtest (*Thursday's Paper*, 7 runs, no verdict): lives on printed only true claims (103 of 103), lives off 97%, 91% and 90%; gaps of 3, 9 and 10 points, between "lives pay" (15) and "they don't" (5), and under one scoring rule the mean gap is 4.5, inside "they don't"; pull favoured lives on in all three pairs; all seven runs asked what moves the paper's meters. The hypotheses that source judgement is learned and that the town hands the paper its stories lean against.
- **Detail.** `roles.md` (The paper).

#### The restorer

*Buy, restore, let and sell old houses after the crash, and decide what of each to keep.*
- **Forms.** The restorer (option 3); the round-2 restorer, reading a house layer by layer; the restoration architect.
- **Loop.** Find and read a house; buy at a price; choose the works and the crew; let or sell; a wrong reading costs money.
- **Precedents.** House Flipper, Unpacking, Pentiment.
- **Uses.** The fabric systems, the coat stack, parts dating a place; criterion 1 directly; owners, works and prices are ours until the engine has them.
- **Multiplayer.** None written.
- **Status.** Øystein, 21 October, on the restorer (with the postal carrier and the photographer): "sounds a bit boring, but ok". It is tested as a thread in the family saga (Combinations, below). Paper playtest: *Old Town* card, not played.
- **Detail.** `roles.md` (The restorer).

## Combinations and connections

**Øystein's read** (21 October). The postal carrier, the restorer and the photographer: "sounds a bit boring, but ok". As standalone games they are lenses with too little at stake. What grips is people you care about, conflict, pressure, and choices that cost something. So they are tested as mechanics inside stronger games, not alone:
- the camera as the journalist's or detective's tool, whose photos are evidence that can be printed;
- the postal round as the way into the murder mystery;
- restoration as a thread in the family saga.

The Courier's editor stays a standalone candidate: deadlines, a rival paper, printing about people you know, and a town whose beliefs shift with every edition.

**The old games' connections.** Øystein, 22 October: "i feel like there are so much more ideas. like think very nostalgic. any games that because of tech or something else that did not fulfill its potential"; then "Now look at connections - could we combine stuff for a truly unique but potential amazing experience?". Each connection now sits with the idea it grows from:
- the long inquest: time is your lever (`mysteries.md`);
- Relay: the town as a chain letter (`lives.md`);
- The Week, together (`mysteries.md`);
- the living board: Monopoly that remembers (`lives.md`);
- the youth team (`groups.md`);
- the ghost (`inquest.md`, Other ghosts).

What the strongest share: time as a lever (loops, jumps, relays), reading as the skill, and records that can be wrong. Those are what only this engine has. They also share most of their systems: inspection and the casebook, jumps, records and sifting.

**Within families.** The band with the venture as its home base (`groups.md`). The ghost and the board of inquiry as one game with two modes (`inquest.md`). The paper, which shares the death's systems and could be its second role (`first-game-options.md`, section 5).

**The paper playtest panels' hybrids,** each written before the sessions. Read them in their panels:
- `docs/playtests/paper-1/panel/experience.md` (Hybrids I'd try): the obituarist, fielding eleven, the anthology, the loft;
- `docs/playtests/paper-1/panel/systems.md` (Hybrids to try): the Courier's beat, the club that keeps the town, the loft's landlord, the role as a dial;
- `docs/playtests/paper-1/panel/narrative.md` (Hybrids to try): the reporter who covers deaths, the band as a role, the reading as an inner voice, the family story as a cold case.

## Not a fit

- **Action-led games** (combat, chases, racing): they hide what the world is good at.
- **Crime sandboxes** built on violence: they run against Rule 8 and against "evidence, not verdicts".
- **A pure city builder of traffic and zoning:** our strength is people and history, not throughput.
