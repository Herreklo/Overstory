# Stone: the reference board

The board each stone part is made and judged against: first the gully steps (the second part in `docs/experiments.md`), later quay walls, kerbs, setts and bedrock. Downloaded with Øystein's OK, 22 October 2026. The free images are kept here at 1920 px wide (Wikimedia's standard size) or smaller where the original is smaller. The copyrighted ones are kept for study only in `study/`, which git ignores; they are listed below with their sources so the board can be rebuilt.

## What makes them work

- **The town's own stone is dark and slaty.** Gamle Stavanger's flagstones and dry-stone walls (02, 03) are thin, flat, blue-grey slabs of the local rock, laid in their beds and stacked; the dressed granite of setts, kerbs and quays (13, 12) came later and from elsewhere. The 1895 stair at Dale (06) is the same kind of stone as split-slab steps.
- **Three values a stone, given by its history.** On dark slaty stone, crustose lichen sits as pale grey-white rounded patches on the parts no one treads (06, 05), moss as a green line along the back of each tread and in every joint (06, 02, 14), and the stone itself is the dark middle. The pattern is the history: lichen where it is quiet, none on the walking line, moss where it is damp and shaded.
- **Wet makes it near-black and a mirror.** In rain the slabs go almost black and reflect the sky (02, 01), the setts take a sheen, and the joints read as green-black lines (02). Water stands in the hollows.
- **Wear shows as shape.** Treads hollowed down the walking line and nosings rounded (07, 08: soft stone, so deeper than ours will be); on split stone the fronts stay rough and the treads are often in two or three pieces with cracks (06).
- **Walls are laid, not tiled.** Thin flat stones in courses that wander, the big ones low, plants on top (03, 10, 11); a coursed big-block wall beside a dry-stone one tells two builders and two dates (09).
- **Painters keep the masses.** Kielland's wall (18) and Hertervig's boulders (17) are a few planes of light and shade with the texture only at the edges; Firewatch's rocks (21) are chunky planes with crisp chamfers, two or three values each, no surface noise; Grimshaw's quay (20) is wet stone as a mirror for the lamps.

## The first piece's target (to agree with Øystein)

The gully steps, built in the 1870s with the dry-stone wall beside them, are split slabs of the local slaty stone, as at Dale (06): each tread one to three slabs with a rough split front, resting on the step below; the cheek wall in thin flat stones laid dry and leaning back. In 1989: the busy middle worn smooth, a shade greyer, with no lichen; pale lichen patches on the quiet ends and the wall's coping, sized by their years; green moss along the back of each tread and in the joints, more on the shaded side; a run of steps reset by the town's crew in 1952 (bedded level again in cement, their joint moss thinner, a few broken slabs swapped for weathered ones from the depot), an earlier run reset in lime in 1911, and patchwork between: corners lost and dabbed with cement in different decades, cracks smeared or left (`docs/upkeep.md`); in drizzle near-black and glossy, water in the hollows. At eye level, the stone's lamination and the lichen's rosettes; from the street stop, three values a step; from 12 m and the city camera, a dark ribbon with a paler worn stripe and green-dark edges. Granite comes with the kerbs, setts and quays.

## Local lanes: walls meeting their steps (approved 22 October)

They are for form only and never used in the game. Each archive's web JPEG was taken, never a TIFF: Stavanger byarkiv's 2400 px preview and Marcus's "JPG - original", both scaled here to 1920 px on the long side. Commons files are its thumbnails at 1920 px on the long side; 45 was taken as the 1920 px wide thumbnail and scaled, since Commons does not serve 1440 px. 30 is the left frame of a stereo pair, at the preview's own size. Stavanger byarkiv's terms are not confirmed. Six are likely still protected (åndsverkloven § 23: the photographer's life plus 15 years, and at least 50 years from the year taken; recalled, not checked), so by this board's rule they are in `study/`: 33, 34, 35 and 43, taken under 50 years ago; 31, whose photographer died in 2012; and 32, from 1975, whose photographer's dates were not found. **What the set shows.** Thirteen of the twenty bear on the question (28, 29, 31 to 33, 35 to 40, 42); 34 and 41 a little; 30 and 43 to 47 almost nothing.
- **Copings: none, or level and stepped, on dry walls; raked only on dressed or cast work.** No dry or rubble wall beside a lane flight has a raked coping. Most have no coping at all: turf, planting, a fence or a pavement on top (32, 33 above, 36, 37, 38, 39). Where a wall has a coping, it is level slabs that drop square at each rest (29, 33 below, 40 behind), and low walls and upstands step in the same way (28, 34). Raked tops appear only on the rendered cheek of the 1927 flight (28), on the granite ashlar cheeks of the formal Kong Carls stair (42), and on cast kerbs (43, 44).
- **Courses beside the pitch are level.** In every dry wall the beds are level and the courses run into the flight, ending on a tread or against a step's end (32, 36, 37, 38). Where a wall ends on a slope, it is racked back course by course (28). The one exception is 42: dressed granite cut as parallelograms in courses parallel to the pitch, an engineered stair of the 1920s or 1930s, not dry work.
- **Landings, foot and head.** Rests are setts behind a long kerb-like top step (29, 31) or large flags (40). A wall beside a rest stays level and drops square at the next flight (29). At a flight's foot there is a square rubble pier (28), a level-bedded granite block where a raked coping ends (42), or nothing: the bottom step is a long slab on the street and the wall starts with a big corner stone (36). At a head there is a newel and bare rock (30) or a level rest (31, 37). No photograph shows a coping turning from rake to level at a rest.
- **Steps built into the walls.** Yes, in three ways. Flights are set in a slot through a retaining wall, with a head of big stones each side (32, 33, 38, 39). Flights are built along a wall's face on an under-wall whose top steps with the treads (37, 28). Dry walls are built on the tread ends, so the wall's foot climbs with the flight (36). How far an end goes into the wall cannot be seen.

**Against the redraw's design** (`reviews/lt1/port/2b/design.md`):
- **SN2, supported:** level beds, with no course following the pitch (32, 36, 37, 38); the footing on the steps, with course lines from the step tops (36, 37); wall ends as heads of the biggest stones (32, 33, 39).
- **SN2, contradicted: the raked coping on a dry slate wall,** with its kneelers and knee stones. Dry walls beside local flights have no coping or a level, stepped one (29, 33, 36, 38, 40). A raked coping, and a level-bedded block at its foot, appear only on dressed granite (42). No photograph shows an intermediate kneeler or a knee stone. This is the case SN2's "What would change our minds" names: stepped slab copings set level (BTCV), now with local photographs behind it, or no coping where the bank runs over the top. It should be A/B'd in grey form against the raked coping before R2b lays copings.
- **SN3, supported:** one or two stones a step, joints moving from the step below (35, 40); each tread lapping the step below (36, 40); rests of setts behind a kerb step or of large flags (29, 31, 40).
- **SN3, in part contradicted: the kind of step.** In the dry-walled lanes the steps are thin split slabs on risers of laid stones (32, 36, 38, 39, 41). Solid block steps belong to the formal flights (28, 29, 35, 42). The gully's record says block steps, so the record decides. The photographs say a block-step flight was built as formal work, so its walls may be dressed and coped (28, 42) rather than dry and uncoped.

## Gaps

- No close photograph of worn granite steps in Norway. The deep hollows (07, 08) are in softer English stone; granite wears far more slowly, so our hollows should be shallower for the same years.
- Gamle Stavanger's own stepped lanes are thin on Commons: its lanes and walls are there, its flights of steps up close are not. The local lanes (28 to 43, from Stavanger byarkiv and Marcus) close this for how walls meet their steps; close views of worn steps in the town's own stone are still missing.
- What stone the town is built of: the walls in 02 and 03 are thin, flat, slaty stones, not granite blocks. Confirm what Stavanger's steps, walls and quays are made of before a part's colour and split are fixed.
- No Stavanger quay wall close up (Vågen's granite edge, its coping, steps down to the water, bollards); 12 is the nearest.
- No Norwegian stone flight photographed in real rain; 01, 02 and 07 show stone wet after rain.
