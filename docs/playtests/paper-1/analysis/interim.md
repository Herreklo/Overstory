# Paper playtest 1: interim note

11 of 24 runs (mystery 4, paper 7); no verdict. Numbers: `interim-numbers.md`. Ledgers not yet audited.

**The mystery** (two pairs). Lives on predicted better, 33% and 25% right against 8% and 10%. But lives on was Sonnet and off Opus, so arm and model are confounded. Lives on solved the case worse: 15 and 25 of 100, against 45 and 55. The whole gap is three facts whose lives-off answer was "nothing on record"; the other seven tied. Both lives-on players heard the cause of death from a witness and didn't use it. All four called the hearing's rules a cheat.

**The paper** (three pairs). Lives on printed only true claims (103 of 103), lives off 97%, 91% and 90%. Gaps of 3, 9 and 10 points: between "lives pay" (15) and "they don't" (5). Game masters scored softened wording differently; under one rule the mean gap is 4.5, inside "they don't". Pull favoured lives on in all three.

**Misreadings.** 100 of 115 were players' own leaps, 7 from a screen or record. Lives-off players told causal stories as often (31% of exit claims, against 28%), and 16 of 22 were wrong. Model players invent causes the world lacks.

**Recurring needs:** who is kin to whom (10 of 11 runs), what moves the paper's meters (all 7), who found and certified the dead (all 4 mysteries; the engine records neither).

**Hypotheses.** Three lean against: lives make the mystery solvable, source judgement is learned, and the town hands the paper its stories. One leans for: the screens are fair. One is unsettled, one untested.

**Fixes before more runs:**
1. Keep lives-off game masters out of the real run. Four of five saw real lines; their own world page holds three real lives.
2. Check setups by script.
3. Ask the mystery's understanding questions before the close gives the answers.
4. Cap screens at 1,200 words, seed small chances, score wording by one rule.

**Contamination.** Nothing needs excluding: no real-run fact reached a lives-off screen. From round 4, three lives-off paper runs rest on the game master's word.

**The other 13 runs:** the mystery's kinds 5 and 6 break the model confound; the paper gets its missing twin; the club's 8 test the only concept with a domain loop. Cost: these 11 took about 14% of the week (other sessions alongside); the 13, about 12 to 15%.
