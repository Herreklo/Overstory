# Old Town (the restorer): card

The playable card for `restorer`, lives on only (cohort X). Game masters, analysts and the lead read it; players see only the pitch, through their screens.

## The question

Does reading what lives did to houses and owners pay a restorer in kroner, with fronts kept true?

Judged by: across the six players, the correlation of reading score (predictions about sellers, faults and tenants, scored) with profit (final net worth minus 150,000 kr); people cited in buy, sell and works decisions (coded); marks on works turns; the memory probe; the 1995 street, read and scored.

For: r of 0.3 or more; people cited in a fifth of those decisions; bored marks on works turns under half; the player on 3006, with no lives behind it, deciding differently. Against: r under 0.3, or buy-early-and-hold beating every reader; works turns mostly bored; people cited in under a fifth.

## The pitch

**Store page.** *Old Town.* In 1989 you come home to the harbour town with some money, a line of credit and an eye for old houses. The cottages on the knuckle are boarded, the cooper's house leaks, and a woman of a hundred and three keeps her garden at the top of the steps. Houses come to market when lives turn: a death, a debt, a job in the city, heirs who won't agree. Read the fronts and the people behind them. Buy, mend the roof before winter, copy the old windows or rip them out, let to newcomers, sell in the right month. The bank wants its interest; a developer wants your street. Come back five years later and see what you saved.

**First minutes.** Your ledger: 150,000 kr, and credit to 150,000 kr at 12%. A front shows condition; a walk round, a look inside or a survey shows more. Each round, up to four turns: inspect, talk, bid, order works, let, sell. A fault you miss gets worse every year. Miss the bank's interest twice and it sells you up. Ask "why?" of anything in your journal: it answers only from what you have seen or heard.

Player 1 (cold start) gets neither.

## Role, goal, resources

**Role:** a returnee, authored, with no engine life.

**Goals.** Short: buy a first house at no more than its value; roof sound before winter. Middle: nothing you own failing at the jump (June 1990). Long: by December 1996, net worth (cash plus houses at value, minus debt) of 400,000 kr, with no listed front stripped.

**Resources:** 150,000 kr; credit to 150,000 kr at 12% a year, interest due each round; four evenings a round; two crews; regard with sellers, neighbours, tenants and the planning officer (−2 to +2, starting 0).

**Leads,** one per player; 3001 (a cooper's house, "the widow can't keep it up", 1983) and 3006 are on every market, with no engine lives behind them.
- **P1: D** (1107), boarded since 1986. Tom Vaughan's heirs, Ken and Jonah Scott, "cannot agree": hearsay only, with no engine event between them, so the why-probe answers "?".
- **P2: E** (304), kept by Agnes Fielding at 103. She dies in December 1989; a lawyer sells for heirs away from March 1990.
- **P3: C's upper flat** (1203): Peter Lane the sitting tenant; below, Leo Lyons in debt, under a shared roof.
- **P4: the loft** (3003), let cheap to a band; two flats would evict it.
- **P5: 3006,** D's twin, boarded since 1986: the control.
- **P6: A** (104), a jugend house let to the café couple (no engine lives) and, from November 1989, Una Waller in the attic; the couple buy in 1996, by its diary.

## Opponents and pressure

- **The bank:** 1% a month on what is drawn, paid each round.
- **The developer** (authored): bids value × 1.1 for any house unsold for two rounds, and strips it; it beats you to a lead on d6 1–2 unless the seller regards you +1.
- **Planning:** the chief officer (Terry Sutton until May 1992, then Lily Lyons); Karen Holmes objects to every change of use, as she did to all six old-bank cafés.
- **The clock:** yearly decay; prices rising from 0.7 (1990) to 1.1 (1996); buyers only in their months.

## Failure

Outright, ending the run: sold up (interest unpaid two rounds, or debt over the limit); a house of yours condemned. Borrowing at 1989's prices, then paying for a late fault, can do it within three rounds.

Partway: a stripped listed front (40,000 kr, officer regard −2); a fault found late; a tenant lost; the developer taking your lead; a seller who won't deal with you.

## Decisions that cost

1. **The crew.** Careful: cost ×1.3, a month slower, never hides a fault. City: ×1.0, strips windows unless told, finds a fault late on d6 1–2.
2. **The front.** Mend or copy windows (+15% value), or fit modern ones (cheaper, −5%).
3. **How deep to look.** From the lane (free); a walk round (an evening, finds a fault on d6 1–4); inside (needs the occupant's leave, finds two); a survey (8,000 kr, all but one).
4. **When to buy and sell.** Prices rise, but interest runs and faults grow.
5. **Pressing a seller in debt or grief:** 0.85 × asking from one who regards you. Their friends are told (the engine's telling) and each cools to you a step. Lives make this harder and better: knowing who is in debt finds the house; knowing whom they talk to prices the deal.
6. **Sitting tenants.** Evict (six months' notice; regard −2 with the family, for Peter Lane his mother Olive Shaw and brothers) or keep the low rent.
7. **Shared roofs.** C's roof is Leo Lyons's too, and he can't pay his half. Pay all of it, or wait: in February 1994 Hannah Tanner breaks her promise to help him, and the leak is left.

## Mastery

Hour 1: learns prices, works and interest; buys by the front; a late fault costs a round. Hour 5: reads who will sell and where faults hide (an owner's debt, grief, drink, age alone); looks before bidding; sells in buyers' months. Hour 10: runs two or three houses across the jump and reads the 1995 street for what five years did.

## Juul's six

- Rules: prices, works, faults, interest, planning, the developer.
- Variable outcome: faults, asking prices, planning dice.
- Valued outcomes: net worth, kept fronts; being sold up.
- Effort: looking, timing, crews, regard.
- Attachment: the houses and their people.
- Negotiable consequences: losing costs only the save.

## The three dials

Control: your houses, money and works; none over who sells, dies or moves. Influence: price, tenancy, eviction, shared roofs, all under the divergence rule. Knowledge: fronts show condition as readings of facts; reasons come through talk and records; faults only through looking.

## Screens and verbs

**Screens:** the street from the lane (front, roof line, windows, garden, lit windows at dusk); the walk round (back walls, gutters); inside (damp, the old windows left in D's gables); the market board; the ledger; the Anchor at 9 pm; a door.

**Records:** the survey report (it misses one fault); the lawyer's letter; the Courier's deaths and notices; the planning list; the Anchor's chatter; dated repairs from the corner's diaries.

**Verbs:** inspect, talk, decide, travel, wait; and bid, survey, order works, let, evict, sell, apply. Hover: occupant, household, how long there. The journal keeps what was seen, dated and sourced. The why-probe answers only from what the restorer could know, with sources, or "?". A turn is one act; up to four a round.

## What the engine feeds and what the game master simulates

**Engine:**
- Deaths, departures, and arrivals for jobs, the only tenants: Lily Lyons, Una Waller (1989); Gary Hughes, the Bakers (1990); the Newmans (1991); Colin Waller (1992); Agnes Tanner (1993); Oscar Ash (1994); Maisie Baker, Trevor Myers, Ellie Eden (1995); Libby Fielding (1996); Amelia Tanner, Leo Holmes (1997).
- Buyers, each only in their month: Eddie Lane, Thea Waller, Gary Hughes, Gary Myers, Lynn Newman.
- Debt, illness, grief, drink, age alone; regard and telling; ventures' objectors. The corner's diaries (authored) and the atlas's casting.

**GM-simulated,** seeded and labelled:
- Value = base × condition × prices. Base: small house 280,000 kr; flat 220,000; A 520,000; the loft 260,000, or 600,000 as two flats. Condition: sound 1.0, tired 0.8, let go 0.6, boarded 0.45, failing 0.3. Prices: 1989 0.75; 1990–91 0.7; 1992 0.75; 1993 0.85; 1994 0.95; 1995 1.0; 1996 1.1; 1997 1.2; 1998 1.3. Kept or copied front +15%; modern windows −5%. Asking: value × (d6 + 9)/10.
- Works: roof patch 15,000 kr (a month), relay 45,000 (two); windows each mend 2,000, copy 4,000, modern 1,500 (six to a small house); rot 12,000 a fault; paint 15,000; inside 30,000 (two months); two flats 120,000 (four).
- Rent a month, × prices: small house 2,000; flat 1,800; A 4,000; the loft 500; × 0.7 if tired.
- Upkeep: each year an owner or occupant is in debt, ill six months or more, grieving, drinking, or 80+ and alone, or the house is empty, it gains a hidden fault. An unrepaired fault drops condition a step a year; failing and unrepaired a year, the house is condemned.
- Planning: yes on d6 ≤ 3 + regard − objections (the engine's venture rule).
- Owners sell only with a reason in the record (a death, a departure, debt, grief, a want to move). Casting a tenant overrides the atlas, logged authored, re-cast.
- Divergence: an act re-decides only the touched person's engine events in the next 6 to 12 months whose top cause it touched, logged GM-simulated, divergent. No kept fact moves.
- Dice: `python3 -I -c "import random; r=random.Random('paper1/restorer/P<n>/r<k>/<what>'); print([r.randint(1,6) for _ in range(10)])"`.

## Memory probe, tenth hour, exit

**Round 4 probe:** a seller or heir; a tenant or neighbour; an official or buyer, met in rounds 1 or 2.

**The tenth hour** sums up 1997–98 under the player's strategy: net worth; each house's condition, front and occupants; what the developer took.

**Exit questions** name the lead's owners (why it came to market, or didn't), a tenant, and a neighbour or official, and ask what the fronts say about the five years.

## Honest weaknesses

- **The economy is ours.** Prices, works, faults and the developer are invented; the engine supplies only who sells, buys and rents, and why houses decay.
- **Buy-and-hold may dominate.** Prices rise 57% from 1990 to 1996; if faults, interest and the developer don't push back, r will be low for a reason that isn't the town.
- **Leads are uneven.** E has a clear death and sale; D's quarrel has no engine event; A and 3006 have no lives in the house; the control is one player.
- **Faults from lives are our rule;** the engine has no buildings.
- **No lives-off arm:** only the weak 3006 comparison tests the world's worth here.
