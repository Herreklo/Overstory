# The Loft (the band): card

The playable card for `band`, lives on and lives off. Game masters, analysts and the lead read it; players see only the pitch, through their screens.

## The question

Do members' lives give a manager enough to decide between gigs, and do refusals, rows and departures read as people rather than dice, more with real lives than generic ones?

Judged by: each round's predictions (who misses, how a gig goes, who leaves), scored; the memory probe; refusals explained by people (coded); empty rounds (no costly decision); marks on the third gig; "I shouldn't have…" tied to the player's own decision.

For: lives-on predictions 15 points or more above lives off; empty rounds under 40%; the third gig not marked bored; regret twice as common in lives on. Against: the arms within 5 points; empty rounds at 40% or more; refusals read as dice in both arms.

## The pitch

**Store page.** *The Loft.* Above a herring warehouse on the quay, a band rehearses three nights a week, and you hold the lease. You don't play. You book the gigs, pay the rent, order the set, put the names on the posters, and keep four people in one room. They have day jobs, families, pride, and songs they care about more than they say. Every gig is a roll you can load, and every choice costs money, the neighbours' patience or someone's goodwill. Play the Anchor for sixty, gamble on the hall at Eide, or take the ferry dance's money for other people's songs. Then come back five years later and climb the same stairs.

**First minutes.** The ledger shows the kitty and rent due; the board shows members, songs and tightness. Each round you have up to four turns: book a gig, set the rehearsals, visit someone, credit a song. A gig rolls a d20 plus tightness and mood; under 8 it falls apart. Two months' rent unpaid loses the loft. Ask "why?" of anything in your journal: it answers only from what you have seen or been told.

Player 6 (cold start) gets neither.

## Role, goal, resources

**Role:** the band's manager and leaseholder of the loft, an authored newcomer with no engine life. The loft is 3003, the herring salter's warehouse ("a band rents the loft cheap", 1987): the Night Shift's for players 1, 3 and 5 (authored, re-cast), Driftwood's for 2, 4 and 6, as the atlas casts it.

**Starts.** The Night Shift (P1, P3, P5): John Holmes 44, Ben Reed 49, Kate Holmes 68, Sam Brook 63; tightness 6, draw 45. Driftwood (P2, P4, P6): formed December 1990 by Oliver Brook 34 and Peter Lane 34, with Andy Shaw, 77; tightness 2, draw 20.

**Goals.** Short: every booked gig played without falling apart; rent paid. Middle: a good night at the festival or Eide, or radio play. Long: the band together in the loft after round 6, draw up (Night Shift 60, Driftwood 40), kitty above 0.

**Resources:** kitty 3,000 kr; rent 500 kr a month; four evenings a round; the songs members bring (the engine's); regard with each member, starting civil (warm, fond, civil, cool, cold).

## Opponents and pressure

- Members' lives: jobs, families, illness, rows, pride.
- The neighbours: a complaint on d6 1–2 in a round of six or more rehearsals; at three, the landlord raises the rent by half (d6 1–3) or gives notice.
- Gatekeepers: Evan Newman at the Anchor; the festival's organiser (Terry Sutton 1989, Ellie Eden 1996); the June harbour day's (Audrey Scott 1989, Harry Myers 1994, Steve Baker 1996).
- The clock: monthly rent and the engine's dates.

## Failure

Outright: two months' rent unpaid (loft lost); kitty below −2,000 kr (the band lets you go); a split, or under two members. Driftwood's split falls in round 6 unless held, and at a draw of 20 its kitty runs dry in about four rounds without paying gigs. Partway: a leaver; a gig that falls apart; a rent rise; a credit row; a member cold to you.

## Decisions that cost

1. **Rehearsals** (0 to 12 a round): four or more in a month give +1 tightness; six in a round risk a complaint. Pressing a member with a 2,000+ event that round costs regard −1 (lives off: on d6 1–2) unless a visit met its cause.
2. **Credit and set order** for each new song and poster. Lives on: a credit against someone's top cause costs regard −1 and a row (tightness −1); one that meets it gains +1. Lives off: a row on d6 1–2 whatever the choice. Lives make this harder and better: Edward Baker's need to matter, against Andy Shaw's songs.
3. **Booking.** The Anchor: 60 people at 15 kr. The hall at Eide: 200 at 30 kr, hire 1,500 kr, so it pays only past a draw of 50. The ferry dance: 2,000 kr for covers, cooling a member proud of their songs (lives off: each on d6 1). The harbour day: unpaid, draw +5 if good.
4. **A studio day,** 2,500 kr: radio play on d20 ≥ 15 − tightness/2, draw +10.
5. **A visit** (an evening) with an offer: time, up to 500 kr, a rest, a promise of credit. Lives on: one that meets the top cause waives the mood penalty, regard +1; money cools the proud. Lives off: works on d6 1–3.
6. **Recruiting** from the record's candidates: Neil Lyons (guitar from 1990); Sarah Lane, Una Waller, Matt Sayer (from 1995). A newcomer costs −2 tightness.
7. **Holding the band** (a turn). When the record has a member leave or the band split, a hold that meets the event's top cause lets it happen only on d6 1–2; otherwise it stands. Lives off: any hold, d6 1–4.

## Mastery

Hour 1: rehearses, books the Anchor; a night that falls apart teaches the costs. Hour 5: times rehearsals, gigs and visits to members' lives; knows whose song should lead and whose name goes on the poster. Hour 10: sees a departure coming from its causes, recruits ahead, holds a split by meeting its real cause. In lives off only mood, from notability, rewards reading.

## Juul's six

- Rules: tightness, gig roll, money, credit and hold rules.
- Variable outcome: gigs, refusals, departures.
- Valued outcomes: good nights, draw; the loft lost, the split.
- Effort: rehearsals, bookings, visits and credits load the dice.
- Attachment: the band; the stakes probe tests whether it is the people.
- Negotiable consequences: losing costs only the save.

## The three dials

Control: loft, money, bookings, set and credit; none over lives or songs. Influence: regard, mood, tightness; engine events move only by the hold rule and divergence. Knowledge: what a manager sees and hears; private events only when told.

## Screens and verbs

**Screens:** the loft on a rehearsal night (who came, who is late, the song on the stand); the ledger; the board (members with job, regard, mood; songs with writer and credit; tightness; draw); the calendar; the Anchor on show nights; Eide's hall; the quay; a member's door; the street below, with the neighbours' windows.

**Records:** posters (the manager writes the credit); the Courier's what's-on and reviews (one item in eight wrong); the Anchor's chatter; the landlord's letters.

**Verbs:** inspect, talk, decide, travel, wait; and book, rehearse, credit, record, recruit, hold. Hover: age, job, household, instrument. The journal keeps what was seen, dated and sourced. The why-probe answers only from what the manager could know, with sources, or "?"; in lives off, "nothing on record says why". A turn is one act; up to four a round.

## What the engine feeds and what the game master simulates

**Engine:** the bands' events (joins, leavings, split, songs, shows, open nights, festivals, harbour days), members' lives and kept facts.

**GM-simulated,** seeded and labelled:
- Tightness 0–10: +1 a month with four or more rehearsals, +1 when the record says "tighter than last time"; −1 a row, −2 a newcomer, −3 a leaver.
- GM-booked gigs: d20 + tightness + the worst member's mood (−2 for an event of 2,000+ this month or last, −1 for 1,000–1,999). Under 8 falls apart, 8–13 plain, 14–19 good, 20+ great. June 1996's harbour day is −3.
- Engine shows keep the record's outcome; their door and draw are ours.
- Draw: great +8, good +4, fell apart −5, radio +10, poster +5 (100 kr). Door: draw (up to the room's size) times the price.
- The festival says yes on d6 ≤ 2 + regard (−1 to +2; in lives on, the organiser's ties to members count).
- Landlord, neighbours, Eide, ferry and studio are authored.
- Divergence: an act re-decides only the touched person's engine events in the next 6 to 12 months whose top cause it touched, logged GM-simulated, divergent. Kept facts never move.
- Dice: `python3 -I -c "import random; r=random.Random('paper1/band/P<n>/r<k>/<what>'); print([r.randint(1,6) for _ in range(10)])"`; `randint(1,20)` for a d20. No arm in the seed.

## Lives on and lives off

**The off arm changes:** generic lives and song titles; re-dealt outcomes; no claimed songs; leavings and the split on their dates, without reasons; credit rows on d6 1–2, visits d6 1–3, holds d6 1–4; no past.

**Identical:** card, screens, members, every date, venues, money, dice.

**We expect** lives on to give departures explained by people, regret tied to credits, better predictions and fewer empty rounds; lives off to play as a slot machine with names.

## Memory probe, tenth hour, exit

**Round 4 probe:** a member; someone outside the band whose life touched a member (Jonah Scott for John; Trevor Lane or Ben Reed for Andy); a gatekeeper or candidate the player dealt with.

**The tenth hour.** Night Shift, 1995–96: songs, shows, John and Ivan Tanner refusing each other's peace. Driftwood after September 1996: an empty loft until Pirate Radio (March 1998) if the split stood, or the band running on, divergent; Andy Shaw dies on his date (October 1998) either way.

**Exit questions** name the main writer (Kate Holmes, Andy Shaw), the member at the centre of friction (John Holmes; Edward Baker), a leaver or the split's reason, and someone outside the band.

## Honest weaknesses

- **Driftwood's first act is thin:** February and March 1991 hold three events, one knowable; rounds 1 to 3 rest on our economy.
- **The Night Shift is calm:** no leaver or split in the run, members 44 to 68; players 1, 3 and 5 may find the stakes low.
- **Engine shows are fixed;** only GM-booked gigs roll, so the big nights may not feel like the player's.
- **The richest scene, the credit claim, falls in the jump;** Andy Shaw's circle, the run's richest, favours Driftwood.
- **The venues, money and neighbours are ours.**
