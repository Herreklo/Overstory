# A death in the town: card

The playable design of concept 6 (`mystery`) for paper playtest 1, for game masters, setup writers, analysts and the lead; players only ever see the pitch, through their screens.

## The question

Does a natural death, read through the lives around it, hold as a game: what happened, and was anyone answerable? The panel warned that natural deaths read as biography and the best puzzles are small wrongs with a scapegoat, so the main case holds both: Edward Baker let people think Andy Shaw's song was his, and two people went at the dying man at the Anchor.

**How it is judged** (the close, `scores.md`). Lives on, players get half the facts, and at most a third of their wrong claims trace to a screen. They get 4 of the 6 facts that have reasons, and name an innocent less often than lives-off players. The sceptic and optimiser pull within 3 of their anchors; fewer than three exits call it a cheat.

**What would change our minds.** For: lives-on players catch two of the obituary's three errors and clear the scapegoat. Against: the lives-on close averages under 50, or the arms' pull is within 1 point.

## The pitch

**Store page.** *Answerable.* A harbour town, 1997. Every death here is heard. You are the coroner's officer. Before each hearing you have days to find out what happened, and whether anyone was answerable. Nobody is murdered. But the dead leave people behind, each telling it their own way: a song credited to the wrong man, a quarrel at the pub, an obituary everyone believes. Every question you ask is heard. Grief makes people short with you. Give an account that holds, and never name the innocent. Then the record shows what you got right. Two deaths, a year apart and more, in a town that remembers you.

**Tutorial.** A turn is one act: visit one person and ask up to three questions, watch a place at a time, read records, or wait. Your journal keeps everything you are told, by whom, where and when. File claims on your board with their sources. People hear of your questions; the coroner strikes hearsay. Once a case you may check three claims, and you learn only whether all three stand. Your standing starts at 3.

## Role, goal, resources

**Role:** the district's coroner's officer (authored, GM-simulated, no engine life). By custom every death is heard; the coroner asks fixed questions.

**Goals.** Short: accepted accounts for Mary Tanner (14 March 1997) and Andy Shaw (11 December 1998), nobody innocent named. Middle: standing 5, for harder cases. Long: a career the record bears out.

**Resources.** Turns: 4 a round (8, then 16), the hearing the last. Standing: 3 of 6. One check a case, from its second round. Each person's willingness: open, guarded (one question a visit) or closed; grieving kin start guarded.

## Opponents and pressure

A fixed hearing date; people who know only what they could, bent by liking, shielding friends; questions that travel; confident, partly wrong records; a coroner who strikes hearsay and summons anyone named.

## Failure

**Outright.** At standing 0 you are taken off the cases. One session can do it: an adjourned first hearing (−1), a complaint (−1), and an innocent named at the second (−2).

**Partway.** Adjourned (two claims struck, or a claim of answerability struck): standing −1, and the town knows. Rejected (anyone named answerable): standing −2, and the person named, with their partner, family and friends, closes to you for the session (GM-simulated; engine lives unchanged). At the close: every fact missed or wrong.

## Decisions that cost

1. **Whom to see, and when.** A visit costs a turn and spreads your question. Kin know most but are guarded and may complain. Ask a rumour's subject first and they stay open.
2. **When to check.** Early guides loose claims but may be wasted; late may be too late to fix.
3. **What to claim.** Contradicted claims cost; "?" scores only where nobody could know; naming anyone answerable is always wrong here.
4. **Watch or ask.** A watch is unheard and shows routines and absences; asking yields words, bent.
5. **Whom to believe,** which lives make harder and better: Olive Shaw, fond of Edward Baker, credits him with the song (A-18); Neil Lyons, who admires Andy's songs, is right (A-20). Lives off, there is no bend to learn.

## Mastery

- **Hour 1:** asks everyone everything, believes the obituary, names whoever shouted last.
- **Hour 5:** asks the subject first, weighs tellers' ties, prefers first-hand and dated sources, watches routines for who could have seen, keeps "?" honest.
- **Hour 10:** reads the past in the present: who stopped coming on Fridays, the poster naming a dead man, the biggest gift to the hat and why it cuts both ways.

Reading pays: Andy is alive in every start's case 1, and what you saw then is first-hand at his hearing.

## Juul's six

- **Rules:** four acts a round, heard questions, cited sources, one check.
- **Variable outcome:** accepted, adjourned or rejected; a scored close.
- **Valued outcomes:** a true account, standing kept, the innocent unnamed.
- **Effort:** choosing whom to ask; weighing tellers.
- **Attachment:** your name is on the account, and those named hear of it.
- **Negotiable consequences:** fictional standing; nobody real is judged.

## The three dials

- **Control: low.** Only your own acts.
- **Influence: low and local.** Questions change who talks; the account changes how the people named treat you. No engine life changes.
- **Knowledge: narrow, and it is the game.** What witnesses could know, bent; fallible records; your own eyes. Truth only at the close.

## Screens and verbs

**Screens:** the case file (notice D-03 or D-04, questions, turns, standing); places (the widower's kitchen, the Anchor on Friday with P-13 still up in November, Thursday choir, the harbour steps at seven, the Courier's bound volumes, the gully steps, the churchyard); the board; the rulings; the close, with causes and weights.

**Verbs:** inspect (free), talk, watch, read, travel (within an act), wait; its own: file (free), check, give the account.

**Interface:** hover names; the journal, keeping every statement verbatim; the why-probe, answering only for public events you have found, with the stated reason (the split, e-8374: "they want it to sound like different things"), and otherwise "ask someone who was there".

## The clock

**Case 1, Mary Tanner** (died February 1997, e-8459). Round 1 runs 3 to 7 March 1997; round 2 runs 10 to 14 March, with the hearing as its fourth turn.

**The jump.** Nineteen months, to 26 October 1998; Andy Shaw died in mid-October (e-8899). Round 3 opens where round 1 did: Olive Shaw gone from the bar, Pirate Radio's poster up, his choir chair empty.

**Case 2, Andy Shaw.** Round 3: 26 October to 6 November. Round 4: 9 to 20 November. Round 5: 23 November to 4 December. Round 6: 7 to 11 December, the hearing its fourth turn.

- **Never hinted:** Andy's debts, the visits, what he told Ben Reed, Mark's drinking, Mary's doubts, why the swimming stopped (e-6593).

**Case 2's ten facts,** 10 points each: (1) cancer; (2) ill from spring 1996; (3) nobody caused his death; (4) two of the four biggest givers to the hat; (5) Karen Holmes's peace, July 1998; (6) Alice Tanner and Sarah Lane went at him, June 1998; (7) nothing settled it; (8) "Christmas Fence" is Andy's, and Edward let people think it his; (9) the split was over the sound, not his illness; (10) the swimming stopped about 1990, long before his illness ("?" is right if the setup finds no witness). Facts 3 and 5 to 10 carry reasons. Partly right scores 5, contradicted −5, an innocent named −20; the total is clamped to 0 to 100. Case 1's four facts (a sudden heart attack, strokes survived, nobody answerable, the hat) are reported apart.

**GM-simulated rules.** Each roll is seeded `mystery-<arm>-<id>-r<N>-t<turn>-<what>`.
1. **Yield** (`q<k>`, d6 a question): 1, "I couldn't say"; 2 to 5, one fact they could know; 6, that plus a join of their own, from their chatter or in telling's shape. Grieving kin take two questions at most; on 1 or 2, "not today".
2. **Bend** (`bend`). Lives on, by liking. Lives off, d6: 1 softened, 6 hardened.
3. **Wrong name** (`name`, d20). A teller with sincerity under −0.8 slips on 3 or less. Lives off, the same tellers slip at random.
4. **Heard** (`heard`, d6). Someone absent you asked about turns guarded: on 4+ if you asked their partner, family or a friend, otherwise on 6.
5. **Complaint** (`complaint`, d6). A second visit to close kin in one round: on 4+, standing −1.
6. **Talk about the dead** takes telling's shape.
7. **Watches** show routines and the month's local events; an ended routine shows as an absence, to the month, without its reason.
8. **The coroner's test.** A claim stands on your own sighting, a first-hand witness, a dated record, or two independent second-hand tellers. Anyone named is summoned; their first-hand evidence defeats the claim.
9. **The check** is compared with the record and logged as CARD.
10. **Andy Shaw** appears alive in every start's case 1.

**Six starts, six doors:** (1) the Courier's back room, (2) the band (Oliver Brook), (3) Tuesday cards (Olive Shaw), (4) the gully steps (Hannah Tanner, Karen Holmes), (5) the widower (Mark Holmes), (6) the Anchor's bar.

## Lives on and lives off

**Off changes:** deaths without cause ("died, aged 85"), no illness first, routines that never lapse, no credit row, a split with no reason, records that slip at random. **Identical:** dates, doors, questions, rules, rolls, screens and fact slots.

**The off case** is a shallow sim's case of who, when and where. Its facts: no illness on record; nobody answerable; who was with him most; his last band; a row that carried nothing; the split's date; the obituary's random error; his routines. Asked why, it answers "nothing on record says why".

**Expected if lives matter:** lives-on players get reasons right, mark more turns engaged and name people they would lose; lives-off players deduce nothing beyond dates.

## Memory probe, tenth hour, exit

- **Probe:** the start's case-1 kin or first witness; a case-1 witness who returns in case 2; and Andy Shaw, as met alive.
- **Tenth hour:** the close, talk of your account, Neil Lyons found out at the sawmill (e-8969), then plainly: no death to the record's end, January 1999.
- **Exit:** "What do you believe happened to Andy Shaw, Edward Baker and Sarah Lane, and why?"

## Honest weaknesses

- **The jump is 19 months,** not a year or five. Ellie Holmes to Mary Tanner (56 months) would fit "five" but loses the rich case.
- **Weak robustness:** only Andy's death carries four rounds; twelve players meet one life through six doors.
- **Case 1 is thin** and may read as biography, as the panel predicted.
- **A well-sourced account can be wrong,** which may feel unfair. The swimming may lack a second route. "Who stood by him" sits beside private visits and money, a leak risk.
- **The panel's risks remain:** the optimiser may find guarded witnesses cheap over 16 turns, and the band's story may outpull the death.
- **No career:** four deaths a decade can't feed one; that needs fatal accidents or more towns.
