# The Family Table (a life and its heirs): card

The playable card for `family`, lives on only (cohort Y). Game masters, analysts and the lead read it; players see only the pitch, through their screens.

## The question

Does playing a real person, then their heir five years on, make a choice's cost felt and readable: do players see a loss coming, change outcomes, and read on their return what the years did?

Judged by: losses named in the exit, tied to a person and a cause; outcomes changed per player (divergent events logged); the return (three round 4 questions on what changed, scored); pull at succession; regret read as caused (a later outcome tied to an earlier choice, as the record agrees).

For: four of six name a loss; two or more outcomes changed per player; the return read two of three right; pull at round 4 within 1 of round 3. Against: under three name a loss; choices that change nothing visible; the heir's start read as a new game; pull falling 2 or more at succession.

## The pitch

**Store page.** *The Family Table.* One kitchen table, two generations. In 1989 you are a real member of a harbour-town family: a mother whose son's confidence was passed round, a father who took the blame for his daughter's partner, a widow deciding whom to let close. Their choices come one at a time: tell or keep quiet, take the money or leave it, mend the roof or help a son. Then five years pass. You sit down at the same table as the next in line, with your parent's notes and a house that shows what you chose. Keep the family together and the roof on, or lose both.

**First minutes.** Spring 1989, your kitchen table. The family board shows each relative and friend, and how close you are. When your life brings a choice, it comes as a card: what is happening, and what you could do. Some choices show their cost only years later. An evening with someone keeps you close; two seasons apart cools you a step. The house sheet shows its condition and your money. Ask "why?" of anything in your journal: it answers only from what you have seen or been told.

Player 1 (cold start) gets neither.

## Role, goal, resources

**Goals.** Short: every close relative civil or warmer; the house losing no step. Middle: at the jump, the house sound or tired, the heir fond or warmer toward the lead. Long: as the heir, end April 1996 with no close relative cold and the house not failing, having chosen what to keep: house, ties, town.

**Resources:** 20,000 kr savings; +1,000 kr a month per wage or pension, after living costs; four turns a round; bonds with relatives and up to three friends (warm, fond, civil, cool, cold); the journal; the house.

## Opponents and pressure

- **The record:** illness (Greta, February 1990; Olive, June 1995; Toby, November 1995); temptation (Eddie, February 1990); blame and confidences (Toby, 1989; Jonah, July 1989); couples drifting (Molly and Ivan Tanner); the city (Jonah, April 1996; Alice, July 1995).
- **Time:** evenings never cover everyone; two rounds unseen cools a bond (the engine's contact rule).
- **The house:** faults from the upkeep rule, shared with the restorer's card.

## Failure

Outright, ending the run: the family breaks (at a round's end the player's person is cold with every living close relative); the house sold up (money below 0 for two rounds) or condemned.

Partway: a bond gone cold; a fault left to grow; a choice whose cost lands on someone else. Two neglected relatives and one hard choice in Act 1 can start the heir cold with the lead.

## Decisions that cost

1. **The record's choices.** Matching keeps the record and its cost; diverging changes the touched people's next 6 to 12 months, helping one relative and perhaps costing another. Lives make this harder: real ties sit behind each card.
   - Toby Waller, October 1989: Colin Berry, Thea's partner, let him take the blame (September, private). Take it (the record) or name Colin, a month before Thea's son Andy is born.
   - Eddie Lane, February 1990: the till at the garage, his private temptation. Take a little (the record) or leave it.
   - Audrey Scott: run the June 1989 harbour day (the record) or not; D's roof, due by March 1990 (authored), for sons who won't agree.
   - Greta Brook, February 1990: her diagnosis. Her foreman John Holmes asks what is wrong: tell him, or say it's nothing (the record).
   - Molly Scott, June 1989: raise Ivan's absence from the family (the record), carefully or bluntly, or let it go again.
   - Olive Shaw: give up the Anchor's Fridays (January 1990, the record) or keep them; Ben Reed's courtship from February 1990.
   - Act 2: Sarah, the open night (April 1996); Jonah, the city; Oliver, what John Holmes told him; Thea, her father's heart attack; Eric, his mother's illness; Alice, the city job.
2. **Evenings,** one person each. A family dinner (a turn, 300 kr) holds the household for a round but warms no one.
3. **Money: the house or the people.** A roof relay (45,000 kr) against a gift or loan to a relative in trouble.
4. **Three standing rules at the jump.** Each costs evenings or money every year of the five; whoever they don't cover drifts.
5. **The inheritance:** keep, let or sell the house; stay, or go as the record goes.

## Mastery

Hour 1: plays each card on its face; learns that evenings run out and costs come later. Hour 5: reads the record's patterns (who confides, who keeps away, who is ailing) and spends evenings ahead of trouble. Hour 10: shapes what the heir inherits (the house, the ties, the grudges) and reads on the return what the years did.

## Juul's six

- Rules: choice cards, evenings and contact, money, the house.
- Variable outcome: the record, and its seeded divergence.
- Valued outcomes: a family together, a house kept; a family broken.
- Effort: evenings, choices, money, standing rules.
- Attachment: one's own family across years.
- Negotiable consequences: losing costs only the save.

## The three dials

Control: one person's choices, evenings and money. Influence: bonds and divergence, 6 to 12 months at a time. Knowledge: the person's own private events and what they are told or see; the heir has only the notes and their own record.

## Screens and verbs

**Screens:** the kitchen table (the lead's, Sunday at six, every round: who sits where, what is on it); the family board (bond, last seen); the choice card (situation and options, never advice); the house sheet; places the record names (the garage, the clinic, the berth, the Anchor, the shore); a relative's door.

**Records:** the journal, handed to the heir as the lead's notes ("your mother's notes"); the Courier; the Anchor's chatter.

**Verbs:** inspect, talk, decide, travel, wait; and spend an evening, choose, mend, give, write a rule. Hover: age, job, household, bond. The why-probe answers only from what the person could know, with sources, or "?". A turn is one act, up to four a round; choice cards arrive as interruptions and take no turn.

## The clock

- **Act 1:** round 1, April–June 1989; round 2, July–October 1989; round 3, November 1989–March 1990.
- **The jump,** five years, up to three standing rules. The GM runs April 1990 to February 1995 on the record, diverging only where rules or choices touched a cause.
- **Act 2:** round 4, March–June 1995, the return to the same table at the same hour, now as the heir; round 5, July–October 1995; round 6, November 1995–April 1996.
- **Tenth hour:** May 1996 to mid-1997, with the August storm for the fleet's families.

## What the engine feeds and what the game master simulates

**Engine:** the lead's and heir's events with decision kinds, causes and visibility; the family's kept facts (births, jobs, partners, departures); relatives' and friends' events, shown only as the person could know them; contact and regard; the base rates (promises, temptations, confiding, grief).

**GM-simulated,** seeded and labelled:
- **Choice cards:** one per event with a decision kind for the person; the options are the engine's for that kind (`engine-rules.md`).
- **Divergence:** a choice against the record re-decides only the touched people's events in the next 6 to 12 months whose top cause, or a cause of weight 300+, it touched; logged GM-simulated, divergent. Kept facts never move: births, deaths, jobs, partners, arrivals and departures keep their dates.
- **Found out:** a hidden act in the record is found out only where the record says; one the player adds, on d20 ≤ 2 a round.
- **Bonds:** an evening +1 (to warm); two rounds unseen −1; a choice against someone's top cause −1 with them.
- **The house:** the restorer's tables (condition, works, upkeep faults from the household's own lives).
- **The notes:** the heir gets the lead's journal as the player wrote it, nothing added.
- **The heir's bonds** start from the record's ties, adjusted by divergence and the standing rules.
- **Dice:** `python3 -I -c "import random; r=random.Random('paper1/family/P<n>/r<k>/<what>'); print([r.randint(1,6) for _ in range(10)])"`.

## Memory probe, tenth hour, exit

**Round 4 probe,** three people from Act 1: the lead's closest relative; someone outside the household who mattered in a choice; someone the lead neglected. Beside it, the return's three questions (what changed at the table, in the house, in one person).

**The tenth hour** sums up the heir's next twenty turns: the house, who left or fell ill, whether the family held.

**Exit questions** name the lead (what their choices cost), the heir's closest relative, someone outside the family touched by a choice, and the house.

## Honest weaknesses

- **No elder in these six families dies in the run.** Losses are illness, departures and cooled bonds, so "name a loss" is a harder test than a death would make it.
- **Some lives are thin.** Audrey Scott and Molly Scott have mostly small events; Jonah has three in Act 2; Sarah Lane has none in Act 1, so P1's heir arrives with no memories of her own. Some rounds will bring few cards.
- **Couple loops.** The engine repeats a couple's row in few shapes (Molly and Ivan; Ken Scott and Sylvia Waller's 33 rows touch the Scotts).
- **The GM writes every conversation;** the words are ours.
- **Divergence compounds.** A player who often chooses against the record plays Act 2 in a world the engine never ran.
- **People barely change** (about 3% in thirty years); the heir carries the change.
- **No lives-off arm:** nothing separates the world's worth from the writing.
