# The paper: card

Concept 5 (`journalist`) for paper playtest 1, for game masters, setup writers, analysts and the lead. Players see only the pitch.

## The question

Does a weekly edition make a game of finding out what happened to someone, when every source has ties? The panel's terms are built in: the edition is the loop, sales trade against trust, and the editor's own family is in the news.

**Judged by** the close, which scores every printed claim against the record (`scores.md`). Lives on, the true share rises 15 points or more from rounds 1–3 to rounds 4–6, and not lives off; a quarter of stories start from something the player saw; half the players hold or soften a story at a stated cost.

**What would change our minds.** For: lives-on players catch the step-down join and say why its teller bent. Against: a flat true share in both arms, pages filled from tips alone, or a sceptic who can't name a risk by round 3.

## The pitch

**Store page.** *Thursday's Paper.* A harbour town, 1990. You are the new reporter on the Courier, the town's weekly. It goes to press on Wednesday night, ready or not. Each week you choose what to chase, whom to ask, and what to print or leave out. Everyone you ask has a reason to tell it their way, and the editor's own family is in the news. Print first and you sell. Print wrong and the correction stays in the bound volume. A year on, you'll open last June's volume and see what the town made of your pages.

**Tutorial.** A round is a week: three chase turns, then the edition. A chase turn is one act: visit someone and ask up to three questions, watch a place, read the back numbers, or wait. Answers are for print or not. Your notebook keeps who said what, where and when. In the edition, pick a front page and two inside stories, and mark how you know each claim. Sales, readers' trust and the editor's patience move every week.

## Role, goal, resources

**Role:** the Courier's second reporter from June 1990 (authored, no engine life), with Ken Scott, editor since 1976, Toby Tanner, and Susie Lyons until January 1991.

**Goals:** kept on through six editions with sales at 850 or more; made permanent by year's end with trust at 7; ten years of volumes the town believes.

**Resources:** three chase turns a week; a 500-word front page, two 250-word inside stories and free notices; sales 850; readers' trust 6/10; the editor's patience 5/10; each source's trust 2/3.

## Opponents and pressure

The deadline. The county paper's man, who prints what you hold. The editor's kin: his partner Sylvia Waller (council office), her parents Toby and Grace, her sister Thea (clinic) and Thea's husband-to-be Colin Berry, and his brother Jonah Scott. Sources who bend, go quiet or warn their friends. Readers who buy trouble and remember corrections.

## Failure

**Outright:** you are let go when patience or trust reaches 0. One session can do it: a libel threat (−2) and a wrong kin story printed against the editor (−3).

**Partway:** a correction in the volume, seen after the jump; a burned source, whose partner, family and friends turn guarded; three editions under 750 (the owner's letter, patience −2); a scoop; at the close, every printed claim the record contradicts.

## Decisions that cost

1. **Print now or check.** Checking costs a turn and risks a scoop; printing unchecked risks a correction.
2. **What a claim says:** the act ("let go from the sawmill"), the act with a cause on record, or "it is said". A cause not on record is a false causal history and scores wrong.
3. **Name or shield.** A wrong story with a named first-hand source costs no trust; naming a "not for print" source burns them.
4. **The editor's kin.** He asks you to soften or hold any story naming them. Comply: free, unless the rival prints it (trust −1). Insist: patience −1, or −3 if wrong.
5. **The cruel true story:** Greta Brook let go four months after her diagnosis. Printed, it sells, and those close to Greta or John Holmes stop talking.
6. **Whom to believe,** harder and better with lives on: Toby Waller thinks the world of Terry Sutton, calls the expenses "a few pounds" (A-08), and is the editor's father-in-law to be. Lives off, slips are random.

## Mastery

**Hour 1:** prints tips as told, leads with trouble, names sources, joins two true facts into a false story. **Hour 5:** weighs tellers' ties, asks the subject first, prints acts without unknown causes, watches to get ahead of the rival. **Hour 10:** knows who tells whom, times stories, spends patience only on what is true and matters, and reads last year's volume for what the town took from it. Reading pays twice: in sources, and at the jump, where the step-down invites a join with the expenses.

## Juul's six

- **Rules:** deadline, chase turns, column space, sourced claims, rival, editor.
- **Variable outcome:** sales, trust, patience, scoops, corrections; kept on or let go.
- **Valued outcomes:** sales and trust up, claims that stand.
- **Effort:** chasing, weighing, wording, holding.
- **Attachment:** your byline, and the people who stop talking to you.
- **Negotiable consequences:** a fictional paper.

## The three dials

- **Control: high on the page:** your acts and the edition.
- **Influence: publication.** Reactions to it are GM-simulated; no engine life changes.
- **Knowledge: what sources could know,** bent, plus records and your own eyes; the truth at the close.

## Screens and verbs

**Screens:** the newsroom (Ken's desk, the spike, the planner, the tip book); the beats (the council counter, the sawmill yard, the garage forecourt, the Anchor on Friday, the school gate, the clinic, the quay); the dummy (slots, claims, source marks); the bound volume; the county paper at the co-op.

**Verbs:** inspect, talk, watch, read, travel (within a turn), wait; plus note a tip (free) and make up the edition (the fourth turn): choose, word, source, soften, hold or spike.

**Interface:** hover names; the notebook, verbatim; source marks (saw it, a witness saw it, heard from someone named, record); the why-probe, answering only for public events found, with the reason given at the time (the step-down: "64, and tired", e-6935), and otherwise "ask someone who was there".

## The clock

**Rounds 1 to 3:** the editions of 14, 21 and 28 June 1990. Each June event falls in a week by a seeded d4 (`r0-t0-week-<event>`): 1, before play; 2 to 4, rounds 1 to 3.

**The jump:** one year. **Round 4** opens at last June's bound volume: your pages, any correction, C-05 and C-06, beside what the town now knows. Terry will step down (April 1991); Lily Lyons dragged up his claims in February; Susie's desk is empty; Gary Hughes works at the sawmill.

**Rounds 4 to 6:** the editions of 30 May, 6 and 13 June 1991.

## What the engine feeds and what the game master simulates

**Engine (lives on), filtered by visibility.**
- **June 1990:** Terry Sutton found out on his expense claims (e-6703, local: coworkers, Mona Sayer, and by chance people whose work is with people); Greta Brook let go by John Holmes (e-6688), four months after her cancer (e-6602), her work noticed for months (e-6611), his dislike never knowable; Sally Baker made head mechanic (e-6690) over Gary Hughes (e-6692), who walks out in July (e-6720); the harbour day (e-6701); Harry Myers confronts Una Waller (e-6706).
- **April to June 1991:** Terry will step down next year (e-6935, public); the race for chief officer among Jonah Scott, Sylvia Waller and Lily Lyons, Jonah conceding to Sylvia (e-6955 to e-6961); Toby Waller drags up Colin Berry at the clinic (e-6982); Driftwood's first night (C-09).
- **Records:** C-05 to C-09, A-07, A-08 (softened). **Never shown:** anything private (Ken and Sylvia's rows; e-6497; Colin's medicine cupboard).

**GM-simulated**, each roll seeded `journalist-<arm>-<id>-r<N>-t<turn>-<what>` and logged:
1. **Tips** (`tip-<event>`, d6): a local event of notability 1,000+ reaches the tip book on 4+, from a named teller who could know it, in telling's shape; public events always do.
2. **Yield** (`q<k>`, d6 a question): 1, "no comment"; 2 to 5, one fact they could know; 6, that plus their own join, from chatter or telling's shape. Subjects asked about themselves give a softened account on 4+.
3. **Bend and slip** (`bend`, `name`): lives on, by liking, and a teller with sincerity under −0.8 names the wrong person on d20 ≤ 3; lives off, a d6 bend (1 softer, 6 harder) and the same tellers slip at random.
4. **Heard** (`heard`, d6): the subject learns you asked on 4+ if you asked their partner, family or a friend, otherwise on 6, and says "no comment" after.
5. **Source trust:** 0 if named against their word; +1 if printed as said and shielded; at 3 they bring tips unasked.
6. **Sales** (`sales`, d20): 850 + the front draw + half each inside draw + the roll − 10. Draw by notability: 2,000+ 60, 1,500+ 40, 1,000+ 20; trouble +20; scooped −30; a correction −15. Over 900: patience +1.
7. **Rival** (`rival-<event>`, d6): an event of 1,500+ unprinted by its week runs in the county paper on 5+ (local) or 4+ (public), from one bent teller. Each scoop: patience −1.
8. **Complaint** (`complaint`, `libel`, d6): a printed claim the record contradicts about a named person brings a complaint on 3+ and a correction: trust −1 (0 with a named first-hand source). An alleged wrong adds a libel threat on 4+: patience −2.
9. **Borne out:** a story the town confirms the next week gives trust +1, once an edition.

**Six starts, six threads,** each opening with its tip: (1) Terry Sutton, council office; (2) Greta Brook, sawmill; (3) Harry Myers, school and harbour; (4) Gary Hughes, garage then sawmill; (5) Lily Lyons, council office; (6) Colin Berry, clinic, the editor's kin.

## Lives on and lives off

**Lives off changes:** no causes, found-out wrongs, races or bends; jobs change as in the record, without reasons; shows' outcomes are dealt again; tellers slip at random; rounds 4 to 6 lead with job, band and arrival news. **Identical:** cast, households, partners (so the kin rule holds), dates, beats, rules, rolls, the rival, and the screens' care and length.

**The off arm** plays as a shallow sim's paper of honest job news; every why gets "nothing on record says why". **Expected if lives matter:** lives-on players misprint early, learn who bends, find more by watching and speak of sources as people; lives off, accuracy stays flat and the paper reads like a notice board.

## Memory probe, tenth hour, exit

- **Probe (round 4):** the thread's subject, the first source you relied on, and someone the editor protected (if none, the first to say "no comment").
- **Tenth hour:** five editions to 18 July 1991 (Colin Berry leaves the clinic, Gary Newman arrives, the race goes on), against the rival, the sales and the editor; then the close, claim by claim.
- **Exit:** "What do you believe happened to Terry Sutton, Greta Brook and <the thread's subject>, and why?" Gary Hughes if the thread is one of the two.

## Honest weaknesses

- **Small for a weekly:** 76 people, about ten local events a month. The setup must check each beat has a weekly lead.
- **The economics are GM arithmetic:** sales, the rival and the editor are ours, not the engine's.
- **The off arm loses its best stories,** so a gap between the arms may only show it had less to report.
- **Publication can't change the town:** the engine takes no commands, so "the town answers" stays thin.
- **The panel's risks stand:** the optimiser may farm safe job news, the loop may repeat by round 6, and as text it may not beat the page.
