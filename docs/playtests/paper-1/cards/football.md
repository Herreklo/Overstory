# Harbour Seven (the club): card

The playable card for `football`, lives on and lives off. Game masters, analysts and the lead read it; players see only the pitch, through their screens.

## The question

Does a squad whose availability, form and refusals come from real lives make a manager's season easier to read and richer to play than the same squad with generic lives?

Judged by: each round's predictions of who will be missing and why, scored against the record; the March 1994 roll call (who hasn't come back, and why); the memory probe; whether the stakes probe names a person or a table position.

For the concept: lives-on predictions 15 points or more above lives off; one missing player in three named right at the roll call; town turns marked no lower than match turns; player 6's town-blind strategy (cover for all, the best seven) finishing below a reading one. Against: the arms within 5 points; the town-blind strategy winning promotion in lives on; both arms' exits naming the table as what they would lose.

## The pitch

**Store page.** *Harbour Seven.* A fishing town, 1989. Twenty-eight people between sixteen and fifty-five, and a seven-a-side league that starts in April. You manage the club the town founded last winter: pick the team, find the money, keep the committee patient. Your players work shifts at the sawmill and sail at dawn. They have babies due, grudges, and lives you don't see until they cost you a Saturday. Knock on doors. Buy a round at the Anchor. Read the noticeboard and the Courier, which is sometimes wrong. Win the league, or fold. Then come back five years on, to the first training of 1994, and see who still turns up.

**First minutes.** Your desk shows the squad, the fixtures and 6,000 kr. A grey name is someone you haven't watched train: their ability is a range. Hover a name for job and household. Put seven on the sheet and three on the bench before Saturday; if you can't field seven, you forfeit. Ask "why?" of anything in your journal: the game answers only from what you have seen or been told.

Player 4 (cold start) gets neither.

## Opponents and pressure

**The league** (authored): Nordvik 42, Strand 38, Eide 35, Holm 32, Berg 28 (team strength); ten matches, April to June. First goes up (opponents +5); last goes down.

**The committee** (authored): patience −1 after a round with a forfeit or under a quarter of the available points; at the season's end +1 for the top half, −1 for the bottom two. At 0 you are sacked.

**The town** pushes back with shifts, tides, births, rows, grief and pride; **the clock** with fixed fixtures and a draining account.

## Failure

Outright (the run ends; the exit still runs): sacked (patience 0); folded (account below −1,000 kr); expelled (two forfeits in a season). Three bad rounds or two forfeits end a run before the jump.

Partway: relegation; a forfeit (0–3, patience −1, 500 kr fine); the sponsor lost; a player lost to a grudge.

## Decisions that cost

1. **The team sheet.** Starting someone whose life just took a blow risks a no-show and −2 form; benching them costs strength.
2. **Shift cover,** 200 kr per player per match, waives the shift roll. Covering four shift workers all season costs 8,000 kr.
3. **A visit with an offer** (an evening): time, a lift (100 kr), cover, a promise, or a rest. Lives on: the offer that meets the cause behind the trouble waives the out roll and adds +1 form; a wrong one does nothing; money offered to a proud person cools them. Lives make this harder and better: knowing the person picks the offer.
4. **Drops and broken promises** risk a grudge (out on d6 1–2). Lives on: read from traits and kept grudges, 6 to 24 months (d4 × 6), told to one friend, who cools on d6 1. Lives off: a base-rate draw, 3 months, untold.
5. **Recruiting** (an evening): −1 form for two matches; in lives on, one side of a feud may cost you the other.
6. **A round at the Anchor,** 400 kr: +1 form next match for all who come; two conversations in one turn.
7. **Half time** in the featured match: a substitution; sit deep (both sides' chance targets −2); or push (+2).

## Mastery

Hour 1: learns strength, chances and the bench; picks by ability; a shift or a forfeit threat catches them out. Hour 5: predicts absences a month ahead; covers only where it bites; knows who is proud. Hour 10: manages lives, not ratings: expects family trouble to cost a regular two months, recruits ahead of births and departures, and at the roll call knows who won't return and why. In lives off, reading pays only through how notable events are.

## Juul's six

- Rules: written strength, chance, availability, money and patience rules.
- Variable outcome: dice and availability.
- Valued outcomes: points, promotion; the sack, folding.
- Effort: picks, visits, money and evenings move the odds.
- Attachment: the club and its people; the stakes probe tests which.
- Negotiable consequences: losing costs only the save.

## The three dials

Control: high over the club, none over anyone's life. Influence: through availability, form, grudges and favour, capped by the divergence rule. Knowledge: a manager's: public and local events via noticeboard, Courier, chatter and doorsteps; private ones only when told.

## Screens and verbs

**Screens:** the desk (squad list with age, job, position, ability or range, form, last two marks; fixtures; account; patience; journal); the Mudbowl in the railway's valley (training; the featured match in five moments, one of them the half-time choice); the sawmill gate at 4 pm; the fleet's berth at dawn; the school yard; the Anchor at 9 pm; a player's door.

**Records:** the co-op noticeboard (fixtures, births, sales); the Courier's results column (one item in eight wrong, both arms); the Anchor's chatter.

**Verbs:** inspect, talk, decide, travel, wait; and pick, train (those who come are watched, +1 form next match), visit with an offer, recruit, buy a round. Hover: age, job, household, position. The journal keeps what was seen, dated and sourced. The why-probe answers only from what the manager could know, with sources ("Vera Holmes, at her door, 12 May: Neil 'has a lot on'"), or "?"; in lives off, "nothing on record says why". A turn is one act (the month's sheet, a training, a visit, a recruit, a round); up to four a round.

## The clock

- Round 1, April 1989: matches 1–3. Round 2, May: 4–7. Round 3, June: 8–10, the table, the patience review.
- The jump, five years: up to three standing orders. The GM summarises 1990 to 1993 with the same rules (one strength roll per match) and lives as recorded; the account floors at 0; the summary cannot sack the manager.
- Round 4, March and April 1994: the return to the first training evening, same pitch, same hour: the roll call, then April's matches. Round 5, May. Round 6, June (Peter Lane leaves for the city).
- Tenth hour: 1995 and 1996, with Jonah Scott and Vera Holmes leaving in April 1996, and the storm in August.

## What the engine feeds and what the game master simulates

**Engine,** from each arm's file: kept facts (cast, ages, jobs, shifts, households, partners, births, deaths, arrivals, departures); events with visibility and notability (lives on adds causes and decisions), reaching records as `records-9989.md` and `world-9989-off.md` describe.

**GM-simulated,** seeded and labelled:
- Ability 2–9, seed `paper1/football/ability/<p-id>`, the same in both arms; −1 per five years over 35. Position drawn keeper:back:middle:forward at 1:3:3:3; out of position −2. Shown ±2 until watched twice. Form −3 to +3: +1 after a win they scored in, −1 after losing by three or more.
- Team strength: the starters' ability plus form. Six chances a side; each scores on d20 ≤ 6 + (own − other)/3, clamped 2 to 14. Three or more aged 41+ on the pitch: the last three chances at −1. Scorers weighted forward 3, middle 2, back 1. Injury: d20 = 1 per player, out 1d3 matches.
- Availability, in order, first "out" stops: (1) kept facts: not arrived or gone; the parent giving birth out from four months before to three after, the other parent that month; a household death, that month; (2) shifts: fleet d6 1–2, sawmill d6 1, waived by cover; (3) life: an event of 2,000+ this month or last, out on d6 1–3, −2 form; 1,000–1,999, out on d6 1, −1 form; (4) grudge, d6 1–2; (5) base, d20 = 1.
- Offers: lives on, work if they meet the top cause or one of weight 300+; lives off, d6 1–3.
- Money: 250 kr a match; home gate (30 + 10 × points from the last two) × 10 kr; the co-op's 3,000 kr a season if patience is 2+ at its start.
- The Courier errs on d8 = 1. Committee, opponents and league are authored.
- Divergence: an act that changes someone's situation re-decides only that person's engine events in the next 6 to 12 months whose top cause it touched, logged GM-simulated, divergent. No kept fact moves.
- Dice: `python3 -I -c "import random; r=random.Random('paper1/football/P<n>/r<k>/<what>'); print([r.randint(1,6) for _ in range(10)])"`; `randint(1,20)` for a d20. No arm in the seed, so both arms roll the same dice.

## Lives on and lives off

**The off arm changes** only the record underneath: cause-free events, so offers work on d6 1–3, grudges are short and untold, the why-probe has no reasons, and the Courier and chatter are generic. Neither arm has a club history.

**Identical:** card, screens, squads, abilities, dice, league, money, committee, shifts, kept facts, and the notability mix (618 of 660 person-years), so the life rule bites about as often.

**We expect** lives on to give better absence predictions, more visits that land, a roll call that names the missing, and a stakes probe that names a person; lives off to lean on cover and the bench, with the town-blind strategy doing as well as reading.

## Memory probe, tenth hour, exit

**Round 4 probe,** from the player's log: the regular with most starts in rounds 1 to 3; a recruit or refuser from rounds 1 or 2; someone outside the squad behind an absence.

**The tenth hour** sums up 1995 and 1996 under the player's strategy: finishes, money, patience; who left (Jonah Scott, Vera Holmes); who joined (Maisie Baker, Trevor Myers, Ellie Eden, Oscar Ash, Hal and Stella Lane at seventeen); the storm; whether the strategy holds.

**Exit questions** name the regular with most absences, a player lost to a grudge or the city, a newcomer, and one person outside the squad.

## Honest weaknesses

- **The squad is old.** 20 of 28 are over 35 in 1989; only 8 or 9 are under 36 in any year.
- **Spring 1989 is thin.** Most of the pool have 0 to 2 events from April to June 1989; only ten have one of 1,000+. The shift roll, identical in both arms, may do most of the work, so rounds 1 to 3 may barely separate the arms; the roll call and rounds 4 to 6 carry the test.
- **The core is ours.** Match, abilities, league and money are invented; a strong result may be our dice, not the town.
- **The offer rule leans on the GM's reading of causes;** an error favours one arm.
- **Player 1 may play it as a lesser Football Manager** and ignore the doors. That is a finding, not a fault.
