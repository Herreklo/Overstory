# What owners and the town did to old houses, 1989 to 2020

What happened to Stavanger's old wooden houses, and what was built among them, after `house-changes.md` ends, written as events the diary can hold (`docs/building-system.md`). Stavanger's sources are good on rules and on what the city conservator saw; Bergen, Oslo and national figures fill gaps and say so. (secondary) marks a claim from a summary or third party; (unverified) one no source confirmed, including our readings.

## In short

- **The years of putting back.** The city has worked to reverse post-war changes "since the 1980s", and owners clearly improved windows, doors, cladding and tiles in the 20 to 30 years before 2020. Yet in 2020 whole areas still had funkis windows, concrete tile, husmorvinduer and plastic cladding, and trim was a "blind spot" (Heng & Windsholt 2020). A street shows what was not yet undone beside restorations whose faults date them: thick sashes, glued bars, all-white frames, kit surrounds (Heng 2015).
- **The money drove it and was the threat.** Prices doubled from 2005 to 2013 and fell 14% by 2016 (SSB). Houses were split into flats, resold and refitted "at short intervals"; the 2010 plan calls the boom as great a threat as decay.
- **The old houses were held; the new came beside them.** Demolition was refused as a rule from 2003. New building came as infill on divided plots, small estates in villa grounds, storeys on old roofs and whole districts on harbour and works land, "partly with building types and density that seem foreign" (Kulturminneplan 2010). Øystein's observation holds, with that shape.
- **The rules asked for fit by size and form, not old style.** From 1974 new buildings were to fit their neighbours; from 2010 by "form, scale and materials", and "may well be given a modern design"; from 2019 the centre's fronts "preferably wood". No rule asked for the gable.
- **Change ran backwards.** Fronts restored to a documented state, copies of lost houses (1994 to 2008), the city drawing reconstructions free: a later year can look older.
- **New services date a wall:** a heat pump's box (mostly 2006 to 2012), wheeled bins at the gate (from 1997–99), a satellite dish (1990s).

## What owners did

- **Windows.** By 2015 owners were replacing husmorvinduer with copies of the originals (Heng 2015). The city can order plastic windows redone (Brochure 2021). How many PVC windows went into old houses in the 1990s: not found.
- **Cladding and insulation.** Plastic cladding was still common in 2020; Eternit needs asbestos removal, which kept it on. The rule is planed boards of the old profile; today's unplaned board greys and greens faster (Kulturminneplan 2010). Insulation from outside is advised against (Fortidsminneforeningen); Stavanger allows it if the cladding does not visibly move out. Enova's grant covered walls from 2016 (secondary). How common: not found.
- **Roofs and colour.** Concrete tile went on from the 1970s to the 1990s and is now banned except where original; red clay tile comes back with zinc gutters (Kulturminneplan 2010; Brochure 2021). Colour is free to change. A paint chain's decades: white in the 1980s; ochre, moss and oxide red around 1994; grey from the mid-2000s; black by 2021 (Fargerike 2021). A 2017 prize praised owners who had not followed fifteen years of grey and white (Verneprisen 2017). Øvre Holmegate got an artist's colour plan in 2005 (secondary).
- **Dividing and adding.** Sectioning gives the attic to the upper flat and the cellar to the lower; each owner wants a balcony (Kulturminneplan 2010). Since 2010 every visible change needs an application; dormers follow the house's form; balconies only on sveitser and funkis houses; no large decks.
- **Services and plot.** Air-to-air heat pumps were in 4% of households in 2004 (secondary) and 27% in 2012, 44% of detached houses (SSB 2014), with a record 92,427 sold in 2010 (TU 2012). Oil heating was banned from 2020; tanks were often left in the ground. Dishes were legal from 1986 (SNL). Wheeled bins stood at every gate from 1997–99 (Lovdata); the densest parts got underground bins from 2006 (TU 2006). Front gardens may not be paved, yet are; most fences gave way to "a hedge and a lawn" (Brochure 2021). Solar panels need an application and were rare before 2020 (SSB 2022).

## What the town did

- **Plans.** The first heritage plan came in 1989; in 1995 the wooden town (about 8,000 houses inside the pre-1965 border) was drawn on the city plan (*Stavangeren* 1/2016). Restoration guidelines in the 1990s, a garden guide in 1998, aesthetic guidelines in 2003, preservation zones in 2009, then the 2010–2025 plan: no demolition, wooden windows, original tile, no paved gardens.
- **Money and people.** Stavanger's price index (2015 = 100) went from 49.7 in 2005 to 107.8 in 2013, 92.7 in 2016 (SSB 07221). Gamle Stavanger was called "gentrified, museumified" (Sognnæs, in *Stavangeren* 1/2016). Pedersgata emptied in the 1990s and had about 40 food places by 2024 (secondary). North-east Storhaug got a living-conditions programme from 2014 (regjeringen.no).
- **Grants.** A "vernemillionen" existed before 2010 and was gone by then; Kulturminnefondet (from 2003) pays for repair, never for work begun. The city's preservation prize has gone out every year since 1999.
- **Fire.** After the Smedgata fire of January 2010, 69 gaps between houses were closed with boarding set 30 to 50 cm back, up to 3 m high; sprinklers and wired glass went onto some walls (fire plan, about 2014).

## New buildings among the old

- **Where.** Densification was half of new homes by 1998 (Stortinget 1998). Plots were split, as lettered addresses show: Eiganesveien 107C (1990), 147A and B (2000) (secondary). Holmegenes put 14 buildings and 45 flats into Eiganes villa grounds in 2015 (Haga & Grov; secondary). On Holmen a burnt block got six storeys and a ten-storey tower, approved in 2002, "out of all scale" to the city conservator (*Stavangeren* 1/2016). Kongsgata 52–60 kept its fronts and added three buildings and a brick link (LINK, prize 2019; secondary). Snøhetta's Herbarium followed in 2019 (secondary).
- **Reused, with storeys on top.** Eilert Smith's 1937 funkis grain building became a hotel with two storeys added, opened 2019–20 (secondary). The 2019 centre plan allows two set-back storeys above the cornice on Klubbgata. Of about 260 sea houses, about 60 remain, many reused as offices in the oil years; their 1993 rules allowed hotels, flats and glass links, never dormers or roof storeys. The brewery became Tou Scene in 2001. At least 52 canneries were torn down and backyard chimneys taken "for densification" (*Stavangeren* 1/2016).
- **Made older.** A front restored to a documented state needs no application from 2010, and the city draws a reconstruction free when evidence is missing. Copies: Smedgata 7 (1843) torn down in 1994 under protest and rebuilt; a burnt sea house rebuilt in 1998; Pedersbakken 5, a 1938 funkis house, demolished 2006 and rebuilt from its drawings in 2008 (Kulturminneplan 2010; our reading of its table).
- **What new buildings were asked.** 1974: fit form and appearance to neighbours, in practice two storeys and a loft. 1996: no higher on the peninsula. 2006: respect existing buildings and vegetation. 2010: "form, scale and materials", "first of all through size and form", modern design allowed. 2019: "reflect and fit in", wood preferred. 2023: plots of 500 m² or more, at most 30% built (KPA 2023). A new gable in glass and brick is the architect's echo, not a rule (our reading).
- **The new villa.** Flat roofs painted dark with white trim appear by 2001 (Gausel); schemes of 1989–96 were white with dark glazed tile; by 2006–07 three or four storeys with roof terraces and gardens "almost gone" (*Nyere årringer* 2008). How far the type spread: not counted.

## Evidence for the corner's authored rules

`fixtures/lt1-corner.json` names eight rules after 1988. **upkeep:** a repaint on sale is backed by fast resale; the cycle is unverified. **trade:** backed (Pedersgata; prices). **restoration:** backed, but the city's help was rules and advice more than money. **two_owners**, **old_style:** backed. **let_go:** no source. **extension:** balconies on funkis houses backed; over a lane, our reading. **fire:** in protected streets after the 1990s a lost house came back as a copy or by the row's rules (1993, 1998), not in the style of the day.
