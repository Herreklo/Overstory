# Animals

The animals of a Norwegian harbour town, its coast and Jæren's farms, 1870 to 2020 with 1989 in most detail, and how shipped games make animals cheap and alive. It extends `what-moves.md` and `animation-and-motion.md`; what grazing does to the ground is in `turf-heath-and-grass.md`. (secondary) marks a claim from a summary or third party; (unverified) one no source confirmed, our readings included; (our arithmetic) our own sums.

## In short

- **Every animal has a cause with a clock.** Gulls commute from fjord colonies to feed in town, most in winter, and many now live on worms turned up by ploughing. Since 2004, dogs must be on a lead from 1 April to 20 August, and cattle must spend at least eight weeks a summer on pasture. Each is a fact with a date.
- **1989's birds are not 2020's.** Herring gulls fell from 233,000 pairs (2006) to 72,000 (2015). Common gulls lost over 95% of their skerry colonies from 1984 to 2019 and took to flat roofs. The canneries closed by 1982.
- **Herds move in bursts.** Ginelli's ewes stood 74% of the time and spread at 0.18 m/s. They packed in fast waves begun by a few at the edge, the biggest about every 15 minutes. That rhythm reads at 120 m, where no leg does.
- **Games make animals point at facts.** Sea of Thieves' gulls circle a wreck until its loot is touched. theHunter's herds walk between need zones on species schedules. Cities: Skylines II's random pets showed its bugs as packs of abandoned dogs.
- **A gull at 60 m spans 50 to 58 pixels at the street stop's lens, not 10;** it is 10 at 300 m (our arithmetic). No measured flock cost exists for an Apple GPU.

## 1. The subject

### Birds

From SNL's species pages (2026), unless marked.

- **Stavanger's gulls** (Museum Stavanger, *URBPOP* 2015, counts of 2013–14). Nesting on roofs is "relatively new" and rising slowly. The crowds in the centre come in winter, common and herring gulls begging bread in the parks beside pigeons and mallards. Worms brought up by ploughing and slurry are now many gulls' main food. Lesser black-backed and herring gulls ringed in town bred on Hidlekjeøy, across the fjord, so they commute in. Tjuholmen, a town islet, held about 60 pairs of common gull.
- **The canneries** ran from 1873 to 1982, 14 of them by 1900 and few after 1970, so none ran in 1989 (lokalhistoriewiki; *Stavangeren* 3/2012; secondary). That their waste fed gulls is likely but unsourced (unverified).

### Dogs and cats

- **Tax.** An 1857 law let towns tax dogs. Bergen taxed them from 1876; the tax was abolished from 1 January 1990, so 1989 was its last year. Bergen had over 4,000 dogs in 1986 (Bergen byarkiv, 2023). Whether every town ended the tax is unconfirmed.
- **Leads.** Before 2004, the lead rule was in the game act (1981, § 52) and the rest in towns' police by-laws (circular G-2003-17). The dog act, in force from 2004, keeps dogs on a lead from 1 April to 20 August, and towns may add streets, parks and churchyards (Lovdata). The dates came from the game act (secondary); those in force in 1989 are unverified. Four fighting breeds were banned in 1991.
- **Numbers.** Norway had 300,000 to 400,000 dogs in 2003 (Storting) and about 565,000 in 2021 (secondary). It had about 750,000 owned cats in 2023 (SNL, secondary). GPS on 925 pet cats in six countries found a mean range of 3.6 ha, most within about 100 m of their door (Kays et al. 2020, secondary).

### Jæren's farm animals

- **Sheep.** The rygja breed was formed in Rogaland in the early 1900s, from spæl sheep crossed with Cheviot and Leicester. It is white and long-wooled (SNL 2024), and helped found the Norwegian white sheep (NordGen, secondary). Old Norwegian sheep (villsau) live out on the heath all year, with horned rams, short tails and a shed double fleece. Their flocks split by sex and keep close, and a ewe weighs about 35 kg (Norsk Villsaulag). Small mixed sheep on the heath, big white ones on the infield (our reading).
- **Cattle.** Norwegian Red (NRF) was founded in 1935 and was the only dairy breed from the 1960s, after about 25 local breeds merged into it (SNL). So 1989's cows are red; before about 1960 they were mixed (our reading). Since 2004 cattle must have at least 8 weeks a summer on pasture, and from 2024 tie-stall cows 16 (Lovdata). No rule was found for 1989.
- **Numbers, all secondary.** Rogaland has Norway's most sheep, about 200,000 (Statsforvalteren). It held 17% of the dairy cows in 2019. A Jæren farm with milk and sheep averaged 33 cows and 94 sheep in 2024 (NIBIO). The 1989 counts were not found.
- **The year.** Sheep are shorn at least 14 days before they go to the hills (Mattilsynet). Gathering starts on September's first weekend, and lambs go to slaughter through September and October (Geno, secondary). Lambing dates are unsourced.

### How they move and rest

- **Speeds** in flapping flight, measured by radar on migration, in m/s: eider 17.9, great cormorant 15.2, common gull 13.4, oystercatcher 13.0, herring gull 12.8 (Alerstam et al., *PLoS Biology* 2007). A flapping gull crosses the street stop's frame in about 5 s at 60 m (our arithmetic). Circling, gliding on updrafts and resting are what keep birds in view (our reading).
- **The herd.** Ginelli et al. (*PNAS* 2015) tracked 100 ewes in an 80 × 80 m field. On average 74% stood, 17% walked (0.18 m/s) and 9% ran (0.76 m/s). The flock's area swung from 75 to 2,329 m², and packed neighbours stood about 1 m apart.
- **The day.** Lambs grazed 64% of daylight and 14% of the night (*Sensors* 2021). On open range, sheep feed early, rest and ruminate at midday, feed again late and bed down at dusk (Utah State thesis, secondary).

## 2. The craft

Chosen because their animals point at facts, keep routines or show the cost.

- **Sea of Thieves.** Small flocks mark floating barrels and large ones shipwrecks. Each size has its own bird count and circling radius, and the gulls leave once the treasure is touched (wiki, secondary). Players lose them against the sky at distance (forum, secondary).
- **Red Dead Redemption 2.** About 200 species. "Scavengers quickly sniff out carrion", and animals sense the player by scent, sound and wind (Rockstar via Gematsu, 2018). The technique is not public.
- **theHunter: Call of the Wild.** Animals roam between need zones (eat, drink, rest) on researched schedules. A leader moves the herd, the others follow once they finish, and random timing stops them moving as one (Cederskoog, Game Developer). This is the nearest model to sheep on an owned pasture.
- **Cities: Skylines II.** Pets are "purely cosmetic", with owners "chosen randomly" (Paradox). Packs of abandoned dogs followed (PC Gamer, secondary).
- **Planet Zoo.** Additive part-animations triggered at random mean "you'll never see the same walk cycle twice". Per-animal flourishes are "a really expensive thing to do" (PCGamesN interview).
- **Stray and Untitled Goose Game** hand-keyed one animal, which does not scale to a town (PlayStation Blog, secondary; Panic).

**Flocks and herds.**
- Reynolds' boids follow three rules: separation, alignment and cohesion. A spatial grid makes their cost nearly linear in the flock's size.
- Unity's ECS Boids sample runs 2 × 25,000 fish, with no timings published.
- Ginelli's model has three states on a one-second step, and switching is boosted by neighbours; the bursts need strong imitation.
- Both are path-dependent, so a jump in time needs closed-form motion or a fixed-step replay from a checkpoint, each draw hashed from the animal and the step (our reading).

**Formats.**
- chenjd's MIT baker draws 10,000 idle cats in 7 draw calls under URP's SRP Batcher, with no frame rate given.
- The GPU Resident Drawer takes only mesh renderers, so a bird's own phase needs a DOTS-instanced property or our own indirect draw (Unity 6 manuals).
- Flipbooks played at one rate flap in step (secondary).
- Memory does not choose between bones and vertices: a 400-vertex gull costs about 192 KB per clip-second as VAT and 17 KB as 12 bones (our arithmetic). A gull has no outfits, which weakens the reuse case for bones. Bones still win twice: a VAT blend from glide to flap shortens the wing halfway, and one bird skeleton serves gull, crow and pigeon. VAT fits a far, looping flock; sheep on slopes need bones (our reading).

- At the far camera (120 m, VD4) gulls and sheep are still 25 to 30 px silhouettes; a bird becomes a dot only beyond about 300 m (our reading).
- 3,000 gulls of 400 vertices make 1.2 million vertices, doubled with shadows (our arithmetic). The eye-on view is already over 16.7 ms (`NOW.md`). The fact rule keeps counts small: one landing brings tens of gulls.
- Animals mostly rest: sheep stand three quarters of the time, and gulls sit on ridges. So a pose with an idle carries most frames (our reading).

## 4. Approaches worth prototyping

- **A. Gulls round a fact, in closed form.** Seeded soaring loops round the landing, flapping or gliding by vertical speed, as VAT in our own indirect draw. Test: a 10 s grey clip from the street stop, 20 gulls over the quay at 60 to 120 m. It holds if a cold reader ties the birds to the catch, none beat in step, and a time jump lands on the same frame.
- **B. A herd in bursts.** Ginelli's states on one-second steps, seeded per sheep and step, replayed from checkpoints, on bones. Test: a grey still of 40 sheep on the tip's pasture at 120 and 300 m, and a 60 s time-lapse. It holds if spread and pack read as grazing and a replay matches.
- **C. One gull three ways.** VAT, bone texture and skinned mesh. Test: a still at 12 m, a glide-to-flap clip, and GPU time at 500, 2,000 and 5,000 birds by `tools/check.py`. It decides on the blend fault at 12 to 35 m and on the cost per 1,000 birds.
