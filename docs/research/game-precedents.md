# Games where reading the world is the play

What shipped games, unmade projects, research prototypes, tabletop, early software and other media teach about games built on reading people, places and records. Part of the Game Lab research. The full survey, with every source, sits in the claude.ai Project as "Brilliant underused game ideas" (October 2026).

## The idea in one paragraph

Every mechanic such a game needs has been built, but each in a different game: traces that fade on readable clocks (Haven & Hearth), two biased accounts of one event (Caves of Qud), beliefs that know where they came from (Talk of the Town), walking one town across decades (A Mind Forever Voyaging), informants who are wrong without knowing it (Blood on the Clocktower). Nobody has joined them. And every recent deduction hit (The Roottrees Are Dead, Type Help, The Séance of Blake Manor) uses a hand-written archive. No game lets a simulation write the archive. That is the opening Understory gives us.

## Rules we take

**1. The simulation writes the evidence; the game checks the conclusion.** A world can feel alive without deep simulation: GTA IV dropped homes and jobs for every pedestrian and players never missed them. Depth pays only where the game checks what the player concluded against TRUTH. Obra Dinn confirms answers three at a time to stop guessing; Sherlock Holmes Consulting Detective (1981) scores the player against Holmes's own route. *Sources: GTABoom on Edge; Obra Dinn; Consulting Detective.*

**2. Every record has an author, a date and a stake.** A record is someone's account written down. Caves of Qud gives each generated event two versions, a plain one and a flattering tomb inscription. Records go wrong in six ways: omission, bias, composite (several events merged), decay, sealing (locked for years) and self-suppression (the subject kept it out). The gap between a record and the event is the case. *Sources: Qud wiki; The Moonstone; the NYT's Overlooked.*

**3. Beliefs carry their source, and gossip must have stakes.** Talk of the Town (2015) tracks for every belief where it came from, how strong it is and what it replaced; characters misremember, lie and update by reading gravestones. It never became a game because its people mostly gossiped about hair and eye colour. Only facts that matter to someone should travel. This is the engine's accounts arc (#20). *Source: Ryan et al. 2015.*

**4. Traces decay on clocks the player can learn.** In Haven & Hearth every crime leaves a scent that fades on a fixed timer. In Death Stranding weather erodes what other players built, so the world shows recent effort rather than clutter. Our moss and desire paths (`nature.md`) are the same rule. *Sources: Haven & Hearth wiki; Death Stranding.*

**5. Small casts read; big ones do not.** Social simulations failed when players could not follow them (Prom Week, Republic). The ones that worked had few people: Midwinter's 32 recruits, Murder on the Zinderneuf's 16 passengers. Simulate the few, show the many. *Sources: Midwinter; Zinderneuf.*

**6. Make off-screen plans visible step by step.** Dungeon World's Fronts show a threat advancing through named stages. A plan, debt or rumour gets a clock the player can watch (`interface.md`). *Source: Dungeon World Fronts.*

**7. Same place, fixed intervals.** A Mind Forever Voyaging (Infocom, 1985) has the player walk the town of Rockvil at ten-year jumps and record what changed. Richard McGuire's *Here* shows one room with windows into other years. The Up series films the same people every seven years. Change reads best side by side. *Sources: AMFV; Here.*

**8. The audience knows more than the participants.** Columbo shows the murder first; the suspense is how the truth gets proven. In The Traitors the viewers know who the traitors are. A simulation always knows the truth, so it can play the audience. *Sources: Columbo; The Traitors.*

**9. Split knowledge forces talk.** In The Past Within one player is in the past and one in the future, and they solve it by describing what each sees. Co-op games built on limited information (Hanabi, The Crew) keep winning the Spiel des Jahres. FELT and TRUTH can be split across players. *Sources: The Past Within; Hanabi; The Crew.*

**10. Narrow channels between players.** The multiplayer fixes that held remove the harmful tool, not the person: Dark Souls' fixed phrases, Journey's lack of chat and names. Moirai let players write free text for each other; most answers turned profane and one script flooded it. *Sources: Journey; Demon's Souls; Moirai.*

**11. Generated words phrase facts; they never decide them.** Where an LLM was the source of truth, it failed: Vaudeville's witnesses contradicted each other, and in Where Winds Meet typing a stage direction made an NPC mark a quest complete. Infinite Craft generates each result once and stores it, so every player gets the same answer. If we generate text, the engine holds the fact and the text is stored as canon. *Sources: KeenGamer; Outlook Respawn; Infinite Craft.*

**12. A hybrid works when one loop feeds the other and fixes its weakness.** NecroDancer, Slay the Spire and Papers, Please each join two genres so the output of one is the input of the other. A deduction game's weakness is that it ends; a simulation's is that it has no goal. Each can fix the other.

## Warnings

- Players say they prefer hand-made mysteries to procedural ones. A generated case must prove it is fair and solvable, or it reads as noise.
- Autonomy breaks authored content. Oblivion's Radiant AI was cut back after NPCs killed a merchant a quest needed. Build the game around the autonomy, not on top of it.
- Majestic contacted players in real life on a subscription and failed. Keep the fiction on screen.
- The Nemesis system is patented (US 10,926,179, adjusted expiry 11 August 2036, with related patents still being granted). People who remember the player are fine; get legal advice before copying its specific structure.

## Closest precedents

| Game | What it does | What it lacks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A Mind Forever Voyaging (1985) | One town at ten-year jumps, the player records change | A real simulation; the town is authored |
| Bad News (2016) | Find a dead resident's next of kin in a generated town | Needed a human to sift and an actor to play every resident |
| Talk of the Town (2015) | Beliefs with source, strength and decay | Stakes; it never became a game |
| Caves of Qud (2024) | Generated history told through biased accounts | Using the gap as play; it is flavour |
| Haven & Hearth | Forensic traces on decay clocks | A history behind them |
| The Séance of Blake Manor (2025) | Scheduled NPCs, every action costs time | A generated case |
| Blood on the Clocktower | Wrong informants; the dead still testify | A world beyond the round table |
| The Past Within (2022) | Two players, two times, one place | Anything generated |
