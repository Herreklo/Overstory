# Weather and seasons

Stavanger and Jæren's weather and seasons as the town lived them, 1870 to 2020, and how the best studios make weather spectacular and cheap. Research for plan step 9, 29 October 2026. It builds on `lighting-and-colour.md`, `sea-at-the-shore.md`, `camera-and-composition.md`, `trees.md`, `ground-cover-and-plants.md` and `animation-and-motion.md` and does not repeat them. Marks: (secondary) a summary or article; (ours) our arithmetic, mostly on MET's daily records and Sola airport's weather reports of 2012–20.

## In short

1. **A mild, wet, sheltered coast.** Something falls on six days in ten, least in April to June, most in September to January. Frontal rain comes on S–SE winds, offshore at the tip, so its water lies flat; drizzle under low cloud from October to January; showers behind fronts on W–NW winds, onshore, with hail and winter thunder.
2. **Snow and fog are events, not seasons.** Snow lies a median of two days, then slush; the winters either side of 1989 had four and five snow days. Sea fog belongs to spring dawns and is gone by midday.
3. **The most spectacular true moment at the tip is the veer behind a winter front:** hours of SE rain, then hail showers marching in over the Roads from the NW, a low hard sun between them and a rainbow standing in the arriving curtain, a few afternoons a month from December to February.
4. **Smoke is a clock of the era.** Until the 1950s every chimney smoked from autumn to spring and the canneries smoked and steamed along Vågen; in 1989 smoke means an evening wood stove.
5. **Spectacle on the M4 is maps and terms, not volumes:** 2.5–5 ms for the worst weather, estimated, none measured on Apple.
6. **Every weather state can be a pure function of the clock,** and every mark a look-back over a bounded window of it, so a time jump gives the same sky and ground.

- **Kinds and winds** (ours, days a year). Frontal rain 197, on S and SE at 6.3 m/s. Drizzle 172, mostly without rain, at 4.8 m/s: wet stone without a downpour. Showers 127. Hail 8, on W–NW at 8.4 m/s, the windiest kind. Thunder 16.
- **Wind.** Gale on 14% of days at sheltered Sola, 45% at exposed Obrestad; the tip lies between. Winter blows SE and S, offshore here; the summer afternoon NW.
- **Fog** on 14–17 days a year, on a light onshore drift, thickest at 04–07 and almost never seen from 10 to 17 in April to July (ours). A foggy noon is false.
- **Storms.** The record water is 184 cm above chart datum, on 8 December 1994, 73 cm over the highest tide; 160 cm comes about once a decade, in midwinter. Spray over the tip is an every-winter sight, water over its shelf a rare one. In the late 1860s storms drove Jæren's sand "like a snowstorm"; marram was planted from the 1880s, so storms before 1900 leave half-buried fences. Salt burns windward leaves after a dry storm (secondary); no Rogaland record.
- **Snow, frost, ice.** Snow lay on 33 days a year at Sola in 1961–90 and about 18 in 1991–2007; 15% of spells last a week. Sleet comes on SE winds near 0 °C, slush the day after. Hard winters: 1901/02, 1915/16, 1935/36, 1962/63, 1968/69 (47 cm on 8 February), 1969/70, 1978/79, 1981/82, 1986/87 (−19.8 °C), 2009–11. Pavements were sanded; salt came to main roads after about 1960 (secondary, national). Clear, still winter nights bring frost and trap wood smoke together (NILU 2020).
- **Seasons on the ground.** The 5 °C season runs mid-March to mid-December, 258 days against 236, and grass grows April to December, so turf stays green all winter (MET 2/2024). Spring came 7 days earlier over 1971–2005 (Nordli 2008). Gale days double from September to October, so exposed trees are stripped before full colour while sheltered gardens hold it into November (ours).
- **Smoke, steam, fire.** Solid fuel heated 68% of Norway's homes in 1960, 21% in 1973 and, with wood back after the oil crisis, 24% in 1980; oil peaked at 39% in 1973; electricity had 66% by 1993–95 (SSB). Heat pumps rose from 4% (2004) to 27% (2012); oil was banned in 2020. The first cannery opened in 1879; by 1920, 60–70 made a "forest of chimneys" along Vågen, smoking brisling over oak and steaming tins; three remained in 1982. The great fires were 1833, on the Holm, and 1860 (210 buildings, secondary), none as large after. Heath was burned in late winter; bonfires burn on 23 June under a sky that never darkens.
- **1989 against 2020.** Sola warmed from 7.4 to 8.4 °C, frost days fell from 81 to 59, January and February grew 30% wetter. Both years see little snow; the change is in the hard winters around them. In 2020 smoke comes from cleaner stoves and cruise ships without shore power; heat pumps hang on the walls.

## How the best build it

- **Horizon's Nubis** cut its PS4 clouds from 22 to 1.2 ms. Guerrilla dropped simulated weather as unreproducible, never animates coverage and paints holes where art wants them.
- **Red Dead Redemption 2** (Bauer 2019): a 512² cloud map over 32 km, a fog map over 8 km; rain curtains are rain fog hung under the cloud map; the rainbow is a light term, never placed. On PS4 the maps cost 0.04 ms, the cloud shadow 0.16 ms.
- **Ghost of Tsushima and Yōtei:** clouds rendered into a texture over 60 frames; a cloud shadow map gives god rays; one wind vector moves 10,000 leaves and every plume.
- **Sea of Thieves:** cloud offsets synchronised across players, so the sky is a function of time.
- **Far Cry 6** (GDC 2022): porosity from smoothness; one animated normal for every puddle; a rain shadow map; "cost must not increase when weather turns on".
- **Uncharted 4:** wet colour lerps toward its own square, so porous things darken most. **ToyShop** (2006): the rain veil is one texture fetch, biased white.
- **Breath of the Wild** and **Death Stranding** make weather play (secondary): rain makes climbing slip, metal draws lightning; timefall ages what it touches, and structures vanish unless repaired.
- **The rest.** Snow cover is sky exposure times flatness (Ohlsson and Seipel 2004). Arkham Origins' tracks refill as snow falls, under 1 ms on PS3. Assassin's Creed Shadows lights each season apart and sends people under roofs (secondary). Dredge makes fog the threat; INSIDE caps fog so lamps shine through. Firewatch's smoke, Alan Wake 2's fog and MSFS's clouds have no published method or cost.

## Answers

- **Wet** within 5 m reads by ripples, drips and streaks; at 12 m by porous darkening and puddles mirroring sky; at 35 m by glossy roofs. From 120 m water reflects only 2–6% (ours), so wet is darker, richer ground. Our phyllite darkens only 20–30%: its gloss carries it.
- **Rain, fog, spray, wind.** Near, streaks fall one way in two or three depth layers; far, rain is fog under a cloud. A fog bank is a map of base, falloff and density. Spray fires where waves meet the rock's lips. Wind shows through witnesses: gust bands on grass and sea, leaning smoke.
- **Snow, frost, seasons.** Snow lies by sky exposure and flatness, drifting into the lee. Hoarfrost forms only under open sky, so an eave leaves a frost shadow the shape of our baked sky view. Seasons are each species' rank on its phenology, ground hue by date, light per season, people who answer the weather.
- **Drama.** Cloud shadows cross the fjord at the wind's speed, free from the shadow map; a curtain is that map read lower down. A rainbow stands wherever the sun is below 42° with rain opposite: always, in winter here. From the tip the horizon is 10.5 km off, so a fog bank must lie within 1–3 km to read large (ours).

## Weather as a fact of the clock

- **Three layers of time.** The climate is the pack's monthly statistics by era; the month and its storms are the engine's (request 18); the hour is ours: fronts and shower cells as seeded objects on tracks along the wind, any instant evaluating only the cells that overlap it. A squall crossing the Roads is then a fact at every minute, and a forecast the same function read ahead.
- **Marks by look-back.** Wet, puddles, snow, slush, frost and salt on glass are each a function of the weather over a fixed window before now: 48 hours for wet, two weeks for snow, an autumn for leaves. A time jump reruns the window, never a simulation from the start.
- **Slow marks** read the climate's rates through growth and upkeep, as they already do; a storm that matters hands upkeep its work.
- **Smoke is people's:** a chimney smokes when its home's heating, the hour's cold and someone home say so.

## Three first tests

1. **After the shower** (a still, cheapest: the water layer exists). The street stop, 15 January at 14:30, the sun 7° up, clearing two hours after rain, in grey. It passes if wet reads at 12 and 35 m by gloss on paving and roofs, crowns drier than joints, darkening by porosity, and no puddle where no worn dish is a fact. At one-eighth size beside `docs/references/stone/02-gamle-stavanger-wet-slabs-and-steps.jpg` and Stålenhag's wet road (`art/13c`).
2. **Spring haar at dawn** (a still). From the street stop on the tip's back, north over the Roads, 15 April at 06:30: visibility 300 m, the bank's top at 12 m, the breakwater light on. It passes if the far layers step in four values, the Bluff's crown stands above a clear top edge, the light throws a halo, and no noise shows. Beside `stone/50-tinganes-overcast-2005.jpg` and INSIDE's road in fog (`games/17a`).
3. **The veer behind a front** (a short clip, the spectacle). The tip on 15 January, 12:00 to 12:40, the wind veered to NW. A hail curtain crosses the Roads from the NW while the sun, 9–10° up in the south, still lights the rock and the customs house. A gust darkens the sea ahead of it, spray lifts off the north lips, and at 12:30 a rainbow's top stands 32° up over the Roads with its western foot in the curtain (ours); in grey, the brighter sky inside the bow. Then the hail arrives. It passes if at one-eighth size it reads as lit land against a near-black sky, the curtain is one shape with a soft foot, the sun patch crosses the sea at the wind's speed, a minute jumped to and back gives the same frame, and it keeps its budget. Beside `stone/51-hertervig-borgoya-1867.jpg` and `trees/14-dahl-birch-in-a-storm-1849.jpg`.

## Whose facts

By FC1, the engine decides that something happened and why, our rules how it looks, the seed which exactly. Weather that matters (18) is open; rites (31), keepers (13), works (14) and feet (16) carry the bonfire, the sanding, storm repairs and trodden snow.

**Candidates for Understory:**
- *18 as a function of the date:* the month and its storms from the pack's climate and the seed, so any date gives the same month without replay; people and town must live one weather, and only the engine can make its acts agree with it.
- *Weather as a cause of acts:* a boat kept in, a roof job put off, a man lost in a gale; these change lives, and made by us from the weather they would be invented.
- *Fires as events,* with place, cause, damage and who was rehoused: a burned gap claims history.
- *How each home is heated and when that changed* (with 15): a household's spend, seen as smoke, an oil tank or a heat pump; derived from the pack's era shares until then.
- *Working seasons* (with 3 and 5): the brisling season and cannery shifts, which decide when their chimneys smoke.

**Ours:** the climate by era; the hour inside the engine's month; the light; marks by look-back; slow marks through growth and upkeep; seasons by phenology; smoke from heating, cold and presence.

**Scene state and texture:** tonight's puddles, footprints in fresh snow, steamed windows; which cloud, drop, ripple or curl of smoke.
