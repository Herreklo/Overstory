# What moves

Cars, boats, wind, smoke, animals and the town's rhythm: how movement makes the world alive while every moving thing stays a true fact. Part of the Game Lab research. Items from general knowledge are marked unverified.

## The rule

Everything that moves on its own carries a fact. If nothing has happened, nothing moves. A sparse town makes this a strength: one car at 06:10 means something.

## Vehicles are records of their owners

**Norway's fleet around 1989.** The best sellers of the 1980s were the VW Golf, Ford Sierra, Volvo 240, Mazda 626 and 323, Opel Ascona and Kadett, Ford Escort and Toyota Carina and Corolla; Japanese brands held 11 of the top 25. The fleet grew by 235,000 cars in 1981 to 1985 but only about 21,000 in 1986 to 1990, and actually shrank in 1988 and 1989, the first peacetime fall on record. Average age rose from 7.2 years in 1980 to 10.4 in 1995. So the 1989 street is full of ageing boom-year cars, and a car bought after 1986 is a rare, readable fact: oil money that survived the crash.

**Rules:**
- A vehicle record holds its owner, purchase year, new or used, home district and whether it is garaged.
- The model comes from that year's pool, weighted by the owner's wealth.
- Wear follows age, salt winters and garaging: rust low on the body first, then faded paint, then dents tied to the owner's habits (rust pattern unverified).
- A mismatched panel shows the car changed hands.
- Norwegian plates since 1971 are two letters and five digits; the letters show where the car was first registered, and the plate stays with the car.

Mafia: Definitive Edition shows the precedent: newer models appear on the road as its story moves forward.

## Boats

- Sjark: a 6 to 10.7 m fishing boat for one or two crew, wheelhouse forward since the war, often with a small mizzen sail to hold its head to the wind. Wood is giving way to plastic and aluminium.
- Snekke: a 4 to 8 m clinker-built boat with a pointed stern and a small inboard, mainly for leisure since the 1950s.
- Express boats: the Westamaran catamaran from 1973; hydrofoils ran Bergen to Stavanger from the early 1960s.
- Supply ships: superstructure at the bow, a large open aft deck, bulk tanks below. Whether many lay idle after 1986 is unverified.

Boats belong to people and businesses, and they move on their owners' trips and timetables.

## Traffic

Shadows of Doubt plans each citizen's day (4 to 10 journeys) before it starts; only a handful leave their routine in real time. **Rules:** no filler traffic; every moving vehicle is a trip the engine scheduled; Unity draws a car's position from its trip and the clock, so jumping to any time gives the same street. Parked cars are the strongest signal: a car outside the house means someone is home, a car outside the pub at 23:00 is a fact, an empty drive on a weekday morning is a commute. Buses, ferries and the express boat run on timetables.

## One wind for everything

Ghost of Tsushima made "everything needed to move" from one wind: an authored direction, strength varied across the map by moving noise, two layers of scrolling gusts for grass, the same gusts for trees, cloth and ropes, and a few hundred invisible "vorticles" for local swirls, chosen for "volume over accuracy". God of War's goals: you can see which way it blows, and it is "believable rather than realistic". Sea of Thieves drives its clouds by wind, with artist-placed pressure zones that open or close the cover.

**Rules:**
- The engine's weather gives a wind direction and strength per hour, from seed and season.
- Unity turns it into one global value plus a scrolling gust texture, computed from the clock, never accumulated frame by frame, so any time jump gives the same state.
- Grass, trees, laundry, flags, awnings, smoke, rain and snow angle, wave chop and clouds all sample the same wind.
- Local swirls only for local events: a passing truck, a ferry's wake.
- Trees lean toward the long-term prevailing wind, a fact about where the town sits.

**Real wind:** on the Rogaland coast, winter wind most often comes from the southeast and summer wind from the northwest, with a daily sea breeze. Gale-force wind blows about 20% of the time, storm-force about 1%.

## Signs of life

- Chimney smoke. From the mid-1980s electricity provided over 70% of household energy, so in 1989 a smoking chimney is uncommon: it means a wood stove, someone home, a cold day. Smoke bends with the shared wind.
- Steam: car exhaust below freezing; kitchen steam at mealtimes.
- Lit windows: at dusk only the rooms the engine says are occupied.
- Gulls follow facts: a fishing boat coming in, fish landed, a bin knocked over. Flocks use Reynolds' three rules (avoid collisions, match speed, stay together), run at a fixed step from a seeded start per event so they replay exactly.
- Dogs are walked on their owners' routines; cats belong to houses.

## Rhythm

- Opening hours: in 1985 most grocers closed at 17:00 on weekdays and 13:00 on Saturdays; kiosks and petrol stations were exempt from the opening-hours law. Saturday afternoon the town empties; Sunday only kiosk and petrol-station traffic moves (Sunday closing unverified).
- July: the shared holiday (fellesferie) is usually the last three full weeks of July: fewer commutes, busy yards and boats, empty workplace car parks.
- Street lights switch on at the real sunset for this latitude.
- Christmas lights appear house by house according to the household (unverified custom).

## Motion style

Spider-Verse animated mostly on twos with no motion blur, giving a "crunchy and crispy" feel; Arcane ran hand-drawn effects at 12 fps over 3D at 24. **Rules:** environment effects (smoke, flags, laundry, splashes) step on twos or threes from a shared clock; people and the camera stay smooth. Mood sets how much moves: moody brings gusts and rain, cozy slow sparse smoke, alive more trips and birds, glam glints and lit windows. What carries no fact stays still.
