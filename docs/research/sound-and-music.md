# Sound and music

Research for "Sound and music" in `docs/systems.md`: Stavanger's harbour town by ear from 1870 to 2020, how studios make sound carry meaning cheaply, and how we would build it free in Unity 6.6 on the M4. Written 29 October 2026 on Opus; nothing here is decided. It builds on `scenes-and-sound.md` (Schafer's keynotes and soundmarks, layered crowds).

Marks: (primary) is a law, rule, archive, measurement, developer or vendor; (secondary) an encyclopedia, wiki or the press; (unverified) a search summary, an inference or nothing.

## In short

- **The town's sound is a timetable.** The cannery bell rang five minutes before 07:00 and 14:00 (1913). The carillon plays at 11:00, Sunday has its rings and 3×3 strokes, the sirens are tested at noon, and in 1989 the news came through windows at 19:30. A listener reads the clock from a few assets.
- **History is heard as a sound that stops.** The cannery bell after 1982, the England boat after 2008, the studs after the 2017 fee. Each stop is a dated fact with an owner.
- **The keynote turns lo-fi, measurably.** Idling supply boats at Strandkaien average 101 dB(A) of sound power; road noise highly annoyed 10,500 people in the area in 2022.
- **Studios start from silence.** Music comes when a state calls for it, one sound with one parameter reads out a fact, and overheard talk is a clue, never a gate.
- **Stack and making.** Unity's audio, Steam Audio (Apache-2.0) and our own scheduler hashing place and clock; FMOD and Wwise are not needed. CC0 instruments, our own patches, scores in code, murmur from CC0 Norwegian speech; Stavanger itself we record or synthesise.
- **First test.** A cue sheet on paper, read blind by model readers before any sound exists.

The keynote by era. Until the 1910s, hooves and iron tyres on setts, which most streets had by the late 1800s (the town's guide to setts, primary). From the 1920s, hot-bulb engines beating in the Basin until diesels came from about 1950, and trolleybuses from 1947 to 1963 (secondary). Asphalt came after 1945, then studs, the Rosenberg yard across the water (heard in town, unverified) and the canneries closing; setts came back on some streets from 1980 (primary). Vessels under 10 m fell from 10,872 in 1996 to 3,461 in 2011 (secondary).

So 1989 had studs all winter, one shared news hour, the England boat and a manned fog signal at Feistein (until 1990; secondary). 2020 has the electric hum, cruise ships and roof gulls. Both have silent canneries, but in 1989 the silence was seven years old. No soundscape study of Stavanger was found; NRK Rogaland's and Norsk Lydinstitutt's recordings are unread.

## 2. How studios carry meaning with sound

- **Silence first.** Breath of the Wild has no field music by design (Wakai: "a logic behind having no music in the open world"; primary). Outer Wilds plays none for most of the game, so its cues work as clues (Prahlow; primary). Firewatch's music manager plays only in "interaction valleys", and no piece plays twice (Remo; primary). Stardew and Dredge are said to drop their music for rain and for night (unverified).
- **One sound, one parameter, read from a fact.** Hitman's Fake Crowd sizes one crowd sound to the head count (Jacobsen; primary). Witcher 3 feeds four murmur emitters, one per quadrant round the listener, with the count, distance and fear of the people there (primary). In The Last of Us Part II a scan cue's pitch gives a target's height (primary). Football Manager 27's crowd follows build-up and late equalisers (primary), from a few dozen short files (modders; unverified).
- **Overheard talk is a clue, never a gate.** Hitman's leads are found by eavesdropping and stay found. IOI: "information should never be a prerequisite for progression" (primary). Obra Dinn plays seconds of sound before each frozen death, and Pope wrote that its accents "provide gameplay-critical clues" (primary).
- **Place themes and clocks.** Disco Elysium's hostel theme has three versions, at 8am, noon and 8pm (secondary). Animal Crossing has a track for each hour (unverified). Red Dead Redemption 2's open-world music has no tempo or key, "almost like Foley", so it layers over anything (Jackson; primary).
- **Music in the world.** Each of Outer Wilds' Travellers plays one part of a song, "like parts of a band" (Prahlow; primary), found with the signalscope. Firewatch's radio is the relationship, and a choice can time out (primary).
- **Cheap and careful.** Obra Dinn was one person, a library and 29 speaking roles; Hitman 2016 had five sound designers and a programmer (primary). In Firewatch two facts meant one thing ("Delilah knows about Julia", "Henry told Delilah") and broke the story hours in (primary): one fact, one source.

No studio documents a town whose sound changes across eras (Pentiment, the nearest, is silent on it), so sound across a time jump is new ground.

## 3. How to build it here

- **Beds and emitters.** Witcher 3's pattern fits (primary). One global bed per weather (wind on a 0 to 1 value, rain in three layers), local beds per place that duck it, and an interior value that mutes the outside. Anything a listener could name (a gull, a bell, a cart, a door) is a one-shot at a place, never baked into a loop. Loops stay featureless, with coprime lengths (secondary).
- **Occlusion.** Unity 6.6 has no HRTF (the filter that places a sound round the head) and no occlusion (primary). Steam Audio 4.8.1 adds both on Apple Silicon, with transmission and baked reflections and paths round corners (primary). On arm64 use its Nearest HRTF (open bug #581) and disable silent sources, which still cost CPU (#459). We lack only Wwise's portals with diffraction; reopen if changing interiors need them.
- **Licences** (fetched 29 October; primary). FMOD Indie is free under $200k company revenue a year and a $600k budget, with registration, a startup logo and a credit; beyond that, $2,000 a title. Wwise is free under a $250k budget, then from $8,000. Neither takes royalties; Steam Audio is free for good.
- **Voices without words.** Murmur takes its density from the people the engine placed and its mood from their states, in the Witcher's quadrants. Its source is NB Samtale, about 24 hours of CC0 Norwegian conversation with Rogaland among its dialects (primary), granulated until no word survives: a word is content, and content is the engine's.
- **Deterministic from the clock.** Unity's Audio Random Container exposes no seed (primary), so we write a scheduler. For each few-second slot and layer, `hash(seed, place, slot, layer)` fires a sound when it falls under the density that hour, weather and facts give; more hashes pick variant, pitch and position, and `PlayScheduled` plays it. A bed's phase is town time plus a hash, set through `timeSamples`. A jump carries nothing over, and era beds crossfade by mixer snapshots.
- **Cost on the M4.** The project runs 32 real voices, 512 virtual and a 1024-sample buffer (`AudioManager.asset`). FMOD's Mac benchmark puts 64 Vorbis voices at 7.8% of an old Intel core (primary), so 32 on an M4 should cost under 1% (unverified). Steam Audio publishes no cost per source. Until `tools/check.py` measures it, the budget is HRTF on the 8 nearest sources, one occlusion ray each per 0.1 s, baked reflections, one shared reverb and under 1.5 ms of audio thread (unverified). A scene's sounds take about 30 MB of the 0.7 GB shared with engine, code and UI (`docs/building-blocks.md`).
- **Making it for free.** Freesound has 7 sounds tagged "stavanger" and no CC0 Norwegian speech (primary), so the harbour, rain on slate and the bells are ours to record or synthesise. Wind, rain and sea can be synthesised from the weather values the light already uses, with rain on each material from its own data (ours, untested). VCSL and VSCO-2-CE are CC0 instruments, without accordion, Hardanger fiddle or guitar. Surge XT and Vital are free synths, played with our own patches since GPL covers their presets (GNU FAQ; primary). Python (`mido`) writes scores from rules and facts; FluidSynth or sfizz renders them.
- **Licences to avoid.** Never NC, ND, BBC or Sampling+; CC-BY only with a kept credit list. Sonniss's GDC bundle is royalty-free, but its licence of 27 August 2026 bars training AI on it: ask Øystein before agents process it. Music from prompts alone has weak copyright in the US (Copyright Office, January 2025; secondary). We may use it, but owning it needs a person's choices of motif and arrangement.

## 4. Holding our rules

- **A sound that tells history is a fact.** Each timetabled signal is a pack row with years, hours and owner. The cannery bell sounds only while the engine's venture runs, and its absence afterwards is the fact (the town plan's "silence where machines were"). Beds, variants and gull timings are texture.
- **The clock drives it.** Sound is a pure function of place, date, hour, weather and seed, so two renders of one seed match sample for sample.
- **Nothing claims what the engine did not decide.** No intelligible words, no bark without a dog, no funeral strokes without a death and its rite. No sound bridge joins what the record does not join (`behaviour-on-screen.md`, rule 7).
- **Music is ours.** Most of the town's music is someone playing it (the carillon, a radio, the band in the loft), from a fact or a derived habit. Score outside the world is rare, comes on a fact and claims no history.

## 5. Three first tests

1. **The cue sheet (paper).** A script lists, in plain words with seconds and direction, what a listener on the Landing hears in two-minute windows: a weekday at 06:55 in 1913, a Sunday at 09:55 in 1989, and a November Tuesday at 19:30 in 1989 and in 2020. *Understand:* the day, hour and year, and why. *Judged blind:* cold model readers name them from the unlabelled sheets with reasons, scored against the truth; claims the sheet does not support count as misreadings.
2. **The same windows by ear.** The scheduler renders the sheets offline, outside Unity, with CC0 stand-ins and synthesised weather. *Understand:* the same. *Judged:* by machine first. Two renders of one seed must be bit-identical, and a jump into a window must match playing up to it. Every signal must clear the bed in its band, with no repeats inside a window. Then people judge blind, since model readers cannot hear: Øystein, or one task in the engine's human test (ER5), naming day, hour and year from shuffled clips.
3. **The gully steps at night, one person.** The grey walker climbs the Gully Steps at 23:10 on a wet November weekday in 1989, after the Anchor's door opens and closes below. The bar's murmur falls away along the baked path. Drips fall off slate and boards, studs pass on the quay road, the sea works at the Holm tip, and one television plays in a lit window. *Understand:* the person left the bar alone, late, on an ordinary wet weekday, in a street asleep but for one room; no sound claims more than the fixture holds. *Judged blind:* people watch the clip with and without sound and say what happened, scored against the fixture. The sound passes if it adds true understanding and misleads no one, at a cost on the M4 that `tools/check.py` measures.

## Whose facts

By FC1 (`docs/facts-contract.md`), the engine decides that a thing happened and why, our rules decide how it sounds, and the seed decides which exactly.

**Candidates for the engine**, checked against `docs/engine-requests.md`, none filed:
- *Overheard, as a way of knowing.* `knownBy` holds `seen` or `heard`, and `heard` means told. A third way, by ear (through a wall, from the stair), would cover loud acts. Who heard the row is a fact about who knows, and it changes beliefs and later talk. It needs 28 and 15.
- *A venture's hours and season*, extending B2 (#22). The cannery bell, its workers' feet and its lit windows must agree, and a late shift on a big catch is history.
- *Household animals.* A dog that barks at the gate, and then stops, is part of a household's history.
- *Broadcasts as records*, extending 9: the local radio's report of a town event, which can be wrong.

Already filed or open: 31 (rites with who came) for funeral and wedding bells, 18 for the gale, 11 and 17 for the boat engine starting at five, 8 for the band heard from the quay.

**Our pack and rules derive** the timetable and beds by place and era, and rain and wind on each material. Radios and televisions come from lit rooms and Statistics Norway's day, murmur from the engine's people and states, and footsteps from surface, wear, body and state. The rules also say when score plays.

**Scene state or texture.** Tonight's door openings and murmur are scene state unless they matter. Gull calls, drips, variants, pitch and wave detail are seeded texture that claims no history.
