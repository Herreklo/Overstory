# What grows where, and how games place it

Research for the nature layer (`docs/nature.md`), October 2026: how games and tools decide what grows where, and the real plants of Stavanger and Jæren around 1989. GDC slides and videos are mostly behind a login, so the game section rests on abstracts, interviews and third-party notes. Claims not checked against a primary source are marked (unverified).

## The bottom line

- The best placement systems are **fields, not noise**: each species has a viability curve over measured fields (slope, wetness, light, exposure, altitude), rivals keep each other away, and noise comes last, as a modulator (Far Cry 5, Houdini's biome tools, Horizon Zero Dawn).
- **Edges, size and clusters** come from rules: smaller and younger plants at an edge (a distance field of the community's depth), children seeded round parents, and one dominant species writing masks its companions follow.
- **Human order is vector data** (roads, fences, parcels, hedges as lines and areas) that overrides the natural fields; it is never another layer of noise.
- **Deterministic and locally stable** (Horizon): moving a river must not reshuffle the world. For us: plants keep stable ids, so the same tree grows old in place.
- **Neglect, mowing, succession, a new hedge read as history**, so they are facts with dates; seeded noise only varies things inside a community (a blade's lean, a clump's shape, a flower count, a leaf's day of fall).
- On this coast in 1989: heath going over to juniper and birch where grazing stopped, rock banded by lichen above the sea, drained bogs on Jæren, a new state forest of spruce and Sitka, an old town with few trees, sycamores everywhere they were once planted, and the first wrapped round bales.

## How games and tools do it

- **Horizon Zero Dawn** (Guerrilla, GDC 2017): nearly all vegetation placed procedurally on the GPU at run time, data-driven, deterministic and locally stable; shaders, placement and sound read the same world data maps (humidity, temperature, distance to a river); a placement rule is written like a shader. 500+ asset types, about 250 µs of GPU time (from a search snippet of the slides, unverified). Each layer's footprint lowers the density left for the next (trees, then bushes, then grass), and painted ecotope maps pick which layers run where (unverified, from memory of the talk). [slides](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf), [summary](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/gpu-based-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn), [interview](https://80.lv/articles/horizon-zero-dawn-interview-with-the-team)
- **Far Cry 5** (Ubisoft, GDC 2018; Houdini, rebuilt nightly, identical on any machine), the most detailed source found ([notes](https://christianjmills.com/posts/procedural-tools-far-cry-5-notes/), [PlayStation blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2018/03/22/the-procedural-world-generation-of-far-cry-5/)):
  - inputs: occlusion, flow, slope, curvature, illumination, altitude, latitude and longitude, a wind-vector map; masks for water, roads, fences, power lines and cliffs;
  - biomes spread sub-biomes, which artists can paint over;
  - each species scores each spot by viability; priority first, viability breaks ties, a viability radius keeps rivals off;
  - size classes tied to viability bands, so the small plants land on the edges; an "age" distance field tapers forest edges and imitates succession;
  - tint from viability, age or terrain (grass greener near water); rotation from slope or wind;
  - plants change the ground: a mound round trunks, root textures, masks that other species follow (forest rocks follow the pines);
  - outputs: points, terrain texture and colour, and a forest mask that feeds the fog and the world map;
  - forest thicker on north slopes, where shade keeps the soil moist.
- **Ghost of Tsushima**: placement and texture rules in a functional language run on the GPU ([Pohlmann](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027352/Samurai-Landscapes-Building-and-Rendering)); grass blade by blade on the GPU ([Wohllaib](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027214/Advanced-Graphics-Summit-Procedural-Grass)); large fields of a single flower and forests of a single tree, because simplifying the mass makes it read ([VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/creating-the-way-of-the-warrior-for-ghost-of-tsushima/)). Wind: one direction with its strength varied by moving noise, so gusts roll over the fields; curl noise on particles; invisible "vorticles" make eddies and gusts for other particles; grass and foliage answer footsteps; up to 60,000 leaf particles in a scene ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/using-vorticles-to-simulate-wind-in-i-ghost-of-tsushima-i-)).
- **The Witcher 3**: vegetation generated procedurally offline and at run time ([GDC 2014](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020197/Landscape-Creation-and-Rendering-in)); placement by water accumulation and light, about 20 large tree and bush species and 40 grasses (unverified).
- **Houdini Labs biome tools**: a viability layer per species from temperature, rain and soil; neighbours compete, won by age, viability or chance within overlap tolerances; a plant's age picks its mesh; separate seeds for variants and positions ([docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/labs--biome_plant_scatter-1.0.html)).
- **Unreal PCG biomes**: placed by volume, spline or texture and layered by priority; children spawned round parents ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/procedural-content-generation-pcg-biome-core-and-sample-plugins-overview-guide-in-unreal-engine)).
- **Unity terrain details**: with GPU instancing the healthy/dry colour switches off, so colour variation moves into the shader ([manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/terrain-Grass.html)).
- **Research**: Deussen et al. 1998, plants as circles, the weaker shrinks or dies where they overlap, similar plants share instances ([paper](https://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/ecosys.sig98.html)); Lane and Prusinkiewicz 2002, plant by plant up or from a density map down, with clustering and succession ([GI 2002](https://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/eco.gi2002.html)); Beneš 2002, seeding near parents makes same-species clusters ([paper](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Benes02ICCVG.pdf)); EcoBrush 2017, pre-run simulations keyed by climate and slope, painted by age and density ([Gain et al.](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01519852)).
- **Seasons and life elsewhere**: Assassin's Creed Shadows extends its baked light per season, grows grass and bushes in summer and freezes ponds in winter ([Ubisoft](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-gb/article/6u0kflsdY20YlnZks7rUeG/inside-anvil-the-technology-powering-assassins-creed-shadows)); Red Dead Redemption 2's food chain and carrion are press material, with no vegetation talk found.
- **Distance**: Far Cry 5 draws terrain colour and the forest mask from the same biome data; Firewatch layers bold colours for depth and puts texture detail on what matters to the story ([Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-2015-the-art-of-firewatch)).

## Stavanger and Jæren around 1989

**The frame.** Låg-Jæren is Norway's largest lowland: moraine of clay, sand, gravel and stones over phyllite, treeless since the Iron Age, stony heath, bog and wet acid land until the clearing from the 1860s; now grass and root crops, dairy, pigs and poultry, sheep on Høg-Jæren ([SNL](https://snl.no/Jæren)). Oceanic climate: October about 9.5 °C, 148 mm of rain, 80 hours of sun; November 6 °C (Sola, 1991–2020).

**Coastal heath** (kystlynghei): heather (røsslyng) with bilberry, cross-leaved heath (klokkelyng), crowberry (krekling), deergrass (bjønnskjegg), purple moor-grass (blåtopp), bog asphodel (rome), tormentil ([SNL](https://snl.no/kystlynghei_-_naturtype)). Kept open by burning every 15 to 20 years (now between 15 October and 15 April), year-round grazing by old Norse sheep and cutting for fodder; burning leaves a patchwork of stands of different ages ([lokalhistoriewiki](https://lokalhistoriewiki.no/wiki/Lynghei)). Faded after the war, between Stad and Boknafjorden by the early 1970s; about a tenth remains. Left alone it goes to juniper, then birch, then pine, spruce and Sitka; bracken spreads as grazing stops ([Gjedrem and Log](https://hvlopen.brage.unit.no/hvlopen-xmlui/handle/11250/2733665)). So in 1989 much former heath near the town is fresh succession: juniper and young birch in old heather. A later dated event: the 2014 drought killed heather along the coast.

**Rock.** Lichen bands above the sea: black *Verrucaria maura*, then orange *Xanthoria* and *Caloplaca*, then grey *Ramalina* and *Lecanora*; tens of metres high on exposed coasts, about a metre in sheltered places ([MarLIN](https://marlin.ac.uk/habitats/detail/96)). Woolly fringe-moss (*Racomitrium*) in grey mats. Map lichen and reindeer lichen on inland rock (unverified).

**Wet hollows and bogs**: Sphagnum, cloudberry, cross-leaved heath, deergrass, purple moor-grass, bog asphodel; bog myrtle and cotton-grass (unverified). On Jæren most bogs were drained after the 1860s.

**The shore.** Northern Jæren: dunes held by planted marram grass, damp lime-rich meadows with orchids behind; south of Sola, ridges of rounded stones; protected landscape since 1977. Cast-up kelp in banks after storms, once carted for fertiliser ([SNL](https://snl.no/drivtare)). Thrift and sea campion; lyme grass (unverified). Rosa rugosa: a garden shrub found wild in Norway from the 1940s, taking dunes, shingle and road banks by suckers, birds and sea-borne hips, now banned ([SNL](https://snl.no/rynkerose)); its extent on Jæren in 1989 is unknown, a good dated spreading front.

**Farmland.** Stone walls from stones cleared off the fields, most from the 1700s and 1800s, double walls with infill or single with the biggest stones at the foot; Hå has more than any other municipality; machines have removed many. Hay racks (hesjer) common from the 1700s, almost gone after the war with silos and later bales ([SNL](https://snl.no/hesje)); gone from Jæren's mechanised farms by 1989 (unverified, likely). Wrapped round bales: Underhaug at Nærbø on Jæren built its first bale wrapper in 1985, taken over by Kverneland in 1986 ([Farmers Journal](https://www.farmersjournal.ie/machinery/farm-machinery/big-bale-silage-a-45-year-retrospection-864249)), so in 1989 new but plausible; early film black (later colours unverified). Shelterbelts of Sitka, sycamore and Swedish whitebeam round farmsteads (unverified).

**Planted forest.** The state's planting (skogreising) on the west coast from 1952, peaking in 1964–65; Norway spruce inland, Sitka and Lutz spruce on the outer coast; the state paid half, the municipality a quarter ([SNL](https://snl.no/skogreising)). Sitka: about 55,000 daa in Rogaland, mostly planted 1950 to 1990; blue-green, dead sharp lower branches, a dark floor, seeds far on the wind ([SNL](https://snl.no/sitkagran)). So in 1989 the plantations are 10 to 35 years old: dense, even, dark. Sørmarka in Stavanger planted from 1908 at Stavanger Aftenblad's initiative, school classes from 1910: spruce, pine, beech, larch, oak, birch, holly, maple, hazel, rowan, aspen.

**The town.** Gamle Stavanger, workers' cottages of 1770 to 1900, protected from 1956 (35 houses), 79 in 1965, 154 in 1975 ([lokalhistoriewiki](https://lokalhistoriewiki.no/wiki/Gamle_Stavanger)); seen from above it has few trees. 7,000 to 8,000 wooden houses in all. Byparken's old beeches, said to come from a shipwreck's salvage, given to the bishop, about 150 years ago; Breiavatnet's fountain from 1924. Sycamore, probably brought in the 1700s, much planted in parks and avenues on the coast, seeds itself into neglected ground and spreads ([SNL](https://snl.no/platanl%C3%B8nn)). Paving joints: annual meadow-grass and procumbent pearlwort, with silver moss in damp shaded joints ([OV20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_NVC_community_OV20)); weed cover is highest where herbicide rules are strictest, so the council's policy is a dated fact ([SLU](https://research.slu.se/en/publications/weed-occurrence-on-pavements-in-five-north-european-towns/)). Wall-rue needs lime, so it marks mortared walls ([SNL](https://snl.no/murburkne)); maidenhair spleenwort and common polypody on walls and rock. Nettles follow phosphate from people and livestock and stay on long-abandoned sites. Plants of old habitation (unverified here): cow parsley, ground elder, dandelion, greater plantain, fireweed, dock, coltsfoot. Gardens: rhododendron thrives in the mild, wet, acid climate; lilac, currants, gooseberry, rhubarb, apple, roses, hydrangea, lawns and hedges of privet, hawthorn, beech or thuja (the thuja hedge of the 1970s and 80s) all unverified, to check against period photographs (Stavanger byarkiv, digitaltmuseum).

## What it suggests for our model

These are inputs for `docs/nature.md`, to be tested before they become design:

- **Fields** computed from the land: elevation, slope, aspect, curvature, flow and wetness, sun hours with the houses' shadows (the sky map), sky view; exposure to the prevailing south-west to north-west wind, with shelter behind walls and houses; salt spray falling off from the sea; substrate (rock, thin soil, moraine, peat, sand, shingle, made ground, paving and its joints). From facts: the nutrient legacy of middens and yards, trampling, years since the last disturbance, distance to a seed source (whether Sitka invades depends on a stand nearby).
- **Communities** as a pack's data, each a dominant species, companions, pattern, edge and response to tending: dry and wet heath, bog, rock banded by distance from the sea, shore banded by height, ley and pasture, a stone wall's foot, garden, paving joints, mown verge, waste ground, park, plantation, shelterbelt, broadleaf scrub. Each species a viability curve per field and a tolerance to each kind of tending.
- **Land use and tending as dated facts** for `docs/engine-requests.md`: parcels with a land-use history ("heath until 1884, ley from 1884, houses from 1966"), regimes with dates (burning, grazing, mowing, hedge clipping, herbicide on paving, each garden's care from its household), planting events (species, spacing, stock), notable trees as entities, and the pack's era tables (rugosa's spread, Sitka planting 1950–90, wrapped bales from 1985, protection from 1956 and 1977).
- **Succession** as a state machine per parcel or cell, run by the years since the last disturbance, with a seeded threshold per cell so a spreading front is ragged but repeatable; **seasons** per species from the date and the pack's climate, shifted by the year's weather and the microclimate (exposure delays, the town's warmth advances), each plant a few seeded days off its neighbours.
- **Placement in tiers** (canopy, shrubs, field layer, ground, cracks), each writing footprint and shade for the next; clusters seeded round parents at a scale per species (clonal plants tight, bird-sown scattered); edges from the community's depth; rare accents from a low-frequency presence field with a cap; plants writing back into the ground (a mound, roots, a worn path).
- **Alive**: one wind field from the day's weather with gusts from moving noise, local eddies at corners, vehicles and feet, each species its own stiffness; falling leaves from the phenology; animals by habitat (sheep are facts; gulls, crows, sparrows in hedges, thrushes on rowans ambient); rain darkening bark and making moss glisten.
- **Distance**: the same species mix gives each cell its colour per season, so the far ground is the colour of what grows on it; heath, ley and plantation kept as three clearly different values.
- **Other cities**: the field computers, the tiered placer, succession, phenology and wind are shared; the pack brings climate, species, communities, tending by era, the shapes of human order and dated spreads (London: planes and buddleia on railway land; New York: street-tree programmes and ailanthus on vacant lots; not researched).
