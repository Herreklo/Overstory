# Houses in games

Which games have the best-looking houses and clusters seen from our city camera (50 to 150 m away, 15 to 25° down), and what craft made them read so well. Øystein asked whether we should go hunting for them. This is the hunt. The references we already hold are not repeated here: Dishonored 2, Disco Elysium, Townscaper, Bad North, Lake, Tiny Glade, Inside, The Witness, Firewatch, Stålenhag, Sohlberg and Hopper.

Written on Opus, 27 October 2026. Nothing was downloaded. Every image below is a candidate for Øystein to approve file by file. Claims from a maker's own words are unmarked; those from write-ups are marked (secondary).

Few games have houses that are amazing from above. Most famous towns win on air, water or density, and their houses are plain kit. Several shots below are here for one lesson only, and they say which.

## The shots, best first

1. Diablo IV, a timber house in Nevesk, from the game's raised camera (Steven Hong, environment artist). A log house under a heavy snow roof, on a stone plinth, with snow drifted against its walls. Page: https://shong1123.artstation.com/projects/Za41g1 (its first image). Why it works: the roof is the biggest and darkest shape, and it sags under its load. The plinth and the drift give a contact shade you can read from well above. Nothing on it is small. The camera is closer than ours, so the lesson is weight, not distance.
2. Manor Lords, a winter village from about 100 m. Page: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1363080/Manor_Lords/. Image: https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/1363080/ce313af5e85391c7ebee3b654a35a7ab78a1fe38/ss_ce313af5e85391c7ebee3b654a35a7ab78a1fe38.1920x1080.jpg. Why it works: each house is a steep roof plane first and walls second. Fences draw the plots. A church tower crowns the cluster. Its summer village (https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/1363080/bffe9f34abaae09855becd6e8ca2ddb9065b2ebb/ss_bffe9f34abaae09855becd6e8ca2ddb9065b2ebb.1920x1080.jpg) adds the plan. Houses stand gable-on to the lane on long plots, gardens run behind, green gaps separate them, and the roads follow the ground.
3. Frostpunk, the city at night from overhead. Page: https://store.steampowered.com/app/323190/Frostpunk/. Image: https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/323190/ss_7828d395f976c49ecc7ffd6b54e691dd215359db.1920x1080.jpg. Why it works: the frame is cold slate blue, and windows and lamps are the only warm. Density reads as warmth. The houses themselves are tokens, so we take the light, not the houses.
4. Kingdom Come: Deliverance II, Kuttenberg from the hill. Page: https://gamerant.com/kingdom-come-deliverance-2-things-want-see-sequel-kcd3/. The same view is in a capture at https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KUU9WjMIhkE (thumbnail https://i.ytimg.com/vi/KUU9WjMIhkE/maxresdefault.jpg). Why it works: the far town is one band of pale lit walls and dark roofs, with spires rising out of it under a big sky. It is the composition of Ruisdael's views of Haarlem.
5. Anno 1800, an Old World town by the sea. Page: https://store.steampowered.com/app/916440/Anno_1800/. Image: https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/916440/8fa91f099e9f4ae991827c8186edc2d2094df37d/ss_8fa91f099e9f4ae991827c8186edc2d2094df37d.1920x1080.jpg. Why it works: steep gables, dormers and chimneys make each roof a character, so the town reads by its roofs. It is busy and airless, though. Take the shapes, not the density.
6. Red Dead Redemption 2, Annesburg from above. Page: https://screenrant.com/rdr2-towns-ranked-valentine-saint-denis-blackwater/. Image: https://static1.srcdn.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/red-dead-redemption-2-annesburg.jpg. Another view: https://w.atwiki.jp/rdr2jp/pages/304.html. Why it works: it is the nearest to our subject. Painted timber with white trim sits on a rocky slope by the water, with smoke rising. In flat light its values go muddy, and the white trim is what keeps the houses apart.
7. What Remains of Edith Finch, the house seen across the forest at dusk. Page: https://www.skritz.com/what-remains-of-edith-finch/. Image: https://www.skritz.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/FinchGame-2017-05-24-19-04-31-79.jpg. Why it works: a stack of additions stands against a gold fog sky. You read the family's history in the outline before you see a window.
8. Draugen, a white sveitserstil house in 1920s Norway, with a veranda, a flag and a picket fence on heather and rock. Page: https://geeksleeprinserepeat.com/2019/05/28/draugen/. Image: https://geeksleeprinserepeat.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/draugen-4.jpg. Why it works: it is our exact subject. A white body, a dark roof, two chimneys, and the fence as a found edge. It is hazy and seen at eye level, good but not stunning. Its lesson is a warning: the white wall almost vanishes against a pale sky, so the roof has to carry the house.

## The craft

1. From above, roofs and broad weathering matter more than fine detail. David Shelton (Ubisoft Mainz) says Anno's camera makes roofs and large weathering more prominent than the grout between bricks. Each building is a hero asset, a character, and shows its work outside ([Ubisoft News](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/355243/Create-The-Unknown-How-Anno-1800-Put-A-Creative-Spin-on-the-Industrial-Revolution)). For us: spend a house's detail on its roof plane, ridge, eaves, chimney and big stains. Clapboard and trim belong to the near view.
2. Detail is added or removed for the camera. Chris Ryder (art director, environments, Diablo IV) describes choosing where to add or remove detail so the space reads. He takes the old masters as a pillar, with Rembrandt's control of detail, tone and colour, and aims for "believability, not realism". Brian Fletcher calls the fixed camera one of their best tools. Ben Hutchings makes fog, soft shadows and bounce central to the look ([Blizzard, March 2022](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/23788294/diablo-iv-quarterly-updatemarch-2022)). Mueller told GameSpot the team works with bigger shapes and cuts what hurts readability ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/diablo-4s-art-direction-emphasizes-hand-crafted-details-plenty-of-customization-and-just-enough-realism/1100-6493411/)). For us: this is our removal rule, practised at scale. Each distance band names what it drops, and we judge it at the camera's own distance.
3. Houses are built from what the place had. In the same update, Matt McDaid describes Scosglen's coastal villages as stone walls, salvaged wood and thatch, in disrepair, with docks and slipways. Kyovashad is local pine boards and birch shingles, function over form. Chaz Head gives each district its own culture kit. For us: material from source, working and exposure, as our rules already say. A quay house can take salvaged and tarred timber, and each area gets its own kit.
4. Wealth climbs, and the crown is seen from afar. Michał Janiszewski says Toussaint sits on a cliff with poor districts at the foot and richer ones higher up ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/world-building-of-witcher-3/)). Kacper Niepokólczycki says Beauclair was placed high to be seen from far off and to guide players without a map, and the city was designed before the terrain ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2016/05/10/how-the-witcher-3-team-built-the-land-of-toussaint-for-upcoming-expansion-blood-wine/)). In Kuttenberg, wealthy citizens paid for the churches that shape the skyline ([Deep Silver](https://www.deepsilver.com/games/kingdom-come-deliverance-ii/news/kuttenberg-a-medieval-metropolis)). For us: the crown is the landmark from the quay, and height follows status only where the town's facts put it.
5. No grid, everything slightly off. Janiszewski again: greyboxes first, then compositions for each street and corner. Many modular meshes are reused, with texture arrays per area. Nothing snaps to a grid, and the slight imperfection makes it believable. Curving streets also limit how much is visible at once. For us: the generator places parts off-grid with small seeded error (non-semantic texture), and our curving lanes earn their keep as a draw-cost tool too.
6. Only the windows are warm, and roofs must differ from the ground. Frostpunk's palette is royal and steel blues, every colour cold and bleached, so the art amplifies the design. Under snow it has no roof models. For autumn, red roofs were added so buildings stood out against the green ([Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2021/07/21/how-the-visual-identity-of-frostpunk-changed/)). For us: this confirms our warm-only-from-lamps rule. It also means a roof must part from the ground in every season and light, not only from its own wall.
7. History reads as addition. The Finch house was built up over generations from added modules. Its height is what stands out, like a half-played game of Jenga, and rooms were sealed when their owners died ([Game Developer, Reeve](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-4d-finch-house), secondary). For us: an added storey, a lean-to or an outhouse from the diaries changes the outline. A house that grew, or was left, then reads at 100 m before its paint does.
8. The plot is part of the house. Manor Lords' residential plots follow medieval burgage plots, so a house comes with its yard, garden and fence. They are laid out without a grid and grow with a workshop or a second family ([nyra.nyc](https://nyra.nyc/articles/serf-city), [Epic Games Store news](https://store.epicgames.com/news/manor-lords-solo-developer-s-highly-anticipated-medieval-strategy-game-hits-this-month), secondary). The plot cycle is already in `urban-design.md`. What is new here is visual: fence, garden and wall make the gaps between houses as designed as the houses.

## What we should take

1. Roofs first. At 100 m a house is its roof plane, ridge, chimney and eave shadow. Design pitch, overhang and chimney as the house's signature. Keep the roof darker than the wall in every light and apart from the ground in every season (Manor Lords, Anno, Draugen's warning).
2. In the cool hour, warm windows are the only warm. The far town becomes one band of pale lit walls and dark roofs with the crown above it (Frostpunk, Kuttenberg).
3. Weight reads from above. The stone plinth is wider and darker than the wall, the junction is shaded, and wet or snow gathers at the foot (Nevesk).
4. History in the outline. Additions, lean-tos, raised storeys and sheds from the facts change the silhouette, so growth and neglect read before paint does (Finch).
5. Plots, not only houses. Fences, gardens, steps and retaining walls draw the gaps. Placement is off-grid with seeded small error, roof tones stay a narrow family, and the walls carry the colour (Manor Lords, Witcher 3).

## Checked and rejected

- The Witcher 3's Beauclair and Kaer Trolde. Beauclair gives every house its own saturated colour and looks like toys from above. Kaer Trolde's harbour houses are dark kit swallowed by the cliffs. The craft notes above are good; the houses are not. Novigrad's single red roof field was a borderline shot, left out because no source page for the image was found.
- Assassin's Creed Valhalla's Rygjafylke, our own region. Cluttered wharves and longhouses, few houses, nothing composed to read from above.
- Clair Obscur: Expedition 33's Lumière. Full detail at every distance, the opposite of our removal rule.

Also looked at and passed over: Alba and Tchia (the cosy stylised look VD1 rules out), Sea of Thieves (cartoon-chunky outposts; its lesson is already in `materials-at-scale.md`), Ghost of Tsushima (plain houses; the beauty is wind and colour fields), Islanders and Dorfromantik (houses as tokens), Pentiment (side-on 2D, though its town changes across decades), Forza Horizon 4, Pillars of Eternity and SEASON. Hellblade 2 and The Sinking City were not reached.
