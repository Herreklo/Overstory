# How the best build trees

Research for the tree system (`docs/trees.md`), October 2026. What shipped games, tools, film studios and research do, and what we take. Claims from summaries or forums are marked (secondary); claims not confirmed are marked (unverified).

## The bottom line

- Our design (a tree's life grown once, deterministically, and read at a date, so age 31 contains age 30) is the right shape. Most games do not do this: they swap whole assets or play a canned growth animation.
- Growth: Palubicki et al. 2009 (self-organising trees): buds compete for light through a cheap voxel shadow grid; apical control, shedding of shaded branches, and a pipe model that keeps the memory of lost branches; pruning between steps. Offline, seconds to minutes per tree.
- Art direction from three places: designed crown volumes as growth envelopes (Runions 2007, Weber & Penn 1995, Disney's canopy shells on *Tangled*); hand-made clumps and twig modules instanced on branch ends (The Grove, Synthetic Silviculture); and normals taken from proxy shapes so a crown shades as masses. The proxies must be structured masses, never one sphere.
- Wind: hierarchical vertex animation driven by a global gust field; near consensus (SpeedTree, Crysis, Pivot Painter, Book of the Dead, Ghost of Tsushima). One or two independent branch levels.
- Seasons: a per-leaf threshold against a seasonal clock (SpeedTree); no game found blends between hand-made season states.
- Unity 6: share clump meshes and instance them through the GPU Resident Drawer (no MaterialPropertyBlock: per-tree data through DOTS-instanced properties or vertex data); each tree a unique, cheap trunk and branch mesh; impostors far off.

## Tools and research

- **SpeedTree.** Generators are the rules; a node edit is stored as an offset and "subject to being lost" when the generator above changes, so hand authoring belongs in parts and masses, not in edits on generated output. Its growth is a timeline animation into the final form (exported as Alembic), not a tree that contains its younger self. Seasons: a 0 to 1 slider where each node takes its own transition from a start offset and time scale with variance, so neighbouring leaves differ; exported as a single state, "not suitable for smoothly animating season changes". LOD: branch vertices lerp to stored positions; "fewer and bigger leaf instances" with distance; a cross-fade to billboards with alpha-to-coverage.
- **The Grove** (Blender, Houdini): grows year by year; new leaves shade old, shaded branches wither and drop; ray-traced light; pruning, dead wood and regrowth after pruning or damage; twigs, leaves, flowers and fruit as instanced models on branch ends; per-branch attributes (vigour, shade, age) drive materials. Game LOD reduction since 2.2, no direct export pipeline (secondary).
- **Sapling** (Weber & Penn 1995 in Blender): seeded, a pruning envelope (PruneWidth, PruneWidthPeak, power curves, PruneRatio), two summed sines per stem for sway; the paper draws a tree at 2 km as about 30 lines and 1,000 points, scales leaves by 1/√quality as their count drops, and rejected discrete levels because they make "resolution waves" flow through a canopy.
- **EZ-Tree** (MIT): seeded; 10k to 40k triangles a tree; LODs at about 40% and 20%.
- **Palubicki et al. 2009.** Each bud adds Δs = a·b^(−q) to a pyramid of voxels below it; light Q = max(C − s + a, 0); a 200³ grid, shadow depth 4 to 8 layers. Extended Borchert-Honda allocation: λ above 0.5 favours the main axis, and reducing apical control with age "yields mature tree forms". A branch whose light falls below a threshold for its size is shed. Pipe model dⁿ = d₁ⁿ + d₂ⁿ, n = 2 to 3, width not reduced when branches are shed: the memory "age N+1 contains age N" needs. A 700,000-internode tree in 82 s in 2009.
- **Space colonisation** (Runions et al. 2007): a crown envelope filled with attraction points, a radius of influence and a kill distance; the envelope shapes follow landscaping's crown types.
- **Synthetic Silviculture** (Makowski et al. 2019): a small set of branch-module prototypes reused across plants, adapted as growth runs; about 500,000 plants drawn with instanced modules. The method of "parts assembled by generators".
- **L-systems** are the classic formalism; Disney found them unintuitive for artists.

## Games and studios

- **Firewatch:** 23 hand-modelled trees, placed 4,600 times; a custom stylising shader (details unverified). Jane Ng: pines are "the worst" to make in 3D.
- **Ghost of Tsushima:** hundreds of procedurally grown trees, rigged to respond to local wind (trunk and branch controls, noise ripple on leaves); two shared gust layers (a large scrolling noise field and a finer one); tens of thousands of falling leaves from one particle system following the camera; art direction: fewer foliage types per biome, stronger values, more translucency, less texture noise; a GPU placement language for millions of instances.
- **Horizon Zero Dawn** (secondary): trees from SpeedTree, cross-plane billboards at the far levels; shadow LOD chosen by distance popped, fixed by a set LOD per shadow cascade with separate shadow casters; a global wind field with three tiers of motion; GPU placement deterministic and "locally stable".
- **Crysis** (GPU Gems 3, ch. 16): main bending renormalised to keep length ("too much bending will ruin the illusion"); vertex colours for edge stiffness, leaf phase, leaf stiffness and occlusion; translucency from a small painted map with wrapped back-light.
- **Unity's Book of the Dead:** the branch hierarchy in a vertex colour channel, pivots baked into UV channels (secondary). **Pivot Painter 2:** pivot, basis, extents, parent and depth per element, four levels.
- **Seasons in games:** Forza Horizon 4 remodelled its world four times; Assassin's Creed Shadows grows grass tall and bushes leafy in spring, bare in winter, with snow that buries; Cities: Skylines II trees age, die and reseed, pause in winter, colour in autumn (secondary); Flight Simulator 2024 follows date and latitude by ecoregion (secondary).
- **Film:** Disney's *Tangled*: artists sketch skeleton curves, twigs grow out to polygonal canopy shells, seeds give variants, stochastic pruning for distance. Pixar interpolates branching between art-directed elements and simplifies aggregate detail stochastically. DreamWorks shades canopies with aggregate normals, because per-leaf lighting gives "unappealing visual noise".
- Not found: how *Breath of the Wild*, *Genshin Impact*, *Red Dead Redemption 2*, *Sable* and *The Witness* build their trees (only community recreations). *The Witness*'s verified contribution is coverage-preserving alpha mipmaps (Castaño).

## Stylised foliage

- **Proxy normals:** normals copied from a hull onto the leaf cards so a crown shades as masses; without it, rotated cards give "ugly, sudden changes". Tutorials use one sphere, which is the round blob our direction forbids; we use a few faceted mass hulls per species and age (the painter's three to five masses, with sky between), each clump taking its normals from its own mass.
- **Air and silhouette** (Simon Barle): leave air between leaves, or cards read as sheets and mip to "an opaque mess"; let branches run long before they split, or trees look like bonsai; a few anchor trees with unique trunks; never line trees up (unless they are an avenue, which is a fact).
- **Painters:** Kazuo Oga gets the essential point across rather than rendering everything, works from observation and a palette of about 21 colours; plein-air practice reduces a tree to a few masses and about four values, the sky holes counting as much as the masses; Stålenhag works from photographs, repainted into a matte, gouache-like finish (nothing found in his words about trees).
- **Back-light:** a wrapped back term times a thickness map; turned up as a style choice in Tsushima.
- **Alpha edges:** ordinary mips thin alpha-tested foliage with distance; coverage-preserving mips fix it; alpha-to-coverage needs MSAA; without it, hashed alpha testing under TAA.

## Wind

SpeedTree's levels: shared sway (the trunk's only motion), branch, ripple, each faster; only one or two branch levels independent, the second the most expensive; gusts with strength, frequency and duration; a setting de-synchronises instances. A global field (Tsushima's two scrolling noise layers; Horizon's force field). Unity's WindZone does not drive SpeedTree trees. For us: hierarchy level, pivot and phase baked into vertex data; a deterministic gust field over world position and time, seeded by the day's weather; fine motion fading with distance.

## Distance and performance

- **GPU Resident Drawer:** needs Forward+, the SRP Batcher and DOTS-instancing shaders; no MaterialPropertyBlock; pays when many objects share a mesh; slightly higher GPU cost, merged draws can add overdraw. Reports (secondary): no gain for terrain trees, no animated LOD cross-fade with it.
- **Cross-fade:** a URP setting, 2×2 stencil dither; falls back to alpha test with GPU instancing. **Mesh LOD** (Unity 6.2+) makes foliage parts disappear at its lowest levels: not for canopies.
- **Canopy LOD:** fewer, larger leaves (SpeedTree, Weber & Penn, Pixar); a few low-poly density shells hidden in the crown's core let the next level drop to about a third of the triangles without thinning.
- **Impostors:** octahedral, a hemisphere layout for foliage, about 16 views, albedo, normal and depth; not automatically faster (one report: 6.4 to 7.3 ms).
- **Budgets** are scarce: 5k to 100k facets (Weber & Penn), 10k to 40k (EZ-Tree), 17.6k at LOD0 (one engine). Ours, to measure: about 20k to 30k triangles at the street stop, about a third at the next level, branches and clumps after that, an impostor past about 150 m. Overdraw is the real cost (8 to 15 times in dense forest, secondary): "triangles over overdraw".

## Seasons and time

- No game blends between hand-made season states. The best model for a shader is SpeedTree's threshold with variance: each clump has a seeded rank; the species' phenology at the date sets colour and the drop threshold; clumps fall in rank order, so the same leaves always go first (deterministic). Snow from a world-up mask on the branches, scaled by the snow's depth.
- Phenology: USA-NPN's phenophases (breaking leaf buds, leaves, coloured leaves, falling leaves); marcescence (oak and beech keep dead leaves) is its own case. Chilling-plus-forcing models predict leaf-out better than degree-days for most species; ours: the date and the year's weather against each species' thresholds.
- Age: read the timeline at the year; calibrate growth to real sizes with the USDA Urban Tree Database (age, trunk diameter, height, crown for 171 species in 16 US climate regions; for New York); Norwegian and London curves not yet found. City packs: New York's 2015 census (666,134 street trees with species, size and health); London: sycamore 7.8%, oak 7.3%, silver birch 6.2%, planes about a tenth of street trees (secondary).

## Why trees in games look cheap, and how the best avoid it

Regular recursion and bonsai proportions (self-organisation gives forms irregular up close and balanced overall); flat card sheets without air; noisy or snapping card shading; alpha thinning and shimmer; LOD waves and shadow popping; over-bending and synchronised sway; too many variants muddying the read; sphere-normal blobs.
