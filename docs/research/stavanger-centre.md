# Stavanger's centre, measured

What central Stavanger is like as it stands, measured from open data, as evidence for the town plan (`docs/town-plan.md`). The town is fictional; these numbers keep its grain and scale believable. Measured 20 October 2026 from OpenStreetMap (buildings, streets, steps and coastline in a 1.6 by 1.6 km window round Vågen) and the EU-DEM elevation model (25 m resolution, sampled every 100 m). EU-DEM is a surface model, so heights over dense blocks may include some building height.

## Vågen

- The inner basin is about 450 m long from its head to the tip of the peninsula on its east side (Holmen and Skagen), widening from about 60 m at the head to about 140 m halfway and about 160 m at the mouth.
- Past the peninsula it opens into a much wider outer harbour (Østre havn and Byfjorden), with islands beyond.
- The basin is lined with quays on both sides; the head is the market square, with the cathedral just behind it.

The old town's houses are small (about ten by seven and a half metres) and close, yet cover only a quarter of the ground: the rest is lanes, yards and gardens. The peninsula covers forty per cent with bigger, deeper buildings: the warehouse grain.

**Steps.** 18 flights in the old town (134 m in all, a median flight of 5 m) and 33 on the peninsula (172 m, a median of 3 m): steps are everywhere, short and frequent, not a few grand stairs.

## Heights

- The old town's slope climbs from the quay to about 25 to 35 m within 200 m (a grade of about 15%), then to a plateau of 50 to 55 m (Eiganes and Bergeland).
- The peninsula is low, 6 to 15 m, with the knoll of Valberget at about 15 m carrying its tower.
- The lake (Breiavatnet) lies in a hollow at about 12 m behind the cathedral.
- To the south-east, Storhaug rises to about 35 m.

So Stavanger's own drama is modest: a 15% slope, a low knoll, a hollow with a lake. What makes it is the grain and the water: dense small houses on a slope above a narrow basin, a packed peninsula of warehouses, and steps everywhere.
