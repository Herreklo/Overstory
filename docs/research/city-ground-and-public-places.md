# City ground and public places

Research for plan step 6: the ground the town walks and drives on, its public places, signs beyond shops and the utilities on the surface, in Stavanger 1870 to 2020 with 1989 first; how games build them, their cost on the M4, and whose facts they are. It does not repeat the town notes. Written 29 October 2026; nothing here is decided.

Marks: (secondary) from a summary or wiki; (unverified) unconfirmed; (ours) our reading. A photo id is Stavanger byarkiv's (`stavangerbilder.no`, archive 5010-Foto), as we read it. Much of the town's street record is offline, so some answers are Oslo's or the nation's.

## In short

1. **1989's ground was studded.** About 90% of cars ran studs in winter (secondary), so busy streets had wheel-path ruts and markings worn off by spring. By 2025 only 10% did in Stavanger, with a fee since 2017.
2. **Each square had its job.** In 1989 Torget sold, Domkirkeplassen held the buses and the Christmas tree, Arneageren held speeches and protests. The town sat on the Torgtrappene (1962 to about 2007); the rebuild took them, so where people sit follows the form.
3. **A stall and a seat are habits.** One fishmonger kept one stall from 1982 to 1993, and the goods follow the year. Counts confirm "sparse is right": about three people in view on 100 m of the Mosvatnet path at its peak hour.
4. **Removal leaves a mark, and keepers change.** A caretaker rolled a 1977 slogan out in white. The power passed to Lyse (1998), the phones to Telenor (1995).
5. **The ground goes in our history pages, not URP decals.** URP's decals are lit by URP, not the town's shade function, their DBuffer path suits tile GPUs badly, and virtual texturing does not run on the M4.
6. **Traffic and people run from the clock,** so a jump draws what watching would have drawn. A crowd round an incident is history: the engine's.

## 1. Roads and traffic

- **Surfaces.** No Stavanger dates for tar or asphalt were found. Oslo asphalted footways from 1886 and carriageways from 1922, only 18% by 1947, and covered worn setts after 1960 (secondary). Concrete pavers belong on newer squares in 1989, not old streets (ours). Ulefos made street castings from 1952, some with the town's arms (secondary). Resurfacing is a dated council work ("Kongsgata – asfaltering", 2019–22, primary).
- **Studs, salt, frost.** Studs came in the 1960s and wear 5–20 g of asphalt per km each (SNL). They are banned from the Monday after Easter Monday to 31 October. Winter ruts are stud wear, summer ruts deformation. Salt spread on public roads from about 1970 (secondary); Stavanger's 1989 practice was not found. The mild coast means fewer freeze-thaw cycles than Oslo (ours, unmeasured).
- **Signs and lines.** Rules of 1967 and 1980 govern a 1989 junction. The crossing sign's man in a hat dates from 1980 and gave way to genderless figures in 2006 (Norsk vegmuseum), so a hatted figure dates a street. Yellow lines divide opposing traffic (N302).
- **Buses.** S.O.T. (1975) started and ended at Torget. On 19 December 1989 a queue stood in rain at Breiavatnet by a small plate on a pole, with no shelter (B232_024). Byterminalen was a building site that September (B268_004).
- **Parking.** Oslo had meters from 1958; Stavanger set up 200 trial ones in 1965 (secondary). A 1994 power cut stopped them (B299_002).
- **Others.** Taxi ranks had huts with a phone from the 1930s (secondary). Bicycles rode with the cars in 1989 (unverified).
- **Kirkegata in 1960 rests only on travel guides.** The council's 2023 status lists a Breigata–Kirkegata pedestrian *trial* for 2019–22 (primary), so the street was probably not wholly car-free before (ours).
- **Paths.** Gravel sheds water at a cross-fall of 1 in 40 (secondary). Paths widen where wet, rough and flat, and soil moisture predicts it best (Evju et al., secondary). The rule from facts is hollow × wetness × feet.

## 2. Public places

- **The stall is a season's clock.** Torget's stalls held vegetables on 13 March 1989 (B286_077), the first carrots on 17 June 1989 (B286_080), berries and knitwear for visitors in summer, then wreaths and Christmas trees (C119_138). Rain empties the square (B286_032). One fishmonger stands at one stall in 1982 and 1993 (B456_030, B456_020).
- **Seats.** The Torgtrappene were full on 13 August 1984 (B286_102). People also sat facing Breiavatnet and on quay edges. A bench burnt in 1983 (C136_044) is a fact about people, never texture.
- **The other squares.** Domkirkeplassen's tree was lit on 4 December 1989 (A632_029). Arneageren had a campaign speech on 14 August 1989 (B214_011) and a protest against McDonald's on 19 March 1990 (C034_041).
- **Parks.** Byparken had birds fed in winter and an orchestra in the pavilion (B232_013, B265_030); "Usikker is" (unsafe ice) stood on Breiavatnet in 1978 (C186_007).
- **Counts.** The Mosvatnet counter (2019, primary) has a median of 1,341 walkers a day. It peaks at 18:00–20:00 on weekdays and 12:00–14:00 at weekends; July is 18% below June. People in view = arrivals per hour × seconds in view ÷ 3,600 (Little's law).
- **Playgrounds** (Stavanger kommune 2021, primary). Swings and sandpits until the 1950s. Old boats in sand in the 1960s, when the 1965 law required play areas in new housing. Standard equipment after the 1976 product law. Rubber after the 1996 regulation and EN 1176 (1998). Use follows kindergarten: one child in five went in 1980, so playgrounds were busy at 10:00; by 2000 they were empty until 16:00.
- **Cemeteries.** Lagård opened in 1834 and Eiganes in 1910 (secondary). Stones date by burial: obelisks in the 1890s, cast iron about 1900, standard rectangles in the 1950s and lawns by the 1970s (Trondheim 2021, primary). A grave is leased for 20 years, renewable, and its holder tends it (Haugesund bylaws, primary), so a neglected grave is a family's fact.
- **Grounds and quays.** Stavanger stadion (1917) also held 17 May (secondary). Outdoor serving shows from 1986 (B232_015). Spring-tide floods drew onlookers in 1990 and 1993 (A157_013).
- **The calendar.** 17 May, 1 May, St Hans, All Saints' candles, the tree lighting, market days, the election (11 September 1989), MaiJazz from 1989; Gladmat's first year is disputed (1998–2000). The date is the calendar's; who marched or stayed away is the people's.

## 3. Signs and utilities

- **Plates and numbers.** A dark plate with light capitals at about 2.5 m in 1909 (Digitalt Museum 021015822568); a small white plate with dark letters at about 3 m in 1981 (BySt015_sefrak_045_037), as sign 729 is now. Fire-insurance numbers ran beside later street numbers (secondary).
- **Notices and paint.** On 12 July 1989 a taped "STENGT!" sheet closed the toilet under Torget (B552_034). Election slogans covered walls in 1977, and a caretaker rolled one out in white (B583_063). 1989's walls carry slogans and paint-outs, before tags (ours). Nuart's murals are a layer from 2001.
- **Wires and kiosks.** A 1930s Kannik pole had eight crossarms of open wire (BySt010_0750). By 1989 they were gone from the inner town, and in 1987 Televerket spliced cable in a dug street (C158_091). A 1985 transformer kiosk in Gamle Stavanger was rendered, with a slate roof, steel doors and the town's arms (A-0408_Uc_1935).
- **Boxes.** Two coin-only red Riks kiosks stood joined in 1989 (C158_068); the last went silent in 2016. Post boxes peaked near 30,000 in the 1980s and are about 15,000 now; yellow local-post boxes joined the red from 1989 (SNL).
- **Bins.** Metal pails in 1982, four-wheeled containers in aggregate housings by 1989 (C037_111, C037_037). The June 1992 refuse strike left Strandkaien's container overflowing (C037_149). Buried containers by 2006 (TU). Roof sirens (from 1936) sound at noon on a Wednesday in January and June.

## 4. The craft

**Ground.**
- The Division 2 stamps markings, oil and tyre tracks into the terrain's base texture, and turned manholes from props into decals (Massive, primary).
- Spider-Man's Houdini roads write vertex colour that blends two layers and paints puddles, so puddles cross the kerb (Insomniac, primary).
- Cities: Skylines II's surface decals take about 1 ms on HDRP (secondary).
- In URP, DBuffer decals need a depth-normal prepass, "less efficient" on tile GPUs. Screen-space decals blend no normals, and both bypass `StreetLooks.hlsl` (Unity 6 docs).

| Mark | How (ours) |
| --- | --- |
| Patches, trenches, ruts, grit, puddles | texels of 6–12 cm in the street's history pages, read once by the street shader |
| Lines and crossings | opaque mesh strips in the street shader, worn from the page |
| Covers, grates, kerbs | instanced fitted parts near, folded into the page far |
| Plates, numbers, posters, notices | opaque quads from one atlas array, by the Renderer Shader User Value |
| Wires | ribbons never under one pixel wide, faded by lost coverage (Humus 2012), sag from the span |

Police Simulator places a prop by rule when it joins traffic or repeats over 50–100 times (Aesir, primary). Watch Dogs' ctOS suggests a utility plays only if its network is a fact (ours).

**Crowds.**
- Hitman 2 has 1,797 agents and 700 visible: 25 within 7 m, 300 within 22 m. It promotes agents to full NPCs from a pool of 30 to 90 (IOI and Intel, primary).
- Assassin's Creed Unity gathers crowds round an incident (GDC abstract), and The Sims 4 invents townies to fill roles (secondary). Both invent history.
- Cyberpunk 2077 sets an hour, a count and a spot per period, the spot random per load (secondary); we seed it.
- Townscaper: use follows enclosure (Stålberg, primary).

**Schedules, not simulation.**
- A position is a pure function of who and when. Departure is the timetable plus hash(person, day, slot), and spots go in hash order.
- Each vehicle is a trip. Signals phase from the clock, and queues follow MATSim, acting only at link ends.
- Through traffic comes from Vegvesen's hourly profiles, as GTA V sets each zone's mix in two-hour slots (secondary).
- Every figure is an engine person or a counted visitor, re-seeded daily, who never takes a regular's spot.

**Cost on the M4** (estimates; the eye-on view is already over 16.7 ms):

| One street stop | GPU ms | CPU ms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ground pages, strips, covers | 0.2–0.4 | ~0 |
| Instead: screen-space decals / DBuffer | 0.3–0.8 / +1–3 | 0.05–0.1 |
| Plates, posters, boxes, wires | <0.2 | ~0 |
| People: 12–25 skinned near, 50–200 baked mid | 0.9–2.0 | 0.5–1.0 |
| Schedules: 2,000 people, 300 trips, Burst | 0 | 0.15–0.3 |

People cost most; skinned figures sit outside the Resident Drawer; 17 May is the worst case.

## Whose facts

### What Understory should own (candidates, extending filed requests)

Numbers are `docs/engine-requests.md`'s; the coordinator collects these.

- **Who uses which place when** (1, 16): stays per place and hour, a seller's pitch, a regular's seat. A decade at one stall is a life; drawn by us, it claims a habit nobody lived.
- **Traffic as people's trips** (1, 11, 16): each trip's mode and vehicle. The car outside is someone's day; only through traffic is ours.
- **A road dug up as an event** (6, 14, 32): actor, purpose, date, extent. A patch claims a dig, and the why-probe must name who dug.
- **Gatherings in public** (28, 31): incidents with place and time; the town's days with route and who came. A crowd round a theft or a flood is history.
- **Regimes as decisions** (23): pedestrian trials, meters, the stud fee, re-plating, each with its fate. What a street allows is a council choice that can lapse.
- **Places' uses and keepers** (13, 25): each use with the year it began, and its keeper (parks, church council, harbour). The market's shrinking was decided.
- **Grave leases** (9, 24): plot, holder, term, lapse. A tended or cleared grave is a family's history.
- **Outages and strikes** (new): dated, with extent. Their marks are consequences on the street (criterion 3).
- **Addresses with history** (15, 25): renaming and renumbering, so the plate and the record agree.

### What our town system and pack derive

- Surfaces, kerbs, covers and bins by era and street class; resurfacing and trenches until the engine has works (`derived`).
- Sign generations, line colours and plate styles by year.
- Marking wear (passes × studded share × years since repaint); ruts, cracks, potholes and puddles from passes, age, frost, hollows and upkeep; path widening from feet.
- Stops and meters from regimes; kiosks and boxes by density; spans from poles.
- A square's edges and pitches from enclosure and sun; playground kit from build year and its rules; a stone's look from the burial year; stall goods from a crop calendar; the calendar's dates.
- Counts by Little's law; through traffic and visitors per day from profiles and the timeline.

### What is non-semantic texture

Aggregate grain, crack layout inside a density that is a fact, joint jitter, oil and gum spots, flake and rust patterns scaled by a dated age, tape residue, sag jitter, a pole's lean, stride phase, the spot inside a slot, a visitor's clothes, birds by season.

## Three first tests

1. **Jump equals watch** (cheapest: capsules and a script). Torget in 1989 from one fixture, with ten trips, a bus on the quay road and people from timetables. *Pass:* the 08:10 frame is the same watched at 60× or jumped to. Counts at 10:00 on a March Tuesday and 11:00 on 17 May are within one of Little's law. CPU stays under 0.3 ms.
2. **The street's ground dates itself** (one history page and strips, in grey). Resurfaced 1979, trenches of 1961, 1974 and 1987, and March 1989's ruts and worn lines beside March 2020's. *Pass:* two cold readers order the patches and pick the earlier year, from the street stop and from 12 m. The ground costs under 0.4 ms on the harness.
3. **The seat moves with the form** (two clay forms of the square). Torget in 1984 with the steps and 2008 without, with the same trips and rules. *Pass:* in 1984 the sitters land where B286_102 and B286_040 show them. In 2008 they find the new edges with no new rule.
